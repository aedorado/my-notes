# Adhyātma Yoga: Spirituality, Mysticism & Sacred Signs
### Adhyātma Rekhā & Graha Yoga · Ring of Solomon, Intuition, Mystic Cross & Sacred Symbols

Clients rarely bring spirituality questions to a reading — career comes first, then married life and children — yet the hand still carries this layer, and the teacher introduces it as a "small chapter" built entirely from material already taught, now gathered from all sides into one place. Before any line or mount is read, the practitioner needs a perspective, because adhyātma means something different to each person, and that meaning must be drawn out through the client's deśa-kāla-paristhiti (place, time, and circumstance) and through their nature. From there, the reading proceeds through the hand's element, the first phalanges of the fingers, the planets Jupiter, Venus, and Ketu, specific mounts and signs, and finally the overall quality, colour, and line-density of the hand — each contributing one piece of a picture that is only ever read as a whole.

:::glance
::::left
### IN ONE MINUTE
◆ **No single "spirituality line" exists**: the reading is a synthesis of hand element, first phalanges, Jupiter/Moon/Ketu indicators, and rare signs — never one isolated mark.
◆ **Element sets the style, not the presence, of spirituality**: fire seeks pilgrimage/mission/mantra-japa; water seeks devotional absorption (prema mārga); earth practices quiet karma-as-pūjā; air researches and questions ("who am I?").
◆ **The rule of two-to-three points**: the teacher states explicitly that only when two, or better three or more, indicators converge should a reader lean toward predicting strong spiritual inclination.
◆ **Clients rarely ask about this**: spirituality questions are far less common than career, marriage, or children questions, and are not guaranteed to come up at all.

## 1. Foundational Principles & Anatomy

Asked what spirituality means, four or five students in the class will each give a different answer — and this variation is itself the starting principle. Five broad perspectives recur, and which one applies to a given client depends, as the teacher puts it, "on our thinking — how we take it":

| Perspective | Description (as taught) |
|---|---|
| **Religious / devotional** | Totally absorbed in īśvara bhakti; nothing else is visible. Does a great deal of meditation/sādhanā, reads scripture, and feels "this is how I follow spirituality." |
| **Practical / service-based** | May not perform pūjā-pāṭh or ring temple bells, but serves others, feeds others, and stays connected with nature — "that is my spirituality." |
| **Seeker / "who am I"** | Arises as ketu influence increases: the person begins asking "Who am I? Where did I come from?" and goes in search of an answer. |
| **Psychological / peace of mind** | A troubled person turns to spirituality for mental peace; this can be understood psychologically. |
| **Event-triggered** | Life was going well, then some event — a depression phase, or a problem with someone close — turns the person toward spirituality. |

In ordinary practice, this question rarely even comes up: career dominates client questions, married life and children follow, and spirituality questions are fewer still, with no guarantee of appearing in a session at all.

## 2. Hand Element Applied to Spirituality

The first and broadest check is the quality — the elemental type — of the hand, already studied earlier in the course and now simply re-applied to this one subject. The teacher is explicit that this gives only a general, "rough" picture; the individual hand always has the final say.

A **fiery hand** wants action in everything, the same thrill and adventure that defines it generally, so its spirituality rarely takes the form of quiet daily pūjā or renunciation (a student's own example). Such a person is drawn to yātrā (pilgrimage and travel), may take up yogāsana and link it to spiritual practice, may start a spiritual mission — gathering people around a purpose — and favours mantra japa precisely because it is a process with activity in it.

A **watery hand** carries emotion, imagination, and intuition into its spirituality. If such a person takes up mantra japa, they sit and become deeply absorbed in it. Their path runs through emotion — the prema mārga, the path of love, exemplified by Mīrā — where once a path or form is accepted, it is accepted with pure feeling. Intuition can run very sharp, and an interest in art naturally draws such a person toward kīrtana and bhajana.

An **earthy hand** is practical and karma-pradhāna (work-first). Such a person may simply continue the ancestral, traditional pūjā-pāṭh handed down by their forefathers, without any show of it — "karma itself is his pūjā." They tend to become nature-lovers, grounded people who take everything practically and look after the basic needs of those around them.

An **airy hand** keeps its bhakti rules changing — it cannot stay fixed in one place — and instead moves toward research and inquiry. It brings thought (vichāra) to the subject: a research mode that searches for answers to "Who am I? What is the ātmā? Where did I come from? Where did creation originate?" Such a person reads widely, may go to a guru, and is drawn to tarka-vitarka — debate. This type also tends to spread (prasāra) its bhakti outward — a student's own example was ISKCON, which the teacher agreed can fit this pattern, while noting that some fire is present there too, "but that's different for everyone."

| Hand type | Likely spiritual style |
|---|---|
| **Fiery** | Action, travel/yātrā, yoga, spiritual mission, mantra japa |
| **Watery** | Deep absorption, prema/bhakti mārga (Mīrā), kīrtana/bhajana, intuition |
| **Earthy** | Traditional family practice, karma-as-pūjā, nature, practical service |
| **Airy** | Research, questioning ("who am I?"), books, guru, debate, spreading bhakti |

## 3. States of Development — The First Phalanges & Which Planets to Check

Three grahas enter this reading: Jupiter is primary, Venus is a minor secondary check ("Guru comes, Śukra okay"), and Ketu is taken not as a mount but as a sign — specifically, the fish sign that should appear on the Ketu mount. The procedure then moves one planet at a time, beginning with Jupiter — first its finger, then its mount.

Before Jupiter specifically, the **first phalanx of every finger** is examined for adhyātma in general:

| State | Physical Sign | Character / Spiritual Manifestation | Real-World Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ideal** | Slightly bigger than the other two phalanges of the same finger; clean, without too many lines; puffed up (phulā huā) | Puffed = active — the same principle used for the willpower-bearing upper phalange of the thumb; a strong, currently active spiritual inclination | Not necessarily present identically on every finger; its absence on some fingers is not itself a problem |
| **Small in area but puffed** | Area is noticeably small, but the puffed quality remains | "Possibility is there" even with a small area, as the teacher read directly on a live hand (see Case 1) | Must be weighed together with the Jupiter mount and other signs, never dismissed on area alone |
| **Flat / heavily lined** | Not puffed; crossed with several lines | Reduced spiritual activeness at that finger | Weakens the overall reading at that point |

A **conical fingertip** (thin, cone-shaped at the top) is independently considered a spiritual finger. Seen from the back of the hand, **knotty fingers** mark a philosophical hand — more of a seeker's (khojī) tendency — and when this is paired with **short nails**, the combination is read directly as "the seeker's nails."

## 4. Signs, Markings & Practical Indications

### The Jupiter Mount

Three things are checked on the Jupiter mount, in order: its **area**, **how raised** it is, and **how clean** it is. Three specific markings then refine the reading:

| Marking on Jupiter Mount | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Teacher square** | Can become a preacher; can give dharma-jñāna; a motivational speaker |
| **Ring of Solomon** | Very sharp intuition; sixth sense; psychic ability (named for a king said to understand the language of birds) |
| **Diagonal lines** (either slant), called the line of sympathy | Compassion toward others |

These markings link directly back to the spiritual styles of Section 1: service or nature-oriented spirituality is strengthened by the diagonal line of sympathy; a wish to spread knowledge (pracāra-prasāra) is supported by the teacher square; and intuition-based work requires the Ring of Solomon. The Ring of Solomon itself can appear in either of two forms — a semicircular arc, in either direction — or can be built from the lines studied earlier under the rings below the finger; one student's hand showed this directly. As a general rule, "on the Jupiter mount, all the symbols we studied are good" — which is why Jupiter is checked first.

### Line of Intuition and the Mind Line

A **line of intuition** emerges from the Moon mount and runs across the palm. It is generally not fully formed — in many hands it appears only short, or only as a partial impression, beginning in one place and missing in the middle — and even such a partial trace is still read as the line of intuition. (Loops on the Moon mount were mentioned as a related, separate topic the teacher intends to compile and teach in a later lecture.)

Where the **mind (head) line slopes toward the Moon mount**, the person is moved by emotion (manas) — even on an otherwise airy hand, a touch of emotion will show through.

The teacher's counting rule governs how these and other signs are combined: **when two or more — in practice, three or more — points converge, the person may be said to lean more toward spirituality.** No single sign is read as conclusive on its own.

### The Mystic Cross and the Moon Mount

A **mystic cross** is a standalone cross sitting between the head line and the heart line. It must be a genuine, independent cross between the two lines — not the appearance created by a fate line crossing together with a line descending from the heart line, which does not qualify. It is very rare in hands.

The **Moon mount** itself should be slightly raised (in the manner that the Mars mount also sticks outward) and clean, without excessive cuts or crosses, before it can be linked to spirituality.

### The Second Phalanx of the Fingers

On the **second phalanx**, vertical lines indicate anxiety, while **horizontal lines on the second phalanx of the Jupiter finger** specifically mark a researcher in the Jupiter field — such lines may trouble the processing of information, but they build a researcher nonetheless. This is good for spirituality, but how far the person actually progresses depends on checking the **thumb**: if the thumb's quality is good, and the Sun finger is also good, the person can go further.

### Rare Signs

| Sign | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Fish sign (matsya)** | Very good for spirituality; can appear on the Jupiter mount, the Sun mount, or the Ketu mount (seen, for example, in Dravin ji's hand) |
| **Lotus** | Very rare; will rarely look like a perfect lotus — at best a "lotus-like feel" |
| **Swastika** | Also rare; can appear anywhere, though marks of this kind generally appear first on the mounts |

Ordinary cuts, crosses, moles (til), triangles, and squares appear in almost everyone's hand; these three signs, by contrast, are genuinely rare.

### Finger Tilts (Yogas for Spirituality)

Two tilts form recognised yogas. First, when **both the Jupiter finger and the Sun finger tilt toward the Saturn finger**, this itself is a yoga for spirituality. Second, when the **whole hand leans toward the Moon mount** — away from the thumb, which represents the self — all the fingers inclining in that direction likewise point to spirituality, and travel can be read as part of this same inclination. The teacher notes that this whole-hand lean, once seen, is distinct and easily recognised.

### Spirituality as a Profession, and Travel

When spirituality is pursued as a profession, the reading shifts to **Saturn and the fate line**. A branch leaving the fate line at any point and reaching **Jupiter** can show a career in this direction (two such branches indicate two careers), and if travel is part of the picture, the same branch can carry that meaning too. (Travel lines proper — lines leaving the life line outward, which show expansion and whose timing is read from where they leave — are a separate topic for a later class.) Supporting impressions for a spiritual career appear on the **heart line** and the **fate line**, or as a **teacher square** on the Jupiter mount; for someone teaching spiritual courses specifically (such as the Gītā or Vedic study), the combination of **square, Solomon line, Ring of Solomon, and line of sympathy** is looked for. A **heart line branch rising to Jupiter** is a further yoga for spirituality, standing alongside the ambition lines (rising from the life line to Jupiter) already studied earlier in the course.

For those who give **pravacana** (spiritual discourse), three things are checked: the **Mercury mount and Mercury finger**, **squares and lines on the Jupiter mount**, and the **quality of the thumb**, especially its first phalanx.

### Hand Quality, Line Density, and Colour

Very few lines on a hand do **not** mean the person does nothing or achieves nothing — a correction the teacher makes directly against the common assumption that fewer lines mean less karma-pradhāna energy or fewer desires. Instead: a **clean hand** shows happiness in life, while a hand **full of lines** — a net or web of lines, many crossing from Venus or Mars — shows a person who overthinks and worries too much; fine lines across the fingers and hand mark someone who carries a different style of bhakti, one in which fear sits in the mind. The ideal is a **clean hand, with good, pinkish colour**.

| Colour | Spiritual Implication (as taught) |
|---|---|
| **Pinkish** | Ideal |
| **Red, with blotches** | Can lead to taking major, extreme steps in spirituality/bhakti mārga — also not a good sign |
| **Yellow / white** | Dull and joyless (nīras); loneliness; may turn to bhakti out of distress, which can disturb family life — a balance issue |

In practice, the reading method is to listen for how the client's own question is framed: whether they are trying to **balance** family and spiritual life, or are considering leaving home entirely for **sannyāsa** — the two call for different readings.

### Further Indicators

A rare **intuition triangle**, formed by the head line, the fate line, and the Mercury line (with the life line left out entirely), indicates good intuition and is very good for spirituality — it can also show the person earning money from spiritual work, and the presence of the Mercury line in this triangle is itself a strong positive.

A rarer line, sometimes called the **pūrva puṇya rekhā**, runs from Mars to the Sun line. When a student suggested this meant accumulated past-life merit carried forward into the present, the teacher corrected the guess directly ("you just made that up from your side"). As actually taught, this line marks a tendency toward **Army, Police, and technical work**; it is very rare, calls for working hard and being careful, draws attention toward Mars, and reflects a desire to do something different.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Teacher's Warning**: Do not read the pūrva puṇya rekhā as proof of carried-forward past-life virtue on its own authority — that reading was explicitly rejected in class as invented. Its taught meaning is a Mars-oriented pull toward disciplined, technical, or uniformed work.

### Classroom Observations

> [!CASE]
> **Classroom Observation**: A 30-year-old woman, connected to ISKCON for eight to ten years, showed a first phalanx that was very small in area but puffed — "possibility is there." Her Jupiter-finger second phalanx carried a slanting line, and her Jupiter mount showed a diagonal line with a possible square not fully confirmable from the photo. A genuine mystic cross was found between her head and heart lines. Her early life read as troubled, with a jolt and a possible depressive phase — read from a heart line of poor quality — which the teacher connected to the period, after age twenty, when the student confirmed she had joined ISKCON. A large island was still visible on her hand, alongside healing lines. Her mind line appeared joined to the life line at its origin; the teacher noted that such a join can be read either as joined, or — if the person left family early — read separately, since palmistry allows the reading to be settled in conversation with the client. Her Moon mount was somewhat raised but of poor quality: drawn inward, with too many lines. A whole-hand tilt toward the Moon mount, suggested by a student, was not in fact present on this hand.

> [!CASE]
> **Classroom Observation**: A long-time devotee who performs Mātā kī chaukī (devotional singing) free of cost with her husband, born in 1962, with a thirty-year fabric business (closed after COVID, she now works in a bank) and two children who are both doctors. Her fingers were long and thin, her Jupiter finger longer than her Sun finger, with philosophical, knotted joints; her first phalanges were good, on an overall pinkish hand. Her second phalanx carried lines in both directions, though her anxiety line stayed free, and a line of intuition was visible near the Jupiter mount. Her thumb showed a very good, wide spread — the teacher noted that such a strong thumb compensates for a deficiency elsewhere. She showed a complete Ring of Solomon (with a light impression where its centre was faint) together with a square, and a line running from Saturn toward the Sun. Her Jupiter mount was flat in height but large in area, while her Sun mount was the one that was actually raised — the teacher's broader rule being that any mount is judged by three things (finger, mount, line), and if any two of the three are good, the reading goes above fifty percent even if the mount itself is flat. By this point the reading had gathered four separate impressions for spirituality: the Ring of Solomon, the square, the fingers, and the line. Her fate line, heading toward Jupiter, showed a break that the referring student read as the business closure after COVID followed by the bank job; her heart line forked into two branches, both toward Jupiter, read as a balance of emotion and practicality. Her Venus mount was flat and bluish, prompting a recommendation to attend to her health.

> [!CASE]
> **Classroom Observation**: The companion of the above singer (who also performs chaukī, with a husband who writes and sings songs) showed a soft, pink hand with long, "artist's" fingers, nicely raised and puffed, with squares and lines on the mount. A line crossing from Saturn was identified, after discussion, as the fate line, originating from the Moon mount; a fish sign appeared near it, and the fate line itself ended in a trident-like, two-branched fork, with a further branch rising upward. Her Mercury mount was well raised and her Sun line good, but Mars "enemy lines" were seen crossing and cutting the Sun line, read as a source of future trouble. Her thumb was noted as very small, read as a lack of willpower.

> [!CASE]
> **Classroom Observation**: A 32-year-old student (Anurag), a computer-science professional who automates systems. His thumb was good, and his Jupiter mount showed a line, a fully formed square, a triangle, and an ambition line running alongside them — read as "Jupiter is good." Both his Jupiter and Sun fingers tilted toward Saturn, marking a second yoga (the first being the Jupiter mount markings). His Mercury finger was small and in poor condition, set aside from the reading. A Mars line was seen cutting the Sun line, but read as no problem, alongside a horizontal line from the Moon mount. A light line running from Mars to the Sun — the pūrva puṇya rekhā — was present; when a student guessed this meant good karmas carried forward from past lives, the teacher corrected this directly, teaching instead that such a line shows a tendency toward Army, Police, and technical work, a rare line calling for hard work and care — which the student confirmed matched his own computer-science and automation work rather than any interest in the armed forces. His mind line showed a branch to Mars and a branch to the Moon, carrying both qualities. Expansion indicators at his current age pointed to property and foreign travel, though he had no passport yet.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Rule of Convergence**: Never conclude strong spiritual dedication from a single sign. The teacher's own rule requires that **two, or better three or more**, of the indicators above converge before that conclusion is drawn.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Exceptions & Balance**: A red, blotchy hand points toward extreme steps in spirituality; a yellow or white hand points toward dullness and loneliness, with bhakti arising from distress and potentially disturbing family life. A hand overly full of lines shows worry, overthinking, and fear entering the person's devotion. Always check whether the client is seeking balance between family and spiritual life, or is considering full renunciation (sannyāsa) — the two require different readings.

## 6. Sanskrit Terms Introduced

- **Adhyātma**: Spirituality; the subject of this chapter.
- **Deśa-Kāla-Paristhiti**: Place, time, and circumstance — the lens through which a client's spiritual orientation must first be understood.
- **Īśvara Bhakti**: Devotion to God; the religious/devotional orientation to spirituality.
- **Sādhanā**: Spiritual practice or discipline.
- **Prema Mārga**: The path of (devotional) love, exemplified in the lecture by Mīrā.
- **Vichāra**: Thought or inquiry; the quality the airy hand brings to its spiritual search.
- **Tarka-Vitarka**: Debate or argumentation, associated with the airy hand's spiritual style.
- **Pracāra-Prasāra**: The spreading or dissemination of one's bhakti or teaching.
- **Khojī**: Seeker; the tendency associated with knotty, philosophical fingers.
- **Phulā Huā**: Puffed up — the active, raised condition of a phalanx.
- **Matsya**: Fish; the rare fish sign on the Jupiter, Sun, or Ketu mount.
- **Pūrva Puṇya Rekhā**: The line from Mars to the Sun, popularly glossed as "past merit" but taught here as indicating Army/Police/technical inclination.
- **Sannyāsa**: Full renunciation of worldly and family life for spiritual life.

## 7. Diagnostic Self-Check

1. Which of the five perspectives on spirituality — devotional, service-based, seeking, psychological, or event-triggered — best fits this client's deśa-kāla-paristhiti, and has this been established before reading any line or mount?
2. What is the hand's elemental type, and does its spiritual style (action/pilgrimage, devotional absorption, traditional practice, or research/questioning) match what is actually being observed on the hand?
3. Are the first phalanges of the fingers puffed and clean, even if small in area — and is a conical fingertip or a knotty, philosophical finger-with-short-nails combination present?
4. What does the Jupiter mount show for area, elevation, and cleanliness, and which of the three key markings — teacher square, Ring of Solomon, diagonal line of sympathy — are present?
5. Is a line of intuition, even as a partial impression, visible from the Moon mount, and does the head line slope toward that mount?
6. Is a genuine, standalone mystic cross present between the head and heart lines — ruling out the false version formed by a fate-line crossing?
7. Have at least two, and ideally three or more, indicators from the full checklist been confirmed before concluding that the person leans strongly toward spirituality?
8. Is the hand's overall line density and colour clean and pinkish, or does it show the overthinking/worry of too many lines, or the joylessness of yellow/white colouring — and is the client seeking balance or full renunciation?

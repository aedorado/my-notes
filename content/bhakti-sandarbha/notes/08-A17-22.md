# Bhakti Sandarbha – Anucchedas 17 onward: Wise Perform Bhakti with Joy; Why Not Worship the Devatās; Vāsudeva as the Goal of All Scriptures

## 1. Recap of Previous Lecture

The lecturer recaps the last few Anucchedas, beginning with the nature of pure bhakti.

- **Bhakti is that which gives joy to the mind.** It is the **supreme dharma**, which satisfies the mind completely, and it was presented as the essence of all scriptures.
- Bhakti should generate **taste for hearing the topics of the Lord**. Other dharmas, including varṇāśrama-dharma, do not help develop this taste.
  - Calling them "a waste of time" (*śrama eva hi kevalam*) means: if prema is the goal, varṇāśrama-dharma cannot give it. Otherwise it is not a waste of time, because **nothing created by the Supreme Lord can ever be a waste of time**.
  - Karma, jñāna and yoga without an element of bhakti give no result.
- Bhakti should be **ahaituki and apratihatā**: not covered by karma, jñāna or yoga, and with no goal other than the desire to please Kṛṣṇa.
- When taste develops, bhakti-yoga characterised by hearing and chanting begins. Citing Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, the little taste for hearing Kṛṣṇa's topics is the qualification for bhakti, and **dry logic does not lead to bhakti**.
- **Bhakti is independent, while the other paths depend on bhakti.** Jñāna and vairāgya manifest automatically by the practice of bhakti and need not be cultivated separately. Where there is bhakti, all good qualities develop. Dharma and the rest give no result without taking shelter of bhakti, so bhakti supports everything and needs the support of nothing.
- The words in *śrama eva hi kevalam* were explained:
  - *eva* indicates the temporary nature of svarga and other results of karma.
  - *hi* indicates that there is ample proof in the śāstras.
  - *kevalam* indicates that even the results of dharma that produce detachment and liberation are not attained, and the temporary nature of siddhis is indicated as well.
- The true goal of dharma is **tattva-jijñāsā**, not artha and kāma (*dharmasya hy apavargyasya…*). Apavargya dharma means that which takes one outside the material world.
- The tattva is **advaya-jñāna**, called Brahman by the jñānīs, Paramātmā by the yogīs and Bhagavān by the devotees.
- This tattva is realised by **bhakti, heard from the guru** (*bhaktyā śruta-gṛhītayā*), in the form of prema, which is the culmination of taste for the Lord's topics.
  - The progression is: śraddhā, sādhu-saṅga, bhajana-kriyā, anartha-nivṛtti, niṣṭhā, ruci, āsakti, bhāva, prema. It all starts with *svalpa ruci*, and it matures into prema.
  - Devotees directly perceive the truth in pure consciousness. Bhakti is served by jñāna and vairāgya, which are not separately cultivated.
  - The words *śraddhā* and *dhana* show that this bhakti is very hard to attain. The words *bhaktyā* and *śruta-gṛhītayā* indicate its rarity.
- The conclusion is that bhakti alone, consisting of hearing, must be performed (*tasmād ekena manasā…*).
  - *Ekena manasā* means a mind devoid of enthusiasm for karma and other processes.
  - *Śrotavyaḥ, kīrtitavyaś ca* means hearing and chanting the Lord's name and qualities.
  - The glorification of hearing was given to develop taste for it. This is the first step, with indifference to difficult karma and other processes, to show the easy stages of bhakti up to prema.
- Verse: *yad anudhyāsinā yuktāḥ karma-granthi-nibandhanam…* One can cut the knots of karma just by remembering Kṛṣṇa, and the means of remembering Kṛṣṇa is hearing about Kṛṣṇa. The lecturer asks: "who would not develop a taste for the topics of the Lord?"
- **How the initial taste (komala-śraddhā) develops:** *śuśrūṣoḥ śraddadhānasya vāsudeva-kathā-ruciḥ syān mahat-sevayā viprāḥ puṇya-tīrtha-niṣevaṇāt*.
  - Sat-kṛpā is not mentioned in the verse but is understood. Then comes mahat-sevā, then vāsudeva-kathā-ruci, then śraddhā.
  - Then comes taking shelter of a guru or sādhu (sādhu-saṅga). This essentially means accepting a guru. The desire to hear then becomes a conscious one (*śuśrūṣā*), which refers to **bhajaneṣu spṛhā**.
  - Serving holy tīrthas is a way to serve great devotees. One may meet devotees who wander to or live at tīrthas, and by seeing, touching and conversing one gets some mahat-sevā.
  - Hearing from great devotees has an immediate effect (*satāṁ prasaṅgān mama vīrya-saṁvido…*).
- The **stages** were traced:
  - Bhajana-kriyā is indicated by *śṛṇvatāṁ sva-kathāḥ kṛṣṇaḥ puṇya-śravaṇa-kīrtanaḥ…*. Kṛṣṇa enters the hearts of devotees and destroys their sinful inclinations, which is anartha-nivṛtti. These are stages 7, 8 and then 9, niṣṭhā.
  - Niṣṭhā: *naṣṭa-prāyeṣv abhadreṣu nityaṁ bhāgavata-sevayā…* Steady devotional service develops.
  - The lecturer's point: even though impurities are not completely destroyed, bhakti can be performed steadily. Jñāna requires complete destruction of impurities for steady practice. **This shows the unimpeded nature of bhakti**: one can practise bhakti with anarthas. He also cites *api cet sudurācāro…*.
  - Stages 10 and 11, ruci and āsakti, are indicated by *tadā rajas-tamo-bhāvāḥ kāma-lobhādayaś ca ye…*. The mind becomes unaffected by lust, greed, anger, hatred and illusion, which arise from rajas and tamas, and becomes fixed in the form of the Lord and satisfied.
  - Stages 12 and 13, bhāva and prema: *evaṁ prasanna-manaso bhagavad-bhakti-yogataḥ…* The mind becomes joyful and satisfied, and prema develops with complete detachment. The devotee experiences the Lord's form, qualities, pastimes, powers and sweetness.
  - Stage 14, **harer mādhurya-anubhava**, is direct darśana of the Lord: *bhidyate hṛdaya-granthiś chidyante sarva-saṁśayāḥ kṣīyante cāsya karmāṇi dṛṣṭa evātmanīśvare*.
- Points the lecturer emphasised on the last verse:
  - The destruction of karma refers to **karma-vāsanās**, the last trace of karma, being destroyed. Karma as reactions is destroyed right from the beginning.
  - **False ego is destroyed right from the day one consciously takes up bhakti**, from the moment of receiving the seed of devotion. The devotee acts in **true ego**.
  - There may be an influence of the **inertia of past false ego**, since one has lived in this world since time immemorial identifying with the body. But there is no false ego from the moment of receiving the seed of devotion.
  - All doubts, including doubts about the Lord's existence, are destroyed completely when one directly sees the Lord, not even an iota remaining.
  - By meditation, mistaken thoughts about the Lord are destroyed. By direct perception, thoughts of non-existence and contrary thoughts are both destroyed. Karmas are diminished, and a shadow of karma remains by the will of the Lord.
- This is where the previous discussion ended, after a long discussion on how karma is destroyed in devotees.

## 2. Opening Q&A

### Q: How do we know whether we are acting on the inertia of past false ego, as opposed to present false ego?

Speaker 3 asked this.

- The lecturer notes this can be understood in multiple ways.
- If someone says, "I am free from false ego, so how can you say I am not acting on true ego?", the lecturer asks which activities the person performs under false ego (for example anger, pride, desire for name and fame). Then he asks: **why call it "false" ego at all?**
  - Only devotees use the term "false ego." In the outside world no one identifies some activities as under false ego and others as under true ego.
  - **The very fact that you identify it as false means it is there, but you do not want it to be there.** It is there because of past inertia.
- This is what Arjuna asked Kṛṣṇa: what is pushing me to perform sinful activities even though I do not want to? The answer is the inertia of past false ego.

> [!important]
> **Test given by the lecturer:** Close your eyes and ask, "In my state of perfection, do I see this quality present in me?"
> - If **yes**, the current state is also false ego. The person has not received true ego.
> - If **no**, but right now the person cannot overcome the urge, then it is **inertia of past false ego**.

> [!example]
> A person aspiring to become temple president is asked whether this desire would be part of the perfected self. If yes, it is current false ego. If the person feels that at perfection he would only want to focus on hearing, chanting and serving Kṛṣṇa, and that he has no such desire then, the present itch is inertia of past false ego.

- Acting under pride, lust, anger, envy, greed or desire for money is false ego.
- Only the person himself can know, by asking himself this question.

**Follow-up (Speaker 3):** We act on the bodily platform because of past inertia of false ego. We want to become servants under true ego, and in our perfected state that is our aspiration. We know our position, but we cannot act on it because of the past inertia. Is that correct? The lecturer confirmed: **correct**.

### Q (chat): Devotees' sufferings are only shadows of karma?

- Yes. It just looks like karma, but **it is provided by Kṛṣṇa**.

### Q (chat): When I think I am the doer, am I under false ego?

- The very fact that you are willing to designate it as false means it is **inertia of past false ego**, not current false ego. If it were not inertia, you would not call it false.
- That is why outside the devotional arena there is no definition of false ego.

> [!example]
> **Train analogy:** A person wants to get down from a running train and wants to stop. But he cannot jump and stop immediately, and the inertia of motion keeps him running for a while.

### Q (chat): Does false ego go all at once, only at bhāva or prema, or gradually after every stage?

- **False ego vanishes the moment one receives the seed of devotion, but the inertia of false ego vanishes only at the stage of prema.** At prema the inertia is completely gone.
- Gradually, by sincere practice of devotional service, the influence of rajas and tamas, characterised by kāma, lobha, moha and mātsarya, gets destroyed.

> [!example]
> **Ceiling fan analogy (attributed by the lecturer to Prabhupāda):** When you switch off a fan, the switch is off, but the fan keeps rotating and slows down gradually. The switch going off is the time of receiving the seed of devotion (*ādau śraddhā*). The continued rotation is the inertia of past motion. After the switch is off the person is in true ego, and what remains is only past inertia of false ego.

### Q (Speaker 2): Even people who are not devotees say "ego" and feel guilty for being arrogant or rude, and repent. How do we distinguish theirs from ours?

- **That is based on a material value system**, which keeps changing.
- The lecturer gives the example of people who each call themselves "a very nice person" while drinking at different levels: never, only at parties, a small peg at home before bed, only inside the house, or with one's own money inside or outside.
- When rajas and tamas become too strong people do things they repent when sattva returns. If sattva stays very low they eventually stop repenting things they used to repent.
- There is **no standardised level** for which they repent. Some repent only for murder, some for stealing something small, some do not repent even serious things. This depends on the current level of modes and **not on any śāstric value system**.
- So the lecturer does not consider their sense of "I acted under ego" to be the same.
  - For them, the rajasic and tamasic aspect is false ego and the sattvic part is true ego.
  - **For us, identification with the material body, whether tamasic, rajasic or sattvic, is false ego.**

### Comment (Speaker 2) on defining a devotee

- Speaker 2 mentioned the discussion of primary and secondary characteristics of Vaiṣṇavas. They cited the 26 qualities (tolerance, forgiveness and so on) as secondary, and the primary one from Kapila's teaching (*mayy ananyena bhāvena bhaktiṁ kurvanti…*).
- They said some devotees consider themselves non-devotees until all these qualities appear. These sessions help them feel "I am also doing some little bhakti."
- **Lecturer's response:** This is a question of terminology. If that is their personal definition of pure devotion, that is perfectly fine. **But if one calls it Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī's definition of uttama-bhakti, that is wrong.**
- He added that he is only repeating what the ācāryas said ("cut and paste material from the commentaries"), and any credit goes to them.

## 3. Main Lecture Notes

### 3.1 Overview of the 14 stages

- The series of verses described the progress from satāṁ kṛpā, mahat-sevā, vāsudeva-kathā-ruci, śraddhā, guru-pādāśraya, bhajaneṣu spṛhā, bhajana-kriyā, anartha-nivṛtti, niṣṭhā, ruci, āsakti, bhāva, prema and harer mādhurya-anubhava.
- The nine stages presented by Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī have been **expanded into these 14 stages by Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura in his commentaries**.
- These are from the first canto, second chapter (the chapter on divinity and divine service).
- At the end the devotee comes in touch with the Supreme Personality of Godhead and becomes part of the Lord's līlā, very blissful (*evaṁ prasanna-manaso…*).

### 3.2 Bhakti is joyful even at the stage of sādhana

> [!quote]
> *ato vai kavayo nityaṁ bhaktiṁ paramayā mudā | vāsudeve bhagavati kurvanty ātma-prasādanīm ||*

- **The question:** If bhakti gives bliss only at the end, is it very difficult to perform during sādhana?
- **The answer:** *Ataḥ*, therefore, the wise (*kavayaḥ*) constantly (*nityam*) perform bhakti, which gives joy to the mind (*ātma-prasādanīm*), *paramayā mudā*, with great joy.

> [!important]
> **Bhakti is very blissful even at the stage of sādhana.** Bhakti purifies the mind, and that is not its only quality: it is performed with the greatest joy. Its performance gives no suffering either in sādhana or in perfection, **unlike the performance of karmas**. The wise therefore perform it at all times.

### 3.3 Who is "wise" (kavi)?

- The lecturer stresses that this has been discussed many times and is very important.
- **"Wise" here means śraddhāvān, not high IQ. It refers to "SQ", sukṛti quotient.**
- Argument:
  1. Wise means knowledgeable, specifically knowledgeable about the process of bhakti.
  2. To acquire knowledge of bhakti one needs faith in the bhakti-śāstras.
  3. To develop faith in the bhakti-śāstras one needs sukṛti.
- **The objection he raises:** If "wise" meant intellectually intelligent, then graduates of IITs, MIT, Stanford, Cambridge or Oxford, who are certified intelligent, would all be practising bhakti, but they are not.
- Therefore "wise" means those who have become wise **by the strength of their sukṛti**. The lecturer says it is better translated as **"fortunate."**
- He quotes: *yadṛcchayā mat-kathādau jāta-śraddhas tu yaḥ pumān | na nirviṇṇo nāti-sakto bhakti-yogo 'sya siddhi-daḥ*. This is rendered in the lecture as *yā kenāpi atibhāgyena jāta-śraddho 'stu yaḥ pumān…*; see verification.
- Those fortunate to develop faith in the path of bhakti become wise.

### 3.4 Why bhakti is blissful even during practice

- Bhakti gives an **opportunity for the engagement of the senses**. Any process that gives opportunity for engagement of the senses is blissful, especially when material desires are still present.
- A process that restricts engagement of the senses is very difficult.
  - Citing Bhagavad-gītā (*kleśo 'dhikataras teṣām avyaktāsakta-cetasām | avyaktā hi gatir duḥkhaṁ dehavadbhir avāpyate*), the process of **jñāna is painful in practice** because it involves non-engagement of the senses.
  - Even to reach the stage of jñāna one has to practise and perfect karma. Only then can one remain peaceful without engaging the senses.
- **In bhakti, even with all kinds of material anarthas, one can engage**, because bhakti offers ample opportunity to engage all the senses, mind and intelligence. Hence it is *sukhaṁ kartum*, very easy and pleasurable.
- Conclusion: the fortunate people who became wise in the teachings of the bhakti-śāstras through the fortunate association of Vaiṣṇavas perform bhakti with great joy.

### Q&A during the lecture

**Q (Mahālakṣmī):** Here "wise" does not even include jñānīs, right?
**A:** Correct, wise here does not mean jñānīs, **because jñānīs do not practise bhakti**.

### 3.5 Why not worship the devatās?

- **Context:** The previous Anuccheda concluded that wise people practise bhakti to attain satisfaction of the heart. So one should give up efforts in karma, jñāna and vairāgya and perform bhakti to the Lord. The question arises: **why only the Lord, why not the devatās?**
- Worship of devatās is a part of karma, so it should also be given up, and worship of other living entities even more so. This is explained in **seven verses**.
- Brahmā and Śiva should not be worshipped by persons desiring the highest goal. If they are not worshipped, then **those less than them are not worshipped either**.

> [!important]
> **Worship versus respect:** We do not worship Brahmā and Śiva as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, but we respect them as very exalted Vaiṣṇavas. The respect we give to exalted Vaiṣṇavas should definitely be given to the demigods. But we do not worship them on the same pedestal as the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

- Though they are guṇāvatāras of the Lord, they are not directly Parabrahman like Viṣṇu. They lack the advantage of pure sattva and have a prominence of rajas and tamas, even though they may not be affected by that. For example, Lord Śiva is in touch with tamas but is not affected by it.

**Paramātma Sandarbha 1.2.23 (as quoted in the lecture):**

- The one Supreme Puruṣa accepts the guṇas of prakṛti (sattva, rajas and tamas) for creation, maintenance and destruction, and is called Viṣṇu, Brahmā and Śiva.
  - Sattva for maintenance: Viṣṇu.
  - Rajas for creation: Brahmā.
  - Tamas for destruction: Śiva.
- Of the three, **the best results for the devotees come from Viṣṇu, who has a body of śuddha-sattva** (*śreyāṁsi tatra khalu sattva-tanor nṛṇāṁ syuḥ*, rendered in the lecture as "Vrinam Shreyam Si… Kalu Sattva Tanor Shihu").

**The fire–smoke–wood explanation:**

- The lecturer calls this "a very interesting verse which needs some explanation." *Dāru* here means superior in context.
- Smoke is superior to dull wood, and fire sacred to the Vedas is superior to smoke. Similarly **rajas is superior to tamas and sattva is superior to rajas**, since sattva is favourable for realising the Lord.
- Of course with material sattva one cannot realise the Supreme Lord. But it is a **favourable launching pad** if one has the seed of devotion.
- Correspondences:
  - Wood = tamas.
  - Smoke = rajas.
  - Fire = sattva.
  - Fire is used in karma, hence it is called *trayīmaya*. The activities of karma represent Brahman.
- Smoke has a portion of fire in it, and so a portion of Vedic action, unlike wood, where fire is not manifest. Rajas is next to sattva and tamas is not. Rajas has a small portion of realisation of the Lord, just as Vedic actions are directly performed with fire and not with smoke or wood.
- The Lord appears in sattva. **One should perform bhakti properly to give up devatā worship.**

> [!note]
> Although the Lord is compared to sattva here, one understands that He is **viśuddha-sattva**. He is in charge of material sattva, **not directly but through the agency of māyā**, and is Himself not made of material sattva.
>
> Likewise the body of Lord Śiva is not made of material sattva or just tamas. The lecturer says one of the five qualities (of the 55) that Śiva has and ordinary living entities do not is that he has a spiritual body. Śiva's body is **transcendental**, though he takes care of material tamo-guṇa.

**Verse: *bhejire munayo 'thāgre bhagavantam adhokṣajam | sattvaṁ viśuddhaṁ kṣemāya kalpante ye 'nu tān iha ||***

- "Therefore the ancient sages worshipped the Supreme Lord beyond the material senses, composed of viśuddha-sattva. Those who follow the sages attain liberation in this world."
- The lecturer observes that *ataḥ* (therefore) is again a conclusion. The earlier *ataḥ* was in *ato vai kavayo nityam…*, and after two or three verses he comes to the same conclusion.
- *Bhagavat Sandarbha* explains how the Lord is beyond material sattva. Therefore one should give up worship of the demigods, **including worship of Lord Śiva**.

**Verse: *mumukṣavo ghora-rūpān hitvā bhūta-patīn atha | nārāyaṇa-kalāḥ śāntā bhajanti hy anasūyavaḥ ||***

- Some persons worship devatās such as Śiva **because they have material desires**. Persons desiring liberation do not.
- Meaning of the words, per the lecturer:
  - *Ghora-rūpān*: the frightful forms of Śiva or other devatās, which are to be rejected.
  - *Mumukṣavaḥ*: persons desiring liberation. Then what to speak of devotees who desire the even higher goal of prema.
  - *Anasūyavaḥ*: without any enmity or criticism towards the devatās.
  - *Nārāyaṇa-kalāḥ*: they should worship the avatāras of Nārāyaṇa.
- Worship of Śiva, the Pitṛs and Brahmā is rejected, however **worshippers of Viṣṇu do not criticise the devatās**.

> [!warning]
> One should not disrespect the demigods, but when it comes to worship it should be Viṣṇu only. If someone wants liberation, let alone prema, they should worship only Viṣṇu.

> [!info]
> If one has material desires, one may worship the demigods and Kṛṣṇa may fulfil those desires through the demigods, **but it is better to worship Kṛṣṇa directly even with material desires**.

### Q&A during the lecture on devatā worship

**Q: What should we do when someone gives prasāda of demigods? I used to throw it away but realise this insults the demigods.**

- We cannot eat it. The bhoga was not prepared for the pleasure of Kṛṣṇa. It was prepared for the demigod, **considering the demigod supreme and with a desire for material benefit**. The consciousness of cooking is itself poor.
- The devatās are Vaiṣṇavas and accept only Viṣṇu-prasāda. Because the food was cooked in such poor consciousness, it is not fit to be offered to Viṣṇu. If not offered to Viṣṇu, the demigods will not accept it.
- So the food is **not actually demigod prasāda**, and **discarding it is not really an insult** to the demigods.
- But we do not have to throw it away. We can accept it and give it to needy people such as beggars.
  - If they merely appreciate the food they may not get sukṛti, but if they appreciate the person giving it they may accumulate some **ajñāta-sukṛti** and come closer to Kṛṣṇa.
- We should not even think of it as prasāda.

**Q: Viṣṇu is in charge of sattva-guṇa, but you explained in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam that material sattva is different from viśuddha-sattva.**

- Viṣṇu is in charge of material sattva through the agency of māyā. In charge of maintenance means He maintains **through His glance** and does not directly touch.
- He Himself is made of viśuddha-sattva only, and not material sattva.

**Q: Devotees also do piṇḍa-dāna during Pitṛ Pakṣa. How to understand this?**

- **Devotees need not do piṇḍa-dāna.** A person who has surrendered to the Supreme Personality of Godhead is not individually indebted (*ṛṇī*) to the devatās, ṛṣis, other living entities, Pitṛs and so on, because **they are directly watering the root**.
- Viṣṇu is the root, so worship of Viṣṇu automatically bestows auspiciousness on the devatās, ṛṣis, bhūtas, āptas and Pitṛs. The benefit to the devatās is much greater than directly offering them material things.
- Piṇḍa-dāna gives the Pitṛs only some material piety by which they do not have to suffer. If a devotee becomes more sincere, a part of the benefit goes to the ancestors and they receive **transcendental benefit**.
- So devotees do not perform piṇḍa-dāna, not because of ingratitude but because they want to give the ancestors transcendental benefit through sincere bhakti.
- **For loka-saṅgraha**, family members who are not devotees may think the devotee has no gratitude and may develop an unfavourable mood. For that purpose one may perform some piṇḍa-dāna and śrāddha, but in reality these are not needed for devotees.
- As for why some devotees do Pitṛ Pakṣa: "we have to ask them."

**Q: I am not a pure devotee and have many anarthas, but demigods work on Kṛṣṇa's orders. Are they not always superior to a bhakta with many anarthas?**

- Demigods are superior to struggling sādhakas, no doubt. But **Kṛṣṇa is superior to the demigods**. The question here is not a comparison between demigods and devotees.
- One need not be superior to worship Kṛṣṇa. Even the most fallen person can take shelter of Kṛṣṇa.
- One should not argue "I am fallen, so I will take shelter of someone also not so great, a demigod." **There is no material disqualification to worship Viṣṇu or Kṛṣṇa.** Anyone can worship.

**Q: If we happen to go to a demigod temple, can we ask for Kṛṣṇa-bhakti?**

- Yes, if we happen to be there we can ask for Kṛṣṇa-bhakti. But **we do not need to travel specifically to a demigod temple** to ask for it.

### 3.6 Why do people worship demigods if worshipping Viṣṇu is more beneficial?

> [!question]
> A person with material desires can worship Viṣṇu. Why does he worship the devatās?

**Verse:** *śriyaiśvarya-prajepsavaḥ… rajas-tamaḥ-prakṛtayaḥ samaśīlā bhajanti vai | pitṛ-bhūta-prajeśādīn…*

- Desirers of wealth, power and progeny, with natures of rajas and tamas, **corresponding to the natures of their deities** (*samaśīlāḥ*), worship the Pitṛs, Śiva, Brahmā and others.
- The lecturer says this needs some explanation, and he revisits a topic seen before.

**How the three modes can affect a person, in three ways:**

1. Śāstrīya-śraddhā (faith based on śāstra)
2. Laukikī-śraddhā (also called sentimental faith, svabhāva-jā śraddhā)
3. Svabhāva (nature)

**(1) Śāstrīya-śraddhā:**

- If pure, the person practises **devotional service**.
- If contaminated by the modes:
  - **Mode of goodness:** niṣkāma-karma-yoga, jñāna-yoga, aṣṭāṅga-yoga, karma-miśrā bhakti, niṣkāma-karma-miśrā bhakti, jñāna-miśrā bhakti or yoga-miśrā bhakti.
  - **Mode of passion:** rājasī bhakti, which can manifest as either pradhānī-bhūta bhakti or, if it is guṇī-bhūta, sakāma-karma-yoga, where people want material benefits.
  - **Mode of ignorance:** tāmasī bhakti, where one worships Lord Viṣṇu for the purpose of harming others.
- Because this is still śāstrīya-śraddhā, **all three types worship only Viṣṇu**.

**(2) Faith not founded on scripture (svabhāva-jā or laukikī śraddhā):**

- It can be in goodness, passion or ignorance. The lecturer cites Bhagavad-gītā chapter 17: *yajante sāttvikā devān yakṣa-rakṣāṁsi rājasāḥ | pretān bhūta-gaṇāṁś cānye yajante tāmasā janāḥ*.
- Per the lecturer:
  - Goodness: worship particular types of devatās.
  - Passion: worship yakṣas and rākṣasas.
  - Ignorance: worship pretas and bhūta-gaṇas.
  - *Samaśīlā* means that those influenced by ignorance worship pretas and bhūta-gaṇas, who are in ignorance. Those in passion are inclined to worship yakṣas and rākṣasas, who are in passion, if their śraddhā is laukikī and has **no śāstrīya component**.

> [!note]
> At one point in the transcript, pretas and bhūta-gaṇas are attributed to "mode of goodness." This appears to be a speech slip and is listed under verification.

**(3) Svabhāva (nature):**

- The modes affect one's nature in several ways. Those in goodness are inclined to eat food in goodness and show qualities such as tolerance and humility. Those in passion are attracted to food in passion and show anger, lust and greed. Those in ignorance are attracted to food in ignorance and show violence and hatred.

**The sādhaka:** A person may have **pure śāstrīya-śraddhā along with a contaminated svabhāva**, and such a person is called a sādhaka.

- Because of pure śāstrīya-śraddhā we want to practise uttama-bhakti.
- But our svabhāva may still be contaminated, and we may feel the anarthas of lust, anger, envy, greed, illusion, pride and desire for name, fame, recognition and glorification.

> [!important]
> People with material desires are not compelled to worship demigods. Those who want these things **can worship Kṛṣṇa also**. It is not that Lord Kṛṣṇa will not fulfil such desires.
> - Even a sarva-kāmī (one with all material desires) or a mokṣa-kāmī can perform tīvreṇa bhakti-yogena, or jñāna-karmādi-anāvṛta bhakti, to attain the result.
> - Generally those who worship demigods do seem to have material desires, but they do not necessarily have to worship demigods to fulfil them.

### 3.7 Vāsudeva is the purport of all scriptures (verses 28 and 29)

> [!quote]
> *vāsudeva-parā vedā vāsudeva-parā makhāḥ | vāsudeva-parā yogā vāsudeva-parāḥ kriyāḥ ||*
> *vāsudeva-paraṁ jñānaṁ vāsudeva-paraṁ tapaḥ | vāsudeva-paro dharmo vāsudeva-parā gatiḥ ||*

- The lecturer links this to the previous discussion: it was already stated that Vāsudeva should be worshipped (*ato vai kavayo…*). Now the conclusion of all scriptures is expressed in these two verses (1.2.28–29).
- Vāsudeva is the purport of the Vedas and the object of all sacrifices. Yoga, varṇāśrama, knowledge and austerities depend on Vāsudeva. Bhakti, prema and liberation depend on Vāsudeva.

**Śrīdhara Svāmī's commentary (as presented):**

- The conclusion of the Vedas is Vāsudeva. Though the Vedas seem to indicate sacrifices, **sacrifices themselves indicate worship of Vāsudeva**.
- Yoga scriptures describe āsana and prāṇāyāma, but these are methods of attaining Vāsudeva. Scriptures of jñāna indicate Vāsudeva, since the object of jñāna is Vāsudeva. *Tapaḥ* means jñāna in the verse.
- Scriptures on dharma involving charity and vows indicate Vāsudeva. Even scriptures that speak of svarga indicate Vāsudeva, **since svarga is a portion of His bliss**. All the Vedas indicate Vāsudeva, since He is the root of all scriptures.

**Objection and response:**

- **Objection:** The scriptures indicate sacrifice, yoga, āsanas and so on, not only Vāsudeva.
- **Response:** Since yoga and other processes are to some degree assistants of bhakti, **they mainly indicate bhakti**. But the scriptures indicate according to **the faith of the person approaching**.
- Some people are not ready to accept pure devotional practices, so for them varṇāśrama, jñāna, yoga and so on are recommended, **but not for pure devotees**.
- The śāstras ultimately want to recommend pure devotional service for everyone, but because people are not yet qualified, the śāstras help them take **baby steps** in that direction, according to where they are.

### 3.8 The Vedas, karma-kāṇḍa and Bhakti

- The lecturer revisits the previous verse (*vāsudeva-parā vedāḥ…*): all Vedic scriptures ultimately recommend only worship of Vāsudeva.
- **Objection:** The Vedas deal with karma-kāṇḍa, and most of the Vedas is karma-kāṇḍa, with only a small part about bhakti.
- **Response:** For the person who has **bhakti for the Lord and bhakti for the guru as much as for the Lord**, the meaning of the scripture is revealed.
- Even though the scriptures seem to speak of demigod worship, jñāna-yoga and karma-yoga, **only a person who has parābhakti and equal bhakti for the guru** can have the actual import of the śāstras revealed to him. A karma-yogī, jñāna-yogī or aṣṭāṅga-yogī cannot understand the complete import. **Only a devotee can.**

### 3.9 Creation by the Lord devoid of material guṇas

- Having shown that bhakti is the means, Sūta establishes the consistency of all the scriptures that Bhagavān is supreme.
- Verse (next in this chapter): the powerful Lord alone, devoid of material guṇas, created this universe by His material energy, composed of the material guṇas and endowed with cause and effect.

**Śrīdhara Svāmī's commentary:**

- **Objection:** In all scriptures the supreme principle, Brahman, is seen as engaged in creating the material world, entering it and regulating it. How can all scriptures indicate Vāsudeva?
- Four verses explain this. Creation here means the process from the manifestation of mahat-tattva up to the manifestation of Brahmā. The Lord's entrance into the world is described in later verses. Sūta is speaking to Śaunaka.
- Conclusion: even in creation, **only Bhagavān is indicated**. If someone says "Brahman," the understanding is *brahmaṇo hi pratiṣṭhāham*: Kṛṣṇa says, "I am the shelter of that Brahman."

## 5. Key Takeaways

- **False ego vanishes the moment one receives the seed of devotion. Only the inertia of past false ego remains, and it ends completely at prema.**
- If one calls a tendency "false" and does not want it, it is inertia of past false ego. The test is whether the quality would be present in one's state of perfection.
- A devotee's sufferings only look like karma. They are provided by Kṛṣṇa.
- The repentance of non-devotees rests on a changing material value system, not on śāstra. For the devotee all identification with the material body is false ego.
- A personal definition of a devotee is acceptable as terminology, but it should not be presented as Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī's definition of uttama-bhakti.
- Bhakti is blissful even during sādhana, unlike karma and jñāna, because it allows the engagement of all senses, mind and intelligence.
- "Wise" (*kavi*) means fortunate and śraddhāvān (sukṛti quotient), not intellectually intelligent, and not jñānīs.
- One should not worship the devatās. Worship is for Viṣṇu alone, and the devatās are to be respected without criticism or envy.
- Viṣṇu and Śiva have bodies that are not made of material guṇas, though they are in charge of the guṇas through māyā.
- Food offered to demigods in poor consciousness is not prasāda, but need not be thrown away. Devotees need not do piṇḍa-dāna, since worship of Viṣṇu benefits the devatās and Pitṛs more.
- Those with material desires are not obliged to worship demigods and can worship Kṛṣṇa.
- The three modes can affect śāstrīya-śraddhā, laukikī-śraddhā and svabhāva. A sādhaka has pure śāstrīya-śraddhā with a contaminated svabhāva.
- Vāsudeva is the purport of all Vedas and processes. The śāstras prescribe lower paths according to the faith and qualification of the person.
- The complete import of the śāstras is revealed only to one with parā-bhakti to the Lord and equal bhakti to the guru.
- Even in creation, only Bhagavān is indicated.

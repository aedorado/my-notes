# Bhakti Sandarbha – Anuccheda 23 and Second-Canto Proofs: Karma and Jñāna Are Useless Without Bhakti; Rejecting Varṇāśrama Considerations; Meditation on the Universal Form and the Worship of the Lord Alone

## 1. Recap of Previous Lecture

The lecturer recaps the discussion from the first canto, second chapter, on the characteristics of pure devotional service, beginning from the third Anuccheda.

- **Pure bhakti alone pacifies the heart.** Varṇāśrama practice does not pacify the heart, because it does not create *viṣvaksena-kathā-rati*. Therefore it is not **para-dharma** (1.2.6, *sa vai puṁsāṁ paro dharmo…*).
- The next verse says that exclusive bhakti-yoga automatically gives rise to **jñāna and vairāgya** (*vāsudeve bhagavati bhakti-yogaḥ prayojitaḥ…*). **The practice of bhakti in itself creates all good qualities.**
- *Dharmaḥ svanuṣṭhitaḥ puṁsāṁ viṣvaksena-kathāsu yaḥ…* The lecturer poses the objection: is varṇāśrama-dharma called dharma? Yes, but it is not para-dharma because it cannot create *viṣvaksena-kathā-rati*. **Without bhakti even the limbs of karma, jñāna and yoga bear no fruit.**
- The goal of any dharma should be **tattva-jijñāsā** (*dharmasya hy apavargyasya…*, *kāmasya nendriya-prītiḥ…*).
  - Material results (artha, kāma) may be obtained but should not be the goal.
  - One should focus on **apavargya dharma**, which means liberation for the jñāna-yogīs and prema for the bhakti-yogīs.
  - The purpose of life is tattva-jijñāsā.
- The tattva is the **absolute truth in three phases**: Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān (*vadanti tat tattva-vidas…*). It is realised by the process of bhakti, which is learned by surrendering to a guru.
- **1.2.12 / 1.2.13:** Bhakti alone is real dharma.
  - Varṇāśrama is called dharma, but it is not the dharma that can really please the Supreme Lord (*ataḥ pumbhir dvija-śreṣṭhā varṇāśrama-vibhāgaśaḥ…*).
  - The real perfection is that which pleases the Lord (Hari), and what pleases the Lord is bhakti.
  - If the result of dharma, namely pleasing the Lord, is bhakti with a taste for hearing, then it must be concluded that **bhakti consisting of hearing must be performed**. "One need not perform anything else."
- *Tasmād ekena manasā…* (1.2.14): The real perfection of all dharma is pleasure to Lord Hari. Therefore, with a mind dedicated only to bhakti, without distraction by karma, dhyāna or yoga, one should every day constantly hear about, glorify and meditate upon the Lord.
- The lecturer ties this to Rūpa Gosvāmī's definition: *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyaṁ jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛtam ānukūlyena kṛṣṇānuśīlanaṁ bhaktir uttamā*.
- The glorification of hearing: *yad anudhyāsinā yuktāḥ karma-granthi-nibandhanam…* Through the sword of remembrance, nourished by hearing and chanting, intelligent people cut the knots of karma. "Who will not develop attraction for the topics of the Lord?"
- **How does one develop vāsudeva-kathā-ruci?** (*śuśrūṣoḥ śraddadhānasya…*, 16th verse)
  - First is **satāṁ kṛpā**: a Vaiṣṇava becomes very compassionately disposed towards the living entity.
  - Then **mahat-sevā**, serving great devotees, from which one develops vāsudeva-kathā-ruci.
  - That matures into **śraddhā**. Then comes **guru-pādāśraya**, which is *puṇya-tīrtha-niṣevaṇa*.
  - Then an inclination to engage in devotional service (**śuśrūṣā**, bhajaneṣu spṛhā).
  - These are the **six stages** indicated in this verse.
- The remaining stages:
  - **Bhajana-kriyā:** *śṛṇvatāṁ sva-kathāḥ kṛṣṇaḥ…* The abhadras within the heart are destroyed, which is **anartha-nivṛtti**.
  - **Niṣṭhā (ninth stage):** *naṣṭa-prāyeṣv abhadreṣu…* When most of the anarthas are gone, naiṣṭhikī bhakti results.
  - **Ruci and āsakti:** *tadā rajas-tamo-bhāvāḥ…* The mind becomes unaffected by lust, greed, anger, hatred and delusion, arising from rajas and tamas. It becomes fixed in the form of the Lord at the stage of āsakti and becomes completely satisfied.
  - **Bhāva and prema:** *evaṁ prasanna-manaso…* The mind becomes joyful and satisfied on attaining rati. Prema develops with complete detachment, and the devotee experiences the Lord's form, qualities, pastimes, powers and sweetness.
  - **Harer mādhurya-anubhava:** direct darśana of the Lord (*bhidyate hṛdaya-granthiḥ…*). Ignorance is cut, and all doubts (such as "will I be able to see Kṛṣṇa?" and "does Kṛṣṇa really hear me?") are completely gone because one is directly seeing and interacting with Kṛṣṇa. The last traces of material vāsanās are destroyed.
- These **14 stages** were described by quoting from the first canto, second chapter.
- **Bhakti is blissful even in sādhana** (*ato vai kavayo nityaṁ…*). The word *ataḥ* comes many times to indicate a conclusion ("therefore"). The wise constantly perform bhakti, which gives great joy to the mind, and even while practising it is very pleasurable (*susukhaṁ kartum avyayam*).
- It was concluded that one should give up efforts in karma, jñāna and vairāgya and perform bhakti to the Lord. **Worship of devatās, being a part of karma, should also be given up.** This is explained in seven verses.
  - Brahmā and Śiva should not be worshipped, though they are guṇāvatāras of the Lord, because they are not directly Parabrahman like Viṣṇu.
  - The one Puruṣa accepts the guṇas of prakṛti and appears as Viṣṇu, Brahmā and Śiva. Still, for all auspiciousness one should worship only Viṣṇu, whose body is made of śuddha-sattva.
  - Just as smoke is better than wood and fire better than smoke, rajas is better than tamas and sattva better than rajas. Hence one should worship the representative of sattva (Viṣṇu), not those of rajas and tamas (Brahmā and Śiva).
  - The ancient sages worshipped the Lord whose body is of śuddha-sattva.
  - Persons desiring liberation or prema reject the frightful forms of Śiva and other devatās and worship only the **forms of the Viṣṇu-tattva** (Nārāyaṇa forms), **but do not criticise the devatās**.
- **Why people worship demigods:** Those who desire material benefits such as progeny, power and wealth, who may be in sattva, rajas or tamas, worship beings who are similarly in those modes.
  - The three modes can affect **śāstrīya-śraddhā**, **laukikī-śraddhā** (sentimental faith) and **svabhāva**.
  - **Pure śāstrīya-śraddhā** inspires uttama-bhakti.
  - **If affected by goodness:** karma-yoga, jñāna-yoga, niṣkāma-karma-yoga, or karma-miśrā, jñāna-miśrā or yoga-miśrā bhakti, depending on the extent of the influence. All of these worship only Kṛṣṇa.
  - **If affected by rajas:** sakāma-karma-miśrā bhakti or sakāma-karma-yoga, but they definitely worship Viṣṇu only.
  - **If affected by tamas:** one may worship Viṣṇu with desires for violence and the like.
  - **Laukikī-śraddhā** is for people unwilling to ask the śāstras whom to worship. Based on their own śīla and the predominance of particular modes they are inclined to worship particular personalities (*yajante sāttvikā devān…*). This is svabhāva-jā faith, explained in Bhagavad-gītā chapter 17.
    - Those predominantly in sattva are inclined to worship devatās, those in rajas the yakṣas and rākṣasas, and those in tamas pretas and bhūta-gaṇas.
  - **Svabhāva:** the modes shape one's conditioned nature, leading to a particular **stimulus-response system** formed by vāsanās. A rajasic person's mouth waters at spicy food, and a tamasic person is very attracted on seeing liquor.
  - **Sādhakas** have pure śāstrīya-śraddhā but are parallelly influenced by lust, anger, envy, greed, illusion, pride and desire for name, fame and glorification, because their svabhāva is influenced by the modes. Śraddhā and svabhāva are "in one sense completely independent." Someone can have pure śraddhā with quite contaminated svabhāva, which is **the position of sādhakas, especially in early practice**.
- **Vāsudeva-parā vedāḥ…** (1.2.28–29): The purport of the Vedas, the object of sacrifices, and the dependence of yoga, varṇāśrama, knowledge, bhakti, prema and liberation are all on Vāsudeva.
- **All scriptures are consistent** in establishing Bhagavān as supreme (*sa evedaṁ sasarjāgre…*, rendered in the lecture as "Sayavedam sasarjagre bhagavan atmamaya…").
  - Having shown that bhakti is the means, Sūta establishes the consistency of all scriptures.
  - This is the conclusion of all the scriptures, not just of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam.
  - The powerful Lord, devoid of material guṇas, created this universe by His material energy composed of guṇas and endowed with cause and effect. **It was the Supreme Lord only who created**, even if it might be ascribed to someone else.
- The previous session ended here, "day before yesterday."

## 2. Opening Q&A

### Q (chat): Is there an example in śāstra of someone worshipping Viṣṇu with a desire to harm another, as in tāmasī bhakti?

- Diti is **one example in the Bhāgavatam** as far as the lecturer can recollect. She started worshipping Lord Viṣṇu with the desire of harming Indra, but because she was properly under the shelter of Kaśyapa, **her heart gradually became purified**.
- **Dakṣa** is another possibility. He had hatred towards Śiva, Nārada Muni and others, and also worshipped the Supreme Lord.
  - Whether this qualifies him as a tāmasī bhakta is **not certain**. He had offensive tendencies, so he was more of a karma-miśrā bhakta who was an offender.
  - To qualify as practising tāmasī bhakti, one should be actively praying for the downfall of someone or actively praying to commit violence. That is not very explicitly seen in the Bhāgavatam for Dakṣa.
  - **Definitely Dakṣa practised rājasī bhakti**, since he worshipped the Lord for material benefits. This is explicitly mentioned by the ācāryas.
- Apart from Diti the lecturer cannot think of another example in the Bhāgavatam.

### Q (chat): Is the worship of film stars or cricket stars laukikī-śraddhā in the mode of passion?

- For most people it is **just admiration, not exactly worship**. They are interested in cricket or movies and do not actively pray to them.
- If someone does actually do so, it could be a kind of rajasic or tamasic worship.

### Q (chat): Should respect to demigods be on the same level, or should greater demigods get more respect? Otherwise is it an offence?

The question quoted a verse in which the Lord speaks of the devotees as dear to Him. The lecturer's answer follows.

- When we offer respect (*maryādā*), there are **different types**. The lecturer cites Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's description in *Śrī Caitanya-śikṣāmṛta*, saying he does not remember the complete hierarchy.
- Maryādā can be on the level of **varṇa**, **āśrama**, **position and power**, and **spiritual consciousness**.
- The hierarchy as stated, from lowest to highest:
  1. Position and power
  2. Varṇa (a person not so powerful but of a higher varṇa deserves respect)
  3. Āśrama
  4. **One's spiritual consciousness**, "given top priority"

> [!important]
> A lesser demigod who is a **pure devotee** should be given higher respect than a greater controller who is a **mixed devotee**. Even so, the respect due to demigods because of their power and position should still definitely be given.

### Q (Speaker 2): Śāstrīya-śraddhā in sattva, rajas and tamas. Would someone taking up śāstrīya-śraddhā not be somewhat qualified and have more exposure to the Lord? How do we understand someone who believes in śāstra, is in tamas, and does bhakti?

- The lecturer notes that there are people following the **karma-kāṇḍa śāstras** with material desires (to go to svarga, to obtain wealth) who nevertheless worship the Supreme Personality of Godhead for those purposes.
- Speaker 2 mentioned varṇāśrama-dharma as śāstrīya-śraddhā where people follow it only from passion or ignorance. The lecturer replied that **depending on their level of faith they will be exposed to the particular śāstra that gives only that much exposure to the Supreme Lord**.
- On whether those in sattva who follow demigods are also following śāstra: the lecturer said they follow **peripheral śāstras**. If they do not follow śāstras at all and whimsically worship demigods according to whims and fancies, it is **even more degraded**.
- Speaker 2 asked if śāstrīya-śraddhā means strictly following varṇāśrama-dharma with the goal of satisfying Viṣṇu. The lecturer: **"Śāstric śraddhā means worship of Viṣṇu."**

## 3. Main Lecture Notes

### 3.1 Shift of source: from the first canto, second chapter, to the first canto, fifth chapter (Anuccheda 23)

- The proofs from the second chapter are over. Now he turns to the **fifth chapter, Nārada Muni's instructions to Vyāsadeva**. In Anuccheda 23 Jīva Gosvāmī puts in some of the important cardinal verses from the first canto.
- Bhakti is indicated as the process in the context of the appearance of the Bhāgavatam during the discussion between Nārada and Vyāsa.

### 3.2 Karma and jñāna are useless without bhakti (1.5.12)

> [!quote]
> *naiṣkarmyam apy acyuta-bhāva-varjitaṁ na śobhate jñānam alaṁ nirañjanam | kutaḥ punaḥ śaśvad abhadram īśvare na cārpitaṁ karma yad apy akāraṇam ||*

- **Jñāna without the bondage of karma is not glorious if it is *acyuta-bhāva-varjitam***, devoid of bhakti.
- **Jñāna without bhakti does not give the result of jñāna, namely liberation.** Even if ignorance is destroyed and one has perhaps reached the stage of a **jīvan-mukta**, one will **fall down** from there if there is no element of bhakti.
- What then of **sakāma-karma**, which causes suffering both during practice and at perfection?
  - During practice, it involves many sacrifices with proper pronunciation of mantras, proper ingredients, time, place and brāhmaṇas. This brings "so much suffering."
  - At the end one gets only **temporary pleasures**.
  - **Even niṣkāma-karma, if not offered to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, gives no result.**
- The commentary points explained by the lecturer:
  - **Brahman is devoid of karma**. Its nature is absence of karma because it alone exists. Actions relate to other objects, and if there is only one object there is no need of action.
  - Jñāna is described as ***nirañjanam*** (spotless) since it is devoid of upādhis. *Añjana* means that by which something is covered.
  - If jñāna, whose goal is Brahman devoid of upādhis, is devoid of bhakti, it has **no value at all**.
  - Karma is useless unless it leads to bhakti, because it is material and devoid of purification of existence.

> [!success]
> **Karma is useless unless it leads to bhakti, and jñāna is useless if not performed along with bhakti.**

### 3.3 Nārada's criticism of Vyāsa (1.5.15)

> [!quote]
> *jugupsitaṁ dharma-kṛte 'nuśāsataḥ svabhāva-raktasya mahān vyatikramaḥ | yad-vākyato dharma itītaraḥ sthito na manyate tasya nivāraṇaṁ janaḥ ||*

- Nārada tells Vyāsa: "You have created a **great disturbance** by teaching a **condemned subject matter** to people attached to material enjoyment, just to make them accept dharma."
- The people will think what Vyāsa taught is real dharma and **will not consider giving it up**. They will say, "Vyāsadeva has allowed enjoyment, said one can eat meat, drink wine, go to svarga."
- The lecturer says **sakāma-karma has to be given up**: desires for kāmya-karma should be given up to move up to niṣkāma-karma, and then niṣkāma-karma should be given up to move up to jñāna, and so on.
- But they do not give it up, because they say *nānyad astīti vādinaḥ*, "there is nothing greater than this."

### 3.4 Anuccheda 23 continued: Giving up svadharma (1.5.17)

> [!quote]
> *tyaktvā sva-dharmaṁ caraṇāmbujaṁ harer bhajann apakvo 'tha patet tato yadi | yatra kva vābhadram abhūd amuṣya kiṁ ko vārtha āpto 'bhajatāṁ sva-dharmataḥ ||*

- **Meaning given:**
  - If a person gives up his duties in varṇāśrama and begins worship of the Lord's lotus feet (*bhajan*)...
  - ...and then deviates, or gives up his body before reaching higher stages of bhakti (*apakvaḥ*), **there is no misfortune for him at all in the future**, because he can **continue from where he left off**.
  - But what does one gain who perfectly follows all his varṇāśrama-dharma but **does not worship the Lord**?
- The word *bhajan* here means he has **actual faith in bhakti**, even if that faith is very feeble.

### 3.5 What "giving up varṇāśrama-dharma" means

> [!important]
> Giving up varṇāśrama means giving up the **varṇāśrama consideration**, not rejecting every activity that falls under varṇāśrama.

- **The two considerations:**
  - Varṇāśrama: *dharmasya saṅkalpaḥ, adharmasya varjanam*. Anything given in the dharma-śāstra according to my varṇa and āśrama I will under no circumstances stop doing. My life is guided by the principles of dharma-śāstra.
  - Bhakti: ***ānukūlyasya saṅkalpaḥ, prātikūlyasya varjanam***. Anything favourable to bhakti I will accept, and anything unfavourable I will give up.
- If some aspect of varṇāśrama is favourable to bhakti, it is accepted because it is favourable. **When anything is accepted on this principle it becomes a limb of bhakti and no longer a limb of varṇāśrama.**
- **The occupation example:**
  - Suppose one accepts an occupation suited to one's nature, one also recommended for that varṇa in the dharma-śāstra.
  - If one accepts it because "an occupation according to my nature will not occupy much of my mental space, I will be peaceful and so can peacefully practise devotional service," it is **a limb of devotional service**.
  - If one accepts the same occupation because "the dharma-śāstras say brāhmaṇas should accept only these occupations and I want to practise varṇāśrama perfectly," then it **becomes a limb of varṇāśrama** and the practice becomes **karma-miśrā bhakti**.

> [!example]
> **The brick analogy:** The same brick can be used to build a temple or a prison. If used for a prison it becomes part of the prison, and if used for a temple it becomes part of the temple. One cannot tell from the activity itself whether it is varṇāśrama or bhakti. One must see **with what consciousness, under what consideration, with what value system** it is performed.

> [!example]
> **The two circles:** The lecturer often draws a varṇāśrama circle, centred on *dharmasya saṅkalpo 'dharmasya varjanam*, and a bhakti circle, centred on *ānukūlyasya saṅkalpaḥ prātikūlyasya varjanam*. The two may overlap hugely. Activities in the shaded overlap may look like varṇāśrama but are in reality activities of bhakti, because they are performed in the mood of ānukūlya. Only a few activities in bhakti are not recommended in varṇāśrama. Activities in the overlap become **secondary limbs of bhakti**, no longer limbs of karma.

- Rejecting varṇāśrama does not mean "this falls under varṇāśrama so I will not follow it." It means whatever it falls under, **if it is favourable to my bhakti I will accept it, and if unfavourable I will reject it.**

### 3.6 Commentary on 1.5.17: nitya and naimittika karma

- The commentary says that **only bhakti to the Lord, without caring for steady practice of one's dharma with nitya and naimittika activities, is taught here.**
  - *Nitya-karma* includes sandhyā-vandanam and similar activities.
  - *Naimittika-karma* are periodic activities such as śrāddha.
- **Objection:** If one perfects bhakti after giving up one's dharma there is no worry. But if one does not perfect bhakti and dies or falls, one becomes worthless because of giving up one's dharma. What if someone practises bhakti for a few months or years and then gives up? Is it better that he at least continued with dharma?
- **Response:** A person who has even **little practised bhakti and given up is better than one who never took up bhakti but only practises varṇāśrama-dharma.**
- One should not worry that one will be worthless if one falls or dies without completing the process, **since the person with a taste for bhakti is no longer qualified for karma.**
  - This holds so long as he has **not lost faith in bhakti**. If he still has faith but, because of circumstances and anarthas, cannot actively practise, he is still not qualified for karma.
  - **The qualification for bhakti is śraddhā, not bhajana-kriyā.** A person with faith in bhakti who has not started bhajana-kriyā is still qualified for bhakti.

> [!warning]
> One should **not** tell such a person, "Anyway you are unable to practise bhakti, so at least start practising karma." The lecturer calls this **a poor argument**. He should rather be gradually encouraged to take **baby steps** towards bhakti.

- **Will such a person take a low birth?** No. Even if one has practised bhakti and then given it up, **the next birth is not determined by karma but by Kṛṣṇa, who takes care.** The word *vā* indicates that it is impossible, because he has **bhakti-vāsanās** (impressions of bhakti).
- The lecturer then says: "On the other hand, what is attained by those who perform only their dharma but do not worship the Lord?" (Nārada speaking to Vyāsadeva).
- This concludes Anuccheda 23, which describes that **karma is useless unless it leads to bhakti, and jñāna is useless if not performed along with bhakti**.

### 3.7 Second canto proofs: bhakti as the means (abhidheya) at the start of Śukadeva–Parīkṣit

- Bhakti as the means was established at the beginning of the discussion between Sūta and the Śaunakādi ṛṣis, and between Vyāsa and Nārada. It is now described at the beginning of the conversation between Śukadeva Gosvāmī and Parīkṣit Mahārāja.
- (The number of this Anuccheda is not stated.)

> [!quote]
> *śrotavyādīni rājendra nṛṇāṁ santi sahasraśaḥ | apaśyatām ātma-tattvaṁ gṛheṣu gṛha-medhinām ||*

- Meaning: O King, there are thousands of topics that can be heard, discussed and remembered by those who do not inquire into the goal of life (*ātma-tattvam apaśyatām*), those attached to the house who commit five types of violence.
- ***Gṛheṣu* indicates not only householders but all materialistic people.** *Gṛha-medhī* means one whose consciousness only rotates around the house, that is, "me and mine." **This is not an indication of a particular āśrama**, and it is not that the unmarried have no material attachment.
- **Matha-medhīs:** The lecturer cites Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's article in *Sajjana-toṣaṇī* on *maṭha-medhīs*, who stay in the maṭha, attached to its facilities, but are not interested in working towards the higher goals of life. So this is not a criticism of any āśrama, though the word *gṛha-medhī* is used.
- ***Medha* means violence.** The **five types of violence** by mere presence in a house:
  1. In cooking, insects may fall in the fire and die.
  2. In water pots, insects may fall in and die.
  3. While sweeping, insects may be caught in the broom and die.
  4. While husking grain with mortar and pestle, insects may inadvertently be killed.
  5. While grinding atta, insects may be caught and ground.
  - This is involved anywhere one has to eat and maintain oneself.
- *Ātma-tattvam* means knowledge of the Lord, as will be understood from later verses.
- The following verse: **the Lord should be heard about, glorified and remembered by those desiring freedom from fear.** *This is bhakti*, the primary limbs of devotional service.
- The commentary: the Lord is addressed as **Sarvātmā** (the best), **Bhagavān** (indicating beauty), **Īśvara** (necessary for everything) and **Hari** (removes bondage). Liberation here means attaining the Lord while becoming free from all suffering.

### 3.8 Bhakti recommended, other forms of meditation rejected (2.1.39–40)

- Near the end of the first chapter of the second canto, **after describing meditation on the universal form** (*virāṭ-rūpa*) in the same chapter, bhakti is recommended while other forms of meditation are rejected.

> [!quote]
> *sa sarva-dhī-vṛtty-anubhūta-sarva ātmā yathā svapna-janekṣitaikaḥ | taṁ satyam ānanda-nidhiṁ bhajeta nānyatra sajjed yata ātma-pātaḥ ||*

**Jīva Gosvāmī's explanation (as presented in the lecture):**

- The Lord is the ātmā of all beings and **causes all beings to experience through their intelligence** (*dhī*, intelligence; *anubhūta*, experience).
- He is like a person experiencing all sorts of things in a dream but **not attached**. The yogī should see what he experiences as a series of dreams, "not very substantial," and should not take it seriously.
- The yogī should worship the Lord, **an ocean of bliss** (*ānanda-nidhi*), **eternal in time and space** (*satyam*), and **not be attached to anything else**, *yata ātma-pātaḥ*, because it would cause degradation.
- Engaging in karma, jñāna, yoga or demigod worship will become an obstacle in his progress.

**Śrīdhara Svāmī's commentary (called "the commentary" by default in the Sandarbhas):**

- The lecturer asks if anyone knows its name. Nobody answers, and he says it is ***Bhāvārtha-dīpikā***.
- The Lord is the ātmā within all beings and causes everyone to experience by the functions of intelligence. He is called ***dhiyāṁ patiḥ***, the Lord of intelligence.
- One should worship this Lord alone, who is eternal, and not be attached to anything else, since by attraction to anything else while pursuing bhakti or yoga, one is distracted from the goal.
- **The dream example:**
  - In a dream one produces many bodies and sees them with the senses.
  - Because the Lord possesses **vidyā-śakti**, He is not bound by these things.
  - A yogī should learn to see whatever material things come from practising bhakti or yoga as **insubstantial as dream bodies**.
- **The lecturer's elaboration on "the Lord sees through everyone's intelligence":**
  - The Lord does not just see **what** we see but **also the way we see**.

> [!example]
> One person sees a fruit on a tree and thinks, "I am hungry, I can pluck and eat it." Another sees the same fruit and thinks, "I am bored, I can use it as a ball and play." Two people see the same object through their own intelligence. The Lord has access both to what we see and to how we see it, since He is the in-charge of our intelligence (*dhiyāṁ-pati*).

- The Lord planned (*sa aikṣata*) and **existed even before the creation of intelligence**. He is the creator of the intelligence also.
- Though the Lord is the creator of the bodies in dreams, it is said that the jīvas create them, because the Lord facilitates the creation of particular bodies **according to the desire of the jīvas**.
- The Lord is **not identical with the intellects**: the intellect is material and the Lord is transcendental.
- The Lord is like a single jīva, the seer of the dream who sees many people and things. His knowledge does not depend on others.
- The commentary cites *Brahma-sūtra*, that the Lord creates objects in dreams because it is stated in the text (given in the lecture as "maya matram tu katsanya…"). Dreams by nature are not revealed to everyone.
- **The Lord has the special quality of being complete as the creator in the waking and dream states.**
- The words ***satyam*** and ***ānanda-nidhim*** indicate that the Lord is the **highest goal**.

**Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's explanation (read separately by the lecturer):**

- The yogī, like any jīva, is furnished with **impressions (vāsanās) from many births** in the material world. These are temporary, like a single dream experience of all sorts of men and enjoyment.
- The yogī sees all material existence as a series of dream bodies and does not get attached. He worships the Lord, **an ocean of bliss, eternal in time and space**, and does not worship anyone else or engage in other processes of karma, jñāna or yoga.
- **The question Viśvanātha raises:** Along with meditation on the Lord there appear certain results such as **enjoyment and power**. Should the yogī enjoy them or not?
  - If he enjoys, he is a **lax yogī**.
  - Quoting "my dear mother, when the yogī's heart is no longer attracted to the abundant enjoyments available only through yoga (siddhis and other material things), he can attain final liberation where death cannot overcome him."
  - Even if he does not enjoy them, it is still difficult to reject these enjoyments. Everything becomes easy by **discrimination**.
- **The method of discrimination:** the yogī's mind is filled with old impressions lasting from thousands of births of enjoyment and powers as lord of devatās and humans. "What is the use of experiencing those things again?" They have no permanence, like the dream-creations of soldiers, friends and ministers with all sorts of enjoyment experienced by a jīva in a dream.
- With this discrimination he gives up endeavouring in that direction and does not become attached to the happiness of the material world, **because it is limited in time and space and is without the ocean of bliss**.

### 3.9 The importance of context in understanding a verse

- The lecturer remarks that the Sandarbhas **quote verses from here and there** in the Bhāgavatam to present a theme. The student is expected to know the context. Otherwise it may be difficult to relate to the verse, and sometimes one has to go back and read a few verses before and after.

> [!quote]
> *pūrvāparānurodhena ko 'rtho 'bhimato bhavet | ity ādyam ūhanaṁ tarkaḥ śuṣka-tarkaṁ tu varjayet ||*
> (Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī, as spoken in the lecture)

- How to understand a particular statement of the śāstras: **what was spoken before, what after, who spoke it, to whom, whether as instruction, as a joke or in the mood of sarcasm.** All of that should be considered before deriving the proper meaning.

> [!important]
> **Understanding the context is a very important aspect of understanding a verse. One cannot take one statement and make it an absolute philosophical conclusion.**

- Here the context is that *virāṭ-rūpa* meditation has been mentioned in the second canto's first chapter and is **contrasted with bhakti**.

### 3.10 Summary the lecturer draws from this Anuccheda

- Bhakti is recommended while other meditations are rejected.
- The Supreme Lord is the controller of intelligence, has access to the intelligence of the jīvas, and creates and facilitates the **dream-like experience of the jīva** in the material world.
- The same Lord is **satyam** (existing in all three phases of time and space) and **ānanda-nidhi** (an ocean of bliss).
- Therefore **one should worship only the Supreme Personality of Godhead, should not be distracted by the secondary benefits of bhakti or by karma, jñāna and yoga, and should stick to bhakti**. Otherwise, *yata ātma-pātaḥ*, degradation.

## 4. Key Takeaways

- Tāmasī bhakti has an example in Diti (desire to harm Indra), who was purified under Kaśyapa's shelter. Dakṣa was clearly a rājasī bhakta.
- Respect to demigods follows a hierarchy: **spiritual consciousness first**, then āśrama, then varṇa, then position and power. A lesser pure-devotee demigod is respected more than a greater mixed-devotee demigod.
- Śāstric śraddhā means worship of Viṣṇu. People get only as much exposure to the Lord as their level of faith allows. Whimsical demigod worship is more degraded than following peripheral śāstras.
- **Jñāna without bhakti does not give liberation, and even a jīvan-mukta falls without bhakti. Niṣkāma-karma not offered to the Lord gives no result, and sakāma-karma gives suffering and only temporary pleasure.**
- Vyāsa's teaching of enjoyment-oriented dharma led people to think nothing is greater than it, so they do not give it up.
- **Giving up varṇāśrama means giving up the varṇāśrama consideration (*dharmasya saṅkalpaḥ*) and accepting the bhakti consideration (*ānukūlyasya saṅkalpaḥ*).** The same activity is a limb of varṇāśrama or of bhakti depending on the consciousness behind it (the brick analogy).
- Someone who has taken up bhakti and then falls or dies loses nothing. He continues from where he left off, his next birth is determined by Kṛṣṇa, and he is no longer qualified for karma.
- **The qualification for bhakti is śraddhā, not bhajana-kriyā.** One should not tell a person with faith who cannot practise to take up karma, but encourage baby steps in bhakti.
- *Gṛha-medhī* indicates materialistic people generally, not a particular āśrama.
- The yogī should regard the enjoyments and powers arising from practice as dream-like, temporary and insubstantial, and worship only the eternal Lord who is an ocean of bliss.
- The Lord sees both what we see and how we see it, being the Lord of intelligence, but is not identical with the material intellect.
- **Context (before, after, speaker, audience, mood) is essential to understanding a verse, and a single statement should not be made an absolute conclusion.**

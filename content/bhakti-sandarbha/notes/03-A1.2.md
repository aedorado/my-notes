## 1. Recap of Previous Lecture

### Style of the Sandarbhas
- An **Anuccheda** is a paragraph. Each paragraph communicates a particular sub-theme and is supported by verses quoted from Śrīmad Bhāgavatam and other literature.

### Sambandha established in the previous Sandarbhas
- The four previous Sandarbhas discussed relationships (**sambandha**). The first Sandarbha established the **pramāṇa**, with Bhāgavatam as the pramāṇa.
- The Bhagavat, Paramātmā and Kṛṣṇa Sandarbhas established **sambandha-jñāna**.
- The supreme principle, endowed with complete eternal supreme bliss, was described by the designations **Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān**. The highest manifestation is Bhagavān.
- The Kṛṣṇa Sandarbha established "kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam". Forms such as Viṣṇu and the Kumāras were discussed, and the highest form of Bhagavān was concluded to be Kṛṣṇa.
- In enumerating the powers of Paramātmā, **jīva-tattva** was also described.

### The jīva's lack of knowledge of the Lord
- The jīva's essential knowledge is covered by Māyā, through the misfortune of being opposed to the Lord. The jīva has therefore, without beginning, possessed no knowledge of the beginningless supreme entity.
- The lecturer said this takes us straight into the discussion of the jīva's fall.
- **īśād apetasya** literally means "being opposed".
- The jīva consequently believes he is made of dull matter composed of dregs and tamas. This is **viparyaya**, false identification with the body, by which he becomes absorbed in matter.

> [!important]
> **Three types of saṁsargābhāva (non-existence)**
> 1. **Atyantābhāva** – absolute non-existence: never existing in the past, present or future.
> 2. **Pradhvaṁsābhāva** – non-existence by destruction: something existed and was then destroyed.
> 3. **Prāgabhāva** – previous non-existence: something did not exist before but appears at a later time.

### Which type applies to the jīva's lack of knowledge of the Lord
1. It is **not atyantābhāva**, because the jīva can obtain that knowledge at some point.
   - The lecturer referred to Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu. When the jīva reaches the stage of **bhāva**, he comes in touch with the **saṁvit and hlādinī potencies** of Kṛṣṇa.
   - Saṁvit is the knowledge potency of Kṛṣṇa. The jīva then knows and realizes Kṛṣṇa and his own relationship with Kṛṣṇa.
2. It is **not pradhvaṁsābhāva**, and the lecturer called this the more important point. If the jīva had knowledge of the Lord, it could not be destroyed.
   - If the jīva realizes Kṛṣṇa and establishes a relationship with Kṛṣṇa, there is no chance that he will forget or abandon that relationship.
   - A **nitya-siddha** cannot have his knowledge of the Lord destroyed. The lecturer stressed that even a **sādhana-siddha** cannot have his knowledge destroyed.
   - He said this is confirmed in the Prīti Sandarbha: knowledge of the Lord, once established in the jīvas, should be eternal. The reason given is that this knowledge is spiritual, and anything spiritual is eternal.
3. If it is argued that the knowledge is merely unmanifested but still present in the jīva, then it is not classed as abhāva at all. By calling it abhāva, Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī indicates that there is a point of non-existence.
4. The jīva's lack of knowledge is therefore **prāgabhāva** (previous non-existence).
   - At some point, or more appropriately **till some point**, the jīva did not have knowledge of Kṛṣṇa.
   - It is then obtained by the influence of devotees' association, or by mercy (**kṛpā-bala**). Once obtained, it is never lost.
   - The footnote states that this does not exclude the possibility of obtaining the knowledge in the future. On attainment, it becomes the eternal possession of the jīva from that point onwards.

### Scriptural support quoted in the recap
- **Bhāgavatam 11th Canto**: Those who do not know Paramātmā and are fixed in goals other than the Lord engage in arguments about the reality and unreality of the world. They do not cease arguing and achieve no results, having turned their thoughts from the Lord, who gives regard only to devotees.
- **For the benefit of the jīva, the Lord teaches scripture filled with the highest mercy.** The lecturer recalled, with help from participants, the Caitanya-caritāmṛta verses beginning "māyā-mugdha jīvera nāhi svataḥ kṛṣṇa-jñāna / jīvera kṛpāya kailā kṛṣṇa veda-purāṇa" (Madhya-līlā 20.122). The next verse was also recited. A participant typed the translation:
  > [!quote]
  > The conditioned soul cannot revive his Kṛṣṇa consciousness by his own effort. Out of causeless mercy, Kṛṣṇa compiled the Vedic literature and its supplements. The forgetful conditioned soul is educated by Kṛṣṇa through the Vedic literature, the realized spiritual master and the Supersoul. Through these he can understand the Supreme Personality of Godhead as he is, understand that Kṛṣṇa is his eternal master and deliverer from Māyā, and so acquire real knowledge of conditioned life and of how to attain liberation.
- Even though the jīva cannot attain knowledge of Kṛṣṇa by himself and the Lord therefore presents the scriptures, only some jīvas benefit.
  - These are jīvas who have accumulated **adhyātma-sukṛti** over many lifetimes (**sukṛti-bala**), or who have obtained great mercy (**kṛpā-bala**).
  - They become favourable to the Lord and gain understanding at the very instant of hearing teachings about the Supreme Lord.
- Rūpa Gosvāmī in Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu: no amount of logic enables one to understand Kṛṣṇa. If one has a little faith in the **bhakti-śāstras** (bhakti-tattva-avabodhikā), one can understand the conclusions of the scriptures and understand Kṛṣṇa through them. The verse was recalled as "svalpāpi ruci…". The wording as spoken is uncertain (see Verification).

> [!important]
> Mere access to the śāstras is not sufficient. One must be endowed with **sukṛti-bala or kṛpā-bala**, and only then can the presence of the śāstras benefit the person.

- When this faith arises, the Supreme Lord is immediately captured in the heart by hearing Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. Such persons give no regard to other teachings.
- It is not that they reject other śāstras (dharma, jñāna, yoga) as bogus. They understand that those śāstras are "not meant for me". The lecturer called this a clear understanding and conviction.

### Other teachings act as stimuli (uddīpana)
- If such devotees hear other teachings, those teachings act as **uddīpana** (stimuli) for their own **rasa**, as if they were hearing pastimes of the Lord.
- **Prahlāda Mahārāja**: on hearing materialistic teachings from his teachers (Śaṇḍa and Amarka), he preached the glories of Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu to his students.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu**: after returning to Māyāpura from meeting his guru Īśvara Purī, he could think of nothing but Kṛṣṇa when teaching grammar. All the **dhātus** (root words) acted as uddīpana, and he spoke "Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa… Hṛṣīkeśa, Govinda, Mādhava, Madhusūdana".
- Disguising themselves like ordinary people, such devotees speak out of humility as if overcome by illusion or lust. Prahlāda's verse "naitan manas tava kathāsu vikuṇṭha-nātha…" was recited. The lecturer's gloss: this mind, contaminated with sin, is wicked and uncontrollable. It is not pleased with topics about the Lord, and is afflicted by lust and disturbed by joy and sorrow.

### The 24 gurus of the Avadhūta Brāhmaṇa (11th Canto)
- The 24 gurus are **not** śikṣā-guru, dīkṣā-guru or path-showing gurus. They are **sphūrti-gurus**.
- The lecturer quoted the line "sthāvara-jaṅgama dekhe, nā dekhe tāra mūrti / sarvatra haya nija iṣṭa-deva-sphūrti". For a self-realized person who has attained realization through association of a true living human guru, whatever he sees reminds him of what he has already realized.
- The Avadhūta, a jñāna-yogī, had realized the nature of the material world, the body and the soul. Seeing earth, water, fire, air and ether reminded him of what he already knew. For example, seeing Mother Earth reminded him of the **law of karma**.
- It is not that he learned about karma from the earth, since we all see the earth and do not learn about karma from it.
- Bhāgavatam is not encouraging devotees to learn from what they observe. We naturally learn according to our level of realization.
- The Avadhūta did not learn from these 24 gurus. They only reminded him of what he had realized through the agency of a real guru. They are stimuli, **reflecting surfaces of the heart** of that great personality.

> [!example]
> - **Gaura-kiśora dāsa Bābājī Mahārāja** would see a black coal-fired engine, shout "Śyāmasundara! Śyāmasundara!" and run behind the train. This is **sphūrti**, natural Kṛṣṇa-smaraṇa.
> - A person completely smitten with love for a girl is reminded of his beloved by ordinary things. The things act as sphūrti according to his level of consciousness and realization.

### Recap conclusion
- The non-existence of knowledge of Kṛṣṇa in the jīva falls into the category of **prāgabhāva**. It did not exist, then exists from a particular point of time, after which it is never lost.

## 2. Opening Q&A

### Q1. (Keśava Priya Prabhu) The distinction between the jīva's eternality and prāgabhāva; do nitya-siddhas fit into prāgabhāva? How does knowledge begin?
- The questioner stated the point as follows. The jīva's knowledge is not eternal in the same way. Prāgabhāva belongs to the category where something appears at some time and is eternal after it appears. He asked whether nitya-siddhas fit into this, since for them knowledge seems to come "in the middle". He also asked whether the beginning of knowledge must be through proper education.
- The lecturer said the discussion is about **nitya-baddha** jīvas. (This word is unclear in the transcript; see Verification.)
- On observation and realization:
  - When we observe things, we learn only what we have already learned. What you see is what you want to see or are trained to see.
  - If one is trained through the guidance of a proper guru, then whatever one sees through those eyes reflects what one learned from the guru.
  - To the extent one matures in devotional service, seeing ordinary things will reflect something according to one's realization.
- The questioner added that initially we perceive through our own mental speculation, and later through guru, sādhu and śāstra. For a totally ignorant jīva, knowledge must first be given properly by guru, sādhu and śāstra, and only then can this stage be explained.
- The lecturer said that even otherwise, ordinary things reflect knowledge according to one's level of realization. He clarified that he was speaking of **material** knowledge.

> [!example]
> - Looking at a coal-fired engine, an ordinary person may remember his grandmother making chapatis in a coal-fired oven and miss that taste now that LPG has come. A person from south India may remember something else. That is our level of realization.
> - A self-realized soul like Gaura-kiśora dāsa Bābājī, seeing the same engine, remembers Kṛṣṇa.
> - Someone who has been asked to give a class the next day may, while walking, look at something and think "I could use this as an example in my class".

> [!success]
> Anything we see acts as an uddīpana (stimulant) for whatever is on the level of our own consciousness.

### Q2. Those who are unaware of Kṛṣṇa
*(The question itself is not captured in the transcript, and the portion just before the answer is garbled. The answer is recorded as heard.)*
- They are "sleeping" and are not to be blamed. It can only be said that they are **unfortunate**.
- They cannot be blamed for not worshipping Kṛṣṇa or not surrendering to Kṛṣṇa.
- For mundane matters they do have experience. They know that doing good brings reward and doing bad brings punishment, and that level of material knowledge of right and wrong they do have. They will receive punishment at that level.
- They can be pitied, and are supposed to be pitied, but they cannot be blamed.

### Q3. Our innate potential for knowledge is like a blank Excel sheet that can hold a lot of data, some data, or remain blank.
- The lecturer agreed: "that is true".

### Q4. If the soul is eternal, does the soul have a beginning?
- No. The word "eternal" is used in many different ways in the śāstra.
- Bhagavad-gītā states: *na tv evāhaṁ jātu nāsaṁ na tvaṁ neme janādhipāḥ / na caiva na bhaviṣyāmaḥ sarve vayam ataḥ param*. There was never a time when I did not exist, nor you, nor these kings, and there will never be a time in future when we cease to exist.
- Applying the three types of abhāva:
  - The non-existence of the soul is **atyantābhāva** (never existed). The soul is always present, so there is no point at which it has been non-existent.
  - The **saṁsāra** of the living entity is **pradhvaṁsābhāva**. It has been there eternally till it is destroyed by the power of bhakti, and is no longer there once destroyed.
  - The conditioned jīva's **knowledge of Kṛṣṇa** is **prāgabhāva**. It did not exist before, and then, when the jīva comes in touch with a devotee, he receives that knowledge, after which it is eternal.
- The lecturer wrote these on the board to make them clearer.

> [!note]
> In the spoken passage the lecturer at times said "pradhvaṁsā abhāva" or "atyantikā" where the sense requires prāgabhāva or atyantābhāva. The assignments above follow the sense of the discussion (see Verification).

### Q5. (Ādi Bhagavān Prabhu) Can kṛpā-bala and sukṛti-bala reveal realizations about things a devotee has never studied or known?
- The questioner's background: a devotee's understanding grows by studying scripture, then by practising bhakti, with anartha-nivṛtti and realizations following. He asked whether the two balas can reveal things never studied and, if so, whether there are examples.
- The lecturer's answers:
  - The very aspect of **kṛpā-bala** is a sudden dawn of realization without visible serious practice. **Sukṛti-bala** is more gradual, leading to śraddhā, then sādhana, then gradual realization. What is called kṛpā-bala here is a sudden heavy dose of kṛpā.
  - Ultimately everything is kṛpā. He confirmed that he was speaking of **kṛpā-siddhi**.
  - The questioner pressed the point: even a sādhana-bhakta needs mercy for knowledge to become understanding and then realization. The lecturer agreed that **sukṛti itself is kṛpā**, since sukṛti is obtained through "satāṁ kṛpā mahat-sevā". He also agreed that any realization within the realm of bhakti needs the support of kṛpā, and that kṛpā is always there. Endeavour is also needed ("two fingers, two shots", as he put it).
  - On realizations a sādhaka did not endeavour for: every sādhaka endeavours to progress, but may have an immature understanding of what constitutes advancement. Seeing that he wants to progress, Kṛṣṇa may sometimes give realizations different from his own prior definition of success. This is at least partly in response to his endeavour with a desire to advance.
  - Kṛṣṇa may reveal a deeper definition or understanding. The lecturer said we often see this in our own lives, when we realize that our definition of success in devotional service was short-sighted and get a chance to recalibrate it.

### Q6. (Ramaṇi/Madhavi Prabhu) How do the three categories of saṁsargābhāva fit with the verse from Bhāgavatam 11.2.37?
- The lecturer said not all three categories relate to that verse. The verse says that for the jīva who is īśād apetasya (averse to the Lord), Māyā covers his essential knowledge. Because the term saṁsargābhāva appears here, Bhānu Swami (the commentator of this edition) explains the three kinds of non-existence. Of these, the jīva's lack of knowledge of Kṛṣṇa falls into the **prāgabhāva** category.
- The second half of the verse: *bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ syād īśād apetasya viparyayo 'smṛtiḥ / tan-māyayāto budha ābhajet taṁ bhaktyaikayeśaṁ guru-devatātmā*.
- If by fortune the jīva, who from the beginning has had no knowledge of Kṛṣṇa, comes in touch with a guru, accepts the guru as his most worshipable deity, and practises bhakti under his guidance, then prāgabhāva ceases. Knowledge dawns by the power of bhakti and the guru's mercy.
- The verse thus shows the state of prāgabhāva and the conditions under which it ends. The questioner confirmed his understanding: the three categories exist, and this verse refers to prāgabhāva.

## 3. Main Lecture Notes

### 3.1 The next question: method of attainment and final goal
- Even though the final conclusion is the existence of the Supreme Lord, there remain the method of attainment and the final goal of the devotee after accepting the teachings about the Lord.
- In the Bhagavat, Paramātmā and Kṛṣṇa Sandarbhas the teachings about the Lord were obtained. The question now is the **abhidheya** (means) and **prayojana** (goal).
- These two should be taught respectively as conclusions.

### 3.2 Definition of abhidheya and prayojana

> [!important]
> **Abhidheya** is the actions producing favourableness to the Lord, since that is the opposite of the jīva's aversion to the Lord.
> - This means **worship of the Lord from which knowledge of the Lord arises**. Worship is abhidheya because it kindles favourableness towards the Lord.
>
> **Prayojana** is realization of the Lord (**sākṣātkāra**): internal and external perception of the Lord, from which all suffering is destroyed.

- The lecturer connected this to the prāgabhāva discussion: the conditioned soul's prāgabhāva-type lack of knowledge of Kṛṣṇa is why he is **Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukha**. The abhidheya is therefore what turns him favourable.

### 3.3 Why these topics are taught again specifically
> [!example]
> Jīva Gosvāmī's analogy: when a poor man hears that there is a treasure in this house, he immediately attempts to find it. Nevertheless, to prevent negligence in the attainment, the teachings are given again specifically to describe the means and the goal.

- It is not that the previous three Sandarbhas had nothing on abhidheya and prayojana. They are mentioned there, but given more specifically and clearly in the last two Sandarbhas so that there is no negligence in applying the teachings.
- The lecturer's reasoning:
  - One cannot speak through three full Sandarbhas without touching abhidheya and prayojana. Otherwise one could not maintain the audience's interest, since people will not study such technical material for long merely for general knowledge.
  - If listeners apply their intelligence, energy and time, there must be a clear understanding of abhidheya and prayojana. Listening to and studying the Sandarbhas should itself be seen as part of abhidheya.
  - No one waits until Bhakti Sandarbha to understand what abhidheya is.
- Even so, to give a very specific and clear understanding of the process of attaining **prema**, two Sandarbhas are dedicated exclusively to these topics.
  - **Bhakti Sandarbha**: abhidheya. It describes in great detail the path of sādhana-bhakti, both **vaidhī** and **rāgānugā**.
  - **Prīti Sandarbha** (the sixth): prayojana. It gives an exclusive and detailed discussion of the state of prema and the rasas exchanged between the Supreme Lord and his devotees.

### 3.4 Scripture as a cure for the cause of illness
- Jīva Gosvāmī: after explaining the jīva's cause of suffering as aversion to the Lord with beginningless ignorance of his eternal existence, the scriptures teach favourableness to the Lord, **which is like a cure for the cause of illness**.
- The lecturer's comments:
  - "Scriptures" can indicate all scriptures, since the purpose of all scripture is to gradually bring the Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukha living entity to become **Kṛṣṇonmukha**. Individual scriptures do this according to the qualification of the jīva.
  - If "scriptures" is taken as **bhakti-śāstras** specifically, they do it more directly. The jīvas qualified for bhakti-śāstras have already indicated that they are willing to become Kṛṣṇonmukha.

### 3.5 Prahlāda's verse: the sequence of the fall and its solution (Bhāgavatam 11.2.37)
Quoted: *bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ syād īśād apetasya viparyayo 'smṛtiḥ / tan-māyayāto budha ābhajet taṁ bhaktyaikayeśaṁ guru-devatātmā*. (The lecturer attributed this verse to Prahlāda in one place and to Kavi speaking to Videha in another; see Verification.)

> [!abstract]
> **Sequence as explained by the lecturer**
> 1. **īśād apetasya** – aversion to the Lord (the beginning).
> 2. **Tan-māyā** – Māyā takes over the jīva.
> 3. **Asmṛti** – the jīva forgets his constitutional position (lack of identification with the self).
> 4. **Viparyaya** – false identification with the body.
> 5. **Dvitīyābhiniveśa** – absorption in material coverings of the soul (the body).
> 6. **Bhaya** – fear of saṁsāra. This is well known as illusion.
>
> **Solution:** *budhaḥ* – an intelligent person, *guru-devatātmā* – taking the guru as the Lord and as his very self, should worship the Supreme Lord (*īśam ābhajet*) with ***ekayā bhaktyā*** (pure bhakti). That is, perform pure devotional service through the guidance of a guru.

- The lecturer said "stages 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5" while summarizing. The numbering above follows the order he stated in the explanation.
- **Śrīdhara Svāmī's commentary**, as reported:
  - One should worship the Lord because fear arises from the Lord's Māyā.
  - Fear arises from identity with the body.
  - This identification arises from **ahaṅkāra**, and ahaṅkāra arises from lack of vision of the **svarūpa** of the jīva.
  - This false identification is called viparyaya.
  - Māyā acts this way because aversion to the Lord causes non-appearance of the svarūpa of the jīva, and the opposite awareness of "ayam" (this body) arises.
  - Absorption in the body is called dvitīyābhiniveśa, and fear arises from it.
- **Parallel in Bhagavad-gītā** (7.14): *daivī hy eṣā guṇamayī mama māyā duratyayā / mām eva ye prapadyante māyām etāṁ taranti te*.
  - The first two lines correspond to the first three lines of the Bhāgavatam verse. Māyā made of the guṇas, fit for the jīva's pleasure, is very hard to surpass.
  - The second two lines correspond to the solution. One who takes shelter of Kṛṣṇa and worships Him through pure devotional service can cross over Māyā.
- One should worship the Lord without deviation (*ekayā*), thinking of the guru as the Lord, dearest to the self.

### 3.6 Conclusion of the Anuccheda's argument
- Because **Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā** is the cause of all suffering, the scriptures encourage **Kṛṣṇonmukhatā** (favourableness towards Kṛṣṇa).
- The **abhidheya** is therefore the actions producing favourableness towards the Lord, as these are the opposite of the jīva's aversion. Those actions are **worship**.
- Jīva Gosvāmī first states abhidheya as the actions producing favourableness, and then substantiates it by quoting the verse above.

## 5. Key Takeaways
1. The jīva's lack of knowledge of Kṛṣṇa is **prāgabhāva** (previous non-existence). It is not atyantābhāva and not pradhvaṁsābhāva.
2. Once knowledge of Kṛṣṇa is established in the jīva, it is spiritual and therefore eternal. It is never lost, even for a sādhana-siddha.
3. The soul's own existence is atyantābhāva of non-existence (always present). Saṁsāra is pradhvaṁsābhāva (destroyed by bhakti). Knowledge of Kṛṣṇa is prāgabhāva.
4. The jīva cannot revive Kṛṣṇa consciousness by his own effort. The Lord gives scripture, the realized guru and the Supersoul out of mercy.
5. Scripture benefits only those with **sukṛti-bala or kṛpā-bala**. Such persons place faith in the bhakti-śāstras and do not take other teachings as meant for them, but they do not call them bogus.
6. A realized devotee's perception reflects his realization. Ordinary objects, other teachings and even the Avadhūta's 24 "gurus" act as **uddīpana/sphūrti**, not as sources of new instruction.
7. Those unaware of Kṛṣṇa cannot be blamed for not worshipping Him. They can be pitied.
8. Kṛpā is always present in any realization within bhakti. Kṛṣṇa may reveal a deeper understanding of progress to a sincere sādhaka.
9. **Abhidheya** = actions producing favourableness to the Lord (worship, from which knowledge of the Lord arises). **Prayojana** = realization of the Lord (sākṣātkāra), from which all suffering is destroyed.
10. The sequence of the fall is īśād apetasya → Māyā → asmṛti → viparyaya → dvitīyābhiniveśa → bhaya. The remedy is pure bhakti, taking the guru as the Lord.
11. Bhakti Sandarbha treats abhidheya in detail (vaidhī and rāgānugā sādhana-bhakti). Prīti Sandarbha treats prayojana (prema and rasa).

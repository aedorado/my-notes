# Bhakti Sandarbha: Recap of Anucchedas 1–4, Sukṛti Discussion, and Anucchedas 5–8

## 1. Recap of Previous Lecture

> [!note]
> **Anuccheda** means a paragraph that describes a particular theme or sub-theme. It presents the theme together with supporting verses (predominantly from Śrīmad Bhāgavatam) and an explanation of those verses to substantiate the point.

### Anuccheda 1: Root cause of the jīva's suffering
- Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī established that the root cause of the jīva's suffering is a **Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukha** nature.
- The conditioned soul's knowledge of Kṛṣṇa is in a state of **prāg-abhāva** (prior non-existence). This holds until the jīva receives the seed of devotion and attains perfection through its nourishment.
- Once the jīva receives knowledge of his relationship with Kṛṣṇa, it is never lost.
- Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja Gosvāmī's expression in Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta, **anādi-bahirmukha**, indicates this prior non-existence of the jīva's knowledge of Kṛṣṇa.
- Some jīvas, through accumulated impressions for understanding the Lord over many births, or by some great mercy, become favourable and gain understanding at the very instant of hearing teachings about the Supreme Lord.
- The Lord has introduced the śāstras to help the jīva turn towards Kṛṣṇa, but taking them seriously requires **sukṛti**. Without sufficient sukṛti, a person will not be attracted to the bhakti-śāstras at all. Even basic attraction needs some sukṛti.
- If a person already has ulterior motives in approaching Śrīmad Bhāgavatam, those motives will simply be fulfilled. This does not lead to accumulation of sukṛti, because **anya-abhilāṣa** prevents assimilation of **ajñāta-sukṛti**. The result is **pratibimba-bhakti-ābhāsa**.
- By the association of Vaiṣṇavas, one develops attraction to the bhakti-śāstras. Such a person will not accept karma-, jñāna- or yoga-śāstras as fit for personal practice. Even if he hears teachings from other śāstras by chance, they only inspire him towards hearing the pastimes of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. The example given was Prahlāda Mahārāja.
- Having got attraction and learnt the **sambandha**, the next topic is **abhidheya**: the practices that lead to favourableness towards the Lord. This is the subject of Bhakti Sandarbha.
- Although the first four Sandarbhas already make it clear that abhidheya is śuddha-bhakti and prayojana is prema, a separate Sandarbha is dedicated to abhidheya (Bhakti Sandarbha) and prayojana (Prīti Sandarbha). Its purposes are to destroy carelessness in the sādhaka's application of the process and to give more clarity to its details.

### Anuccheda 1 (continued): Bhakti as the only way to Kṛṣṇa-unmukhatā
- The only way to address the root cause is to make the jīva **Kṛṣṇa-unmukha**. This is possible only by taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa through bhakti.
- Verse recalled (as spoken, partly garbled): *māmeva ye prapadyante māyām etāṁ taranti te*.
- Verse recalled (as spoken): *bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ syād īśād apetasya viparyayo 'smṛtiḥ tan-māyayāto budha ābhajet taṁ bhaktyaikayeśaṁ guru-devatātmā*.
- Only bhakti addresses the root of the problem and makes the jīva Kṛṣṇa-unmukha.

### Anuccheda 2: Overcoming saṁsāra by bhakti
- Verse recalled (as spoken): *evaṁ sva-citte svata eva siddha ātma-priyo 'rtho bhagavān anantaḥ taṁ nirvṛto niyatārtho bhajeta saṁsāra-hetūparamaś ca yatra*.
- The one who worships the Supreme Personality of Godhead can destroy the ignorance that is the root cause of repeated births (*saṁsāra-hetu-uparamaḥ*).

### Anuccheda 3: Nature of pure bhakti
- Verses from the First Canto describe the nature of pure bhakti.
- **Objection:** hearing and contemplation are methods of jñāna, so jñāna and karma should also be glorified because they support bhakti. Why does the lecturer say only to worship the Lord with bhakti, with no regard for karma and jñāna?
- **Response:** one should worship by direct bhakti consisting of hearing and chanting about the Lord. Sūta Gosvāmī says real joy of mind is achieved only by performing bhakti.
- The practice that really satisfies the heart is that bhakti which is **ahaituki** and **apratihatā**.
  - **Ahaituki:** having no goal other than establishing a relationship with Kṛṣṇa (explained as *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam*).
  - **Apratihatā:** not covered by karma and jñāna.
- **Why:** varṇāśrama-dharma does not produce attraction for the topics of the Lord, so it is a waste of time (*śrama eva hi kevalam*).
  - When varṇāśrama practice contains some element of bhakti, it gives svarga or other material benefits. At most it may lead to purification of the heart and eventually impersonal liberation.
  - It will not lead to prema, because it does not lead to *viṣvaksena-kathāsu ratiḥ*. The varṇāśrama system is not designed that way.
  - *Viṣvaksena-kathāsu rati* comes only through the association of Vaiṣṇavas. A prior practice of varṇāśrama is not a prerequisite for this at all.
- This point is repeated heavily by Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī in Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, and now by Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī, especially in Bhakti Sandarbha, which is the Sandarbha of abhidheya (what is to be practised).

> [!important]
> The lecturer says that whenever Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava ācāryas talk about śuddha-bhakti, the "first attack" is always on karma. He says he can speak only for Gauḍīya ācāryas. The reason is that in all other sampradāyas it is often misunderstood that one can never give up *nitya-naimittika* karmas. The ācāryas therefore say at the outset that varṇāśrama duties are not limbs of bhakti and will not lead to *viṣvaksena-kathā-rati*.

> [!info]
> The lecturer acknowledges that this can look like an unfair "under the belt" attack. He says it has to be stated clearly because it is so often misunderstood. Kṛṣṇa's system of creating taste and attachment for bhakti comes only through Vaiṣṇavas, who may themselves not come under any varṇāśrama and may be born as **antyajas**. There are many examples in our own paramparā. This is **para-vadharma**.

- If the practice of karma, jñāna or yoga lacks even a small element of bhakti, it gives no result.
- Verse recalled (as spoken): *naiṣkarmyam apy acyuta-bhāva-varjitaṁ na śobhate jñānam alaṁ nirañjanam kutaḥ punaḥ śaśvad abhadram īśvare na cārpitaṁ karma yad apy akāraṇam*.
- Verse recalled: *ataḥ pumbhir dvija-śreṣṭhā varṇāśrama-vibhāgaśaḥ svanuṣṭhitasya dharmasya saṁsiddhir hari-toṣaṇam*. The complete perfection of dharma according to the divisions of varṇāśrama is pleasing the Lord.
- Bhakti is distinct from the dharma described in the text. Its **svarūpa** qualities are described: it must be ahaituki (*anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam*) and apratihatā (not covered by jñāna and karma).
- When a taste for bhakti develops, or when there is a small **śraddhā**, bhakti-yoga characterised by hearing and chanting begins. **Śraddhā itself is also bhakti**, even if it has not yet matured into **bhajana-kriyā**, the tangible practice.

> [!example]
> Śraddhā may mature into bhajana-kriyā immediately for some people and after some time for others. The lecturer shares from his own life that there was a gap of about five to five-and-a-half years between the stage of śraddhā and bhajana-kriyā. He developed the faith that what these people were doing was right, but taking up the practice himself took that much longer.

- Verse recalled (as spoken): *svalpāpi ruci revasyād bhakti-tattva-avabodhikā*. He identifies this as quoted from Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu by Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī.
  - To understand bhakti, one needs **svalpa-ruci**, which here refers to svalpa-śraddhā.
  - When svalpa-śraddhā in the teachings of the bhakti-śāstras is there, it gradually matures into bhajana-kriyā. One can then see the person tangibly engaged in śravaṇam, kīrtanam, smaraṇam and so on.

### Anuccheda 4: Bhakti is independent, the other parts are dependent on bhakti
- Verse recalled: *yasyāsti bhaktir bhagavaty akiñcanā sarvair guṇais tatra samāsate surāḥ*. If one practises bhakti, all good qualities develop automatically.
- One develops knowledge of the Lord's svarūpa and detachment (jñāna and vairāgya, the goals of the path of jñāna) without practising any jñāna, simply by practising bhakti.
- Verse recalled: *vāsudeve bhagavati bhakti-yogaḥ prayojitaḥ janayaty āśu vairāgyaṁ jñānaṁ ca yad ahaitukam*. Bhakti, with only a little hearing, produces knowledge beyond dry logic, as stated in the Upaniṣads.
- Verse recalled: *dharmaḥ svanuṣṭhitaḥ puṁsāṁ viṣvaksena-kathāsu yaḥ notpādayed yadi ratiṁ śrama eva hi kevalam*.
  - One needs only a little taste for hearing the bhakti-śāstras (*svalpa-ruci*).
  - If dharma does not take the support of Vāsudeva and does not produce taste for the descriptions of the Lord's pastimes, it gives no result. The lecturer gives this as another meaning: if the practice of varṇāśrama lacks the element of bhakti, it gives no result.
- Meaning of the words in *śrama eva hi kevalam*:
  - **eva:** indicates the temporary nature of svarga.
  - **hi:** indicates that there is suitable proof in Śruti. This is a conclusion of śāstra, not anyone's personal opinion.
  - **kevalam:** indicates that the results of dharma that produce detachment and liberation are also not to be attained. This indicates the temporary nature of the siddhis.
- The lecturer says further proofs are then given, and that the previous discussion ended with the fourth Anuccheda.

## 2. Opening Q&A

### 2.1 Mercy of the Lord and the devotee: Madhurya-kādambinī (Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura)

A devotee shared a passage on Sukṛti from Madhurya-kādambinī, in connection with the previous week's discussion. The lecturer read it.

> [!quote]
> Viśvanātha Cakravartī writes (as read): in proposing the unqualified mercy of the devotee as the cause of devotion, one should not see a fault in the devotee's partiality in distributing mercy. The *madhyama-bhakta* seems to show partiality: *premā-maitrī-kṛpopekṣā yaḥ karoti sa madhyamaḥ*.
> - **Prema** towards Kṛṣṇa
> - **Maitrī** (friendliness) towards other Vaiṣṇavas
> - **Kṛpā** (compassion) towards the innocent
> - **Upekṣā** (avoidance) towards those hostile to bhakti
>
> In this case the Lord becomes subservient to his devotee, bestowing His mercy in accordance with the devotee's mercy.

Points the lecturer draws out:
- What the devotee distributes is not directly bhakti. The devotee distributes only an **opportunity for accumulating sukṛti**.
- The Lord's mercy follows the devotee's mercy.
- There is no irregularity in this arrangement, because the mercy appears in the devotee only because bhakti is in his heart. Without bhakti, there is no possibility of the devotee manifesting mercy to others.
- The mercy is thus the **self-manifesting independent nature of bhakti**.
- The lecturer says this is the system the Lord has created: there must be the intervention of a devotee through which one actually gets sukṛti.

### 2.2 Can Śrīmad Bhāgavatam itself give ajñāta-sukṛti?

**Question (raised by a devotee, per the lecturer's reference to the previous week):** the mercy of devotees can manifest in many ways. Prahlāda Mahārāja is a devotee, his speech manifests in the Bhāgavatam, it was spoken by Śukadeva Gosvāmī and compiled by Vyāsadeva. If someone reads that Bhāgavatam, is that not also a manifestation of devotees' mercy?

**The lecturer's answer:**
- It is not wrong to say so. Possibly another devotee distributed it to the person. The recipient may appreciate the distributor, and that will definitely give some sukṛti.
- The distributor may have spoken sweetly, which could give more sukṛti. The book may be colourfully printed and nicely bound on good-quality paper, which he may appreciate and so get more sukṛti.
- However, attraction to studying the *subject matter* requires some genuine faith in what is being read.
- That faith may sometimes be a reflected faith (**pratibimba-bhakti-ābhāsa**). The person takes shelter of the book to get ulterior motives fulfilled: for example, reading a pastime to get money or a child. With such an ulterior motive there is no question of getting ajñāta-sukṛti, because the desire he aspires after will be fulfilled.

> [!example]
> The lecturer recounts sitting in a Bhāgavatam class at Chowpatty the previous week. A devotee next to him was marvelling at Vyāsadeva's poetic ability: every verse fits the metre perfectly, without even a half syllable more or less. The lecturer notes that this devotee already had faith in the transcendental nature of the Bhāgavatam. He then contrasts a **Sanskrit scholar** who picks up the Bhāgavatam and appreciates the language, the poetry and the ornaments (he cites *nayad vacaś citra-padaṁ harer yaśo jagat-pavitraṁ pragṛṇīta karhicit*; the Bhāgavatam says glorification of the Lord may lack poetic ornaments, but the Bhāgavatam is itself filled with them).
> - Such a person, leafing through the book out of curiosity, with no deviant desire and no belief in any transcendental value of his appreciation, may get some sukṛti.
> - Appreciating the *subject matter* of Bhāgavatam requires prior faith.

> [!example]
> A **language editor** who edits the Bhāgavatam text and is paid for it gets no sukṛti from the payment. If he internally appreciates the author's command of language, which he is not paid for, he may get some sukṛti from that.

- Appreciating the real core presentation of the Bhāgavata-siddhānta needs prior existence of faith, which develops through prior assimilation of ajñāta-sukṛti.
- The **peripheral aspects** (quality of paper, binding, pictures, texture, language) can be appreciated without prior faith, and that can potentially give ajñāta-sukṛti.
- So: sukṛti can come through the Bhāgavatam, but not through appreciating its subject matter. Appreciating the peripheral ingredients can lead to assimilation of sukṛti without essential faith.

### 2.3 Keśava Priya: how can appreciating non-transcendental features give transcendental faith?

**Question:** It is tricky to understand. Someone can understand Bhāgavatam after sufficient faith, which comes from sufficient accumulation of ajñāta-sukṛti. But ajñāta-sukṛti seems to be just appreciating something not transcendental. How does appreciation of material things give transcendental faith?

**Answer:**
- The lecturer clarifies that the object is transcendental, but it is appreciated for its perceived material value.
- This is **Kṛṣṇa giving the benefit of doubt**: "You appreciated something connected to me. Because you did not ask for any material benefit in return, I wish to give you some transcendental benefit. If you had asked for a material benefit, I would have given you that."
- The questioner restates it as appreciation without expectation, which on being collected sufficiently yields transcendental understanding. The lecturer accepts this.

**Follow-up (garlands for the Lord at Ratha-yātrā):** Onlookers may not believe but may appreciate the Lord's dress or garland. Does this give ajñāta-sukṛti, since it is directly connected with the Lord?
- The lecturer says yes, provided they do not already know that this is the Supreme Personality of Godhead and appreciate with material motives or a mixed devotional understanding. In that case it will not give it.
- When the questioner clarifies that people mainly appreciate the garland-makers' colour combinations, the lecturer says that gives sukṛti.

### 2.4 Role of the devotee after śraddhā

**Question:** If devotees can distribute only the opportunity to accumulate ajñāta-sukṛti, what is the role of the devotee after one gets śraddhā? We are always dependent on devotees, and listen to many sessions.

**Answer:**
- "Everything. You can't do anything without the association of devotees."
- When asked how to define what a devotee does for those listening, the lecturer says **the faith gets strengthened**. If we connect with a devotee who has more faith and he teaches us, our faith is strengthened.

### 2.5 Ananga Sundarī Mātājī: from ajñāta-sukṛti to solid faith

**Q1:** Is accumulated ajñāta-sukṛti translated into faith, solid faith?
- **A:** Not solid faith, but at least **feeble faith to begin with**.

**Q2:** What is the cause of solid faith?
- **A:** Solid faith comes when the feeble faith is put into practice and, with good practice, we progress more and more. There is **anartha-nivṛtti** on one side and **artha-pravṛtti** on the other. As that becomes stronger, the faith also becomes more solid.

**Q3:** Many devotees struggle for decades without solid faith, while some pick it up very quickly. What causes the difference?
- **A:** Several factors are given:
  1. Struggling devotees may have **improper practice or improper association**.
  2. Those who progress quickly may have **practised in previous lives** and reached a certain level, so they get a quick head start.
  3. Or in this life they may be fortunate to get really nourishing association of devotees.
  4. The others may have had no past practice of devotional service, may not have been fortunate with association, or may have good association but also associate with other elements and ideologies that weaken the faith.
  5. They may not practise very sincerely from their own side.
- Two people apparently taking up devotional service at the same time can therefore progress very differently.

### 2.6 Discussion with Ādi Bhagavān Prabhu (on Madhurya-kādambinī and the role of personal association)

**His point:** Viśvanātha Cakravartī analyses how sukṛti develops in a person. Of the many spiritual factors in a person's life, he takes one: **association of a devotee (sādhu-saṅga)**. Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu says all five limbs are very important, and that even contact with one of them can give a very high level of prema or bhāva if there are no offences. Sādhu-saṅga is the *aṅgī*, the basis. Objects such as deities, Mathurā and Bhāgavatam are glorified as fulfilling desires, but only devotee association can **change the person's desires and value system** (he refers to the Sanātana-śikṣā section of Caitanya-caritāmṛta). He says he disagrees that a book can take the place of the interaction described in the verse, since *prema-maitrī-kṛpopekṣā* has to occur in interaction with a devotee.

**The lecturer's responses:**
- Agreement that it is ultimately the association of Vaiṣṇavas. The person who gave the Bhāgavatam, bound it, printed it, translated it and desired it were Vaiṣṇavas. Similarly with a cup of prasādam: the cook was a Vaiṣṇava. That prasādam is in one sense a form of sādhu-saṅga, because it gives an opportunity to appreciate something connected with Kṛṣṇa.
- Appreciating the *subject matter* of the Bhāgavatam needs prior sukṛti. The peripheral aspects can be appreciated, and because Vaiṣṇavas are involved in them, they can generate sukṛti.
- **The sukṛti being discussed is not every kind.** It is a sukṛti that matures into pure devotional faith and then prema. The lecturer says that whenever he says sukṛti, 99.99% of the time he means this. He is not interested in mixed devotional service so much.
  - If the same appreciation is directed to a Bhāgavatam compiled by a non-pure or mixed devotee (he gives a Gorakhpur Press edition as an example), it will not lead to pure devotional sukṛti, though some mixed devotional sukṛti may be gained.

> [!important]
> **Ajñāta-sukṛti and motive.** When a person studies the Bhāgavatam (or takes prasādam) *with a goal* (material benefit or liberation), he will not get ajñāta-sukṛti; he moves toward his goal. If there is a **generic appreciation without a transactional mentality**, the sukṛti comes. This holds even if the person has a mixed devotional seed.
> - The lecturer's reasoning: if appreciation of peripheral aspects could not give sukṛti to someone who already has a mixed devotional seed, then once someone receives a mixed devotional seed he would be permanently barred from becoming a pure devotee.
> - If there is no conscious appreciation of the **transcendental nature** of the object (prasādam or Bhāgavatam), it can potentially give sukṛti to anyone. This includes **non-humans**: an animal receiving Bhāgavat-prasādam or hearing the holy names gets some sukṛti. The only condition is that there be no transactional mentality.

> [!example]
> A person may pray in front of the deity, "let me pass my exam," with a transactional mentality. Afterwards he goes to eat the offered food and simply enjoys it, not seeing it as a transcendental activity that will help him progress. If the prasādam was sincerely prepared and offered, and he eats and appreciates it, he will get pure devotional sukṛti even though he has a material goal and mixed devotional understanding.
> - Reading Bhāgavatam to get material benefit or liberation will not bestow it. Appreciating peripheral aspects without a transactional mood leads to accumulation of ajñāta-sukṛti.

- Ādi Bhagavān Prabhu agrees that offensive mentality will reduce in such a case.

### 2.7 Need for personal guidance (Amṛta-vāṇī of Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura)

Ādi Bhagavān Prabhu raised the importance of personal guidance and quoted a passage on whether a Jagad-guru's instructions suffice without an initiating spiritual master.

**Question as read:** If Jesus Christ was a Jagad-guru and his instructions are capable of bringing deliverance, why do we need an initiating spiritual master?

> [!note]
> The lecturer first says the context is different. The discussion here is about the realm of sukṛti, whereas this passage concerns the conscious practice of bhakti by one who has already developed śraddhā and needs a spiritual master. It is similar in theme but not identical.

**Answer as read:**
- We accept both the universal spiritual master and the initiating spiritual master.
- By accepting only the Jagad-guru, we may face many anarthas. If we think we do not need an initiating guru, we will face doubt about how well we can follow his orders, because every time we need a **live person to contextualise** and say "this is the way it is possible."
- The Supreme Lord or Jagad-guru delivers instructions about the absolute truth only **through the disciplic succession**.
- **Analogy (Ganges):** One sits on the bank of the Ganges in Navadvīpa, far from the Himalayas where it originates, and can touch her water. The initiating spiritual master brings the Ganges of pure devotional service, which emanates from the Lord's feet, and places it in one's hand and on one's head. Without that flow, an ordinary powerless person could not climb the Himalayas to touch her water. If the flow were interrupted on the way, one would face the danger of accepting a polluted flow instead of pure Gaṅgā.
- If the instructions Jesus Christ gave 2,000 years ago do not come through disciplic succession, or we must sort them out from books, we may accept a perversion of the truth taught in the name of Christianity. We may even accept something opposite to what he taught, thinking it to be his philosophy.
- The lecturer identifies the source as Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura's Amṛta-vāṇī.

**Discussion on this passage:**
- Ādi Bhagavān Prabhu pointed to the line about "many anarthas": the offensive mentality is present even before gaining śraddhā. The lecturer says that prior to śraddhā one cannot hope that the person will consciously want to accept a guru.
- The lecturer notes this passage would be a perfect anti-rtvik-style quote (as spoken: "anti-rhythmic"), and that it is more about the guru–disciple context.
- Both agree one cannot achieve the seed of devotion without the intervention of a devotee. The lecturer's qualification: **a person being there to clarify doubts comes much later**.
- **Bālotpāditā-śraddhā vs. svābhāvikī-śraddhā:** In some cases clarification by a devotee plays a role (he refers to *bālotpāditā śraddhā*). But even for developing **svābhāvikī śraddhā** a devotee is involved, not necessarily in giving clarification. It can be just being nice to people so that they appreciate the devotees, which is not directly spiritual.
- Ādi Bhagavān Prabhu notes that there are shades of devotees among practitioners, and that not everyone giving mercy through prasādam and books is giving pure devotional sukṛti. The lecturer agrees this is possible.
- The lecturer clarifies that the topic is sukṛti, not śraddhā. For it to **fully mature into śraddhā**, a more tangible, live presence of a devotee is not denied at some point. The live active presence of a devotee is "not at all denied."
- He says direct contact is not necessary for assimilating sukṛti. This is why Bhaktisiddhānta Ṭhākura and Śrīla Prabhupāda emphasised **prasādam distribution**.
- **Personal guidance vs. personal touch:** Personal guidance applies when the person is ready, with some faith, to seek it. The lecturer prefers the term **personal touch** for the earlier stage.

> [!example]
> The lecturer contrasts an exalted devotee, **Vibhu Caitanya Prabhu**, cooking for Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma and Rādhā-Śyāmasundara with great love, with a **beginner devotee** giving personal touch. If a non-devotee happens to receive a piece of that prasādam, eats and appreciates it, the lecturer is "pretty sure" the power of that prasādam to induce sukṛti is **far more powerful than even the personal presence of an immature devotee**. Such an immature devotee may even end up giving an opportunity to accumulate **duṣkṛti**.
> - The lecturer is not trivialising personal presence, and is not claiming that this is sufficient at every stage. He is saying only that sukṛti can definitely be accumulated this way.

### 2.8 Questions from the chat box

- **One can attain prema only in the association of devotees.** The lecturer says this is not denied. Even after prema, the association of devotees is very critical.
- **Can reading Bhāgavatam influence a mixed devotee to take up pure devotional service?** Yes, provided he is connecting with pure devotee practitioners and with Śrīla Prabhupāda's purports. If he gradually develops appreciation for Prabhupāda through the purports, he may develop pure devotional faith over time.
- **Rāmānujācārya threw prasāda in the river and the fish got liberated.** The lecturer says "true", as an example.
- **Is praying for our relatives to develop faith a transactional mood?** No. That is a manifestation of **kṛpā** (*prema-maitrī-kṛpopekṣā*) and is natural, not transactional.
- **We need a guru; it can be a living śikṣā-guru.** The lecturer says that acceptance of a guru comes after śraddhā. The present discussion concerns the stage *prior to śraddhā*. After śraddhā, everyone definitely needs a guru.

### 2.9 Keśava Priya: films and television series

**Q:** If appreciating Bhāgavatam's subject matter requires faith, what about a film like *Bhakta Prahlāda* (in Tamil), where one appreciates how a small boy is so strong and devotional? Will this give ajñāta-sukṛti? What about Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa TV series?
- **A:** If they appreciate it, they get some minimal sukṛti, along with some **duṣkṛti** because of wrong portrayal. The people involved may not be practising pure devotees, so the effect will be minimal.
- The questioner notes he knew the story fully before touching Bhāgavatam. The lecturer says that after coming to Kṛṣṇa consciousness, we understand some details were right but much of the **mood** was incorrect.
- **Q:** People try to distribute faith by focusing on spreading Hinduism. Will these methods give pure devotional sukṛti?
- **A:** If pure devotees are involved, it can. If there is absolutely no involvement of pure devotees, and it is just commercial people preparing it, then even though the subject matter may be connected to Hinduism, it will "probably not" give tangible ajñāta-sukṛti that matures into pure devotional faith.

### 2.10 Keśava Priya: telling guests that food is prasādam

**Q:** When inviting people home for a pūjā (for example Dāmodara pūjā) and telling them "this is prasādam," will they not get sukṛti? Should he tell them?
- **A:** Depends on the person. One does not necessarily have to tell non-devotees.
  - If they think "this is Bhagavat-prasādam, I have been praying for my business, maybe this will do it," they will not get sukṛti from that.
  - But because the host invited them **without ulterior motive**, they may appreciate him as a good person, and that may give sukṛti.
  - Even if they accept prasādam for material benefits, if they also appreciate how it is prepared ("this is so nicely prepared, how did you make it?"), that appreciation gives sukṛti, because there is no transaction in it.
  - It is not black and white. Appreciating that it is tasty without onion and garlic will "give sukṛti for sure."

### 2.11 Chat box: sukṛti for mixed bhakti, and distributing prasādam to mixed devotees

- **Does one need sukṛti for practising mixed bhakti?** Yes (he cites *caturvidhā bhajante māṁ janāḥ sukṛtino 'rjuna*). One may need some mixed devotional sukṛti for mixed devotional service.
- **Does that sukṛti come through mixed devotees?** Quite possible, yes.
- **Can sukṛti given by a pure practising devotee give rise to mixed bhakti, depending on the soul's desire?** Yes, very much. If the person thinks "Bhagavat-prasādam has a lot of potential to fulfil all my desires," the prasādam will nourish the mixed devotional seed already in his heart.
- However, if in eating with a mixed mood he also appreciates the quality of the prasādam and the endeavour of the cook and distributor, that appreciation involves no transactional mentality and can definitely give rise to proper sukṛti.

> [!success]
> **Conclusion stated by the lecturer:** it is never a loss to distribute prasādam even to mixed devotees, on the thought that it will only nourish their mixed devotional seed. There will be some minimal appreciation of the prasādam, its cook and its distributor, and that, without a transactional mood, will definitely give rise to accumulation of pure ajñāta-sukṛti. The lecturer calls this "my understanding."

## 3. Main Lecture Notes

### 3.1 Anuccheda 5: Dharma, artha, kāma, and the true goal of dharma

#### The position being rejected
Others think the goal of dharma is material objects, in a cycle:
1. **Dharma** leads to **artha**.
2. The result of artha is fulfilment of desire, **kāma** (pleasure of the senses).
3. That pleasure gives rise to more acts of **dharma**, and so on.

This is rejected in the next two verses (as spoken):
> *dharmasya hy apavargyasya nārtho 'rthāyopakalpate*
> *nārthasya dharmaikāntasya kāmo lābhāya hi smṛtaḥ*
>
> *kāmasya nendriya-prītir lābho jīveta yāvatā*
> *jīvasya tattva-jijñāsā nārtho yaś ceha karmabhiḥ*

#### Meaning of the first verse
- Material results are not suitable as the goal for persons dedicated to higher spiritual goals.
- **Apavargya-dharma** is dharma meant for a transcendental goal (the lecturer lists liberation, prema or Vaikuṇṭha as possible goals).
- **Artha** has two meanings in the verse: (a) material benefits, as in *dharma-artha-kāma-mokṣa*, and (b) goal (*arthāya*, "for the goal"). So *artha* (material benefits) cannot be the *artha* (goal) of dharma.
- This applies to a jñāna-yogī or aṣṭāṅga-yogī aspiring for liberation or a bhakti-yogī aspiring for prema.
- *Nārthasya dharmaikāntasya kāmo lābhāya hi smṛtaḥ*: for the person dedicated to the higher path, attainment of material assets is not the desire.

> [!important]
> The lecturer explains that, as part of devotional service, one may get some material happiness as a side benefit. This is described under **śubhadā**, one of the aspects mentioned in the lecture as follows (as spoken, garbled): *śubhatvaṁ trīṇāṁ sarva-jagatām anuraktatā sad-guṇādi-pratattvam sukha-pratattvam*. Śubhadā means attaining these four aspects, one of which is **sukha-pratattvam** (happiness), and one type of this is material happiness. But this must not become the goal. The peripheral benefits cannot be the goal of the dharma of devotional service.

#### Meaning of the second verse
- For one who desires apavarga, sense pleasure from enjoying sense objects is not the goal **as long as one lives** (*jīveta yāvatā*). Such pleasures may come as side benefits of bhakti.
- **The goal of life is *jīvasya tattva-jijñāsā*:** inquiry into the highest truth.
- *Yaś ceha karmabhiḥ na arthaḥ*: what is accomplished by prescribed duties (varṇāśrama duties, or what is promised in the dharma-śāstra) is not the goal. One should not practise for that.

#### Meaning of "apavarga"
- According to Śrīmad Bhāgavatam, **apavarga means bhakti**. The lecturer cites the Fifth Canto, in the context of Jaḍa Bharata (as spoken, garbled): *apavargaś cāpi bhavati yo 'sau bhagavati sarva-bhūtātmani anātmye 'nirukte 'nilayane parātmani vāsudeve ananya-nimitta-bhakti-yoga-lakṣaṇo nānā-gati-nimitta-avidyā-granthi-randhana-dvāreṇa yadā hi mahā-puruṣa-puruṣa-prasaṅgaḥ*.
- The lecturer's rendering: liberation is then achieved, that liberation **whose essential nature is unmotivated bhakti-yoga** (*ananya-nimitta-bhakti-yoga-lakṣaṇa*), to the Lord who is full of qualities, who attracts the minds of all beings, who is not the object of merging, who is not described by material words, who remains beyond destruction of the universe, who is the most excellent ātmā, the son of Vasudeva. It takes place by **destruction of the knot of ignorance that causes various material goals, when there is association with devotees of the Lord**.
- Although *apavarga* can often refer to generic or impersonal liberation, it is not uncommon to use it to indicate bhakti.
- **Skanda Purāṇa** (as spoken): *niścalā tvayi bhaktir yā saiva muktir janārdana muktaye vāhi bhaktas te tava viṣṇo yato hare*. The lecturer's explanation: unswerving bhakti is itself called mukti. The word *mukti* is used to indicate *niścalā tvayi bhaktir yā*.
- Thus **apavarga means that which produces bhakti**.

> [!abstract]
> **Why this discussion arises.** The series of Anucchedas is explaining the true nature of pure devotional service. People may naturally ask "what do I get from this?" The answer is that the purpose of this kind of apavargya-dharma, like bhakti, should never be for material benefits if one aspires for apavarga. The lecturer says that here the ***anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam*** aspect of bhakti is being elaborated.

#### Tattva-jijñāsā and knowledge as a secondary result
- The goal is not svarga through performing karmas. It is inquiry about the truth only (*tattva-jijñāsā*).
- **Knowledge of this truth is a secondary result of bhakti.** The lecturer explains that this knowledge is the *highest* result of karma, because karma matures into jñāna, which eventually matures into liberation. But for bhakti, it is a secondary, **unsought** result.
- He cites again: *vāsudeve bhagavati bhakti-yogaḥ prayojitaḥ janayaty āśu vairāgyaṁ jñānaṁ ca yad ahaitukam*.
- The lecturer says Anucchedas 5 and 6 come from the two famous verses of the Second Chapter of the First Canto (*dharmasya hy apavargyasya…* and *kāmasya nendriya-prītiḥ…*).

> [!question]
> The lecturer asked for quick questions or comments on Anucchedas 5 and 6. There were none.

### 3.2 Anuccheda 7: What is tattva, and how is it realised?

> [!question]
> *Jīvasya tattva-jijñāsā*: the purpose of dharma should be tattva-jijñāsā. **What is that tattva, and how is it realised?**

#### What is tattva?
> *vadanti tat tattva-vidas tattvaṁ yaj jñānam advayam*
> *brahmeti paramātmeti bhagavān iti śabdyate*

- The knowers of truth (*tattva-vidaḥ*) call the truth **advayam jñānam**, indivisible knowledge.
- It is called **Brahman** by the jñānīs, **Paramātmā** by the yogīs and **Bhagavān** by the devotees.
- In pointing out that the highest truth is indivisible (*advaya*) with the three elements being in oneness, it is accepted that **the highest principle possesses śakti**.

| Aspect | Explanation as given in the lecture |
|---|---|
| **Brahman** | Pure knowledge (*kevala-jñānam*) **without manifestation of śakti**. The impersonal aspect of the absolute truth. |
| **Paramātmā** | A portion of the Lord **endowed with cit-śakti and māyā-śakti**, functioning as the **antaryāmī** within the material world. |
| **Bhagavān** | The form that **manifests all śaktis completely**. |

- This was described in the previous three Sandarbhas (the lecturer names Bhagavat, Paramātma and Kṛṣṇa Sandarbhas).
- This entity, with three types of manifestation, **is produced directly by bhakti**.
- This truth is the **shelter of the svarūpa-śakti, jīva-śakti and māyā-śakti**.

#### How is it realised?
- The seriously inquisitive student or sage, well equipped with knowledge and detachment, **realises the absolute truth within his heart by rendering devotional service** after hearing and assimilating from the guru.
- By bhakti one attains jñāna, and from bhakti they realise the absolute truth.
- By **bhakti in the form of prema**, which is the culmination of taste for the topics of the Lord, the devotee sees this truth in pure consciousness (*ātmani*), in the pure heart. They do not merely know it, but directly perceive or realise it.
- **Bhakti is served by jñāna and vairāgya** which arise from it. Bhakti is not dependent on jñāna and vairāgya. From the practice of bhakti these develop, and they serve the bhakti, but they are a result of the practice of bhakti only.

#### Two ways of realising the entity
The sages see and attain this entity in either of two ways:
1. **Without manifestation of its śaktis** (as Brahman). This may be realised by jñānīs who also approach a guru and learn the process through an element of bhakti. The lecturer's explanation of *bhaktyā śruta-gṛhītayā* here is that their practice should have **an element of bhakti** rather than being completely śuddha-bhakti.
2. **With manifestation of its śaktis.** This is realised by **pure devotees** who approach the guru and hear.

#### The words *śruta-gṛhītayā*, *munayaḥ*, *śraddhayā*
- The words *śruta-gṛhītayā*, *munayaḥ* and *śraddhadhānāḥ* show that bhakti is **hard to attain**. The lecturer clarifies: it is not hard to practise, but hard to attain.
  - There must be **śruta** (hearing).
  - There must be **gṛhīta**. *Munayaḥ* also means contemplation (**manana**).
  - **Śraddhā** is also a qualification. Without proper śraddhā the same result is not possible.
- By hearing the conclusions of all scriptures from an authorised guru, one attains it. Thus **hearing from the guru is understood to be the most necessary practice for the individual**.

> [!example]
> **Brahmā's example (as spoken):** *bhagavān brahma kārtsnyena trir anvīkṣya manīṣayā tad adhyavasyat kūṭastho ratir ātmann yato bhavet*. Brahmā reviewed the Vedas completely three times with his intelligence, and determined the process that produces **prema in the self**. The lecturer's reading: contemplation (**manana**) is indicated by this example, where Brahmā reviewed the full Vedas again and again, with great contemplation, and concluded that the highest dharma is that which gives rise to prema.

- Therefore: **for *śruta* you need a guru, for *gṛhīta* you need manana (contemplation).** The expression *śruta-gṛhītayā* indicates both the importance of a guru and the importance of contemplation.
- If there is qualification for contemplation and then absorption in contemplation, which makes one give up all conflicting thoughts, then those with faith can develop bhakti through worship.

> [!success]
> Anuccheda 7 teaches: tattva is the absolute truth in three phases; it is realised by bhakti, by surrendering to a guru, hearing from him, and contemplating on what is learnt from him.

### 3.3 Anuccheda 8: Bhakti alone is the real dharma

#### Recap leading into it
- Anucchedas on 1.2.11 and 1.2.12: *jīvasya tattva-jijñāsā*. The tattva is *brahmeti paramātmeti bhagavān iti śabdyate*. It is understood by *bhaktyā śruta-gṛhītayā*: by practising bhakti after hearing from the guru.
- The bhakti received through hearing from the guru is the real dharma.

#### The Śruti statement
- Śruti explains the same fact: the Lord should be **seen, heard, thought of and worshipped**.
  - The previous Anuccheda's word *gṛhītayā* is the same as **manana** (contemplation).
  - **Nididhyāsana** means worship.
  - **Seeing** means meeting directly.
- That rare bhakti is attained even from performing one's dharma if dharma is endowed with pleasing the Lord, and pleasing the Lord is the highest result of dharma.
- Verse as spoken: *ataḥ pumbhir dvija-śreṣṭhā varṇāśrama-vibhāgaśaḥ svanuṣṭhitasya dharmasya saṁsiddhir hari-toṣaṇam*. The complete perfection of dharma according to the divisions of varṇāśrama is pleasing the Lord. The goal of dharma undertaken faultlessly with great effort (*su-anuṣṭhitasya*) is ultimately to please the Lord. **Using dharma to gain svarga is not suitable.**

#### The key statement and its reading

> [!quote]
> "If the result of dharma consisting of pleasing the Lord is bhakti with taste for hearing, which brings about jñāna and vairāgya which follow after the practice of bhakti, then it should be concluded that bhakti alone consisting of hearing must be performed."

The lecturer asked devotees to share their understanding. The responses were:
- **Jyoti Mātājī:** if one practises bhakti, knowledge and renunciation happen automatically. Pure bhakti is attained by hearing from the guru. She recalls reading (source forgotten) that jñāna can arise in an illiterate person doing pure bhakti, and mentions **Gaura Kiśora Bābājī** who, though illiterate, had complete knowledge because he heard from his guru.
- **Keśava Priya:** anything done to please the Lord yields bhakti; when we practise bhakti beginning from hearing, jñāna and vairāgya develop. So bhakti alone is enough for attaining anything, and those other things need not be practised in order to get bhakti.
- **Gaurāṅga Prabhu:** this might refer to varṇāśrama-dharma done to please the Lord. When we perform bhakti with taste for hearing, jñāna and vairāgya follow, so varṇāśrama-dharma need not be given priority; one should practise only **uttama-bhakti**.
- **Pracaṇḍī Mātājī** (read from chat): there is no separate endeavour needed for the results of dharma if one is practising bhakti with taste for hearing.

#### The lecturer's explanation
- The statement asks what should be understood as the purpose of dharma. **If the actual result of dharma is to attain bhakti with taste for hearing, then it should be concluded that bhakti alone, consisting of hearing, must be performed.** This is because it has already been proved that **varṇāśrama-dharma cannot give rise to taste for hearing**.
- *Only bhakti can give rise to bhakti* (*bhaktyā sañjātayā bhaktyā*).
- So if the purpose of dharma is to give taste for bhakti, that dharma has to be **the limbs of bhakti only and not any other limbs**.

> [!success]
> This is the eighth Anuccheda: **bhakti alone is the real dharma**, as illuminated by this last line.

## 5. Key Takeaways

1. The root cause of the jīva's suffering is Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā. Only bhakti makes the jīva Kṛṣṇa-unmukha.
2. Pure bhakti must be **ahaituki** (free of any goal other than a relationship with Kṛṣṇa) and **apratihatā** (not covered by karma and jñāna). Varṇāśrama-dharma that does not produce attraction for the Lord's topics is wasted effort.
3. Śraddhā is itself bhakti; svalpa-ruci/svalpa-śraddhā for the bhakti-śāstras matures gradually into bhajana-kriyā, and the time this takes varies.
4. Bhakti is independent. Jñāna and vairāgya are dependent on bhakti and arise from it as secondary, unsought results.
5. **Sukṛti (ajñāta-sukṛti)** that matures into pure devotional faith comes through the intervention of devotees or things connected to them. A devotee distributes only the *opportunity* to accumulate sukṛti.
6. Genuine appreciation of **peripheral aspects** of Kṛṣṇa-connected objects (prasādam, the book's printing and binding, language, a garland's colours, the host's kindness), **without a transactional mentality**, can give ajñāta-sukṛti. Doing the same thing with a goal (material benefit or liberation) will not.
7. Sukṛti gives at least feeble faith. Solid faith depends on practice, association, previous-life practice and sincerity.
8. After śraddhā, the guru and the live association of devotees are indispensable. The present discussion concerns the stage before śraddhā.
9. It is never a loss to distribute prasādam, even to mixed devotees.
10. The goal of dharma is **tattva-jijñāsā**, not artha, kāma or svarga. Material benefits may come as side effects but must not be the goal.
11. **Apavarga** here means bhakti.
12. Tattva is **advayam jñānam**, realised as Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān. It is realised by bhakti, after hearing from the guru (*śruta*) and contemplating (*gṛhīta*).
13. **Bhakti alone is the real dharma.** Only bhakti can give rise to bhakti.

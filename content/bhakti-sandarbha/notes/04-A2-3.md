# Bhakti Sandarbha – Anuccheda 2 and 3: Proof That Bhakti Is the Abhidheya, and the Nature of Pure Bhakti

## 1. Recap of Previous Lecture

The previous session completed the **first Anuccheda of Bhakti Sandarbha**. The lecturer recapped it as follows.

### The Jīva's ignorance and its category

- In the earlier Sandarbhas, while describing Paramātmā, the Jīva-tattva was also discussed (in the Paramātmā Sandarbha).
- The Jīva's essential knowledge is covered by Māyā, owing to the misfortune of being opposed to the Lord. The Jīva is therefore without knowledge of the beginningless Supreme Entity.
- Knowledge of Kṛṣṇa in the conditioned soul is of the category of **Prāg-abhāva** (previous non-existence): it never existed before. Consequently the Jīva believes he is made of dull matter composed of sattva, rajas and tamas.
- Prāg-abhāva means a previous non-existence. After the Jīva comes in touch with a Vaiṣṇava, receives the seed of devotion and that seed fructifies (or through strong Kṛpā-bala he comes in touch with that knowledge), the knowledge remains eternal.
- The Jīva's lack of knowledge is only Prāg-abhāva, among the forms of saṁsarga-abhāva (not having previously existed in the Jīva). This does not exclude obtaining knowledge of the Lord in the future. Once attained, it becomes the eternal possession of the Jīva; until then it did not exist.

> [!important]
> The Jīva's lack of Kṛṣṇa-knowledge is **Prāg-abhāva**: it did not exist before, but once attained it becomes eternal.

### Scripture, śraddhā and sukṛti

- For the benefit of the Jīvas, the Lord teaches scripture filled with the highest mercy.
- Some Jīvas, either by accumulating impressions (**ajñāta-sukṛti**) over many births or by obtaining a great dose of mercy, become favourable to the Lord. They gain understanding of the Lord the very instant they hear teachings about the Supreme Lord.
- Otherwise, if proper śraddhā has not arisen through assimilation of ajñāta-sukṛti, it is not possible for such persons to benefit even though scriptures exist. The lecturer cited the idea (the quotation was garbled in the transcript, see Section 6) that Kṛṣṇa showed mercy to the Jīva by giving the Veda and Purāṇa because the Jīva, covered by Māyā, has no knowledge of Kṛṣṇa by himself. But the Veda and Purāṇa will not affect a Jīva who lacks either kṛpā-bala or sukṛti-bala through which some faith develops.
- Once sukṛti and śraddhā come, such persons give no regard to other teachings. Even if they happen to hear other teachings, those teachings act as **uddīpana** (stimuli) for their own rasa.
- **Example:** Prahlāda Mahārāja. When he heard teachings on materialism from his teachers, he preached the glories of Viṣṇu.
- Sometimes, out of humility, such persons say they have no knowledge and are covered by illusion, but this is only humility.
- The Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa is cited: as long as the heart is contaminated with sins, the intelligence will not accept scripture as true and will not accept the Guru as a spiritual teacher. This is not possible. The result of accumulated pious acts over many births is great. This accumulation is called accumulation of **adhyātma-sukṛti**, which leads to **śraddhā**.
- However, **prema arises only from hearing the scriptures in the association of devotees**.

### Abhidheya and Prayojana

- Even though the final conclusion is the existence of the Supreme Lord, the method of attainment (**Abhidheya**) and the final goal (**Prayojana**) must be taught respectively. The Sambandha-jñāna is already there.
- Abhidheya is actions producing favourableness to the Lord, since that is the opposite of the Jīva's aversion to the Lord. Because the Jīva has Prāg-abhāva of Kṛṣṇa-knowledge, he should perform actions that create favourableness towards Kṛṣṇa. These actions are Abhidheya. This means worship of the Lord, from which knowledge of the Lord arises.
- The goal (Prayojana) is realization of the Lord. Realization means internal and external perception of the Lord, from which all suffering is destroyed.

> [!example]
> These two topics were already indicated in the earlier teachings. It is like hearing there is a treasure in a house: a poor man immediately attempts to find it. But to prevent negligence in attainment, the teachings are given again, specifically.

- The earlier Sandarbhas (Tattva, Bhagavat, Paramātmā, Kṛṣṇa) are completed, but it is **not** the case that the reader still does not know Abhidheya and Prayojana. The reader definitely knows that śuddha-bhakti is the Abhidheya and prema is the Prayojana. They are given more specifically, describing the means and the goal, to emphasise them and prevent negligence. The Bhakti Sandarbha and Prīti Sandarbha are meant for this.

### The cause of suffering and its cure

- The Jīva's cause of suffering is aversion to the Lord, with beginningless ignorance of his eternal existence. This is jñāna-saṁsarga-abhāva, of the type Prāg-abhāva.
- The scriptures teach favourability to the Lord, which is like a cure for the cause of the illness.
- Verse recalled (garbled in transcript, see Section 6): the *bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ…* verse, ending with *bhaktyaikayeśaṁ guru-devatātmā*. The lecturer's explanation:
  - By aversion to the Lord, **asmṛti** (non-appearance of the Jīva's svarūpa) arises, and the opposite awareness "I am the body" arises. When Māyā sees the Jīva is averse to the Lord, she makes him forget his svarūpa and identify the body as the self.
  - From this arises **dvitīyābhiniveśa**, absorption in the body, which leads to **bhaya**, fear of saṁsāra.
  - The solution: accepting the Guru as one's life and soul, taking teachings from him, and through those teachings worshipping the Supreme Personality of Godhead with unalloyed devotion (**ekayā bhaktyā**). That is the means of attaining perfection.

> [!success]
> **Essence of the first Anuccheda:** the root cause of the Jīva's suffering is his **Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā**, and he can overcome it by becoming **Kṛṣṇa-unmukha** through the practice of bhakti.

## 2. Opening Q&A

- The basic knowledge that one is not the body but the soul, and similar knowledge, is eternal.

## 3. Main Lecture Notes

### 3.1 Anuccheda 2: Proof that Bhakti removes the cause of Saṁsāra

**Theme:** the proof of how, by bhakti, one actually overcomes saṁsāra.

#### The verse

The verse is from the **Second Canto**, spoken by **Śukadeva Gosvāmī to Parīkṣit Mahārāja**. The lecturer's word-by-word and overall meaning:

- *Ātmā*: the Lord, situated thus (in the heart).
- *Priyaḥ*: filled with prema / most dear.
- *Arthaḥ*: most valued (also glossed as *tangible* and *real*).
- *Bhagavān*: full of all qualities.
- *Anantaḥ*: everywhere.
- *Svataḥ … siddhaḥ*: appears spontaneously in his heart (*sva-citte*).
- *Nirvṛtaḥ*: the yogī filled with bliss.
- *Niyatārthaḥ*: aiming at prema.
- *Sambhajeta*: he should worship him.
- *Yatra … saṁsāra-hetūparamaḥ*: by this worship, the efflorescence (as transcribed) that is the cause of repeated births is destroyed.

> [!quote]
> Thus being situated, the Lord, who is filled with prema, who is most valued, who is full of all qualities and who is everywhere, appears spontaneously in the heart. The yogī, filled with bliss and aiming for prema, should worship him. By this worship the cause of saṁsāra is destroyed.
> (Lecturer's rendering of the verse)

#### Why this verse is quoted

- It is quoted especially for its last line: to prove that by the practice of bhakti the **saṁsāra-hetu**, which is **Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā**, is destroyed.
- The earlier verse (*bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ…*) said that only under the guidance of a Guru, by practising ekayā bhakti (unalloyed devotional service), can one overcome this situation. The present verse gives the **proof** for that.
- *Saṁsāra-hetūparamaś ca yatra*: by this worship, the root cause of saṁsāra, the Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukha nature of the soul, is uprooted (*uparamaḥ*) and destroyed.

#### Definition of Abhidheya

- **Abhidheya** is that which addresses the root of the problem: those activities which create favourableness towards the Supreme Personality of Godhead. The root cause of saṁsāra is Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā, so the Abhidheya must be what produces favourableness to Kṛṣṇa.
- The activities that do so are **bhakti**, and the proof is this second-canto verse. Therefore **bhakti is the Abhidheya**.

#### The commentary on the verse

- Question: what should the yogī do? He should serve the Lord. Becoming detached, he should worship the Lord.
- The reasons for worshipping the Lord:
  1. The Lord is present independently in the heart since He is **Ātmā**.
  2. He is **Priya**: a form of happiness that can be experienced by service to Him.
  3. He is **Artha**: tangible and real; He actually exists, not false like objects that are not Ātmā.
  4. He is **Bhagavān**: full of all qualities and eternal.
- Having determined His form, the person becomes joyful with the bliss of realizing the Lord. The verse indicates that the Lord is joyful by His very nature.
- When one worships the Lord, destruction of ignorance (the cause of saṁsāra) takes place. The lecturer said the word "acha" (as transcribed) indicates that **besides destruction of ignorance, one also achieves the Lord**.

#### Sādhana versus Abhidheya

> [!important]
> - **Sādhana** is the means to attain *an* end; any such means is called sādhana. There can be many sādhanas: karma, jñāna, bhakti.
> - **Abhidheya** is the means to attain the *highest* end. There is only **one** Abhidheya.
> - Bhakti is also a sādhana, since it is a means to attain the end of prema. But **bhakti alone is the Abhidheya**, because it is the only means to attain the highest end of prema.

- Jñāna and karma are therefore not Abhidheya.

### 3.2 Anuccheda 3: The Nature of Pure Bhakti

The lecturer said that, having established that Abhidheya is bhakti alone (activities leading the Jīva to develop favourableness towards Kṛṣṇa, proved from the second canto), the next step is to understand the **nature of pure bhakti** from verses of the **First Canto**.

#### The Pūrvapakṣa (objection)

Jīva Gosvāmī writes (as read by the lecturer):

> [!quote]
> Even though hearing and contemplation are methods of jñāna, they also produce favourableness to the Lord. Because they produce realization of the form of Brahman and are thus suitable for gradual development of realization of the Lord, even Sāṅkhya, Aṣṭāṅga-yoga and Karma are classed as being favourable. To some degree they produce bhakti.
> Thus karma is a cause of bhakti because it is a form of following the Lord's order and involves offering to the Lord, and jñāna is an attendant to bhakti by causing detachment from material objects. However, the previous verse quoted stated that one should worship the Lord with bhakti. Karma and jñāna were not given any regard there. One should worship by direct bhakti consisting of hearing and chanting about the Lord. The reasons are shown in the introductory part of the teachings of Sūta Gosvāmī.

The lecturer's explanation of this passage:

- This is the **Pūrvapakṣa**. Someone may say that hearing and contemplation are limbs of jñāna, so they too produce favourableness to the Lord, because they produce realization of the form of Brahman and are suitable for gradual development of realization of the Lord. Therefore even Sāṅkhya, Aṣṭāṅga-yoga etc. should be considered favourable, and one should accept that to some extent they produce bhakti. "Favourable" here means somewhat favourable, since they contribute to the gradual elevation of the Jīva.
- Yet the previous verse (*saṁsāra-hetūparamaś ca yatra… bhajeta*) mentioned only bhakti. Karma and jñāna were not mentioned and were given no regard; only worship by direct bhakti consisting of hearing and chanting was mentioned.

> [!question]
> Why were karma and jñāna not mentioned there? Why was only exclusive bhakti mentioned?

The reason is given by Sūta Gosvāmī in the First Canto.

#### Sūta Gosvāmī's verses

**1. Verse (1.2.22 per the lecturer):** *atho vai kavayo nityaṁ bhaktiṁ paramayā mudā / vāsudeve bhagavati kurvanty ātma-prasādanīm*

- *Ataḥ*: therefore. *Vai*: certainly. *Kavayaḥ*: the wise people.
- The wise constantly (*nityam*) and with great joy (*paramayā mudā*) perform bhakti to the Supreme Lord Vāsudeva.
- Result: *ātma-prasādanīm*, it gives great joy to the mind.

**2. Verse:** *sa vai puṁsāṁ paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhokṣaje / ahaituky apratihatā yayātmā suprasīdati*

- *Puṁsāṁ paro dharmaḥ*: the supreme dharma for all human beings is that by which **prema-bhakti** to the Supreme Lord arises. The lecturer also called this **sādhana-bhakti**, the practice by which prema-bhakti arises.
- **Ahaitukī**: not caused by anything other than itself.
- **Apratihatā**: cannot be obstructed; not covered by jñāna, karma, yoga etc.
- *Yayātmā suprasīdati*: by this practice the mind becomes completely satisfied. The lecturer noted that the earlier verse also speaks of ātma-prasādanī, joy to the mind, so both verses say the mind is satisfied.

**Context of this verse:** at the beginning of the great Purāṇa, Śravaṇakādi Ṛṣis (as transcribed) asked Sūta Gosvāmī to declare the **final essence of all scriptures**. In answer, Sūta spoke this verse. The lecturer's reading of the answer: the final essence is that dharma by which prema-bhakti to the Lord arises, which is ahaitukī, apratihatā, and completely satisfies the mind.

> [!important]
> From this dharma, **taste for hearing the topics of the Lord** arises. By following the process of bhakti one develops taste for hearing about the Supreme Lord. The lecturer said this is later shown (in verse 8) by a negative statement.

**3. Verse 1.2.8:** *dharmaḥ svanuṣṭhitaḥ puṁsāṁ viṣvaksena-kathāsu yaḥ / notpādayed yadi ratiṁ śrama eva hi kevalam*

- *Svanuṣṭhita-dharma* means perfectly followed **varṇāśrama-dharma** of the human being.
- If it does not produce *rati*, attraction for the topics of the Lord (*viṣvaksena-kathā*), it is **only wasted effort** (*śrama eva kevalam*).

##### Qualification: in what sense is varṇāśrama "wasted effort"?

> [!warning]
> "Wasted effort" refers specifically to **attaining the Prayojana of prema**. It is not that varṇāśrama is absolutely useless.

- How could the Supreme Lord recommend a process that is absolutely wasted effort? It is definitely not wasted effort for ordinary people of this world: it helps them gradually elevate themselves to higher and higher status.
- But for those aspiring for prema, depending on varṇāśrama is wasted effort, because varṇāśrama-dharma does not produce attraction for the topics of the Lord.
- The true dharma should develop, at least, a taste for hearing the topics of the Lord. Varṇāśrama does not do that. The dharma of bhakti is undertaken to please the Lord.

##### Other limbs need bhakti

The lecturer's reading (the sentence "It is the best of all since it is not only concerned with renouncing, it does not distinguish whether one is [adhikārī or not]" was garbled, see Section 6) is that even someone without much detachment can still practise bhakti.

**4. Nārada's verse (as quoted):** *naiṣkarmyam apy acyuta-bhāva-varjitaṁ na śobhate jñānam alaṁ nirañjanam / kutaḥ punaḥ śaśvad abhadram īśvare na cārpitaṁ karma yad apy akāraṇam*

The lecturer's explanation:

- Even the state of **jñāna without the bondage of karma** is not glorious if it is devoid of bhakti to the Supreme Lord.
- What is the use of having destroyed ignorance?
- What then to speak of **sakāma-karma**, which causes suffering both during practice and at the stage of perfection?
- And what to speak of **niṣkāma-karma** when not offered to the Lord?
- The lecturer then quoted (garbled in transcript) the verse beginning *tapasvino dāna-parāyaṇā yaśasvino manasvino mantra-vidaḥ sumaṅgalam* and ending *tasmai subhadra-śravase namo namaḥ*, and concluded: all these practices (karma, jñāna, yoga etc.), **without the element of bhakti, will not give any result**.

**5. Verse beginning *ataḥ pumbhir dvija-śreṣṭhā varṇāśrama-vibhāgaśaḥ / svanuṣṭhitasya dharmasya saṁsiddhir hari-toṣaṇam*:**

- The complete perfection of dharma, according to the divisions of varṇāśrama followed by men, is **pleasing the Lord**.
- The lecturer's comment: the complete perfection of dharma is when someone takes up pure bhakti.
- He noted that the discussion was still in the **third Anuccheda**.

#### Svarūpa-qualities of bhakti

- *Saḥ* means "this alone is the best." Bhakti is **distinct** from the dharma described in the text, and the svarūpa qualities of bhakti are described.
- **Ahaitukī:** it is costless and devoid of other goals or motives, since it is by its nature the very form of happiness sought by the other methods.
- **Apratihatā:** it is impossible to obstruct this bhakti, since no other object exists which gives higher bliss. It is not covered by jñāna, karma, dhyāna etc.
- When a taste for bhakti develops through that bhakti, **bhakti-yoga characterised by hearing and chanting begins**.

> [!note]
> **Progression described by the lecturer:** taste for bhakti is called **śraddhā** → leads to **sādhu-saṅga** → leads to **bhajaneṣu spṛhā** (desire to practise bhajana) → leads to **bhajana-kriyā** (actual practice of bhajana).

#### Lecturer's summary of Anuccheda 3

1. Abhidheya = actions leading the Jīva to develop favourableness to Kṛṣṇa. This is only bhakti, and the proof was given in the second Anuccheda: by this worship the saṁsāra-hetu, Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā, is destroyed.
2. The third Anuccheda explains the **nature** of bhakti and why it is so glorified.
3. Objection: jñāna and karma also lead to gradual development of the Jīva, so they should be accepted at least as assistants to bhakti. Answer: the previous verse mentioned only bhakti, and the reason is given by Sūta Gosvāmī in the second chapter of the First Canto ("Divinity and Divine Service").
4. The wise constantly and joyfully perform *only exclusive bhakti*, because it gives joy to the mind. **Ahaitukī** (no other goal) and **apratihatā** (not covered by jñāna, karma, yoga etc.); such bhakti completely satisfies the mind.
5. Sūta spoke the verse because Śravaṇakādi Ṛṣis asked for the final essence of all scriptures. From the dharma of bhakti one develops taste for hearing and chanting about the topics of Kṛṣṇa. The popularly known dharma, varṇāśrama-dharma, does not produce that taste, and is therefore wasted effort for attaining prema, though not wasted in itself.
6. The other limbs (karma, jñāna, yoga) need the support of bhakti, otherwise they give no result.
7. The essential qualities of bhakti are **ahaitukī** and **apratihatā**. This is the true dharma, producing taste for hearing Hari-kathā, whereas ordinary dharma or varṇāśrama does not.

## 4. Closing Q&A

Questions and discussion followed the Anuccheda 3 summary, in several threads.

### 4.1 Does varṇāśrama lead a person towards bhakti? (Vasundharā Mahal)

> [!question]
> Varṇāśrama is recommended (by Kṛṣṇa, in the law books) to regulate human life so that one gradually understands the purpose of life and chooses an appropriate yoga system. If so, how can verses call it futile? Surely a regulated life could gradually lead a person to some yoga system, and possibly bhakti-yoga?

- Lecturer: it leads him to karma-yoga, jñāna-yoga, aṣṭāṅga-yoga and **impersonal liberation**.
- Does it not lead to bhakti-yoga at all? As a **secondary practice** there will be some element of bhakti, **not as the primary practice**.
- Does a Jīvātmā following varṇāśrama never get the opportunity to take up bhakti-yoga? **Not by being in varṇāśrama.** Only if he comes in touch with a devotee may he take it up. By merely practising varṇāśrama, one will not automatically take up bhakti.
- What determines whether Vaiṣṇavas connect with such persons? It depends on whether, **by chance**, the Vaiṣṇavas happen to be around and come in touch with them.

> [!question]
> Do all Jīvas have the same potency to receive the seed of devotion? Do they pick a yoga system based on the association of the seekers they meet? Is it arranged by Kṛṣṇa?

- Lecturer: yes, all Jīvas have the same potency to receive the seed of devotion, and the choice of path depends on association.
- It is **not** designed or arranged by Kṛṣṇa; there is no such involvement by Kṛṣṇa in Jīvas picking different yoga paths.
- On how to understand destiny, given that Jīvas did not create their bahirmukha state: there is **no cause** for being averse to Kṛṣṇa, but having been bahirmukha, the actions one performs yield results.
- When asked whether access to a given yoga system or to devotional service comes by chance: yes.

> [!question]
> Does this not create a distinction among Jīvas by the Creator, since I was fortunate to be given bhakti while others take up jñāna, karma or aṣṭāṅga-yoga? Does it not violate equal treatment of Jīvas?

- Lecturer: others may get it a little later. There is **no distinction**, because **the Lord did not choose who will get it and who will not**. In response to the questioner's statement that she did not choose either: "You didn't choose. The Lord did not choose. You didn't choose. Whoever came, they chose." (see Section 6).
- He asked how Jīvas are getting different treatment. Asked why only some Jīvas get associated with Vaiṣṇavas, he said this had been discussed in detail in **Jaiva Dharma**, and went on to give the following explanation.

> [!important]
> **No vaiṣamya from the Supreme Personality of Godhead.** Every Jīva has the chance to take up bhakti.
> - It is not that the Lord hopes some Jīvas never come in touch with devotees, or that some should only get jñāna and never meet devotional service.
> - The Lord has invested His **bhakti-śakti in the devotees** and is "rooting for them" to distribute it to as many people as possible. Sometimes He Himself comes as **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** to kick-start and expedite that distribution.
> - He wants the distribution to happen through those who have themselves tasted it as recipient Jīvas.

> [!example]
> **Shopkeeper analogy:** a shopkeeper saying "this is the best" may carry prejudice, since he wants to sell. When a person who has purchased and enjoyed the object says "I have tasted it, this is extraordinary," it is impactful. So Kṛṣṇa invests the power of bhakti in the Vaiṣṇavas, who have received it, so they reach out to as many conditioned souls as possible.

- Even a practitioner of karma, jñāna or aṣṭāṅga-yoga, or a materialist, can become a bhakti-yogī. One just has to be a human being; the human being's purpose is to practise bhakti.
- Even other species can **accumulate ajñāta-sukṛti**. Kṛṣṇa created a system by which non-human species can assimilate it: feeding a dog prasādam; being kind to certain animals; offering water that has been offered to the Lord to plants; all living entities around get sukṛti from the chanting of the holy names.
- Everyone can potentially become a devotee, but Kṛṣṇa Himself does not want to canvass; He wants the devotees to do it, and they are doing so. No one is bereft of the opportunity.
- Because Vaiṣṇavas are not omnipresent, omniscient and omnipotent, they reach different people according to their own ability. Whomever they reach, bhakti flows to them from Kṛṣṇa without discrimination.

> [!question]
> Does this mean every Jīvātmā, whichever path he starts on, ultimately takes up bhakti and is perfected, and that even one who attained perfection on a non-bhakti path can still take up bhakti?

- Lecturer: yes, anyone. **Examples:**
  - **Sūta Gosvāmī and the Śravaṇakādi Ṛṣis** (as stated): the Ṛṣis were practitioners of karma, and by association with Sūta Gosvāmī gave up karma and took up bhakti.
  - **The Nava-yogīndras** were yogīs; they gave up yoga and took up bhakti by association with Ṛṣabhadeva.
  - **The four Kumāras** were jñānīs; they gave up the path of jñāna and took up bhakti by association with Jaya-Vijaya and the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- When they come in touch with devotees, and the association is powerful, they give up past practices and take up devotional service.

> [!question]
> For Jīvas who attained the Brahman aspect through non-bhakti yoga, the Jīva still maintains his individual identity, so can he still take up bhakti?

- Lecturer: it is **not impossible, but the chances are very less**. (The questioner mentioned hearing that Lord Śiva visits the Brahman space and offers something; the lecturer agreed it is possible but very rare.)

> [!question]
> Why are some people who associate with Vaiṣṇavas, and hear the bhakti process and the Lord's features and qualities, still inclined to the Brahman aspect and to merging, rather than the personal feature of the Lord? What is lacking?

- Lecturer: **sukṛti**. That is why they are not yet interested. Endeavour on the path of jñāna will not lead to sukṛti. But if by chance they come in touch with devotees and develop a favourable attitude or mood towards the devotees, gradually over a period of time they can get that sukṛti and may become Kṛṣṇa-unmukha.

### 4.2 Is there a bhakti element in varṇāśrama that gives sukṛti? (Speaker 4)

> [!question]
> If varṇāśrama contains some bhakti element (as a minor part), will that bhakti, practised by brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras according to their duties, give them sukṛti?

- Lecturer: **it will not give sukṛti.** They first consider it a limb of karma and practise it to get material benefits or impersonal liberation. That bhakti is therefore spent in giving them those material benefits, so it does not lead to sukṛti.
- On Bhagavad-gītā chapter 3 (everything done for the satisfaction of Viṣṇu, varṇāśrama too): "that is different." By satisfying Viṣṇu one wants either material benefits or liberation and gets that. The questioner summarised that the part of **love** is missing: they see the Lord only as a means to attain what they want. The lecturer agreed.
- However deeply one follows varṇāśrama with perfection, even if one follows bhakti within varṇāśrama, it will not lead to sukṛti. The questioner concluded that sukṛti is given purely by Vaiṣṇavas, and the lecturer agreed.

> [!question]
> Even when Vaiṣṇavas reach out, not everyone takes up bhakti. Do they at least collect sukṛti?

- Lecturer: Vaiṣṇavas distribute **an opportunity to accumulate some sukṛti**, that's all. When that sukṛti accumulates, it matures into **faith**, which is when bhakti starts.

### 4.3 Do other Jīvātmās feel themselves equally fortunate?

- A comment was raised that other Jīvātmās feel equally fortunate to have taken the path of jñāna or karma. The lecturer agreed: we think they are unfortunate; they do not feel unfortunate; they feel *we* are unfortunate to be stuck on the path of bhakti. Everyone feels fortunate.

> [!important]
> **The moment you feel someone else is fortunate, you belong to their party.**
> - If a karma-adhikārī or varṇāśrama practitioner thinks the devotees are more fortunate, it means he is **already a devotee**. His bhajana-kriyā has not started, but his **śraddhā is already in**. He is a bhakti-adhikārī already.
> - If he thinks he is more fortunate than the devotees, he falls into the category of his own practice.
> - Every practitioner of karma, jñāna, yoga thinks he is most fortunate; every practitioner of bhakti also thinks so.

- To the remark that no one chooses, and that Bhagavān does not decide who gets the title: the lecturer said "that's true." To the remark that it also depends on what level of bhakta they come in association with: "that is true." The sentence that followed ("if they come in touch with a more advanced…") was cut off by an audio interruption (see Section 6).

### 4.4 Is demigod worship part of varṇāśrama?

- Lecturer: in the strictest sense, **demigod worship is not part of varṇāśrama**. Varṇāśrama means worship of Viṣṇu. If there is no worship of Viṣṇu, it is not strictly varṇāśrama.
- The four varṇas and four āśramas are limbs of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Just as limbs serve the whole body, persons of varṇāśrama are supposed to worship Lord Viṣṇu. If they do not, they fall down from their position and get out of varṇāśrama. So demigod worshippers cannot, in the strictest sense, be considered varṇāśrama practitioners.

### 4.5 Varṇāśrama in the Rāmānuja and Madhva sampradāyas (Speaker 3)

> [!question]
> In the Rāmānuja and Madhva sampradāyas there is much emphasis on varṇāśrama, and they are elevated to Vaikuṇṭha. Does this mean varṇāśrama takes them to Vaikuṇṭha?

- Lecturer: **no.** That is not varṇāśrama alone; there is very strong bhakti there, with some attachment to varṇāśrama. Their teachings are essentially bhakti with some attachment to karma, and that is why they attain Vaikuṇṭha. For common people it may be mixed with karma or appear as varṇāśrama.
- To the point that they follow the rules strictly, so it is **pradhānī-bhūta-bhakti**: they may follow, and how closely is another matter, but the element of devotion is definitely strongly present in their teachings.

> [!question]
> Would followers of Rāmānuja interpret a verse (such as the *anyatra* karma verse) differently from a non-devotee following niṣkāma-karma?

- Lecturer: it depends, and it is quite possible. In our own sampradāya, Śrīla Prabhupāda speaks of bhakti only in the initial six chapters (of the Gītā), whereas other ācāryas explain that verse as indicating **karma-yoga**, and the seventh and eighth chapters as indicating pradhānī-bhūta-bhakti. They may or may not give different explanations; sometimes the same interpretation is given, but we call it karma-yoga and explain it that way, and others explain it as karma-yoga as their own practice too.
- For them, varṇāśrama is the best way of pleasing Kṛṣṇa; for us it is an **inferior** way of pleasing Kṛṣṇa.
- **Example:** a verse often quoted says there is no other way of pleasing Kṛṣṇa than by performing varṇāśrama duties. **Jīva Gosvāmī's explanation** is that this verse is an instruction for those who have **karma-niṣṭhā**, not for those who have **bhakti-niṣṭhā**.
- Depending on the path proposed, the Śrī Vaiṣṇavas may give a slightly different explanation, saying that performing nitya-naimittika karmas is very much part of pleasing the Supreme Lord and should never be given up.

> [!question]
> So when they do varṇāśrama, it is basically pradhānī-bhūta-bhakti mixed with karma or jñāna?

- Lecturer: yes, **or** it can also be that varṇāśrama is followed with **absolutely no material desires**: no abhilāṣa including sukha and aiśvarya, only the desire to please the Lord, practising those limbs simply because their ācāryas recommended them. In such cases it can be **pure devotional service (śuddha-bhakti)**. But because of the influence of varṇāśrama, that bhakti is heavily flavoured with **aiśvarya** and leads to a **predominance of reverence** in worship. It leads to **Vaikuṇṭha**, but **not to Goloka Vṛndāvana**.
- He clarified that it is **not** niṣkāma-karma-yoga.
- To the questioner's observation that they go directly to Vaikuṇṭha without passing through jñāna and other levels, the lecturer agreed ("yeah").
- On ISKCON devotees who speak of varṇāśrama and aim for Goloka: the lecturer said he did not know, and that "we will have to understand what exactly they are speaking." The questioner noted that these devotees also propagate varṇāśrama as favourable, like the Madhva and Rāmānuja ācāryas. The lecturer said "we will discuss" (the discussion was left for later).

## 5. Key Takeaways

- The root cause of the Jīva's suffering is **Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā**; it is overcome by becoming **Kṛṣṇa-unmukha** through bhakti.
- The Jīva's lack of Kṛṣṇa-knowledge is **Prāg-abhāva**; once gained, it is eternal.
- **Abhidheya** = activities that produce favourableness to Kṛṣṇa, i.e. **bhakti alone**. **Sādhana** is any means to any end; there are many sādhanas but only one Abhidheya, because bhakti alone attains the highest end, prema.
- Proof (Second Canto): *saṁsāra-hetūparamaś ca yatra*, by worship the cause of saṁsāra is destroyed and the Lord is also attained.
- Reasons to worship the Lord: He is Ātmā, Priya, Artha (real), Bhagavān.
- Pure bhakti is **ahaitukī** (not caused by anything else, devoid of other goals) and **apratihatā** (cannot be obstructed or covered by jñāna, karma, yoga; no higher bliss exists).
- Bhakti is the supreme dharma and produces **taste for hearing the topics of the Lord**; varṇāśrama-dharma that does not do so is wasted effort *for attaining prema* (though not wasted for ordinary worldly elevation).
- Karma, jñāna and yoga without bhakti give no result; they need the support of bhakti.
- Progression: **śraddhā → sādhu-saṅga → bhajaneṣu spṛhā → bhajana-kriyā**.
- Prema arises only from hearing scriptures in the association of devotees; sukṛti comes only from Vaiṣṇavas and matures into faith.
- No **vaiṣamya** on the Lord's part: bhakti-śakti is invested in devotees, who distribute it; any Jīva, even a non-human species, can accumulate sukṛti; anyone who comes in touch with devotees can take up bhakti.
- Bhakti elements within varṇāśrama do not generate sukṛti, because the Lord is approached for material benefit or liberation.
- Śuddha-bhakti mixed with varṇāśrama leads to Vaikuṇṭha (reverence), not Goloka Vṛndāvana.

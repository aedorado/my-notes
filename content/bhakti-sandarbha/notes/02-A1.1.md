# Bhakti-sandarbha, Session 2: Recap of the Ṣaṭ-sandarbha and Anuccheda 1 (Part 1), The Jīva's Beginningless Lack of Knowledge of the Lord

## 1. Overview

**Subject.** This session opens with a recap of the previous (shortened) introductory session on Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī's [[Ṣaṭ-sandarbha]]. It then begins the **Bhakti-sandarbha**, the fifth Sandarbha, with the **first Anuccheda, of which only the first part is covered**. The remainder of the Anuccheda is left for the next session.

**Theme of the Anuccheda.** The lecturer's own heading, not Jīva Gosvāmī's, is the "Jīva fall-down issue". The theme runs as follows:
- The root cause of the jīva's suffering is *kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā* (turning away from Kṛṣṇa).
- The remedy is to become *kṛṣṇa-unmukha* (turned towards Kṛṣṇa) through the practice of bhakti.

**Central argument.**
1. The jīva's essential knowledge is covered by Māyā because of being opposed to the Lord (*bahirmukhatā*).
2. Jīva Gosvāmī's text says this means the jīva has, **without beginning, possessed no knowledge of the beginningless Supreme Entity**. The jīva's lack of that knowledge is a case of **prāg-abhāva** (previous non-existence). It is not atyanta-abhāva and not pradhvaṁsa-abhāva.
3. Knowledge of the Lord, once obtained, is eternal and cannot be lost.
4. Scripture is given by the Lord out of mercy. It yields understanding of the Lord only to those who have *ajñāta-sukṛti* (matured into śraddhā) or *kṛpā* (mercy), in the association of Vaiṣṇavas.

**Main conclusion.** The jīva need not blame himself for "turning away". His condition is that of an unplugged refrigerator, a potential never connected to its source. The potential is eternal, and it is realized only through contact with the Lord's saṁvit and hlādinī śaktis, which comes through devotees.

**Legend used in these notes.**
- **Text** means Jīva Gosvāmī's Bhakti-sandarbha as read in the lecture.
- **Commentary** means the footnote by Bhānu Swāmī Mahārāja, as cited by the lecturer.
- **Lecturer** means the speaker's own explanation.

## 2. Detailed Study Notes

### 2.1 Recap: the Ṣaṭ-sandarbha and its place in the tradition

#### Purpose and standing of the work
- Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is open to varied interpretation, and different people have given different explanations. A thorough analysis was therefore needed to resolve disputed issues of interpretation on particular subjects.
- A second purpose was to synthesize the message of the entire body of Vedic literature.
- Through the Ṣaṭ-sandarbha, Jīva Gosvāmī gave the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava school a clear identity on par with those of Śrī Rāmānujācārya, Śrī Madhvācārya and others.
- **Text (as shown on the slides):** he drew freely from the entire heritage of Vaiṣṇava philosophical thought available to him. He wrote no important conclusion without supporting scriptural references, yet his conclusions are not mere repetitions. They bear the mark of originality and deserve independent consideration.
- The work is widely acknowledged within the Gauḍīya tradition as his philosophical magnum opus.
- **Original name:** the Ṣaṭ-sandarbha was originally called **Bhāgavata-sandarbha**. This indicates that it is an exposition and analysis of the essential message of Śrīmad-Bhāgavata Purāṇa.
- In this work Jīva Gosvāmī offers a comprehensive and exhaustive analysis of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. He concludes that the highest feature of the Absolute is a **personal God**.

#### Repetition versus originality (lecturer)
- Repeating a pūrvācārya's presentation is **not a black mark** for a Vaiṣṇava. It is an honour to be a proper representative of a pūrvācārya.
- *Illustration:* Śrīla Prabhupāda was once approached in Canada by a senior Śrī Vaiṣṇava scholar. The scholar said he had read Prabhupāda's Bhagavad-gītā but was not impressed, because Rāmānujācārya had already written the same many years earlier. In effect he was criticizing the lack of philosophical originality. Prabhupāda thanked him and took it as a compliment.
- Ācāryas also present their own reflections and revelations, and often explain particular verses in significantly different ways.
- *Reason:* this is the nature of Bhāgavatam. It is non-different from Kṛṣṇa and therefore multifaceted like Kṛṣṇa.

#### Bhāgavatam is multifaceted (lecturer)
- *Analogy of the vaidūrya stone:* viewed from different directions, it shows a different colour.
- Different facets are revealed to different ācāryas. Within a facet, an ācārya may reveal different layers of understanding. The same ācārya may reveal several facets, each with several layers.
- In every śloka and every akṣara there can be an unlimited number of bona fide meanings.
- *Illustration:* Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu revealed **18 meanings** of the Ātmārāma verse to Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya. Without touching any of those 18, He revealed **61 other meanings** to Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī. These were not speculations. They were proper explanations and revelations.

#### Why Jīva Gosvāmī argues at such length (lecturer)
- Some conclusions may seem unquestioned to us, and we may wonder why Jīva Gosvāmī gives so many arguments and references to establish a point we never debated.
- We are in one sense a "pampered lot". We received everything directly and were not exposed to the opposing arguments of Māyāvādīs, Tattvavādīs and others. We also lacked the interest.
- Even Caitanya Mahāprabhu's philosophy is difficult to understand. We tend to want only "tell me what I should be doing", without the time or interest to understand why.
- We will be astonished at how many pūrvapakṣa arguments there were, and how much interest people had in philosophy in those days.

#### Relation to Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu (lecturer)
- The subject matter of the **Bhakti-sandarbha** corresponds, in an elaborated sense, to the **first 19 chapters of the eastern part (pūrva-vibhāga)** of Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu.
- **Prīti-sandarbha** is the rasa part. It corresponds to the southern, western and northern parts of Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, plus some portions of higher literature such as Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi. It discusses prayojana.
- The Friday sessions are expected to have completed the southern, western and northern parts of Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu before Prīti-sandarbha is reached.

#### Order of study (lecturer)
- Ideally one would go Tattva → Bhagavat → Paramātma → Kṛṣṇa → Bhakti.
- The course starts with **Bhakti-sandarbha** for these reasons:
  - The subject is more familiar. Hopefully everyone has completed at least Bhakti-śāstrī.
  - The treatment is lighter and not as technical as the others, so devotees can ease into the heavily technical parts.
- Tattva-sandarbha is fairly technical, though not the most technical. Paramātma-sandarbha is very technical.
- Subsequent order: Tattva, Bhagavat, Paramātma, Kṛṣṇa, then finally Prīti.

### 2.2 The opening of the Bhakti-sandarbha

#### Attribution to Gopāla Bhaṭṭa
- **Text:** Jīva Gosvāmī says he repeatedly examined the original work written by Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, who greatly pleased Rūpa and Sanātana. He found parts of it in order, parts not in order and parts incomplete. He wrote this work after putting it in systematic order.
- **Lecturer:**
  - We cannot know how much is Gopāla Bhaṭṭa's and how much is Jīva Gosvāmī's. Gopāla Bhaṭṭa may have supplied the seed of the idea, or may have actually written it.
  - Ācāryas who do not want credit sometimes pass it on to another, and may give more credit than is due. No percentage is stated.
  - The Gosvāmīs, immersed in the bliss of kṛṣṇa-prema, do not seek the bliss of accomplishment.
  - Practically all later ācāryas attribute the work to Jīva Gosvāmī alone.

#### Structure: Anucchedas and the relay race
- The book is in prose, divided into **Anucchedas** (paragraphs).
- Each Anuccheda presents a philosophical point, explains it and substantiates it with verses from Bhāgavatam and other literature.
- *Analogy of a relay race (4 × 100 m or 4 × 400 m):* one runner covers a stretch and hands the baton to the next. Likewise, each Anuccheda hands its conclusion to the next, which builds upon it.
- Some Anucchedas are very long, because a point may need elaborate explanation and substantiation.
- **Themes:** Jīva Gosvāmī gave no themes for the Anucchedas. The lecturer says that when the text was read previously (in "GEV"), the readers finalized a theme after completing each portion and sent it out with the notes.

### 2.3 Anuccheda 1 (first part): root cause and remedy

#### The governing verse (Navayogendra teaching, Eleventh Canto)
The lecturer introduces the Anuccheda through one verse from the teachings of the Navayogendras in the 11th Canto. Only fragments are quoted (full details in Section 3, entry 1).

**Lecturer's explanation of the sequence in the verse:**

| Step | Term | Explanation given |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *īśād apetasya* | The jīva turns his face away from Kṛṣṇa. The lecturer says the questions "when did he turn?" and "was he looking towards Kṛṣṇa before turning away?" are parked for now. |
| 2 | *tan-māyā* | Kṛṣṇa knows that some jīvas will look away from Him. He has assigned the territory of Māyā Devī to facilitate the enjoyment of those jīvas in the way they wish to enjoy, without the visible involvement of Kṛṣṇa. Nothing can happen without Kṛṣṇa's involvement, but it is not visible. Māyā Devī takes over. |
| 3 | *viparyaya* and *asmṛti* | Māyā makes the jīva forget his original position as soul and servant of Kṛṣṇa. She makes him identify with the body, an alternate identification. |
| 4 | *dvitīya-abhiniveśa* | Absorption in matter, in the material body and the objects of the world. The jīva is supposed to be absorbed in topics of Kṛṣṇa. |
| 5 | *bhaya* | Fear of saṁsāra (repeated birth and death), since the body is temporary. Identifying with the body brings constant fear of death or of losing something. |

**The remedy (lecturer's reading of the verse):** one performs pure devotional service to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, accepting the guru as one's life and soul and acting under his guidance (*guru-devatātmā*). The whole essence of the Anuccheda is beautifully summarized in this verse.

#### Jīva Gosvāmī's opening statement
Each point is **Text** as read, followed by the lecturer's gloss.
1. The previous Sandarbhas are recalled. The highest manifestation of the Absolute, among the three, was said to be **Bhagavān**. Forms such as Viṣṇu and the Kumāras were discussed, and the highest form of Bhagavān was concluded to be **Kṛṣṇa**.
   - *Lecturer:* this was established in Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha ("Kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam") and rests on the Bhāgavatam's statement of three manifestations of the Absolute Truth, of which Bhagavān is the highest ("Paramātmeti bhagavān iti śabdyate" is cited).
2. In enumerating the powers of Paramātmā, the jīvas were described, being forms of the **taṭasthā-śakti**, whose unchanging nature is consciousness. The lecturer says jīva-tattva was thus described in the Paramātma-sandarbha.
3. The jīva's essential knowledge is covered by Māyā, by the misfortune of being opposed to the Lord.
4. This means the jīva has, **without beginning, possessed no knowledge of the beginningless Supreme Entity**. The jīva consequently believes he is made of dull matter composed of sattva, rajas and tamas. The lecturer calls this false identification with the body.

*Lecturer:* "opposed" does not mean hostile. It means the jīva never knew the Lord and had no access to knowledge of Him. This is expanded in Section 4, Q6.

### 2.4 Saṁsarga-abhāva: the three kinds of non-existence

#### Why the term is introduced
Jīva Gosvāmī's statement that the jīva "possessed no knowledge" of the Lord "without beginning" rests on the term *saṁsarga-abhāva* (non-existence of a relation or connection). **Commentary (Bhānu Swāmī Mahārāja's footnote)** explains that it has **three categories**.

#### The three categories

| Category | Meaning | Beginning / end (as stated) | Lecturer's application |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Atyanta-abhāva** | Absolute non-existence. Never existing in past, present or future. | Neither beginning nor end | The *non-existence of the jīva* is atyanta-abhāva. There was never a time when the jīva did not exist, and there never will be. The jīva cannot be destroyed by anything. The jīva is thus a classic example of *anādi* and *ananta*. |
| **Pradhvaṁsa-abhāva** | Absence by destruction. Something existed and was then destroyed. | Has a beginning (*ādi*) but no end (*ananta*) | Applied to the *liberation* of the conditioned soul. When the conditioned soul, by practising bhakti, becomes qualified, liberation begins. Having attained it, there is no turning back and no non-existence of that liberation. |
| **Prāg-abhāva** | Previous non-existence. Something did not exist but appears at a later time. | No beginning (*anādi*) but an end (*anta*) | Applied to the *conditioning* of the living entity. It has no beginning but ends when, by bhakti, it comes to an end. |

**Supporting verse cited by the lecturer** (Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura): *anādi karama-phale, paḍi' bhavārṇava-jale* ("no beginning, through the fruit of karma"). Karma is *anādi* but has an *anta* when bhakti ends it. The lecturer's account of the end of the conditioning (*bahirmukhatā*):
- The jīva comes in touch with Vaiṣṇavas and receives *sukṛti*.
- Sukṛti turns into śraddhā.
- Through śraddhā the jīva pursues *bhajana-kriyā*.
- Through this he understands the Supreme Lord, and the *bahirmukhatā* slowly ends.

*Editorial note:* during the lecture the lecturer stopped and corrected how the categories map to the jīva's states ("this should come here and this should go here"). The table above gives the mapping as first read from the footnote, with the ādi/anta pairs as stated there. See Section 6 for the in-session inconsistency.

#### Which category does the jīva's lack of knowledge belong to?
**Text:** The jīva's non-existence of knowledge of the Lord is:
1. **Not atyanta-abhāva**, since the jīva can obtain knowledge at some point in time.
2. **Not pradhvaṁsa-abhāva**, since if the jīva had knowledge of the Lord it could not be destroyed.
   - A nitya-siddha cannot have his knowledge of the Lord destroyed.
   - Even a sādhana-siddha cannot have his knowledge destroyed.
   - *Support from Prīti-sandarbha, as read by the lecturer:* knowledge of the Lord, if established in the jīva, should be eternal. The text says "the destruction of ignorance concerning Ātmā is eternal", because the knowledge which destroys the ignorance is a manifestation of the Supreme Entity's nature of self-revelation. "The absolute destruction of suffering is eternal because that ignorance existed but was destroyed." The lecturer identifies this as pradhvaṁsa-abhāva: the ignorance existed, was destroyed, and no longer exists.
3. If it is argued that the knowledge is simply **unmanifested but still present** in the jīva, then it would not be classed as *abhāva* at all. The very fact that it is called abhāva (non-existence) means that at some point it did not exist. It was not merely covered.
4. Therefore the jīva's lack of knowledge is **prāg-abhāva**. The knowledge did not previously exist in the jīva.
5. This does **not exclude** the possibility of obtaining knowledge of the Lord in the future. On attainment it becomes the **eternal possession** of the jīva.

**Lecturer's further supports:**
- The Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu commentary also explains clearly that when the jīva comes in touch with Kṛṣṇa's **saṁvit and hlādinī** śakti, there is no way he will again turn his face towards Māyā.
- "Without beginning" means eternally: particular jīvas have been looking away from Kṛṣṇa since time without beginning.
- *Lecturer:* Jīva Gosvāmī "takes the bull by the horns" in the very first Anuccheda.

### 2.5 The categories of jīvas and the "fourth category"

- **Lecturer:** books such as Jaiva-dharma, Prameya-ratnāvalī and the works of Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa describe only three categories of jīvas:
  1. **Nitya-siddhas** (or nitya-muktas).
  2. **Baddha-muktas** (also called sādhana-siddhas): those who were bound and eventually became liberated.
  3. **Nitya-baddhas**: eternally bound.
- None of these books mentions a fourth category, **mukta-baddhas**, who were first liberated and eventually became bound.
- The point from Jīva Gosvāmī's text (a nitya-siddha cannot lose knowledge, nor can a sādhana-siddha, and established knowledge is eternal) fits this.
- The lecturer says he is not discussing the whole fall-down question now. He says it was discussed for about 35–40 hours in the Jaiva-dharma discussion, and that it will come up later in the course. His answers on it in the Q&A are marked as cautious (Section 4, Q5).

### 2.6 Scripture as mercy, and who can understand it

#### The Eleventh Canto verse on those without knowledge of Paramātmā
**Text:** in the 11th Canto, Kṛṣṇa tells Uddhava of those who lack knowledge of the Self. The lecturer's gloss follows.
- *Ātmā-aparijñāna-maya*: because they do not know Paramātmā, they are fixed (*niṣṭha*) in goals (*artha*) different (*vi-*) from Kṛṣṇa.
- *Yasti nāsti iti vivāda*: they argue about the reality or unreality of the world.
- *Vyartho 'pi naivoparameta*: they do not cease even though the arguments are useless. Such arguments, carried on by people who do not know the Supreme Lord, can never lead to a fruitful conclusion.
- *Mattaḥ parāvṛtta-dhiyām*: their thoughts are turned away from the Lord.
- *Sva-lokāt*: the lecturer glosses this as "I give regard only to the devotees".

#### The Lord's mercy in giving scripture
- **Text:** for the benefit of the jīvas, the Lord teaches scripture "filled with the highest mercy".
- **Lecturer:** this corresponds to the Caitanya-caritāmṛta teaching that the conditioned soul has no spontaneous (*svataḥ*) Kṛṣṇa-knowledge, and that Kṛṣṇa out of mercy compiled the Vedas and Purāṇas. The jīva cannot revive his Kṛṣṇa consciousness by his own effort.
- **Lecturer:** the conditioned soul does not have Kṛṣṇa-jñāna on his own. Kṛṣṇa presented the Veda-Purāṇa to bestow mercy.

#### The two enabling factors
**Text (as read):** *some jīvas*, by accumulating impressions (*saṁskāra*) for understanding the Lord over many births, become favourable to the Lord. Or jīvas who have attained **only great mercy** become favourable to the Lord. They gain understanding of the Lord **at the very instant** of hearing teachings about the Supreme Lord. The lecturer calls this a very important statement.

| Factor | Lecturer's explanation |
|---|---|
| **Sukṛti-bala** (the strength of *ajñāta-sukṛti*, also called *bhakty-unmukhī sukṛti*) | Accumulated over many lifetimes. It matures into śraddhā. Those with it become *kṛṣṇa-unmukha* and gain understanding at once when they hear teachings about the Supreme Lord. |
| **Kṛpā-bala** (the strength of mercy) | Those who attain it are called *kṛpā-siddhas*. By mercy they suddenly attain the qualification to understand the Lord. |

- **Lecturer:** the "suddenness" in the Bhāgavatam's statement is only apparent. The desire has manifested after many lifetimes. In other cases "sudden" can refer to kṛpā-siddhas.
- **Lecturer:** those who lack either factor cannot gain understanding of the Lord through the scriptures, even though the scriptures exist. They give no regard to the scriptures.
- Those who have either factor give no regard to other teachings and accept the bhakti-śāstras. If they hear other teachings, those teachings act as **uddīpana** (stimulus) for their own rasa, as though they were hearing about the Lord's past pastimes.
- *Illustration (lecturer):* in the days of coal-fired trains, either Vaṁśīdāsa Bābājī Mahārāja or Gaurakiśora Dāsa Bābājī Mahārāja (the lecturer is unsure which) would run behind a black engine shouting "Śyāmasundara!". The black colour stimulated his own rasa.

#### Perfected devotees speak with humility (illustration of Prahlāda)
- **Text:** the example given is Prahlāda Mahārāja. On hearing teachings about materialism from his teachers, he preached the glories of Viṣṇu. Yet perfected devotees, disguising themselves as ordinary people out of humility, speak as if overcome by illusion or by karma.
- Prahlāda's verse (Section 3, entry 7) says his mind is contaminated, wicked, uncontrollable, not pleased with topics of the Lord, afflicted by lust, and disturbed by joy, sorrow, fear and desire for wealth.
- **Lecturer:** the verses of Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura and Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura are written in the same way.

#### The Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa verse: how scripture-hearing bears fruit
- The first half says that as long as the heart is contaminated with sins, the intelligence will neither accept scripture as true nor accept the guru as a spiritual teacher.
- **Lecturer's caution:** read alone, this first half gives a slightly incomplete or even incorrect understanding. The next half must be read.
- The second half says the result of accumulated pious acts over many births is great. The lecturer explains that the *puṇya-rāśi* here refers to **ajñāta-sukṛti** accumulated over many lifetimes.
- **Lecturer's sequence:**
  1. Many lifetimes of ajñāta-sukṛti lead to **śraddhā**.
  2. Śraddhā leads to desire for **sādhu-saṅga**.
  3. In satsaṅga one engages in **śāstra-śravaṇa**, which the lecturer calls *bhajana-kriyā*.
  4. Prema arises **only** from hearing scripture in the association of devotees.
- The lecturer ties this to the line that devotional hearing (*śravaṇādi*) purifies the heart and prema awakens, and that śravaṇa-kīrtana is like water that nourishes it.

### 2.7 The lecturer's closing summary
- The jīva's essential knowledge is covered by Māyā because of being opposed to the Lord, which means that without beginning he has no knowledge of the Supreme Lord. Therefore he believes himself to be dull matter.
- The Eleventh Canto proves that those who do not know the Lord are fixed in goals other than Kṛṣṇa and engage in useless arguments.
- For the benefit of jīvas the Lord teaches scriptures. Those who lack both **sukṛti-bala** and **kṛpā-bala** cannot gain understanding of the Lord through scripture and do not regard it.
- Those who have either give no regard to other teachings. They accept the bhakti-śāstras, and anything else they hear stimulates what they have learned from them. Prahlāda is the example. Such perfected persons may speak with humility as though overcome by illusion.
- From the Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa: a sinful heart will not accept scripture or guru. But with much ajñāta-sukṛti accumulated over many births, faith develops. With that faith, śāstra-śravaṇa in the association of Vaiṣṇavas leads to prema, that is, realization of Kṛṣṇa.
- To realize Kṛṣṇa through the śāstras, one needs either sukṛti-bala or kṛpā-bala, which comes through the Vaiṣṇavas. In their association alone does the jīva's good fortune arise.
- The first Anuccheda is **not complete**. One more part remains for the next session.

## 4. Questions & Answers

### Q1. Does Jīva Gosvāmī's statement contradict Maitreya's teaching to Vidura?
**Questioner:** Govinda Hari Prabhu.

**Question.** In the morning Bhāgavatam class (Vidura–Maitreya discussion), Vidura had asked how the jīva is caught in ignorance, and how Bhagavān can be purely spiritual (*cinmaya*) while dealing with the material element. The questioner understood Maitreya to have said that the jīva's knowledge is **never destroyed**, using the examples of a dream and of wearing a gold necklace in the dark and thinking one does not possess it. Jīva Gosvāmī now says the jīva **never had** knowledge of the Supreme. These seem to be opposite conclusions.

**Lecturer's answer.**
- The "knowledge" Maitreya speaks of is like the knowledge of a **dream state**: eternal (*anādi*) forgetfulness. Once you wake up you realize it was a dream, but until then it is as good as real.
- *Constitutionally the jīva does not come in touch with matter* (*asaṅgo hy ayaṁ puruṣaḥ*), yet in the dream state he acts as if he has a relationship with matter.
- A very similar question was asked by Parīkṣit Mahārāja to Śukadeva Gosvāmī. The answer is that no real relationship occurs, just as in a dream.
- The jīva constitutionally cannot establish a relationship with matter, but he can dream as though he has had one since time immemorial.
- **The reconciliation:** the *potential* to establish a relationship with Kṛṣṇa is eternally present in the jīva and is never lost, but it is forgotten. When Kṛṣṇa's **saṁvit and hlādinī** come in touch with the jīva, the potential is realized. When the same potency in the form of bhakti comes in touch with inert matter, nothing is realized.
- That realization happens when the jīva comes in touch with a devotee, receives the seed of devotion, cultivates it, and it reaches **bhāva and prema**. Then the jīva, who has eternally looked away, turns to Kṛṣṇa and attains knowledge he never loses.
- **Maitreya** speaks of the *potential*, which is never lost. **Jīva Gosvāmī** speaks of the *realization* of that potential. So there is no contradiction.

**Follow-up clarifications.**
- The questioner summarized: the potential is *anādi*, the realization is what has an end, and once realized it cannot be lost. He asked what ignorance the Text refers to. The lecturer replied that the potential is eternal because the jīva is eternal and his *dharma* is eternal.
- *Does the potential then also have an end, since once realized it is no longer a potential?* **Lecturer:** if you take "potential" that way, yes. The potential is no longer unrealized. Now it is realized.
- *Digression on the "elsewhere" view.* The questioner said he was not surprised that some devotees conclude that the jīva never really comes to the material world, being spiritual and unable to mix with matter, so that material existence is only a question of a dream or alternate state. The lecturer said this had been discussed in the morning class, and that a devotee had asked if the material world does not exist except in the jīva's dream. **This is not our understanding.** The jīva is real. The material world is real. The jīva's existence in the material world is real. What is a phantasmagoria, an imagination and ignorance is the **identification with the material body**. To say the world itself is unreal is Māyāvāda.
- A *red-hot iron* analogy was raised by the questioner (a spiritual particle behaving like fire without mingling with material elements). The lecturer did not comment on it separately.

**Related concepts:** [[Saṁsarga-abhāva]], saṁvit and hlādinī, *asaṅga*, Māyāvāda versus the reality of the world.

### Q2. If the jīva is sac-cid-ānanda like the Lord, in what sense is a nitya-baddha bound?
**Questioner:** Ānanda Sundarī Mātājī.

**Lecturer's answer.**
- The jīva **is** sac-cid-ānanda and an eternal part and parcel of Kṛṣṇa.
- Often it is said that the jīva is *qualitatively one* with Kṛṣṇa. The more appropriate statement is that he is **qualitatively similar**, not one.
- *Illustration of the difference:* Kṛṣṇa has **64 qualities**. The jīva has only **50** qualities, of which a few he may show at some time. The other **14**, found exclusively in Kṛṣṇa, are not in the jīva even in a small quantity.
- Because of his minute size and his *taṭasthā* (marginal) nature, there is always a chance that the jīva does not realize his sac-cid-ānanda potential.
- *Analogy from the morning Bhāgavatam class (iron filings):* iron is much denser than air, but iron filings, being tiny, are blown away by a strong gust. Similarly the jīva is a superior energy of Kṛṣṇa, in comparison with the inferior material energy, yet because he is infinitesimal he can come under the influence of Māyā and misidentify with the body.
- **Nitya-baddha** does **not** mean destined to remain conditioned forever. It means conditioned since eternity, but able to be liberated in the right association of Vaiṣṇavas.

**Related concepts:** [[Nitya-baddha]], taṭasthā-śakti, jīva's anutva.

### Q3. Before misusing free will, was the jīva liberated in the spiritual world?
**Questioner:** Ānanda Sundarī Mātājī (follow-up).

**Question.** Śrīla Prabhupāda explains that the jīva in the spiritual world was sac-cid-ānanda and, by misusing his little independence, became conditioned. So before misusing it he was liberated. She said she had heard this from senior disciples of Prabhupāda, out of non-offensive curiosity.

**Lecturer's answer.**
- The question is why a sac-cid-ānanda jīva who had all knowledge and bliss would misuse his free will.
- "Let us keep reading and see if that is the explanation that is given."
- Prabhupāda said **both** things:
  - Many times he clearly said the jīva does not fall from Vaikuṇṭha.
  - He also said the jīva falls by misuse of his free will.
- These two apparently contradictory statements must be reconciled in a way that establishes Prabhupāda as **on par with the paramparā**.
- The lecturer says the pūrvācāryas' writings explain this through the **three categories** of jīvas (nitya-baddha, nitya-siddha, baddha-mukta or sādhana-siddha). He says the whole matter will be taken up later, as and when it arises, since only the first Anuccheda has begun.

**Related concepts:** the fall-down issue, three categories of jīvas, [[Nitya-baddha]].

He confirmed that the jīvas under discussion were nitya-baddhas, conditioned eternally.

**Further related points raised in chat.**

But the question remains why, having experienced Kṛṣṇa, the jīva would feel like leaving Him at all. The next question, from the other party, is: if he left once, what guarantees he will not leave again? **That is not true**, the lecturer says. Prīti-sandarbha says the destruction of ignorance concerning Ātmā is eternal, because the knowledge that destroys the ignorance is a manifestation of the Supreme Entity's self-revelation. ### Q6. How can the jīva be called "opposed to the Lord" if it was so from the beginning? (chat)
**Lecturer's answer.**
- "Opposed to the Lord" does not mean outright hostility (as in "if you say Kṛṣṇa's name I will kill you"). It means the jīva **never knew** the Lord and had **no access** to that knowledge.
- *Analogy of the unplugged refrigerator:* a refrigerator that was never connected to electricity never realized its potential of cooling. Nitya-baddhas are like such refrigerators, patiently waiting for a devotee to pick up the plug and connect it to the electricity, so that the potential can be realized.
- *Limit of the analogy (lecturer):* material examples are limited. A refrigerator came into existence at a particular time, leaving the factory. Assume that time is eternal, and since that time the refrigerator has not been plugged in.
- **Conclusion:** the jīva need not take the blame for having turned away from Kṛṣṇa, and need not get depressed about it. Perfected devotees, out of natural humility, take that blame upon themselves, but in reality the jīva need not take that responsibility.
- When the jīva meets a devotee who "plugs him in", the potential is realized.

## 5. Key Takeaways

1. **The Ṣaṭ-sandarbha** is Jīva Gosvāmī's philosophical magnum opus, originally named Bhāgavata-sandarbha. It gave the Gauḍīya school an identity on par with Rāmānuja's and Madhva's. Repeating a pūrvācārya is an honour, and Bhāgavatam, like Kṛṣṇa, is multifaceted.
2. The six Sandarbhas are organised as: epistemology (Tattva), sambandha (Bhagavat, Paramātmā, Kṛṣṇa), abhidheya (Bhakti), prayojana (Prīti).
3. The **cause** of the jīva's suffering is *kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā*. The **remedy** is bhakti under the guru, becoming *kṛṣṇa-unmukha*.
4. Per the Navayogendra verse, the sequence is: turning away → Māyā takes over → forgetfulness and misidentification → absorption in matter → fear.
5. **The jīva's lack of knowledge of the Lord is prāg-abhāva.** It is not atyanta-abhāva, because the jīva can obtain knowledge. It is not pradhvaṁsa-abhāva, because knowledge of the Lord, once established, cannot be destroyed. It is also not merely "covered", because then it would not be abhāva at all.
6. Knowledge of the Lord, once obtained, is **eternal**. This is why no mukta-baddha (liberated, then bound) category is found in Jaiva-dharma, Prameya-ratnāvalī or Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa's works.
7. **Potential versus realization.** The potential for a relationship with Kṛṣṇa is eternal. Its realization, through saṁvit and hlādinī, is what has a beginning and, once attained, never ends. This reconciles Maitreya's "never lost" with Jīva Gosvāmī's "never had".
8. The jīva's relationship with matter is like a dream. The world is real. The jīva is real. Only identification with the material body is imagination.
9. The jīva is qualitatively similar to Kṛṣṇa, not one with Him. Kṛṣṇa has 64 qualities and the jīva 50. Being infinitesimal, he can fall under Māyā.
10. The nitya-baddha need not take blame for having turned away, like an unplugged refrigerator. Free will does not manifest without a devotee's intervention.
11. Scripture is the Lord's mercy to the conditioned soul, but one gains understanding only through **sukṛti-bala** (ajñāta-sukṛti) or **kṛpā-bala**. Those who have either value the bhakti-śāstras, and other teachings become uddīpana.
12. The progress is: ajñāta-sukṛti over many lives → śraddhā → sādhu-saṅga → śāstra-śravaṇa (bhajana-kriyā) → prema. A sinful heart accepts neither scripture nor guru.
13. A madhyama-adhikārī shows compassion, not criticism, towards ignorant non-devotees.
14. The lecturer says "it looks like" the jīva did not fall from the spiritual world, going by the Sandarbhas, Jaiva-dharma and the other ācāryas. He says Prabhupāda's statements on this must be reconciled with the paramparā, and he defers the full discussion.

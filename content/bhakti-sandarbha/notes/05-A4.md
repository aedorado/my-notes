# Bhakti Sandarbha – Anuccheda 4: Bhakti Is Independent; Other Practices Depend on Bhakti

## 1. Recap of Previous Lecture

The lecturer recapped Anucchedas 1, 2 and 3.

### Anuccheda 1

- The whole Bhakti Sandarbha begins from **Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā**. This is caused by the **prāg-abhāva** (prior non-existence) of the Jīva's knowledge of Kṛṣṇa. The Jīva can overcome it by becoming **Kṛṣṇa-unmukha** through the practice of bhakti. This is the essence of the first Anuccheda.
- The Jīva's essential knowledge is covered by Māyā, owing to the misfortune of being opposed to the Lord. Since without beginning the Jīva has possessed no knowledge of the Supreme Lord, he believes he is made of dull matter composed of sattva, rajas and tamas.
- This lack of knowledge is prāg-abhāva among the forms of **saṁsarga-abhāva** (not having previously existed in the Jīva). It does not exclude obtaining knowledge of the Lord in the future. Once attained, that knowledge becomes the eternal possession of the Jīva, though it did not exist before.
- For the benefit of the Jīva, the Lord teaches scripture filled with the highest mercy. Jīvas who have accumulated **ajñāta-sukṛti** or great mercy, through either **sukṛti-bala** or **kṛpā-bala**, become favourable to the Lord. They gain understanding of the Lord the very instant they hear teachings about Him.

> [!important]
> The scriptures alone, especially the Bhakti Śāstras, cannot help the Jīva if he has not been nourished by the association of Vaiṣṇavas. Without ajñāta-sukṛti, which matures into **śraddhā**, the Jīva will not have faith in the Bhakti Śāstras even though they exist.

- When a Jīva is nourished by sukṛti-bala or kṛpā-bala and accepts the Bhakti Śāstras sincerely, he gives no regard to other teachings. Even if he hears them by chance, they act as stimuli (*uddīpana*) for his own rasa.
- **Example:** Prahlāda Mahārāja. When he heard teachings on materialistic things from his teachers (named in the transcript as "Shanda and Amartha"), it inspired him to preach the glories of Viṣṇu.
- Even though the final conclusion is the existence of the Supreme Lord, one must still learn the method of attainment (**Abhidheya**) and the final goal (**Prayojana**). These were mentioned in the earlier Sandarbhas, but in the Bhakti Sandarbha the Abhidheya is described very clearly, as specific conclusions.
- **Abhidheya** = actions producing favourableness to the Lord, since that is the opposite of the Jīva's aversion to the Lord. This means worship of the Lord, from which knowledge of the Lord arises.
- The goal is **realization of the Lord**: internal and external perception of the Lord, from which all suffering is destroyed. The lecturer said realization is **prema**: at the stage of prema there is internal and external perception of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- These two topics are presented in much more detail in the **Bhakti Sandarbha (5th)** and **Prīti Sandarbha (6th)**, so that the recipient will not be negligent in attainment.
- After explaining that the Jīva's cause of suffering is aversion to the Lord, with beginningless ignorance of His eternal existence, the scriptures teach favourability to the Lord, which is like a cure for the cause of the illness. Through the scriptures describing Kṛṣṇa-unmukhatā, Kṛṣṇa brings the Jīva closer and closer to turning his face towards Him.
- The verse (*bhayaṁ dvitīyābhiniveśataḥ…*, garbled in the transcript, see Section 6) explains the sequence:
  - Aversion to the Lord leads to non-appearance of the Jīva's svarūpa, and the opposite awareness "I am the body" arises. For the Jīva who has looked away from Kṛṣṇa since time immemorial, there is lack of identification as the soul and unfavourable identification with the body.
  - From this comes absorption in the body and in things, people and places connected to the body, which eventually leads to **fear of saṁsāra**.
  - The solution: accept a Guru as one's life and soul, accept the path of bhakti through his teachings, and worship the Lord through bhakti. Then one overcomes Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā and becomes Kṛṣṇa-unmukha.

> [!success]
> **Essence of Anuccheda 1:** the root cause of the Jīva's suffering is Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā, which he overcomes by becoming Kṛṣṇa-unmukha through the practice of bhakti. For the practice of bhakti he needs faith in the Bhakti Śāstras, developed through sukṛti-bala or kṛpā-bala.

### Anuccheda 2

- A verse from the **Second Canto, Second Chapter** is quoted: *taṁ bhajeta*, by worshipping the Supreme Personality of Godhead, *saṁsāra-hetūparamaḥ*, the cause of saṁsāra is destroyed. The cause of saṁsāra is ultimately being Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukha, turning away from Kṛṣṇa.
- The verse clearly says that the practice of bhakti destroys the very root of saṁsāra. The yogī, becoming detached, should worship the Supreme Lord.
- The verse indicates that the Lord is joyful by His very nature. Worship destroys ignorance, the cause of saṁsāra, and the word **ca** indicates that besides destruction of ignorance one also achieves the Supreme Lord. Śukadeva Gosvāmī speaks this verse.
- **Essence of Anuccheda 2:** the proof that by practising bhakti one overcomes saṁsāra, whose root cause is Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukhatā.

### Anuccheda 3

- Theme: the nature of pure bhakti, through verses from various cantos of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam.
- **Pūrvapakṣa:** hearing and contemplation are methods of jñāna; hearing and contemplating the nature of Brahman and the soul (how the soul is different from matter) gives realization of Brahman, and is thus suitable for gradual development of realization of the Lord. So Sāṅkhya, Yoga and Karma should be favourable for producing bhakti.
  - Karma is a cause of bhakti because it follows the Lord's order and involves offering to the Lord. The lecturer's illustration: performing varṇāśrama duties and offering the fruits to Kṛṣṇa.
  - Jñāna is an attendant to bhakti because it causes detachment, which is favourable for bhakti.
  - The objector asks: why then is exclusive bhakti emphasised, with no credibility given to karma, jñāna and yoga?
- **Answer, from Sūta Gosvāmī in the First Canto:**
  - The wise constantly and with great joy perform only bhakti to Lord Kṛṣṇa, because pure bhakti alone gives great joy to the mind.
  - The supreme dharma for all human beings is that by which prema-bhakti to the Lord arises. This bhakti is not caused by anything other than itself and cannot be obstructed, and by it the mind is completely satisfied. It should not be covered by jñāna, karma etc.; it should also be *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnya*.
  - At the beginning of the great Purāṇa, the Śravaṇakādi Ṛṣis (as transcribed) asked for the final essence of all scriptures. In answer, Sūta spoke this verse: from this dharma, taste for hearing the topics of the Lord arises. The lecturer said any action which eventually leads to a desire to hear about the Supreme Lord is the **para-dharma**.
  - Commonly known dharma is varṇāśrama-dharma, but the Bhāgavatam says varṇāśrama-dharma which does not produce attraction for the topics of the Lord is only wasted effort.
  - **Vairāgya and jñāna are not prerequisites:** a lot of detachment is not required to practise bhakti. Detachment and knowledge are **byproducts** of the practice of bhakti.
  - Jñāna, sakāma-karma and niṣkāma-karma are not glorious if devoid of bhakti. It is the **bhakti element** in jñāna, karma etc. that gives potency to those practices.

> [!important]
> It is not that karma, jñāna and yoga support bhakti; rather, **bhakti makes those other paths fruitful**. Jñāna and karma can never support bhakti; they can only **cover** it. Without bhakti, the practices of jñāna and karma are futile.

- **Qualities of bhakti:** *ahaitukī* means *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnya* (devoid of other desires); *apratihatā* means not covered by jñāna, karma, dhyāna etc. The perfection of dharma according to varṇa and āśrama is pleasing the Lord. *Saḥ* means "this alone is the best." Bhakti is distinct from the dharma described in the text (varṇāśrama).
- **Progression:** a taste for bhakti (**śraddhā**) → **sādhu-saṅga** → **bhajaneṣu spṛhā** (desire to perform hearing and chanting) → **bhajana-kriyā** (actual hearing and chanting).
- Pure bhakti, which truly satisfies the heart, is *ahaitukī apratihatā yayātmā suprasīdati*.

## 3. Main Lecture Notes

### 3.1 Anuccheda 4: Theme

> [!abstract]
> Having discussed the nature of pure bhakti (the Abhidheya), the theme of the fourth Anuccheda is that **bhakti is independent, whereas the other practices are dependent on bhakti.** The lecturer said Jīva Gosvāmī proceeds by giving proofs, beginning with the First Canto.

### 3.2 Verses and proofs

#### Bhakti produces knowledge and detachment

- The lecturer cited the verse quoted as Bhāgavatam 5.18.12 (see Section 6), and read: *vāsudeve bhagavati bhakti-yogaḥ prayojitaḥ / janayaty āśu vairāgyaṁ jñānaṁ ca yad ahaitukam*.
  - By bhakti one develops knowledge of the Lord's svarūpa; detachment from all else follows after this.
  - Bhakti dedicated to Lord Kṛṣṇa, **endowed with special moods** (*prayojitaḥ*), quickly produces detachment from material goals and knowledge of the Lord devoid of the desire for liberation.

> [!note]
> **Ahaitukī jñāna:** knowledge needed *exclusively for establishing a relationship with Kṛṣṇa*. This is not the kind of knowledge in which Kṛṣṇa becomes our "order supplier" ("I want something else and Kṛṣṇa gives me that"). That is a different type of knowledge and is not *jñānaṁ ca yad ahaitukam*.

- Bhakti, quickly and just by slight hearing, produces knowledge **beyond dry logic**, as stated in the Upaniṣads.

#### The negative statement: Bhāgavatam 1.2.8

The lecturer read: *dharmaḥ svanuṣṭhitaḥ puṁsāṁ viṣvaksena-kathāsu yaḥ / notpādayed yadi ratiṁ śrama eva hi kevalam*.

- Varṇāśrama-dharma of the human being, if it does not produce attraction for the topics of the Lord, is only wasted effort.
- **Essence:** actions should create a taste for hearing the topics of Kṛṣṇa (*viṣvaksena-kathāsu rati*).
- If dharma, not taking the support of Vāsudeva, does not produce taste for the descriptions of the Lord's pastimes, it will not produce any results. If varṇāśrama-dharma has no element of bhakti, it gives no result.
- This is stated because taste for the Lord's topics is **best and in every case the first result**. Taste for other types of worship is also indicated: not just hearing, but also chanting and worshipping the deity forms. Actions that create this taste are top-class actions.

##### The three emphasising words: *eva*, *hi*, *kevalam*

All three emphasise the same point: because dharma does not create taste for hearing the topics of Kṛṣṇa, it is wasted endeavour.

1. **Eva:** indicates the temporary nature of swarga and other results of karma. It is definitely useless because whatever else it may give, such as swarga, is only temporary.
2. **Hi:** indicates there is suitable, ample proof in Śruti, and that this is well substantiated by the conclusions of the Śāstras. For instance, the lecturer said the results of karma are temporary, citing the Chāndogya Upaniṣad (the quoted Sanskrit is garbled; see Section 6).
3. **Kevalam:** indicates that the results of dharma which produce **detachment and liberation** are also not to be attained, and it indicates the temporary nature of the **siddhis**. Aspiring for siddhis or even liberation is not a very laudable desire.

#### Proofs indicated by the word *hi*

1. ***Yasya deve parā bhaktir yathā deve tathā gurau…*** One who has devotion to the Guru as much as he has for the Lord has the scriptures' true imports (*tasyaite kathitā hy arthāḥ prakāśante mahātmanaḥ*) revealed to him. The lecturer's gloss: the true imports of the Śāstras are revealed only to one who respects the Guru on the same level as Kṛṣṇa.

> [!note]
> The lecturer described this verse and the next two as verses indicating that without the element of bhakti, the paths of karma, jñāna, yoga etc. give no result.

2. ***Śreyaḥ-sṛtiṁ bhaktim udasya te vibho kliśyanti ye kevala-bodha-labdhaye…*** (tenth canto verse): O Lord, if fools give up bhakti, the all-inclusive path, and suffer to attain realization of the self without bhakti, they simply attain suffering and nothing else.

> [!example]
> They are like fools who **beat empty husk** (*sthūla-tuṣāvaghātinām*). The more one beats, the more tired one becomes, but there is no rice, because the rice has already been removed from the husk. So even the path of jñāna, if it lacks the element of bhakti, gives only trouble, with no result in terms of self-realization or liberation.

3. ***Ye 'nye 'ravindākṣa vimukta-māninas tvayy asta-bhāvād aviśuddha-buddhayaḥ / āruhya kṛcchreṇa paraṁ padaṁ tataḥ patanty adho 'nādṛta-yuṣmad-aṅghrayaḥ*** (tenth canto verse). The lecturer's explanation:
   - Though these non-devotees accept severe austerities and penances to achieve the highest position of a jīvan-mukta, they only *think* themselves liberated (*vimukta-māninaḥ*); they are not actually liberated.
   - Their intelligence is impure (*aviśuddha-buddhayaḥ*) because they think that as soon as they attain liberation they will give up bhakti along with jñāna.
   - When they give up bhakti, they do not attain the result and fall from their position of imagined superiority, because they give no regard to the Lord's lotus feet. Not giving regard to the lotus feet means **rejecting the element of bhakti in their practice of jñāna**.
   - The result is like beating empty husk: no result, only trouble.

#### Conclusion of Anuccheda 4

- The two verses above indicate that **bhakti is independent, and jñāna and vairāgya are dependent**.
- What is stated is that dharma which results in bhakti is successful. Here "dharma" refers to activities, and "results in bhakti" refers to taste for hearing and the other limbs.
- Jñāna and vairāgya, commonly understood as the results of the practice of jñāna, are obtained by the practice of bhakti only (*vāsudeve bhagavati…*).

> [!success]
> To attain the result of jñāna, **bhakti does not need the support of jñāna**, whereas jñāna, to attain its own goal, **needs the element of bhakti**. This is what the two tenth-canto verses prove: practised without bhakti, jñāna is like beating empty husk and gives no result.

### 3.3 Commentaries on Bhāgavatam 1.2.8 (*śrama eva hi kevalam*)

These were read by the lecturer in response to a question (see Section 4, question 4), to see how the ācāryas explain the verse. The lecturer read the commentary of Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, then a further commentary (name garbled in the transcript, see Section 6), then Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport.

#### Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura

> [!question]
> Why is dharma in the form of varṇāśrama not considered **para-dharma**? The previous verse (1.2.6) called para-dharma that which leads to prema.

- This verse answers it: dharma undertaken by men that does not produce attraction for talks about the Supreme Lord is completely useless.
- The Śruti says by performing varṇāśrama duties one goes to the pitṛ-loka in the material world. Varṇāśrama duties do not produce attraction for the Lord. This is clear from **Nārada's statements in the Fourth Canto**: without that service, what is the use of taking three births (by semen, upanayana and initiation), of the ritual acts in the Vedas, of the lifespan of a devatā, and (in the next verse) of yoga, Sāṅkhya, sannyāsa, study of the Vedas and other auspicious acts, if the Lord does not give realization of Himself?
- Because of the temporary nature of the results of varṇāśrama duties, one should give up those duties and perform para-dharma, consisting of hearing and chanting about the Lord.
- A statement is also quoted (source not named in the transcript): one situated in his prescribed duty, free from sinful activities and cleansed of material contamination, in this very life obtains transcendental knowledge, **or, by fortune (*yadṛcchayā*), devotional service** unto the Lord. By fortune, not by mere practice of varṇāśrama, devotional service develops.

##### Objection and answer: is karma the cause of bhakti?

- **Pūrvapakṣa** (the lecturer said not to take it literally): the Lord Himself says karma is the cause of bhakti. How then can bhakti be "without cause" (ahaitukī)?
- **Answer:** the statement can be explained as karma giving rise to jñāna but **not directly producing bhakti**. This is understood from the word **yadṛcchayā**, meaning "by itself": bhakti produces bhakti, not karma. Pure bhakti is independent and indifferent to other processes.
- If by good fortune it appears in a person, he attains attraction for the Lord's topics. That good fortune comes **only through the association of Vaiṣṇavas** (by sukṛti-bala or kṛpā-bala).
- Explaining the verse in another way would lose the self-manifesting nature of bhakti, so such meanings are rejected. Thus even **niṣkāma-karma is not the cause of bhakti**.

##### Meaning of the verse

- Para-dharma is understood from verse **1.2.6**, and is śuddha-bhakti. All dharmas other than para-dharma, even if perfectly carried out (*anuṣṭhitaḥ*) and even if niṣkāma (no material goals), if they do not produce **rati** for the Lord's topics, are only useless labour.
- The word **yadi** here indicates disgust generated from wasted labour. The lecturer read the Medinī: *yadi* indicates disgust or doubt. His illustrations from the commentary:
  - *yadi gaura nā hoito*: "what if Caitanya Mahāprabhu had not appeared."
  - Doubt where no doubt exists, as in *yadi vedāḥ pramāṇam*: "if the Vedas are proof," which actually means the Vedas are true.
  - In "O protector, place your foot on the head of obstacles," *yadi* expresses **certainty**.
- The lecturer said this is how the commentator (given in the transcript as "shuddha shuddha swami", see Section 6) explains *yadi*. So certainly varṇāśrama-dharma does not give rise to attachment to the Lord's topics.

##### The two meanings

- **Another meaning:** it is said that from varṇāśrama attraction for the Lord's topics sometimes arises. That is true, because one cannot attain the results of dharma without some affection for the Lord's topics (the **meagre element of bhakti**). But that affection is **appearance only, not genuine**. If activities of varṇāśrama, whether *kāmya* or *nitya*, do not produce affection for the Lord's topics, they are a waste of labour, for no results come.
- The lecturer summarised the **two explanations**:
  1. **To an aspirant for Kṛṣṇa-prema:** do not run after varṇāśrama duties, because they will not produce a taste for hearing Kṛṣṇa-kathā, and only by developing that taste can one attain the ultimate perfection. Hence *śrama eva hi kevalam*.
  2. **To one aspiring for the goal of varṇāśrama** (swarga if sakāma, liberation if niṣkāma): ensure that your practice has at least a minimal taste for Kṛṣṇa-kathā, the element of bhakti. Otherwise you will practise everything and get no result.

##### Further commentary (name garbled)

- **Analogy:** for farmers, agriculture must generate affection for the king; otherwise they cannot attain its results. Intelligent people, seeing there will be no results without attraction for the Lord's topics, perform dharma that produces that affection, which means they add the element of bhakti to their practice. The foolish who perform duties without such affection labour for nothing, just as farming is wasted labour if the results cannot be obtained for lack of loyalty to the king. Likewise it is wasted labour if one cannot get the results of swarga and knowledge because of lack of devotion to the Personality of Godhead.
- So even to attain the result of varṇāśrama, the practice should have the element of bhakti. Whether sakāma or niṣkāma karma, if it is *acyuta-bhāva-varjitam* (devoid of the bhāva of bhakti), it does not shine (*na śobhate*) and gives no result.
- **Genuineness:** just as affection for the king arises only because of attachment to agriculture, attraction to the Lord's topics in varṇāśrama arises only because of attachment to varṇāśrama and its material results. This attachment to Kṛṣṇa is a **byproduct of attachment to the goals of varṇāśrama**, so it is not real or genuine.

> [!important]
> **Genuine attraction to Kṛṣṇa** is attraction for establishing a relationship with Kṛṣṇa, not for getting any other result through one's connection with Kṛṣṇa. The lecturer noted that Prahlāda expressed a pure relationship.

#### Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport (the second explanation, per the lecturer)

The lecturer said that Prabhupāda gives the second explanation. In his own words, as read, the purport runs as follows:

- There are different occupational activities according to different conceptions of life. The gross materialist sees nothing beyond the body, so his activities are limited to **concentrated and extended selfishness**: concentrated around the personal body (generally seen among lower animals) and extended in human society. Above them are the **mental speculators** (the lecturer said this describes gradual elevation from karma to jñāna etc.).
- Above body and mind is the dominant **spirit soul**, whose absence makes the whole range of bodily and mental selfishness null and void. Less intelligent people have no information about the soul, and so are not satisfied in performing their occupational duties.
- The self is beyond the gross body and subtle mind. Without knowing the need of the dormant soul, one cannot be happy with the emoluments of body and mind, which are superfluous outer coverings.

> [!example]
> **Cage and bird:** simply by cleansing the cage of the bird one does not satisfy the bird; one must know the needs of the bird himself.

- The soul's need is to get out of the limited sphere of material bondage and fulfil his desire for complete freedom. That freedom is achieved when he meets the **complete spirit**, the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- There is a **dormant affection for God** within everyone. Spiritual existence manifests through the gross body and mind as **perverted affection for gross and subtle matter**. Therefore we must engage in occupational engagements that evoke divine consciousness, which is possible only by hearing and chanting the divine activities of the Lord.
- Any occupational activity which does not help one achieve attachment for hearing and chanting the transcendental message of Godhead is simply a **waste of time**. Other occupational duties, of whatever "ism," cannot give liberation to the soul. Even the activities of the salvationists are useless because of their failure to pick up the fountainhead of all liberties.
- The gross materialist can see that his material gain is limited to time and space, in this world or in another. Even in swarga-loka he finds no permanent abode. The anchoring soul must be satisfied by the perfect scientific process of perfect devotional service.

> [!success]
> The lecturer's summary: (1) without the element of bhakti, the practice of karma, jñāna etc. gives no result; (2) varṇāśrama-dharma itself will not give rise to taste for Kṛṣṇa-kathā, so it is a waste of time to think one can attain Kṛṣṇa-prema through it. Likewise, if one thinks one can attain the result of varṇāśrama (sakāma or niṣkāma) without the element of bhakti, that also is wasted. These are the two shades of meaning given for the verse.

## 4. Closing Q&A

### 4.1 Short questions from the chat

> [!question]
> **If our endeavour is sincere, is it possible in this life itself to go back (to Godhead)?**

- Lecturer: **Yes, it is possible.**

> [!question]
> **Why are First Canto proofs mentioned above? Why exactly the First Canto?**

- Lecturer: Jīva Gosvāmī is going to give proofs from **multiple cantos** to establish the nature of bhakti. He is starting with the First Canto, then the Second Canto, the Third Canto, and so on.

> [!question]
> **What does "dharma" refer to here, since dharma has different meanings?**

- Lecturer: a very good question. Dharma could mean different things; as Prabhupāda often says, it can mean **nature** or **duties**. When it is used in connection with varṇāśrama and such matters, it refers to **duties or activities**, i.e. *prescribed* duties.
- Prescribed duties are always prescribed **according to one's adhikāra**:
  - One with **karma-adhikāra**: varṇāśrama-dharma.
  - One with **bhakti-adhikāra**: śuddha-bhakti, the limbs of pure devotional service.
  - One with **jñāna-adhikāra**: the jñāna-śāstras prescribe the particular activities of jñāna-yoga.
  - One with **yoga-adhikāra**: the yoga-śāstras prescribe yama, niyama, āsana, prāṇāyāma, pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā, dhyāna and samādhi for the aṣṭāṅga-yogīs.
- So dharma is defined according to one's adhikāra.

### 4.2 Without bhakti varṇāśrama gives no result, but the goal of varṇāśrama is to develop taste for Kṛṣṇa-kathā? (Madhurī)

> [!question]
> From the First Canto we understood the goal of varṇāśrama is to develop taste for hearing Vāsudeva-kathā. Here it says without bhakti varṇāśrama gives no result. Can you help me understand? Is the goal of varṇāśrama to develop bhakti?

- Lecturer: **not exactly.** The goal of varṇāśrama is not to develop bhakti, and it does not work directly like that.
- Someone practising varṇāśrama has *karma-śraddhā*, and a part of that karma-śraddhā may contain a **secondary element of bhakti**. But does one practising varṇāśrama with a secondary element of bhakti automatically develop **śuddha-bhakti**? Is the system designed that way? **No.**
- Śuddha-bhakti comes only through the **intervention of pure devotees**, and for a pure devotee to intervene he does not look for the presence of jñāna or karma. The prior existence of jñāna, karma or yoga is not a prerequisite for a devotee's intervention.
- If the system were designed to evolve itself into pure bhakti, separate **sādhyas** (states of perfection) would not be described for sakāma-karma, niṣkāma-karma, jñāna-yoga and so on. The lecturer listed them:
  - The goal of jñāna is liberation; aṣṭāṅga-yoga is also liberation.
  - The perfection of **karma-miśrā bhakti** leads to **sālokya-mukti**.
  - The perfection of **jñāna-miśrā bhakti** and **yoga-miśrā bhakti** leads to **śānta-rati**.

> [!example]
> **Assembly line:** if the system were designed to auto-evolve, one would enter varṇāśrama and at the end of the "assembly line" automatically come out with prema. That does not happen, because the system is not designed that way.

- The system is designed so that, at least until a pure devotee touches one's life, a person can be situated in a life of **piety and goodness**. It maintains him and helps him progress gradually towards **impersonal liberation**.
- If there is additional fortune and bhakti matures through favourable association into **pradhānī-bhūta-bhakti**, he develops a desire for Vaikuṇṭha or a more active relationship with Kṛṣṇa such as śānta-rati, and may attain that.
- If he is extremely fortunate to come in touch with pure devotees and **receive the seed of pure devotion**, he can attain Kṛṣṇa-prema at the maturity of that. Otherwise varṇāśrama itself is not designed to auto-elevate faith to pure devotional faith.

**Follow-up:** the questioner said the verse sounds as if properly performed varṇāśrama should help develop attraction to Bhagavān. The lecturer then read the ācāryas' commentaries (Section 3.3).

**Second follow-up:** if a person follows varṇāśrama and there is a bhakti element in his practice, he gets results according to his adhikāra; when he comes in contact with a devotee, he develops taste for hearing and evolves to prema? Lecturer: if he is fortunate to come in touch with a pure practitioner and his faith gradually transforms into pure devotional faith, it is possible he develops a desire for Kṛṣṇa-prema and attains it.

The questioner then asked whether the statement "without bhakti varṇāśrama gives no result" means that, even if a person has karma or jñāna adhikāra and practises varṇāśrama, he will not get its results without bhakti, and that those results of karma and jñāna are themselves *śrama eva hi kevalam* if no taste for hearing develops. The lecturer answered by reading the commentaries (Section 3.3), concluding with the two explanations. The questioner thanked the lecturer for the detailed explanation.

### 4.3 Without attaining prema, can one not go back to Godhead? (chat; Bhāgavatam 5.5.6)

- The chat question cited **5.5.6** (Ṛṣabhadeva's teachings): when the living entity is covered by ignorance, he does not understand the difference between the individual living being and the Supreme, and his mind is subjugated to fruitive activity; therefore until one has love for Lord Vāsudeva he is not delivered from accepting a material body again and again.
- Lecturer:
  - For those **aspiring after prema**, without attaining prema they cannot attain the spiritual world. They will keep taking birth again and again (facilitated by the Supreme Personality of Godhead) and will not gain entry into the spiritual realm until they reach the stage of prema.
  - For those **not aspiring after prema**, who practise only **karma-miśrā bhakti** or **jñāna-miśrā bhakti**, they may never attain prema but may still attain the spiritual world (sālokya, sārṣṭi etc.). This is attained by personalities who are not necessarily aspiring after prema.

### 4.4 Can reading scriptures give sukṛti without association of devotees? (extended discussion with Rādhā Prabhu, as transcribed)

This was the longest discussion. The questioner argued repeatedly for the possibility that scripture alone can give sukṛti; the lecturer's responses are grouped by theme.

> [!question]
> Following varṇāśrama, a person is told to read scriptures. Hearing Prahlāda's devotion, he gets association. Can his heart change and can he take to bhakti?

- Lecturer: **yes**, provided the personality from whom he is hearing has **pure faith**. Such a person, narrating Prahlāda's story, would emphasise the proper devotional conclusions.
- If the person reads directly (the questioner emphasised he gets Vyāsadeva's association), he can become a devotee, but the **Bhāgavatam also acts as Mohinī-mūrti**, keeping away those not yet properly qualified. If someone lacks pure devotional faith, the Bhāgavatam allows them to understand alternative meanings from the same descriptions.
- Lecturer referred back to the end of the first Anuccheda: some Jīvas, through accumulated impressions (*saṁskāra*, i.e. ajñāta-sukṛti) or great mercy (**kṛpā-siddhas**), become favourable and gain understanding at the very instant of hearing.

> [!important]
> Even to access the Bhakti Śāstras and understand their proper meaning, the recipient needs **prior sukṛti**. The scriptures in themselves may be sufficient, but to get their proper understanding one must be nourished by proper **bhakti-unmukhī sukṛti**. When that is present, one reading the Bhakti Śāstras comes to the proper conclusions.

> [!question]
> Why does reading Vyāsadeva's Bhāgavatam not give bhakti-unmukhī sukṛti? Is it not hearing from Vyāsadeva?

- Lecturer: the Bhāgavatam is **Kṛṣṇa-svarūpa** (Bhagavat-svarūpa), just like the holy name. If one chants the holy name with ulterior motives, one does not necessarily get bhakti-unmukhī sukṛti. Similarly, directly approaching the Bhāgavatam or the holy name does not automatically give rise to sukṛti. It becomes possible when, through the **intervention of a devotee**, one develops pure devotional faith.
- On Vyāsadeva: the words are **eternal**; Vyāsadeva is a compiler. Vyāsadeva is Bhagavān; he is not even a śaktyāveśa avatāra but directly **Viṣṇu-tattva** (as the lecturer put it). Coming in touch with Vyāsadeva's words is coming in touch with the words of Viṣṇu-tattva, not just a devotee. When we read Prabhupāda's Bhāgavatam, we come in touch with the words of a **devotee of Kṛṣṇa**, which has a totally different impact. It has to be explained, in person, by a devotee.

> [!question]
> Hearing Brahmā's prayers from Brahmā or reading what he wrote should be the same. And people read Prabhupāda's books and immediately get sukṛti and faith. Why not Vyāsadeva's or Brahmā's written words?

- Lecturer: yes, it should be the same benefit, but **to assimilate it, the ears and heart need to have been processed by association** of devotees. Someone in the chat noted that *praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā* is missing.
- Even though they are Brahmā's words, they are part of the Bhagavat-svarūpa, eternal, coming in the form of Brahmā's words. As seen in the beginning of the First Canto, the Bhāgavatam also acts as Mohinī-mūrti, and the Lord plays that role: when unqualified people approach, it does not necessarily reveal itself, because the words must be understood with devotional perception, which one gets only through the association of devotees.

> [!question]
> What about the purports given by Nārada, or Jaḍa Bharata's explanation to Rahūgaṇa?

- Lecturer: they are eternal words, but it is not mere conversation. There is the **personal presence** of the speaker. A proper teacher, while communicating to his student, observes bodily gestures and facial expressions to see whether the message is getting across, and if not, explains through other means. It is a **customised presentation** for that person, at that time, place and circumstance, by a speaker who knows the listener's qualification.
- Vyāsadeva and Śukadeva Gosvāmī, in compiling these stories, compiled only those appropriate for creating a particular impact. If the recipient lacks proper qualification, hearing the story will not give the essential effect.
- Otherwise **commentaries would be redundant**. Commentaries are needed because without the association of Vaiṣṇavas it is not possible to understand the scriptures.
- Lecturer also observed: if anyone can pick up the book and benefit regardless of prior varṇāśrama practice, then prior practice of varṇāśrama does not really give a serious edge. The questioner replied that a varṇāśrama practitioner has faith in scriptures and is told to read them, so he gets Vyāsa's, Nārada's, Prahlāda's and Jaḍa Bharata's association. Lecturer: attraction to Bhakti Śāstras will not generally happen without sufficient (sukṛti); **by reading the Bhāgavatam alone one will not get sukṛti** unless he has the opportunity to come in touch with Vaiṣṇavas.

> [!question]
> Can someone not get sukṛti from Caitanya-caritāmṛta, or from Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura's or Kuntī's prayers, which express emotions?

- Lecturer: a valid point. The Bhāgavatam is definitely Bhāgavata-svarūpa; Caitanya-caritāmṛta's status is not being debated either, and through it one can get the association of Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja. But this seems to make **live association of devotees redundant**.
- There is a chance that readers approach those verses in their own way; there is also a chance of **duṣkṛti** through this medium, since one may misunderstand or lack the faith to process this direct knowledge correctly. Sukṛti is a **theoretical possibility**, though it may not necessarily happen.
- Why would someone appreciate a devotional prayer if he has no sukṛti? Lecturer: a completely devotional prayer requires some devotional faith to process. Those accustomed to hearing "what will give me swarga, material benefits, liberation" suddenly hear a prayer saying "I don't want any material benefits or liberation, I just want Your lotus feet." Why would that even be attractive?
- On Prabhupāda's commentaries: explanations appealing to a particular class of people may create sukṛti, but may not appeal to everyone.
- The questioner raised Prabhupāda's statement "I made you qualified" and cases of people (e.g. hippies) with no sukṛti being attracted; the lecturer replied that we cannot say whether they had sukṛti, since **there is no way to measure sukṛti**. Similarly, if Prabhupāda met someone and the person left, how does one know whether he assimilated sukṛti? One cannot measure it. The lecturer agreed that Prabhupāda gives sukṛti to those who read and appreciate. The question is why one would appreciate if one has no sukṛti.
- Lecturer on the position that scripture is sufficient: if so, since practically all scriptures are words spoken by great sādhus, the association of scriptural sādhus would suffice and there would be **no need for any live sādhu**.
- The questioner's position: proper books in remote places can give sukṛti, but to make progress one then needs devotee association. Lecturer: even among many who got the books, only some got attracted; whether sukṛti was created in those who were not attracted, we cannot know.
- Lecturer cited what the ācāryas say: those who already have **sukṛti-bala**, when they come in touch with the books, at the very instant of hearing become favourable to the Lord and gain understanding. Becoming favourable depends on some Jīvas having accumulated impressions over many births.

> [!question]
> Does appreciation of Prahlāda, Vyāsadeva, or even Prabhupāda ("such a nice person, he worked hard") give sukṛti, even without faith?

- Lecturer: one who appreciates Prahlāda as a great devotee of Nṛsiṁha after hearing his prayers already has faith. The questioner stressed that he meant sukṛti, not faith.

> [!important]
> **What ajñāta-sukṛti is.** Sukṛti means appreciating something connected with Kṛṣṇa **not for its transcendental nature but for some material aspect**: someone hears a kīrtana or bhajana and appreciates it for its musical ability, or gets prasādam and appreciates how nicely it is cooked. This is why it is called **ajñāta** (unknown): one does not know that it has transcendental potency or will give a transcendental benefit. If someone appreciates it *for* its transcendental nature, they must already have sukṛti.

- Lecturer agreed it is **possible** that appreciating Prahlāda, Vyāsadeva or Jaḍa Bharata as "nice" or "hard-working" gives sukṛti. But to **continue reading** the Bhāgavatam, interest comes only with devotional faith, because attraction to literature is according to one's **śraddhā**.
- The questioner noted Māyāvādīs and outsiders read the Bhāgavatam and appreciate Prahlāda. Lecturer: that appreciation has Māyāvādī overtones and they assimilate offences or twisted understanding; hearing from a Māyāvādī may lead to **duṣkṛti** rather than sukṛti ("that has to be seen").
- On the Rāmāyaṇa serial: some sukṛti may be there, but if depicted in a way that leads to unfavourable emotions towards the personalities, it may lead to **accumulation of duṣkṛti**. There is scope for sukṛti, but assimilation of sukṛti also depends on the **purity of the person presenting it** (e.g. the actors).

> [!warning]
> **Material motive blocks ajñāta-sukṛti.** The lecturer's example: someone reads the phala-śruti of the Dhruva Mahārāja episode, where material benefits are mentioned, and reads the story to get the promised material benefit. Because he has a material motive, it does not generate ajñāta-sukṛti. For ajñāta-sukṛti to arise, there must be no thought of personal benefit, material or liberation. The moment one thinks "I will get benefit or liberation by hearing this pastime," it becomes **pratibimba-bhakti-ābhāsa** (reflection / semblance of bhakti), which gives only a secondary benefit and not ajñāta-sukṛti.

- The questioner concluded that this explains why varṇāśrama practitioners do not get sukṛti from reading the Bhāgavatam: even in "niṣkāma" stage they desire liberation. Lecturer: **according to our definition, desire for liberation means it is not niṣkāma**; they may call it niṣkāma-karma-yoga, but it is not so for us. With such a motive the reading becomes pratibimba-bhakti. The lecturer said: "yes, this is clear. Very good."

Lecturer: **yes, they definitely get sukṛti just by touching the books**, but the interest to read the book will come provided they already have some faith. ### 4.6 Offensive mentality, chance association and the need for devotees (Ādi-bhagavān Prabhu)

- Ādi-bhagavān Prabhu's point: the **offensive mentality** does not go away without the experience of association with devotees. If unfavourability remained, then by chanting one holy name or reading the Bhāgavatam they would immediately attain the goal. Lecturer: that is why people who are not nourished by sukṛti would not even be attracted to read and appreciate the book.
- Such people may hear by chance (e.g. Prahlāda's story), without having sought it. Lecturer: yes, some part or aspect heard by chance and some material appreciation.
- The offensive mentality can go away only with **favourable association of sādhus**, since sādhu-saṅga is told to be **bhakti-mūla**. Ādi-bhagavān said this is a different, long discussion and that he had a quote to share. The lecturer asked him to share the essence and said they could build on it.
- He read a question to Bhaktisiddhānta (as presented): if Jesus was a jagad-guru and his instructions can deliver us, why do we need an initiating spiritual master? The conclusive part of the answer: in any shāstra or book, even in the material world, it is not possible to read by oneself and understand the real fact. If the instructions given 2,000 years ago do not come through **reciprocal succession**, and we have to sort them out from books, we may create a blunder and accept a perversion of the truth taught in his name, and may even accept something opposite to what he taught.
- His point: offensive mentality can lead to **perversion of sukṛti**: it may give not favourable sukṛti but **duṣkṛti**. So the substrate one has when coming across a book by chance is very important.
- Lecturer: in theory it is not impossible, but **generally it is rare**. About 99 percent of the time, if someone consciously picks up the Bhāgavatam and reads it, there is either **pūrva-sukṛti**, or chance (e.g. he liked the cover), or a **material motive** (someone told him that reading a particular pastime will give a child). In the last case one does not get sukṛti, because of **anya-abhilāṣa**.

## 5. Key Takeaways

- Anuccheda 4 shows that **bhakti is independent** and that **jñāna and vairāgya are dependent** on it. Jñāna and vairāgya are produced by bhakti, and jñāna needs bhakti to give its result; bhakti does not need jñāna.
- *Jñānaṁ ca yad ahaitukam* is knowledge needed exclusively for a relationship with Kṛṣṇa, not knowledge that treats Kṛṣṇa as a supplier of wants.
- Dharma that does not produce **taste for hearing the topics of Kṛṣṇa** (rati for viṣvaksena-kathā) is only wasted effort (*śrama eva hi kevalam*). *Eva* shows temporariness of results; *hi* shows scriptural proof; *kevalam* shows that even detachment, liberation and siddhis are not the goal.
- Practices without bhakti are like **beating empty husk**; non-devotees who strive for liberation only think themselves liberated and fall down because they disregard the Lord's lotus feet.
- *Śrama eva hi kevalam* has two meanings: (1) to an aspirant for prema, varṇāśrama will not produce the taste for Kṛṣṇa-kathā; (2) to an aspirant for varṇāśrama's results, those results too are not attained without the element of bhakti.
- Attachment to Kṛṣṇa that is a byproduct of attachment to other goals is not genuine; **genuine attraction** is for a relationship with Kṛṣṇa itself.
- **Bhakti arises *yadṛcchayā* (by itself, by fortune)**, not from karma. Even niṣkāma-karma is not the cause of bhakti. The fortune comes through the association of Vaiṣṇavas.
- Varṇāśrama is not designed to evolve into pure bhakti: its perfections are separate sādhyas (sālokya, śānta-rati etc.). Pure bhakti comes only through the **intervention of pure devotees**.
- Dharma is **prescribed according to one's adhikāra**.
- Those aspiring for prema cannot enter the spiritual world before attaining prema; those practising karma-miśrā or jñāna-miśrā bhakti may attain it without prema.
- To understand the Bhakti Śāstras one needs **prior bhakti-unmukhī sukṛti** and devotee association; the Bhāgavatam acts as Mohinī-mūrti for the unqualified. Reading alone does not generally give sukṛti, and may give duṣkṛti.
- **Ajñāta-sukṛti** comes from appreciating something connected to Kṛṣṇa without knowing its transcendental potency; a material motive or desire for liberation turns the practice into **pratibimba-bhakti-ābhāsa**, which does not give ajñāta-sukṛti.
- Sukṛti matures into śraddhā; sukṛti cannot be measured; and the theoretical possibility of sukṛti from scripture or chance contact exists but is generally rare.

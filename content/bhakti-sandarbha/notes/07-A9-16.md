# Bhakti Sandarbha: Anucchedas 9 Onwards and the Fourteen Stages of Bhakti (Satāṁ Kṛpā to Harer Mādhurya-anubhava)

## 1. Recap of Previous Lecture

### Anuccheda 1: The root cause of the jīva's suffering
- The root cause of the jīva's suffering is his **anādi-bahirmukha** nature: a beginningless turning of the face away from Kṛṣṇa. As seen earlier, knowledge of Kṛṣṇa in the conditioned state is in **prāg-abhāva**.
- The jīva can overcome this by becoming **Kṛṣṇa-unmukha**, and this is possible only by the practice of bhakti.
- For the benefit of the jīvas, the Lord teaches scripture "filled with the highest mercy". The scriptures can be accepted only if there is **sukṛti-bala**.
  - Some jīvas, through impressions accumulated over many births, or through great mercy, become favourable and gain understanding of the Lord at the very instant of hearing teachings about Him.
  - The śāstras alone are not sufficient to benefit a person. The **intervention of a devotee** is needed, through which one gets that sukṛti. Endowed with sukṛti-bala, such a person takes shelter of the scriptures and gets their proper benefit.
  - When such persons develop faith in the bhakti-śāstras, they give no regard to other śāstras. Even if they hear other śāstras by chance, those act as a stimulus for hearing the Lord's pastimes. The example given is Prahlāda Mahārāja.
- Although the first few Sandarbhas mention only sambandha, abhidheya (śuddha-bhakti) and prayojana (prema) are already clear in them. To give clarity and prevent misunderstanding, and to **prevent negligence in the attainment**, the teachings are presented again more specifically in the fifth and sixth Sandarbhas.
- *māmeva ye prapadyante māyām etāṁ taranti te*. By taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa through bhakti (*ekayā bhaktyā*), the jīva becomes Kṛṣṇa-unmukha and māyā gives up her hold.
- **Essence of the first Anuccheda:** the root cause of the jīva's suffering is Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukha nature, and it is overcome by becoming Kṛṣṇa-unmukha through the practice of bhakti.

### Anuccheda 2: Proof that bhakti addresses the bahirmukha nature
- Verse recalled: *evaṁ sva-citte sva-ta eva siddha ātma-priyo 'rtho bhagavān anantaḥ taṁ nirvṛto niyatārtho bhajeta saṁsāra-hetūparamaś ca yatra*. This is from the Second Canto.
- If one performs bhajana of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, *saṁsāra-hetu-uparamaḥ* is accomplished: the root of saṁsāra, the bahirmukha nature, is uprooted.

### Anuccheda 3: The nature of pure bhakti
- A series of verses from the First Canto, second chapter, is quoted to establish the nature of pure devotional service. The lecturer asks: what is this *ekayā bhakti*, this *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyaṁ jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛtam ānukūlyena kṛṣṇānuśīlanaṁ bhaktiḥ*?
- **Objection:** you keep saying bhakti, but jñāna and karma can also support bhakti. Why not give them regard and emphasise only exclusive bhakti?
- **Response:** *atho vai kavayo nityaṁ bhaktiṁ paramayā mudā vāsudeve bhagavati kurvanti ātma-prasādanīm*. "Atho" (therefore) means the reasons were already presented earlier. Intelligent people regularly perform bhakti with great joy to Vāsudeva, and this gives great joy to the mind.
- *sa vai puṁsāṁ paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhokṣaje ahaituky apratihatā yayātmā suprasīdati* (1.2.6, as spoken).
  - The supreme dharma of all human beings is that by which prema-bhakti to the Lord arises.
  - This bhakti is **ahaituki and apratihatā**: *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam, jñāna-karma-dhyānāvṛtam*. This is the **parodharma**.
  - Śaunakādi ṛṣis had asked for the final essence of all scriptures, and this verse was spoken in response. By this, one's heart becomes completely satisfied, because only from this dharma does taste for hearing the Lord's topics arise.
- **Objection:** Isn't varṇāśrama-dharma the topmost dharma, since "dharma" generally refers to it? **Response:** no. *dharmaḥ svanuṣṭhitaḥ puṁsāṁ viṣvaksena-kathāsu yaḥ notpādayed yadi ratiṁ śrama eva hi kevalam*. Varṇāśrama-dharma does not develop taste for *viṣvaksena-kathā*, so it is only *śrama* (wasted effort) and not the proper dharma.
- Other practices: *naiṣkarmyam apy acyuta-bhāva-varjitam na śobhate jñānam alaṁ nirañjanam kutaḥ punaḥ śaśvad abhadram īśvare na cārpitaṁ karma yad apy akāraṇam*. Nishkāma-karma, sakāma-karma and jñāna attain no success without the element of bhakti.
- **Conclusion:** bhakti does not need the support of karma, jñāna and yoga; karma, jñāna, yoga and so on need the support of bhakti.
- *ataḥ pumbhir dvija-śreṣṭhā varṇāśrama-vibhāgaśaḥ svanuṣṭhitasya dharmasya saṁsiddhir hari-toṣaṇam*. Bhakti is distinct from the dharma described in the text.
- The **svarūpa-lakṣaṇa** (primary qualities) of bhakti are ahaituki and apratihatā: it must be *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam* and *jñāna-karma-ādy-anāvṛtam*.
- When taste for bhakti develops, bhakti-yoga characterised by hearing and chanting begins. *bhaktyā sañjātayā bhaktyā bibhraty utpulakāṁ tanum*: **only by bhakti does one get bhakti**, not by karma, jñāna or yoga.

### Anuccheda 4: Bhakti is independent; the other parts depend on it
- One need not practise anything else to develop jñāna, vairāgya and other qualities. By bhakti to Vāsudeva, **jñāna and vairāgya manifest automatically as a by-product**.
- Other practices without the element of bhakti give no result: not svarga, not siddhis, not liberation. This was proved by quoting a different meaning of the same verse (*śrama eva hi kevalam*).
- Further proofs of the dependence of other processes on bhakti:
  - *teṣām asau kleśala eva śiṣyate nānyad yathā sthūla-tuṣāvaghātinām*. Practising jñāna without the element of bhakti is like beating empty husks.
  - *ye 'nye 'ravindākṣa vimukta-māninas tvayy asta-bhāvād aviśuddha-buddhayaḥ āruhya kṛcchreṇa paraṁ padaṁ tataḥ patanty adho 'nādṛta-yuṣmad-aṅghrayaḥ*. A person on the path of jñāna who eventually gives up bhakti falls down from his position as a *jīvanmukta* and never attains liberation.
- **Worldly dharma-artha-kāma cycle:** in the world, people perform dharma for artha, use artha for kāma, and then perform dharma again for more artha. Because of enjoyment (*bhogena puṇyam*), punya gets dissolved, so they keep performing dharma to get artha for kāma.
- *dharmasya hy apavargyasya nārtho 'rthāyopakalpate nārthasya dharmaikāntasya kāmo lābhāya hi smṛtaḥ*:
  - A person dedicated to apavargya-dharma (liberation or prema) should not use dharma for artha and kāma.
  - The bhakti-yogī should perform **parodharma bhakti for the purpose of prema**; the jñāna-yogī should perform dharma for the higher cause of liberation.
  - A person dedicated to the **ekānta dharma of bhakti** should not perform varṇāśrama duties. He should not even perform bhakti for the purpose of artha, kāma and so on.
- For one who desires apavarga, sense pleasure from sense objects is not the goal. The goal is **tattva-jijñāsā**. What is accomplished by prescribed varṇāśrama duties should not be the goal.

### Anucchedas 5–8 (as recapped)
- **Tattva:** the truth to be inquired into is understood in three stages as **Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān**: the jñānīs understand Him as Brahman, the yogīs as Paramātmā, and the devotees as Bhagavān. The tattva is the Supreme Lord realised in three stages.
  - **Bhagavān** is the form that manifests all śaktis completely. The superiority of Bhagavān over the Brahman and Paramātmā features was established in the previous three Sandarbhas (the lecturer names Bhagavat, Kṛṣṇa and Paramātma Sandarbhas).
  - This entity with three types of manifestation is produced directly by bhakti.
- **How tattva-jijñāsā is accomplished:** by rendering devotional service after hearing from the guru. The seriously inquisitive student or sage realises the absolute truth by hearing from the guru and rendering devotional service on that basis.
  - This bhakti is served by jñāna and vairāgya, which arise from practising it.
  - *śruta-gṛhītayā*, *munayaḥ* and *śraddhadhānāḥ* show that bhakti is very hard to attain. By hearing the conclusions of all scriptures from an authorised guru one attains it. Hearing from the guru is thus the most necessary practice for the individual. (This was verse 1.2.12.)
- **Pleasing the Lord is the highest result of dharma.** The perfection of dharma undertaken faultlessly with great effort is ultimately to please the Lord. Using dharma to gain svarga is not suitable. The dharma by which Kṛṣṇa is really pleased is bhakti.
- The conclusion recapped: if the real purpose of dharma (pleasing the Lord) is to develop *vāsudeva-kathā-ruci*, taste for hearing, then **bhakti alone consisting of hearing and chanting should be performed, and nothing else**.
- The lecturer says the previous discussion covered up to the eighth Anuccheda, with the ninth to be started in this session.

### Recap of the verses 1.2.6, 1.2.8–1.2.13 (second recap before Anuccheda 9)
- The series of verses from the First Canto, second chapter ("Divinity and Divine Service") is explaining the nature of pure devotional service and of **parodharma**, starting with 1.2.6 (*sa vai puṁsāṁ paro dharmaḥ…*).
- In the eighth verse (*dharmaḥ svanuṣṭhitaḥ puṁsām…*) the practice of dharma should develop *viṣvaksena-kathā-rati*, taste for hearing the topics of Kṛṣṇa.
- The question of why only bhakti and not karma is emphasised was addressed.
- Verses 9 and 10 (*dharmasya hy apavargyasya…*): even though one may get secondary results from performing dharma including bhakti, one should not be distracted by them. This **elaborates the ahaituki nature of bhakti**.
- The goal of life is *jīvasya tattva-jijñāsā*. The tattva is *brahmeti paramātmeti bhagavān iti śabdyate*. The means of realising it is *bhaktyā śruta-gṛhītayā*, by devotional service after hearing from the guru.
- The complete perfection of dharma is pleasing the Lord. The conclusion again: bhakti alone, consisting of hearing and chanting, must be performed, and nothing else.

## 3. Main Lecture Notes

### 3.1 Anuccheda 9 (1.2.14): Mind dedicated only to bhakti

> *tasmād ekena manasā bhagavān sātvatāṁ patiḥ śrotavyaḥ kīrtitavyaś ca dhyeyaḥ pūjyaś ca nityadā* (as referred to by the lecturer; the verse text is not spoken in full)

- **Since** it has been established that other goals and means are not the purpose, *tasmāt* ("therefore") leads to the instruction. **Ekena manasā** means the mind should not be distracted by any other goal or by any other means to attain that goal.
- With the mind dedicated only to bhakti, devoid of karma and jñāna, one should **constantly hear about, glorify and meditate upon** the Supreme Lord, the master of the devotees. This means a mind devoid of enthusiasm for karma and other processes. Hearing and chanting means hearing and chanting the Lord's name and qualities.
- **Conclusion:** bhakti characterised by hearing and chanting should be performed without the covering of karma and jñāna. This is **parodharma**, as established here.
- The lecturer says that in 1.2.14 a glorification of hearing is given in order to **develop a taste for hearing the Lord's topics**. This is the first step, with **indifference to karma and the other processes, which are difficult**. It is described to show the **easy stages of bhakti up to the final stage, prema**. Bhakti unobstructed by jñāna and karma (*jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛta*) is not difficult to perform; actually it is very easy.

### 3.2 Anuccheda 10 (1.2.15): The power of remembering Kṛṣṇa

> *yad-anudhyāsinā yuktāḥ karma-granthi-nibandhanam chindanti kovidās tasya ko na kuryāt kathā-ratim* (as spoken, in garbled form)

- **Who is the intelligent person (*kovida*) who would not develop taste for hearing Kṛṣṇa's topics?** By the **sword of remembrance** of that personality, one can cut **the knots of karma** (*karma-granthi-nibandhanam*).
- The lecturer's summary: with a mind dedicated only to bhakti, without the covering of karma and jñāna, one should constantly hear about, glorify and remember the Supreme Personality of Godhead. The result is that persons with discrimination and controlled minds cut the knot of karma and **ahaṅkāra** that binds one to various bodies. Who would not develop attraction for the topics of the Lord, which deliver one from the greatest suffering?

> [!info]
> **Who is a *kovida*?** The lecturer says a *kovida* is one who possesses discrimination. How does he possess it? Because he has been **fortunate to come in touch with devotees and receive the seed of devotion**. That fortune is what makes him a kovida. Those who are unfortunate cannot develop taste for the Lord's topics.

### 3.3 Anuccheda 10–11: The easy method up to niṣṭhā: five verses

The lecturer says five verses explain **an easy method to teach bhakti up to the stage of niṣṭhā after starting the process**.

#### Verse (1.2.16): *śuśrūṣoḥ śraddadhānasya vāsudeva-kathā-ruciḥ syān mahat-sevayā viprāḥ puṇya-tīrtha-niṣevaṇāt*
Sūta Gosvāmī addresses the Śaunakādi ṛṣis, who are all brāhmaṇas (*viprāḥ*). Attraction for topics concerning Kṛṣṇa (*vāsudeva-kathā-ruci*) arises through **service to great devotees (mahat-sevā)**.

The lecturer unpacks the sequence in this verse:
1. Before *mahat-sevā* comes **satāṁ kṛpā**: a devotee becomes favourably and compassionately disposed towards a person and engages him in service.
2. When he is engaged in that service (**mahat-sevā**), it leads to **vāsudeva-kathā-ruci**.
3. Following *vāsudeva-kathā-ruci* is **śraddhā**.
4. Following śraddhā is **sādhu-saṅga**, characterised by **surrender to the guru**.
5. After surrender to the guru there is **śuśrūṣā**, the desire to hear. This is called **bhajaneṣu spṛhā**, the desire to perform bhajana. It comes before bhajana-kriyā.

> [!abstract]
> **The first part of the sequence as stated by the lecturer:** satāṁ kṛpā → mahat-sevā → vāsudeva-kathā-ruci → śraddhā (inspiration to surrender to a guru) → sādhu-saṅga → bhajaneṣu spṛhā (conscious desire to hear and then to practise).

> [!example]
> **Mahat-sevā in the form of unknowing service.** If one goes to a holy place for other purposes, one may meet devotees who wander to or live in holy tīrthas, and may perform service in the form of seeing, touching and conversing with them.
> - This is all **ajñāta-sukṛti**: the person has no idea that the other is a devotee. The lecturer imagines someone thinking "maybe he could tell us about this tīrtha."
> - Similarly, one may go to a holy tīrtha to give charity and end up giving charity to bona fide devotees. In this way some mahat-sevā is unknowingly accomplished.
> - By conversing with them and seeing and touching them, one develops faith in worshipping the Lord. This happens **prior to developing devotional credits**.

- *vāsudeva-kathā-ruci* here means a **generic favourable disposition towards bhakti**, which leads to śraddhā. Then one develops the desire to hear what the devotees are discussing when they speak spontaneously about the Lord. This is **bhajaneṣu spṛhā**.
- *bhajaneṣu spṛhā* comes within the heart of those who come in the association of Vaiṣṇavas and **consciously take shelter of that association**. They naturally want to hear what the Vaiṣṇavas speak. It is a desire for conscious performance of devotional service.

#### Verse (10th Canto, ch. 87, prayers of the personified Vedas)
*bhuvi puru-puṇya-tīrtha-sadanāny ṛṣayo vimadās ta uta bhavat-padāmbuja-hṛdo 'gha-bhid-aṅghri-jalāḥ dadhati sakṛn manas tvayi ya ātmani nitya-sukhe na punar upāsate puruṣa-sāra-harāvasathān* (as spoken, garbled).
- As rendered by the lecturer: sages free from false pride live on this earth by frequenting sacred pilgrimage places, because such devotees keep the Lord's lotus feet within their hearts. The water that washes their feet destroys all sins. Having turned their minds towards the Lord, the ever-blissful soul of all existence, they no longer dedicate themselves to family life at home, which robs a man of his good qualities.
- The verse is presented to indicate the importance of **sādhu-saṅga / mahat-saṅga**. Generally, taste develops by association with great devotees (**mahat-saṅga, satāṁ kṛpā, mahat-sevā**). Serving holy tīrthas allows one to serve great devotees, and by serving great devotees taste for the Lord's topics develops.

#### Verse (1.2.17): *satāṁ prasaṅgān mama vīrya-saṁvido bhavanti hṛt-karṇa-rasāyanāḥ kathāḥ taj-joṣaṇād āśv apavarga-vartmani śraddhā ratir bhaktir anukramiṣyati*
- Hearing from great devotees has an **immediate effect**.
- *satāṁ prasaṅgāt*: from association with such great devotees one starts realising the glorious pastimes of the Supreme Lord (*mama-vīrya-saṁvidaḥ*) and reaches the stage of niṣṭhā.
- *taj-joṣaṇād āśv apavarga-vartmani śraddhā ratir bhaktir anukramiṣyati*: after niṣṭhā one passes through the stages of ruci, āsakti, bhāva and prema. All of this is possible only through association with these devotees.

> [!success]
> The lecturer's summary of 1.2.16: it describes how one develops *vāsudeva-kathā-ruci* (by mahat-sevā). After this generic favourableness comes śraddhā. Śraddhā leads to surrender to a guru (sādhu-saṅga). That leads to *bhajaneṣu spṛhā*, the desire to perform the other limbs of devotional service such as śravaṇam and kīrtanam.

### 3.4 Mid-lecture Q&A (during Anuccheda 10–11)

#### Q: What is the difference between satāṁ kṛpā and mahat-sevā? (chat box)
- **Satāṁ kṛpā** is the source: a devotee becomes compassionately disposed towards another living entity. This compassion is an **emotion or feeling**.
- It takes a more active form as **mahat-sevā**, when the devotee somehow engages those living beings in some form of service connected with Kṛṣṇa.
- *Mahat-sevā* is when the emotion takes a practical, tangible shape in activities. This engagement then leads to a **generic favourableness** towards devotional service.

#### Q: Can you give an example of satāṁ kṛpā? (chat box)
> [!example]
> A devotee desires that **blind children** get an opportunity to practise bhakti. They look for shelter for the children; if devotees go and give that shelter, there is a good chance the children may eventually take up bhakti. **That desire is satāṁ kṛpā.** Activities such as publishing books in braille so the children can read them, giving them personal time and making them feel valued are **mahat-sevā**. One gives them association with Vaiṣṇavas and with objects connected with Kṛṣṇa.
> - Shyām Mukunda Prabhu's comment, as quoted: "it takes a whole village to raise a child"; it takes many Vaiṣṇavas to give many installments of bhāgyodaya-sukṛti. The lecturer agrees: "that is true."

#### Vrajasundarī (as spoken "Vrachisundari") / a participant: Why does the commentary say "generally" the taste for hearing develops by association with great devotees?
The participant asked to see the slide before Anuccheda 11 (the one beginning "generally the taste for hearing develops…" and the line "but those who are unfortunate cannot develop taste for the Lord's topics").

**The lecturer's answer:**
- The word **"generally"** indicates a **generic rule**. The generic rule Kṛṣṇa has created is that one gets bhakti, or taste for bhakti, through the agency of Vaiṣṇavas.
- It also indicates that there could be exceptions: **direct intervention of Kṛṣṇa**, sometimes through some extraordinary kṛpā. Exceptions to rules always exist in bhakti also.
- The participant linked this to the previous discussion of sukṛti through scripture and association. The lecturer says he did not deny that sukṛti can be gained directly through the Bhāgavatam, but that this **does not constitute an exception**; it falls within the rule.

> [!important]
> **Why the Bhāgavatam case is not an exception.** One receives the Bhāgavatam only because compassionate Vaiṣṇavas compiled it, printed it, bound it, painted the pictures in it and handed it over to that person. It was all an act of compassion. When a person appreciates these things, which are manifestations of the compassion of great Vaiṣṇavas, he is **indirectly engaged in mahat-sevā**.
> - Appreciating the binding, the paper quality or the paintings is indirectly appreciating the Vaiṣṇavas. This is all mahat-sevā, and by this mahat-sevā one gets *vāsudeva-kathā-ruci*.
> - When the participant said it is sometimes very indirect, because no devotee gave the book directly and someone just finds it somewhere, the lecturer replied that the very fact it is available is because some compassionate Vaiṣṇava wrote, printed and bound it. So *satāṁ kṛpā* and *mahat-sevā* are present there too.

#### Q (Madhumatī Priyasakhī): Niṣṭhā seems to take forever from anartha-nivṛtti. What should we do to get there?
- By **sincerely practising**. We do not know the burden of anarthas we are carrying from past lives and practices, so it may take time.
- What is in our hands is to **practise diligently, with sincerity, in the association of sincere devotees**. With good association, one can progress very quickly and anartha-nivṛtti becomes faster.
- **Follow-up:** What if there are not many opportunities to personally serve pure devotees (mahat-sevā)?
  - Service can be done even in the devotee's physical absence: one can **serve their vāṇī** and try to follow their instructions diligently, even the generic instructions for all disciples, with various grades of sincerity. This is also seva, and **serving the mahat's instructions is definitely mahat-sevā**.
  - It is not only an opportunity to cook for them or clean their clothes. More important is how, day to day, we diligently try to live according to their guidelines and instructions.
  - **Is the present discussion following the instruction?** Yes, because this is one of the instructions of the guru.

> [!example]
> The lecturer recounts that one of the spiritual masters in "our movement" was asked by a sincere disciple: a day may come when we do not have your physical association, so what one thing do you want your disciples to hold on to? Without missing a beat, he began to speak *mac-citta mad-gata-prāṇā bodhayantaḥ parasparam kathayantaś ca māṁ nityaṁ tuṣyanti ca ramanti ca*: come together, discuss Kṛṣṇa-kathā and relish that association.
> - One need not wait for the guru's departure. Even while the guru is alive, he may be in another part of the world, and the only access is through his vāṇī, so the instruction holds good.
> - The lecturer adds that Śrīla Prabhupāda has given many other instructions, such as cooperating with godbrothers ("you show my love to me by how you cooperate"). The more diligently we follow these, the more we serve the great souls.

#### Q (Kalpataru, as spoken "Kalpataru Janavam"): Dealing with non-devotees who "run over us"
**Question:** Among devotees we are careful about offences and soft by character. Among materialistic people we have the same attitude, and they just run over us. How should we deal with them when they are offensive towards us?

**Answer:** As far as possible, our reaction should be such that **we do not allow them to exploit us**. At the same time, if by tolerating we can make them (or the people observing) a little more favourable, we can try to be favourably disposed. If that becomes too difficult, we do not have to. **Without stretching too much**, if one can tolerate, one can do so.

#### Q (a participant): Why is śraddhā only the fourth stage, if we already have śraddhā while doing satāṁ kṛpā and mahat-sevā?
- The lecturer clarifies the sequence is **from the standpoint of the recipient** of the mercy and service, not of the performer.
  - **Satāṁ kṛpā** is felt by the devotee who is trying to distribute bhakti, and he engages the recipient in mahat-sevā.
  - **Śraddhā** is experienced by the recipient of that kṛpā and sevā.
- In the first two stages there is **no conscious acceptance of bhakti**. There may be some conscious appreciation of peripheral aspects of the devotee, but conscious practice of bhakti starts later.
- The recipient may develop appreciation for the devotee not because he is a devotee but because he is a nice or kind person. In the recipient's performance of seva he is **unconscious of the transcendental nature** of the person or activity. He may see it as an act of compassion, but not as a *transcendental* act of compassion; he may engage in seva, but not see it as mahat-sevā.
- **Śraddhā in this sense:** the faith should be in connection with Kṛṣṇa. The person should think: "your God is good, your books are good, your practices are good, and I want to be a part of what you are doing." That is śraddhā.
- It is not the generic trust a person may have in a colleague, boss or subordinate at a purely material level (for example, trusting a devotee colleague with an important responsibility). We are talking about **faith in the bhakti-śāstras, in the paramparā, and in all the devotees in general**.

#### Q (Keśava Priya): What is *vāsudeva-kathā-ruci* here, and how does śraddhā relate in practical life?
- **Ruci here** is a **generic favourableness**. Because of satāṁ kṛpā and mahat-sevā the Lord bestows this generic favourableness, which then consolidates into **tangible faith**. That is actually śraddhā.
- **Q:** So when we come to the conviction "what the devotees are doing is right and I should also follow," that is śraddhā? And someone who just likes coming to the temple, coming and going without seriousness, is in the first three stages?
- **A:** It does not matter whether they come. Someone may already be at the stage of *bhajaneṣu spṛhā*, or even the sixth stage, but not yet practising. They may desire to practise in future. It may take some time for the desire to manifest into practice. That is one possibility; it is not that they must be in that category. They might come to the temple just because they like the food, in which case they may be **prior to śraddhā**.
- **Can we identify which stage a devotee is in?** If we spend sufficient time with them, we may be able to understand.

#### A chat-box point
- "Impediments to bhakti are generally destroyed by constant service to devotees in Bhāgavatam." The lecturer says yes, and that the higher level is to give mercy even to the offenders; tolerance.

### 3.5 Satāṁ kṛpā → bhajana-kriyā → anartha-nivṛtti

After *bhajaneṣu spṛhā*, the lecturer continues the First Canto stages. Sūta Gosvāmī says Kṛṣṇa, **who purifies by the process of hearing and chanting**, indicates actual **bhajana-kriyā**. The desire transforms into the actual action of hearing and chanting.

- *śṛṇvatāṁ sva-kathāḥ kṛṣṇaḥ puṇya-śravaṇa-kīrtanaḥ hṛdy antaḥ-stho hy abhadrāṇi vidhunoti suhṛt satām* (as spoken, garbled). Kṛṣṇa, the benefactor of devotees who hear about Him, **enters the heart** and destroys their sinful inclinations and sins. This is **anartha-nivṛtti**.
- *praviṣṭaḥ karṇa-randhreṇa sva-nāṁ bhāva-saroruham dhunoti śamalaṁ kṛṣṇaḥ salilasya yathā śarat* (as spoken). Kṛṣṇa enters the heart of the devotee through the ear holes and purifies the heart of all the anarthas: **lust, anger, envy, greed, illusion, pride** and so on.
- The lord entering by the topics and remaining in the thoughts destroys desires (*abhadrāṇi vidhunoti*).

> [!abstract]
> **Sequence so far (as stated by the lecturer):** satāṁ kṛpā, mahat-sevā, vāsudeva-kathā-ruci, śraddhā, sādhu-saṅga, bhajaneṣu spṛhā, bhajana-kriyā, anartha-nivṛtti.

### 3.6 Niṣṭhā

> *naṣṭa-prāyeṣv abhadreṣu nityaṁ bhāgavata-sevayā bhagavaty uttama-śloke bhaktir bhavati naiṣṭhikī* (1.2.18, as spoken)

- *Abhadra* means impediments to bhakti. *Naṣṭa-prāyeṣu*: when they are **mostly destroyed**, and anartha-nivṛtti is **almost accomplished** (*prāyikī*).
- **By constant service to the devotees and to Bhāgavatam** (*nityaṁ bhāgavata-sevayā*), the stage of **naiṣṭhikī bhakti** (niṣṭhā) to Bhagavān, who is praised by the great sages, becomes established.
- **Meaning of niṣṭhā:** even though impurities are not completely destroyed, bhakti can be performed **steadily**. There is *prāyikī* anartha-nivṛtti, not *ātyantikī* (absolute). Some anarthas may remain, but they are **not strong enough to become an obstacle to the steady practice** of bhakti. It does not affect the steadiness of the person.
- The lecturer's reading of the commentary: in the process of jñāna, steady performance requires complete destruction of impurities; this shows the unimpeded nature of bhakti. Bhakti in the form of meditation becomes constant (*naiṣṭhikī*) by service to the devotees or to Bhagavān. It becomes steady.

#### The four types of anarthas (from Mādhurya-kādambinī)
Anarthas are desires unfavourable for progressing on the path of bhakti, classified by source:

| Type | Source / explanation as given |
|---|---|
| **Sukṛtotthā** | Unfavourable desires born of a past pious life. Pious life does not necessarily have to be favourable to devotional service. Most pious inclinations are generally favourable and can be dovetailed, but some can be unfavourable to Kṛṣṇa consciousness. |
| **Duṣkṛtotthā** | Unfavourable desires born of a past sinful life. |
| **Bhakti-yotthā / Bhaktyutthā** | Arising from proper practice of bhakti: when *pūjā, lābha, pratiṣṭhā* (name, fame, adoration) come, one may be distracted by them. |
| **Aparādhotthā** | Unfavourable desires born of the commission of offences. |

- The lecturer states that at niṣṭhā the first three (*duṣkṛtotthā, sukṛtotthā* and *bhaktyutthā*) are completely gone, and that only **aparādhotthā anarthas have started to get destroyed**. (See Points Requiring Verification.)
- Therefore this stage is **prāyikī anartha-nivṛtti**. The remaining anarthas are not strong enough to affect the steady practice of bhakti.

> [!question]
> The lecturer asked for quick comments after niṣṭhā. The question from Madhumatī Priyasakhī on how to reach niṣṭhā is recorded in Section 3.4.

### 3.7 Ruci and āsakti (the next Anuccheda)

The lecturer then moves to the next two stages, **ruci and āsakti**, described in the next Anuccheda.

- The verse concerns the person whose **remembrance of the Supreme Lord is not lured away even for a moment** even if he gets dominion over the three worlds, and who does not move even half a second away from the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, which are sought by the devatās who also meditate on the Lord. That person is **the best of the devotees (vaiṣṇava-agrya)**.
- *kāma-lobhādibhir bhāvair ajātāsya sattvam sthitam* (as spoken, garbled): through destruction of all desires, the heart becomes merged in **śuddha-sattva** and is thus suitable for direct perception of the Lord.
- After attaining steadiness, the mind becomes **unaffected by lust, greed, anger, hatred and illusion** (and by rajas and tamas). The mind becomes **fixed in the form of the Lord at the stage of āsakti** and becomes satisfied (*prasīdati*).
- The lecturer numbers these: 8th stage anartha-nivṛtti, 9th niṣṭhā, **10th ruci, 11th āsakti**.

### 3.8 Bhāva, prema, and harer mādhurya-anubhava

- When one continues further on the path of devotional service, one reaches **bhāva and prema**. The mind becomes joyful and satisfied on attaining **rati** (here, bhāva). *bhagavad-bhakti-yogataḥ*: finally prema develops, **accompanied by complete detachment** (*mukta-saṅga*).
- *bhagavat-tattva-vijñānaṁ jāyate*: the devotee experiences the Lord's **form, qualities, pastimes, powers and sweetness** at the stage of prema.
- The verses in the First Canto, second chapter thus describe the gradual progression: how, **even prior to receiving the seed of devotion**, a jīva receives it; how that seed gets nourished; and how it progresses to prema.
- *evaṁ prasanna-manaso bhagavad-bhakti-yogataḥ…* (as spoken): from performing bhakti-yoga with detachment from all desires, one develops realisation of the Lord internally or externally, without having to meditate. These two stages indicate bhāva and prema.

#### Verse (1.2.21): *bhidyate hṛdaya-granthiś chidyante sarva-saṁśayāḥ kṣīyante cāsya karmāṇi dṛṣṭa evātmanīśvare*
- **Bhidyate hṛdaya-granthiḥ:** the ignorance (knot) in the heart is cut. This means **no trace of false ego remains**; even the **inertia of past false ego** is completely gone.
  - The lecturer explains: as sādhakas we have a theoretical understanding of the true ego ("I am not the body, I am the soul, and my svarūpa is that I am the servant of Kṛṣṇa," as in *jīvera svarūpa haya kṛṣṇera nitya-dāsa*). But it is not deeply realised, because there is a strong inertia of past false ego and bodily identification. At the stage of prema even the last trace of that inertia is gone.
- **Chidyante sarva-saṁśayāḥ:** all doubts are destroyed, such as "will I ever get to see the Lord, will I ever establish a relationship with the Lord?" This is on seeing the Lord in the mind and with the eyes (*dṛṣṭa evātmanīśvare*).
- **Kṣīyante cāsya karmāṇi:** "all his karmas are destroyed." The lecturer says **karma here means karma-vāsanās**, not karmic reactions, because karmic reactions are gone at the beginning of bhakti itself. The last trace of karma-vāsanās is completely gone.
- This stage is **harer mādhurya-anubhava**, the sweet, direct realisation of Kṛṣṇa.
- Numbering: 11th āsakti, **12th bhāva, 13th prema, 14th harer mādhurya-anubhava**.

> [!info]
> **Commentary points (as rendered by the lecturer):** (a) "the knot in the heart is cut" uses a reflexive form from *karma-kartari*, which indicates that **destruction of ignorance is not the main result sought in bhakti**. (b) Doubts such as "the attainment is impossible" are destroyed on seeing the Lord. (c) Thoughts about the **non-existence of the Lord** and **contrary thoughts** are destroyed by hearing, and stray and mistaken thoughts are destroyed by meditation, but both are completely destroyed by direct perception of the Lord. (d) Karmas are diminished; **a shadow (ābhāsa) of karma remains by the will of the Lord**, but that is not really karma. (e) Both *sphūrti* of the Lord in the mind and direct appearance to the eyes are indicated.

> [!success]
> **The fourteen stages as listed by the lecturer:** 1. satāṁ kṛpā, 2. mahat-sevā, 3. vāsudeva-kathā-ruci, 4. śraddhā, 5. sādhu-saṅga, 6. bhajaneṣu spṛhā, 7. bhajana-kriyā, 8. anartha-nivṛtti, 9. niṣṭhā, 10. ruci, 11. āsakti, 12. bhāva, 13. prema, 14. harer mādhurya-anubhava. These have been described in the consecutive verses of the second chapter of the First Canto ("Divinity and Divine Service").

## 4. Closing Q&A

### 4.1 Do karmas get destroyed, or only karma-vāsanās? (Ādi Bhagavān / Speaker 2)

**Question:** We understand that karmas are destroyed right at the beginning. Here the lecturer said that this speaks of karma-vāsanās, but the translation says "karma", and also says a shadow remains. If someone argues that "you are not at that stage, you are still under karma," what reference do we give? Does Cakravartī Ṭhākura clarify this in his commentary?

**The lecturer's response:**
- He shares the commentary on 1.2.21 on screen. In the Cakravartī commentary quoted above, the word *karma* is not explicitly specified as *karma-vāsanā*, because the word *karma* **refers to karma-vāsanās also**. So the word can be understood in two ways: karma-vāsanās, or karmic reactions. It is not explicit here and could refer to both.
- Cross-referencing **Mādhurya-kādambinī**, also by Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, makes it clear. There it is stated in multiple places that **the suffering of devotees, even from the very beginning stage, is not due to their karma but is directly given by Kṛṣṇa**.
- The lecturer recalls the verse *yasyāham anugṛhṇāmi hariṣye tad-dhanaṁ śanaiḥ* but says it is not the one he was thinking of; he could not recollect the verse with the elaborate purport on why a devotee's suffering is not due to karma.

> [!warning]
> **Misunderstanding the lecturer warns against:** the presentation may sound as if "devotees can do anything and get away with it." The ācāryas are **not** communicating that a devotee is not responsible for his actions. He is very much responsible, and **any improper act will be punished**. The only point is that **the punishment is not given by the law of karma but by Kṛṣṇa**. This shows that Kṛṣṇa, even in punishing an errant devotee, does not take his shelter away from the devotee. It does not mean there is no punishment.

#### Why an exalted devotee says it is his karma, and why others say so
- An exalted devotee may see his own suffering as due to his own karma **out of natural humility**. For some inconceivable reason, at such an exalted status they cannot see themselves as devotees; they say "my happiness or distress means my karma is not gone." This is due to their inconceivable actions inspired by prema.
- When devotees tell someone "your suffering is due to your own karma," it can mean: **you will not get something you do not deserve**, so do not become angry in the mood that an injustice has happened to you. It helps give the right perspective for dealing with reversals. The lecturer cites *bhuñjāna evātma-kṛtaṁ vipākam* and calls this "a very healthy approach for the devotee."
- That usage means "you are suffering because of your own past mistakes." **Who is giving the reaction** (the law of karma or Kṛṣṇa) is a different discussion.
- Depending on the communication needed:
  - To tell someone to **own their mistakes and take responsibility** and not push the blame on others, it may be said "you are suffering due to your own karma."
  - To communicate that **Kṛṣṇa has not abandoned you**, one says the reaction is given by Kṛṣṇa, who gives His shelter even through that reaction or punishment.

### 4.2 The Pāṇḍavas and Kuntī as examples; why Kṛṣṇa does not make a devotee's exemption obvious

**Point raised (Speaker 2, partly inaudible):** people give examples such as the Pāṇḍavas and Kuntī, saying Kṛṣṇa does not change the law of nature just for devotees; whether suffering comes by Kṛṣṇa's direct action or through the laws of karma by Kṛṣṇa's permission, shelter is available according to how sincerely the devotee takes it.

**The lecturer's response:**
- Kṛṣṇa also has the responsibility to **protect other people's faith in karma-jñāna and the other paths**. He will not make it obvious that as soon as someone takes up bhakti the laws of karma stop working (for example, that their body stops aging, or that they need not bathe).
- If this were obvious, people would give up karma and jñāna and take up the external practices of bhakti for wrong reasons, **without faith in bhakti**. It would not work for them, and they would lose faith in bhakti too, having already lost faith in karma-jñāna-yoga, and would end up living a materialistic life without any faith. Krṣṇa would never do that.
- **Krishna has created a system for developing faith in bhakti: the agency of devotees.** Those who have not developed faith through that agency, and who simply see that the laws of karma stop working, would not have real faith.
- **Pratyakṣa-pramāṇa:** from an external point of view the Pāṇḍavas and Kuntī were suffering, but that is all *pratyakṣa-pramāṇa*. **We are supposed to see the experience of devotees through the eyes of śāstra**, not just as it is described externally.
- The **target audience** of books like the Mahābhārata is people with *karma-niṣṭhā*, so the natural message is "no one can escape karma; be very careful," and the depictions are shaped accordingly. For the bhakti-śāstra insight, one must come to Śrīmad Bhāgavatam and the explanations of the ācāryas. We can quote many pastimes, but the **real lesson of the pastimes for devotees** comes through the ācāryas' explanations.

### 4.3 Keśava Priya's summary, and the practical conclusion

- **Keśava Priya:** He appreciates the explanation of why Kṛṣṇa does not bring obvious change in devotees: to prevent unqualified people from shifting to bhakti without deep faith. He says, as a doubt of a Mātājī: whether the devotee's suffering is due to karma or to Kṛṣṇa, **the solution is only bhakti-yoga** (devotional service). Some say "till your karma is completely over you should do something else," and that is wrong. He adds that, before contact with the transcendental, a jīva follows his mind's likes and dislikes (his value system), and is under the law of karma. Once one changes the value system to bhakti and follows the bhakti-śāstras, one need not shift to other dharma-śāstras for results; results are given according to one's present faith.
- **The lecturer's agreement:** "correct." If they say your suffering is due to karma and therefore you should do all these karma-kāṇḍīya practices, that is a problem. If they say **"be humble, don't think Kṛṣṇa has taken over, it is only your own karma, so surrender more sincerely to Kṛṣṇa,"** then "we will happily accept it."
- **Ādi Bhagavān (Vrajasundarī / Speaker 2):** whether punishment comes through Kṛṣṇa directly or through the laws of karma by Kṛṣṇa's will, **the shelter is available to the extent we seek it**. Once one takes up devotional service, one always knows that Kṛṣṇa's shelter is available, "although punishment may come from here or there."
- **The lecturer's agreement:** if understanding that it is only one's karma keeps one humble and helps one seek deeper shelter of Kṛṣṇa, that is fine. If it pushes one to take shelter of karma-kāṇḍīya prāyaścitta and so on, it is unfavourable.

## 5. Key Takeaways

1. The **first Anuccheda** essence: the root cause of suffering is Kṛṣṇa-bahirmukha nature, overcome by becoming Kṛṣṇa-unmukha through bhakti. Śāstra alone is not enough; sukṛti-bala, obtained through the intervention of a devotee, is needed.
2. **Bhakti is independent**, whereas karma, jñāna and yoga depend on bhakti for their success. Jñāna and vairāgya arise automatically as by-products of bhakti.
3. The goal of life is **tattva-jijñāsā**: inquiry into the tattva realised as Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān. It is realised by bhakti after hearing from the guru.
4. The mind should be dedicated only to bhakti (**ekena manasā**), without the covering of karma and jñāna. Hearing and chanting is easy, and the remembrance of Kṛṣṇa cuts the knots of karma.
5. A **kovida** is one who has been fortunate to receive the seed of devotion through devotees; the unfortunate cannot develop taste for the Lord's topics.
6. **Satāṁ kṛpā** (compassion, an emotion) takes practical shape as **mahat-sevā**, which gives a generic favourableness (*vāsudeva-kathā-ruci*); this consolidates into tangible śraddhā in the bhakti-śāstras and in the paramparā.
7. Mahat-sevā can be unknowing (ajñāta), and can be **indirect** through appreciation of what Vaiṣṇavas have produced. "Generally" in the commentary indicates the rule that taste comes through Vaiṣṇavas; exceptions are possible by direct Kṛṣṇa intervention, but the Bhāgavatam case is not an exception.
8. **Serving the instructions (vāṇī) of great devotees is mahat-sevā.** Following the guru's instruction to discuss Kṛṣṇa-kathā together is one example.
9. The sequence from śraddhā: sādhu-saṅga (surrender to the guru) → bhajaneṣu spṛhā → bhajana-kriyā → anartha-nivṛtti → niṣṭhā → ruci → āsakti → bhāva → prema → harer mādhurya-anubhava. **Fourteen stages in all.**
10. **Niṣṭhā** is steady practice even though some anarthas remain. The four types of anarthas (sukṛtotthā, duṣkṛtotthā, bhaktyutthā, aparādhotthā) are classified by source.
11. At prema, the last trace of past false ego and **karma-vāsanās** is gone, doubts are destroyed, and Kṛṣṇa is directly realised. Only a shadow (ābhāsa) of karma remains by the will of the Lord.
12. A devotee's suffering is **not technically a karmic reaction**. It is given by Kṛṣṇa, who does not withdraw shelter, but the devotee remains responsible for his actions and takes responsibility for his suffering. The teaching is favourable only when it leads one to surrender more to Kṛṣṇa and not toward karma-kāṇḍīya remedies.
13. Kṛṣṇa does not make a devotee's exemption from karma externally obvious, in order to protect people's faith in karma and jñāna paths and to prevent insincere shifts to bhakti.

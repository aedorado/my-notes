# Āyu-Gaṇanā: Age Calculation Systems
### Kāla Gaṇanā · Saptāṁśa & Three-Line Average Method

Reading a sign on a line is only half the task — the other half is knowing *when*, in a person's life, that sign takes effect. This chapter gathers the different systems for turning the length of a line into a timeline of years, from retired historical methods to the one now in current use, and shows how each applies directly to real hands.

## 1. Foundational Principles & Anatomy

Age calculation on the hand works by treating the length of a chosen line as a scale running from birth to the end of an assumed lifespan, then estimating what fraction of that scale a given point — a sign, a break, a branch — falls at. Because the "end of the lifespan" assumed by this scale is itself a matter of convention, every method of age calculation is really a statement about what total span of years is being mapped onto the line, and this assumption shifts according to deśa-kāla-paristhiti — place, time, and circumstance.

This shift can be traced historically: in earlier India, palmists did not use the Life Line for age calculation at all — they took the **Heart Line** to be the "life line," reasoning that if the heart stops working, life ends. It was only later, as more experimentation was carried out in the West, that the Life Line itself came to be used for timing age. The assumed total lifespan has also risen over time for a concrete reason: medical improvement. Where a woman could once die in childbirth, this risk has reduced; even during the Coronavirus pandemic, many people survived who might once not have. By deśa-kāla-paristhiti, the assumed age used in these calculations has therefore been raised correspondingly over the generations.

## 2. Structural Variations: The Four Methods

### 2.1 Method 1 — the old Indian ṣaḍaṁśa paddhati (division by six)

This older method, no longer in use, measured the Life Line from its thumb-side origin (zero) to the middle of the maṇibandha rekhā (wrist line), assuming a total age of **70 to 72**. A thread or paper was laid along the line following its actual curve, not as a straight measurement, and the resulting length was divided into parts of six years each — 0, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, and onward to 72, giving twelve equal parts, "like the table of six." In this older scheme, age 30 does not fall at the exact midpoint of the line as it does in the method taught now — it falls earlier, at roughly the 24 mark on the line.

### 2.2 Method 2 — Cheiro's saptāṁśa paddhati (division by seven)

![Saptamsha Paddhati](/lecture-notes-images/27/02-saptamsha-01.png)
![Saptamsha Paddhati](/lecture-notes-images/27/02-saptamsha.png)

Cheiro, a Western palmist whose research is still followed by some practitioners, took the total assumed age as **98 (approximately 98 to 100)**, dividing this span by seven — "the table of seven" — into fourteen equal parts. The procedure otherwise follows the same logic: zero at the Life Line's start, the centre/base line (maṇibandha) as the far end, a thread or paper laid along the line, divided into the fourteen equal parts. This is the pattern most commonly taught elsewhere. The method described in §2.4 below is followed here, not Cheiro's, because its results have proven fairly reliable in practice — though readers are free to try Cheiro's pattern themselves. Cheiro's own advice, regardless of method, was never to predict **more than about seven years ahead, or even less**, because the lines of the hand keep changing.

### 2.3 Method 3 — the three-line average (100 years per line)

This method uses three lines together — the **Life Line, the Mind Line, and the Heart Line** — while deliberately leaving the Fate Line out. Each line's full length is treated as 100 years (following Cheiro's 98–100 convention), and the point at which the line appears to end is estimated as a fraction of that span:
- The **Life Line** is measured from its start (thumb side) to its visible end — for instance, if it appears to cover 70% of its full theoretical length, that end is read as age 70.
- The **Heart Line** is measured from its start at the edge of the palm to its very end — which may finish on the Saturn Mount in some hands and elsewhere in others — taking the far edge of the palm as 100 and estimating the line's actual end proportionally, for example at age 75.
- The **Mind Line** is counted from wherever it actually starts: from its joining point if it begins joined to the Life Line, or from the edge of the palm if it starts away from it; its far end is likewise estimated proportionally, for example at age 60.

The three estimated ages are added together (for example, 70 + 75 + 60 = 205) and divided by three, giving an average overall age estimate (here, about 68, stated as "68 to about 70"). This 100-year convention is Western in origin; because Cheiro's method assumes a long Western lifespan, the figure must be adjusted by deśa-kāla — in India, the average age is closer to 70 or 72, so this method may be run on 70/72 or 75–80 instead of 100, and the current approximate local lifespan should be researched rather than assumed. The best way to practise this method is to calculate the age of hands belonging to people who have already passed away, where the true endpoint is already known; with a live client, the client's actual current age can instead be used to calibrate the scale, after which one or two confirmed events (such as a triangle on the Life Line corresponding to a known property purchase) are used to verify which age that point actually corresponds to.

### 2.4 Method 4 — the current method: "30 at the midpoint"

![Fate Line Age 30 Method](/lecture-notes-images/27/03-fate-line-age-30-method.png)

This is the method now followed, applied to both the Life Line and the Fate Line.

> [!IMPORTANT] The 30-midpoint procedure (Life Line)
> Zero is placed at the Life Line's start (thumb side); a thread or paper is laid along the line up to the maṇibandha rekhā and joined there. A second centre reference point is found between the Mercury line and the palm's last line, the **sandhi rekhā**, by folding the paper in half — this centre point should land "a little above" the hand's natural crease mark. This centre point is then joined to the starting point, and **this centre point is always fixed at age 30**. After it, the average age taken is **75**; the second half of the line, running from 30 to 75, is itself halved again and again to find further reference ages.

The Fate Line is aged in exactly the same way, but measured from the maṇibandha rekhā regardless of where the Fate Line itself visibly begins: even if a person's Fate Line starts from Venus or from the middle of the palm, its age is still counted from the maṇibandha rekhā up to 75 at the top, with 30 at the centre, halved onward from there. If the Fate Line starts higher up the palm than its theoretical zero point, the resulting gap can mean the person did not focus on career as strongly until later — but the line's age calculation still starts from the maṇibandha rekhā regardless.

> [!FAQ] Q: If the Fate Line is not visible, how is its age calculated?
> **A:** Normally the Fate Line will be visible. If it genuinely cannot be seen, do not attempt to calculate its age — read events instead on the Life Line, and do all the aging there. Many hands have very faint lines; if the Fate Line is joined to the Life Line and can be traced rising from it, its age can still be calculated, but otherwise the Life Line should be used.

### 2.5 Heart Line age: two methods

![Heart Line Age Method A](/lecture-notes-images/27/04-heart-line-aging-01.png)

> [!IMPORTANT] Heart Line Method A (preferred)
> From where the Heart Line starts, the first block is **18 years**; the gap in the middle is **6 years**; then another **18 years**; then **6**; then **18**, and so on — 18 + 6 + 18 + 6 + 18 … — continuing until the point where the Heart Line actually stops. If the final block is incomplete (covering only part of an 18-year span), it is estimated — for example as 15 or 14 — and added to the running total, e.g., 18 + 6 + 18 + 6 + 18 + 6 + 18 + 6 + 14.

![Heart Line Age Method B](/lecture-notes-images/27/04-heart-line-aging-02.png)

> [!INFO] Heart Line Method B
> The Heart Line's age is taken as **16** up to the Mercury finger, **36** at the next finger (Sun), and **50** at the end of the Saturn finger, with "50 and above, any" beyond that point. Method A is considered better, since Heart Line data is generally only found up to about age 50 — beyond that, the line usually gives little further to read.

### 2.6 Mind Line age

![Mind Line Age](/lecture-notes-images/27/05-mind-line-age.png)

The Mind Line's age is generally **not** calculated at all — "all aging should be done on the Life Line; the best aging is obtained there." If a reader still wishes to time the Mind Line, the method is identical to the Heart Line's Method A: start at zero where the Mind Line begins, and proceed in the same repeating 18, then 6, then 18, then 6 pattern.

> [!SUMMARY] The four methods taught, in the order presented
> The old six-based Life Line method (total 72) → Cheiro's seven-based method (total 98) → the three-line average (100 each, for Life, Heart, and Mind Lines) → the **current** 30-midpoint method (applied to the Life Line and Fate Line), with the 18+6 repeating block used for the Heart and Mind Lines.

## 4. Signs, Markings & Practical Indications

### 4.1 The paper-strip technique

Across every method, the physical technique for measuring a line accurately is the same: a thread or a strip of paper is laid **along the line's actual curve**, not stretched straight, so that the true length of the line is captured. The strip is marked at the line's full length, then removed and folded in half to find the precise midpoint, and folded again as needed to locate quarter-points and further subdivisions — a far more exact approach than estimating fractions by eye.

### 4.2 Calibrating against a living client

With a living client, the recommended practice is to first use their known, actual age to calibrate the chosen scale, and then to ask about one or two confirmed life events — for instance, asking when a visible triangle on the Life Line's path corresponds to a known property purchase — to check what age that point on the scale actually represents in practice. To sharpen the method generally, practise on the hands of people who have already died, since their full lifespan is already known and the calculation's accuracy can be checked directly against it.

### 4.3 Case Applications

![Example Hand — Harishchandra ji's Father](/lecture-notes-images/27/08-example.png)

> [!CASE]
> **Case Example — Harishchandra ji's father, age 57.** Using the current 30-midpoint method on the Life Line, a line beside a break in the Life Line was identified as, specifically, the Life Line's own continuation (not a supportive line) and was aged at **around 30** — well before his then-current age of 57 — and read as a period of expansion, a shift from one place to another, or an increase in comforts. This matched directly: the client confirmed his father had in fact opened other businesses around that age. A separate line running alongside and later joining the Fate Line was aged to roughly **age 50**, corresponding to what the client currently does; he confirmed starting an entirely new line of work — property work — about seven to eight years before the reading (that is, around age 50), after running a medicine shop for ten to fifteen years prior. A cross at the very start of the Life Line was read, by its position in the first 8–10 years, as childhood problems within the family — a pattern many hands show.

![Example Hand — Arvind ji's Uncle](/lecture-notes-images/27/07-example.png)

> [!CASE]
> **Case Example — Arvind ji's uncle (tāūjī), age ≈ 70.** The Life Line in this hand was read as not of good quality and somewhat spread (broad), indicating a life that has not been smooth; a particular patch of the Life Line was notably dark near the age corresponding to his known heart trouble (bypass surgery in 2017, pacemaker in 2024), illustrating how a specific stretch of the aged line can be checked directly against a confirmed medical event.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Caution**: never predict more than roughly seven years ahead from a single reading — a caution originating with Cheiro himself — because the lines of the hand continue to change over a person's lifetime, and a prediction pinned too far ahead risks being overtaken by lines that have since shifted.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Caution**: the total lifespan assumed by any of these methods is never a fixed, universal number — it must always be adjusted for deśa-kāla-paristhiti. A Western-derived method assuming 98–100 years should not be applied unadjusted to an Indian context, where the average age is closer to 70–75; the correct current figure should be researched rather than assumed outright.

## 6. Sanskrit Terms Introduced

- **Āyu-Gaṇanā**: age calculation, the overall subject of this chapter.
- **Ṣaḍaṁśa Paddhati**: the old Indian six-part division method, no longer used.
- **Saptāṁśa Paddhati**: Cheiro's seven-part division method, assuming a 98-year span.
- **Maṇibandha Rekhā**: the wrist line, used as the fixed far-end reference point for both the Life Line and Fate Line in the current method.
- **Sandhi Rekhā**: the palm's last line, referenced alongside the Mercury line when locating the second centre point for the fixed age-30 mark.
- **Deśa-Kāla-Paristhiti**: place, time, and circumstance — the governing adjustment behind every assumed total lifespan used across these methods.

## 7. Diagnostic Self-Check

1. Which line am I timing, and does it call for the 30-midpoint method (Life Line, Fate Line) or the 18+6 repeating-block method (Heart Line, and Mind Line if timed at all)?
2. What total lifespan am I assuming for this calculation, and have I adjusted it for deśa-kāla-paristhiti rather than defaulting to a figure (98–100) drawn from a different time or place?
3. Have I calibrated the scale against the client's actual age or at least one confirmed life event before committing to a prediction, and am I keeping that prediction within a cautious horizon rather than projecting many decades ahead from a line that can still change?

# Finger Proportions & Universal Phalanges
### Aṅguli Pramāṇa & Parva Lakṣaṇam · Measurement, Base Alignment & The Three Universal Segments

Before any individual finger can be read, the hand's measurement method and the universal structure of the three phalanges must be understood, since these same rules apply to every finger studied afterward — the Jupiter, Saturn, Sun, and Mercury fingers each build on this shared foundation.

::::left
### IN ONE MINUTE
◆ **The Saturn finger is the ruler**: every other finger's length is measured against the midpoint of the Saturn finger's first phalange (except the little finger, measured against the Sun finger instead).
◆ **No single feature stands alone**: a finger's apparent length must be checked against where its base starts, since a low-starting base can "borrow" length from the mount beneath it — finger, mount, and lines must always be read together.
◆ **Three phalanges, three questions**: the first asks what a person received (mentality/inheritance), the second asks what they did with it (implementation), and the third asks what they got from it (result).
◆ **Both hands, one emphasis**: both hands are always examined, but the active/working hand carries more weight in the reading.

## 1. Foundational Principles — The Measurement Benchmark

The Saturn (middle) finger is always taken as the base/reference finger for measuring its neighbours. Its first phalange is mentally divided in half, and that halfway point becomes the benchmark: the **Jupiter finger** (index) should reach this midpoint to be considered **mediocre** (सामान्य) — neither good nor bad, simply average — and the **Sun finger** (ring finger) should reach the same mark, with the important exception that extending slightly *above* it is still considered good, not a flaw. The **little (Mercury) finger** is measured differently, against the joint line of the Sun finger's first phalange rather than the Saturn finger; not reaching that line is the normal condition, reaching above it is even better, and falling below it is unfavourable.

Both hands should be examined, but the person's active/working hand should be weighted more heavily in the reading.

## 2. Base Alignment — Finger Roots and the Mounts Beneath

Every finger visually "starts" from a base point on the palm, beneath which lies its corresponding mount. On almost every hand, the Mercury finger's base sits lower than the others, and a perfectly even alignment of all the finger bases is very rare. Where a finger's base begins lower than normal — the Jupiter finger's base starting further down the palm, for instance — that finger has effectively gained length at the expense of the mount beneath it, which now has less area of its own. A finger that looks short at first glance should therefore always be checked at its base before being classified as genuinely short: the apparent shortness may simply be a base-position effect, and the mount's own condition can compensate for, or complete, what the finger's length alone would suggest. As with astrology, where no single yoga or placement ever gives a complete prediction, no single hand feature — finger, mount, or line — can be read in isolation.

## 3. Structural Variations — The Three Phalanges (Parva)

Unlike the thumb, which shows two or, rarely, three divisions, **every finger reliably shows three phalanges**, separated by joint markers (sandhi). Some fingers also show an extra horizontal or vertical line within the second phalange, comparable to the long vertical lines commonly seen on the Mount of Venus. Fingers primarily reveal a person's behaviour; while they also contribute to predicting results, their strongest application is behavioural.

**First Phalange — Ātma Parva / Buddhi Parva (Mentality).** The topmost segment of any finger represents mentality (मानसिकता) — what a person carries through genetics, inherited from family lineage. This same segment is also where fingerprint patterns (loops/शंख and whorls/चक्र) are examined, a topic reserved for a dedicated fingerprint class. It can be short or long, bulged or sunken; a **long and bulged** first phalange indicates a good family inheritance and a leaning toward spirituality — expressed differently by different people (regular worship, charitable giving, or simply treating service to others as one's dharma), but sharing the same underlying inclination.

**Second Phalange — Implementation / Logical Talent.** This segment reveals how a person uses or implements what the first phalange received — their "logical talent." A **horizontal line** here indicates reduced implementation: the person is not applying what they have learned as fully as they should. A **vertical line** indicates stress connected to implementing what has been learned. Where both lines appear together, the stress increases further, and the crossing forms a rough square — generally a protective sign elsewhere on the hand, but here indicating that logic is applied while implementing something, alongside a proportionate mental exertion. The exact outcome is further shaped by the relative size of the Sun and Jupiter fingers: a larger Sun finger increases the tendency to act despite the stress, while a larger Jupiter finger favours relying on intellect over risk-taking.

**Third Phalange — Result.** This segment reveals the outcome that follows from mentality and its implementation. It is read independently: a person whose first two phalanges are only average can still show a strong, open third phalange and achieve very good results, while a person with good input and implementation but poor results will typically show a thin, lined, or marked third phalange. Hardened or calloused skin on the fingers — as commonly seen on people who train regularly — is not considered favourable for result-related readings.

Only the first phalange carries an established traditional name; no specific classical names have been found for the second and third.

## 4. Chapter Summary & Comparison Table

| Universal Parameter | What It Reveals | Reading Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Length vs. Saturn finger's first-phalange midpoint** | Whether a finger is mediocre, long, or short | Mercury finger is the exception — measured against the Sun finger's joint line instead |
| **Base position relative to the palm** | Whether apparent length is genuine or a base-position effect | A low base "borrows" length from the mount beneath; always check before judging a finger short |
| **First Phalange (Mentality)** | Inherited disposition and family lineage | Also where fingerprint patterns are read |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Reading order principle**: these three universal parameters — length, base alignment, and the three phalanges — are the starting point for every finger studied afterward. No single parameter completes the reading; finger, mount, and lines must always be combined.

## 5. Diagnostic Self-Check

1. Which reference point applies to the finger being measured — the Saturn finger's first-phalange midpoint, or (for the Mercury finger) the Sun finger's joint line?
2. Does the finger's apparent length hold up once its base position is checked against the mount beneath it?
3. What do the three phalanges show in turn — the inherited mentality (first), the quality of implementation (second), and the strength of the actual result (third)?
4. Is a horizontal line, a vertical line, or both present on the second phalange, and how do the relative sizes of the Sun and Jupiter fingers shape the outcome?
5. Is the skin on the fingers notably hardened or calloused, and how should that temper a result-related reading of the third phalange?

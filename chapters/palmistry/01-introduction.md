# Foundations & Structural Anatomy of the Hand
### Śāstra Paricaya & Hasta Racanā · Philosophy, Naming, Mounts & Lakṣaṇa Vidyā

In popular conception, *hastarekhā* (palmistry) is often misunderstood as the art of deciphering only the lines etched across the palm. In the classical approach, lines are the last thing studied — only after the hand's shape is fixed, its parts correctly named, and its proportions measured does the serious work of line-reading begin.

:::glance
::::left
### IN ONE MINUTE
◆ **Lines come last**: palmar creases are approached only after skin, nails, hand hardness/temperature, dorsal structure, and shape are mastered.
◆ **Shape is fixed, lines are not**: every hand is born "square" and takes its adult shape after age 7 — that shape never changes again; only the lines keep changing, roughly every 2–4 months on the active hand.
◆ **Active vs. Destiny Hand**: the left hand is always the fixed "destiny hand" (like a birth chart); the hand one eats and writes with is the changing "active hand" — this rule applies equally to men and women today.
◆ **Mounts have no fixed border**: a mount's territory is read by how full it is and which direction it extends, not by a drawn boundary line.

## 1. Foundational Principles & Anatomy

Palmistry (**hastarekhā vijñāna / hastarekhā śāstra**) is one part of a larger discipline called **Sāmudrika Śāstra**, also known as **Lakṣaṇa Vidyā Śāstra** — the "science of physical signs/characteristics." This wider science makes predictions by observing physical signs (**lakṣaṇ**) across the entire body: the forehead and its lines, moles/marks (**til**) anywhere on the body, the feet, and even a person's gestures and the events around them. The hand is simply the most detailed and accessible portion of this larger science.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Lines Come Later**: The lines of the hand are approached only once the outer structural features of the hand are understood — nails, skin texture and color, hand hardness/softness, hand temperature, and overall shape. Just as noticing someone's nails looking yellow, dry, or pale might prompt a question about their health, the skin and structure of the hand carry the same kind of information even before a line is examined.

Every person is born with a square **hand** — not palm, a distinction explained in the measurement section below. The shape of the hand only begins forming after age 7, and once fixed, it never changes again for life; only the **lines** keep changing over time. Because of this, no prediction can begin until the hand's shape has first been established — shape precedes every other reading.

A hand is also not read before the age of 12, because its lines are not yet formed, and the independent capacity to think and decide for oneself has not yet developed.

> [!CLASS]
> **Classical References**: Two texts stand as primary reference material — the **Bṛhat Saṃhitā** (2nd volume, by Suresh Chandra Mishra), which contains separate sections on male (page 711) and female (Chapter 69, page 752) physical signs, covering feet, eyebrows, ears, hair, and palm lines; and the **Bhaviṣya Purāṇa** (edition by S.N. Khandelwal, Chowkhamba Sanskrit Pratishthan), whose Chapter 5 describes auspicious/inauspicious signs of women, with further relevant material in Chapters 6 and 24–28. No single text contains all of Sāmudrika Śāstra — its verses (śloka) are scattered across many sources, including the **Garuḍa Purāṇa** and **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa**. Among independent reading material, **सम्पूर्ण हस्तरेखा शास्त्र का सरल अध्ययन** and the two-volume **प्राचीन समुद्र शास्त्र** by Prem Kumar Sharma are also noted.

> [!CLASS]
> **Origin of the Name**: Tradition holds that this vidyā was given by **Lord Shiva** to a sage named **Samudra Ṛṣi**, who is credited with compiling (**saṅkalan**) the scattered body of knowledge — hence the name Sāmudrika Śāstra, also called Lakṣaṇa Vidyā Śāstra. Very little is documented about the sage himself beyond scattered references such as "Samudra said this."

There is no conclusive evidence of exactly where palmistry originated — some say China, others say India.

> [!CASE]
> **Cheiro — Father of Palmistry**: Cheiro, a very well-known palmist, is called the father of palmistry. He is said to have learned this vidyā in India from people of the **Joshi caste/community** in northern and western India, who reportedly possessed an old text written on human skin in red ink. Cheiro is also said to have performed the sādhanā of **Pañcāṅgulī Devī** (the goddess of the five fingers), whose method and effects remain unverified. Cheiro's skill was reportedly so refined that he could identify a hand's gender from behind a curtain, without seeing the person at all.

## 2. The Cardinal Rule: Deśa, Kāla, Paristhiti

> [!CAUTION]
> **Do Not Be Rigid**: Never treat any single sign as a rigid, guaranteed rule. Two people — even within the same family — can have very similar-looking hands (for example, both soft-handed) yet have completely different natures and completely different fates. Every reading must factor in **deśa, kāla, paristhiti** — place, time, and circumstances.

Readings are conducted for people of any gender or age, and are frequently done from photographs rather than in person, which demands comfort with reading from a screen image. A magnifying tool (battery/light-equipped) or a magnification app can help with fine lines.

### Guidelines for Collecting Hand Photos

Because correct photographic technique underlies every later reading, the following rules apply from the outset:

1. **Let the hand rest first** — do not photograph a hand right after rushing around, washing, or rubbing hands together in cold weather, as this temporarily changes hand color/temperature. Wait 10–15 minutes in a resting state.
2. **Good lighting is essential** — natural sunlight works well; dim room lighting obscures fine lines.
3. **Take both hands.**
4. **Take a photo with fingers closed together** as well, since finger length is measured this way.
5. **Do not ask the subject to hold the hand perfectly straight** — this makes them self-conscious. A naturally relaxed hand shows the gaps between fingers, which carry their own minor predictive significance; a deliberately straightened hand hides them.
6. **Take photos from multiple angles**: palm-side (front), back of the palm, and from the side (needed, for example, to see marriage lines).
7. **Take the thumb separately.**
8. **Take nails separately** if not sufficiently visible in the back-of-palm shot.

> [!TIP]
> **Enhancing Line Visibility**: Applying ink (e.g. blue ink) and taking a handprint, applying powder, or applying a little moisturizer/cream can all bring out lines more clearly — very dry hands often do not show lines well.

> [!TIP]
> **Observing One's Own Hand Over Time**: Noting the date of one's own hand photograph and revisiting it over the following months reveals how the hand's lines gradually change — a direct way to witness the concepts below in practice.

### Why Lines Change: Active Hand vs. Destiny Hand

Once both hands' photographs are available, the central question becomes: which hand is actually read for prediction?

| | Left Hand — Destiny Hand | Active Hand |
|---|---|---|
| **What it represents** | Comparable to the birth chart (janma patrikā) — fixed at birth, its daśā periods fixed | Whichever hand the person predominantly eats and writes with (right hand for ~90% of people) |
| **Does it change?** | No — only in very rare cases of extraordinarily single-minded willpower | Yes — its lines change, typically every 2–4 months, as thought process and effort change |
| **Applies regardless of** | Which hand a person actually works with | — |

**Eating** and **writing** are the two reliable indicators of the active hand — eating because it is a basic need a person naturally performs with whichever hand is most comfortable, and writing because it connects directly to **graphology**: subconscious thought gets imprinted into the small strokes of handwriting, and over time these accumulated thoughts shape habit, character, and personality.

> [!CASE]
> **Graphology and Graphotherapy**: Handwriting analysis is taken seriously even in criminal investigations — a suicide note, for instance, may be examined for clues that even astrology might not reveal. **Graphotherapy** (deliberately changing one's handwriting or signature) is considered one of the most effective remedial techniques, because a changed way of writing reflects and reinforces a changed thought process, which in turn changes the lines on the active hand. Healing and hypnotherapy disciplines ultimately converge on the same principle: training the mind changes the active hand's lines.

> [!CASE]
> **A Documented Example**: A palmist who once carried many cuts/marks on her thumb found those cuts disappear entirely during a numerologically significant "1" period (daśā) — an illustration of how strong a mindset-driven effect on the hand can be.

> [!TIP]
> **Remedial Application**: People doing very hard physical labor tend to have rough, tough hands, reflecting a tougher life; softer hands correlate with a greater *chance* of comfort (not a guarantee — it forms a "base platform," much as a rāśi forms a base platform in astrology). As a simple remedy, keeping the hands well cared for (manicure, moisturizing, especially at night) genuinely appears to have some effect, and repeated knots or cuts should be avoided, as their impact shows up around meaningful life events.

**Determining active vs. destiny hand in practice:**
- If someone clearly eats/writes with one hand, that is the active hand; the other is the destiny hand.
- For non-writers (e.g. elderly relatives), computer use is treated as equivalent to writing; if a person neither writes nor types, the eating hand becomes the basis for determination.
- For ambidextrous clients, one method is to ask them to repeat the same simple task an odd number of times (e.g. 5) and note which hand they instinctively extend first; another, in person, is to toss a ball unexpectedly and see which hand rises first. For online consultations, this is adapted through conversation or a small on-camera task.
- If one hand does virtually everything, that hand is treated as **both** destiny and active hand.
- In practice, both hands are always considered together — just as neither the birth chart nor transits are ignored in astrology — but it is essential to be clear which hand is which before beginning.

> [!CLASS]
> **Gender and the Active/Destiny Rule**: Traditionally, only the left hand was read for women, since women historically had little independent role in decisions. In modern times this convention is outdated — women today exercise far more independent thought and action, so the **same active/destiny procedure now applies to both men and women**, with prediction drawing primarily from the active hand while the destiny hand is not ignored.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Two Common Myths**: (1) "Don't read your own hand" — incorrect; one's own hand is as valid a starting point as any, since negativity only takes hold where inner weakness already exists. (2) "Don't read palms at night" — this myth arose purely from poor historical lighting (candlelight) and no longer applies under modern lighting.

## 3. Naming the Hand's Parts

Before any line can be interpreted, every part of the hand must be correctly named.

### Wrist Lines — Maṇibaddha Rekhā / Paṅkaṇa Rekhā

Found at the wrist, different people show one, two, or three such lines (up to four has been observed in practice, and not more than four is documented). To see them clearly, the hand should **not** be held perfectly straight — tilting it about 30 degrees often reveals a faint additional line not visible on a flat hand. This tilted-hand method should always be used when doing time predictions from these lines.

### Fingers and Their Planetary Names

| Finger (English) | Sanskrit/Hindi name | Ruling planet / palmistry name |
|---|---|---|
| Thumb | अंगूठा | (studied independently) |
| Index finger | तर्जनी | **Jupiter finger** (गुरु की उंगली) |
| Middle finger | मध्यमा | **Saturn finger** (शनि की उंगली) |
| Ring finger | अनामिका | **Sun finger** / Apollo finger (सूर्य की उंगली) |
| Little finger | कनिष्ठा | **Mercury finger** (बुध की उंगली) |

The thumb carries an entire independent field of study: it reveals information about property, married life, and the self — one's logic, willpower, and decision-making capacity.

### Phalanges (पर्व) and Joint Lines (संधि रेखा)

The lines crossing each finger, dividing it into segments, are called **saṇdhi rekhā (joint lines)**. Each finger is generally divided into **three phalanges (पर्व)** by these lines: one at the base, and two above it.

- Some fingers — most often the Saturn (middle) finger — show what looks like a fourth, often faint line partway across a phalanx that does not cross the finger's full width. This is **not** counted as a fourth phalanx; it belongs to a separate category of marking, read apart from phalanx analysis.
- The topmost joint line, closest to the palm at the base of the finger, is also called the **family ring**. If this base line is absent or does not fully cross the finger, that finger is treated as having only two phalanges — the line only "counts" if it fully crosses.
- The thumb is read differently and may show two or three phalanges depending on the source consulted — there is no single fixed rule here, and judgment is required.

### Knots, Hair & Nails

- **Knots on fingers (गांठें)** are visible from the back of the hand at the finger joints — some hands show them clearly, others not at all — and are useful for predicting mindset.
- **Hair on the hand (रोएं)** — the small, fine hairs on the fingers and thumb — is naturally more visible on male hands and typically not visible on female hands (though this may also be due to waxing, so it should not be assumed).
- **Nails** give strong health-related predictions and must always be examined; because nail extensions are now common, it should always first be confirmed whether a client's nails are natural or artificial.

## 4. Mounts (पर्वत)

A mount is the raised, "peak-like" fleshy area beneath each finger, named after its associated planet.

| Mount (English) | Hindi name | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Jupiter mount | गुरु पर्वत | Below the Jupiter (index) finger |
| Saturn mount | शनि पर्वत | Below the Saturn (middle) finger |
| Sun mount | सूर्य पर्वत | Below the Sun (ring) finger |
| Mercury mount | बुध पर्वत | Below the Mercury (little) finger, extending across that whole area |
| Lower Mars | निम्न मंगल | Near the thumb, revealed by the crease formed when the thumb is folded toward the palm — between the thumb-crease and the true Venus bulge |
| Venus mount | शुक्र पर्वत | The most prominently raised area near the base of the thumb |
| Upper Mars | उच्च / ऊपरी मंगल | On the Mercury (little-finger) side, below the Mercury mount, above the base of the palm — always counted as Mars regardless of how flat or raised it appears |
| Rāhu's area / Ketu | राहु का पर्वत स्थान / केतु | The central space of the palm (between the finger-mounts above and Venus/Moon below) is Rāhu's area, sometimes called "Middle Mars"; the area specifically between Venus and Moon mounts is called Ketu |

> [!CAUTION]
> **Mounts Don't Have Fixed Boundaries**: A mount's territory is not marked off by a fixed drawn line. Like a plant's roots spreading unpredictably from a seed, a mount's raised area can extend beyond or shrink within its "textbook" boundary. What matters is observing **how full/puffed** a mount is and **which direction it extends toward** — not a fixed geometric boundary.

## 5. Major Lines — Names & Origins Only

At this stage, only the **names** and **points of origin** of the major lines are introduced; full interpretation belongs to the dedicated chapters that follow. There are far more minor lines than major ones, so names and origins are learned first.

| Line (English) | Hindi name | Origin notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Life line** | जीवन रेखा | Outlines/borders the Venus mount; its **origin point** (not its end point) matters most for time-prediction — the endpoint varies widely between hands |
| **Fate line** | भाग्य रेखा | Originates lower in the palm, near the base; may appear as two parallel lines; may end at different points, even mid-palm |
| **Mind line** | बुद्धि रेखा | Generally runs toward the Moon mount, mid-palm; its origin point is the **same origin point as the Life line** |
| **Heart line** | हृदय रेखा | Has its own separate origin point |
| **Marriage lines** | विवाह रेखाएं | Small lines near the base of the little finger/Mercury mount, said to represent one's entire married life despite their tiny size |
| **Mercury line** | बुध रेखा | Not present in every hand; origin varies, but when present it is always directed toward the Mercury mount |
| **Sun line** | सूर्य रेखा | Origin varies; in most hands only a short segment is visible, in some a much longer near-full-length course; directed toward the Sun/ring-finger area |

> [!CASE]
> **A Variant Case — Mercury Line**: In at least one documented hand, the Mercury line appeared only on the left hand, originating at the Life line and running up to the Mind line without extending further — a reminder that a Mercury line's exact origin requires inspecting that hand individually rather than assuming a single fixed starting point.

## 6. Measuring the Palm and Fingers

### Palm vs. Hand

- **Palm** = only the area from the wrist (maṇibaddha rekhā) up to the base of the fingers (the topmost joint line).
- **Hand** = the entire hand including the fingers. This is why the newborn's "square shape" is described as a **hand**, while from age 7 onward classification shifts to the "square **palm**" specifically.

### Measurement Steps

1. **Locate the maṇibaddha rekhā** precisely (tilt the hand ~30° as above).
2. **Palm length** — measure from that point straight up to the last/topmost sandhi rekhā.
3. **Finger length** — measure from the same base point up to the tip of the middle (Saturn) finger, excluding the nail if nails are long.
4. **Palm width** — measured side to side, roughly across the mount area.
5. **Palm shape** — only two shapes are possible:
   - **Rectangular**: length and width are clearly unequal.
   - **Square**: all four sides are approximately equal.

> [!TIP]
> **Handling Close Measurements**: If length and width are very close (e.g. 9 inches vs. 9.5 inches), the palm leans toward being classified as **square**; a larger, clearer gap is classified as **rectangular**. There is no perfectly exact cutoff — approximation improves with practice.

> [!TIP]
> **The "Paper Strip" Method**: For precise or remote readings from a photo, long, thin paper strips can be cut and used to measure against the image on screen. This technique becomes especially important later for **age calculations on the Life and Fate lines**, where even a small measurement error can create roughly a 10-year discrepancy.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Photograph Angle Matters**: When measuring from a photo, it must be taken straight-on from directly above the hand. An angled photo makes the hand/fingers appear artificially elongated, producing inaccurate measurements.

## 7. States of Development: The Four Front-Palm Hand Types

Once palm shape (rectangular/square) and finger length relative to the palm (short/long, compared to the palm, never judged in isolation) are both determined, they combine into four possible front-palm hand types. The back palm has a separate system of 7–8 types, covered elsewhere.

| Palm Shape | Finger Length (relative to palm) | Hand Type |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangular | Short | **Fiery hand** |
| Rectangular | Long | **Watery hand** |
| Square | Short | **Earthy hand** |
| Square | Long | **Airy hand** |

> [!CASE]
> **A Worked Example**: A palm length of roughly 10 cm against a middle-finger length of roughly 8 cm (fingers shorter than the palm) and a rectangular palm shape combine to give a **Fiery hand**.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Hand-Type Quality Match Is Not 100%**: Even once a hand type is determined, its listed personality qualities will typically match the person only about **70%** of the time. Deviations are largely explained by fingerprints, a separate field of study, since fingerprints define much of a person's nature and can even indicate career.

## 8. The Three Branches of Palmistry

Together, three studies make up palmistry as a whole:

| Branch | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Chirognomy** | Study of hand/finger **shapes** — roughly 11–12 shape types across front and back palm combined |
| **Chiromancy** | Study of **lines** (major and minor) and **symbols/markings** |
| **Dermatoglyphics** | Study of **fingerprints** |

## 10. Sanskrit Terms Introduced

- **Sāmudrika Śāstra**: The comprehensive science of physical signs across the body, of which palmistry is one part.
- **Lakṣaṇa Vidyā Śāstra**: Synonym for Sāmudrika Śāstra — the science of discerning character/destiny from physical signs.
- **Hastarekhā Vijñāna / Hastarekhā Śāstra**: Palmistry proper.
- **Maṇibaddha Rekhā / Paṅkaṇa Rekhā**: The wrist lines.
- **Sandhi Rekhā**: Joint lines dividing each finger into phalanges; the topmost is also the "family ring."
- **Pañcāṅgulī Devī**: The goddess of the five fingers, associated with Cheiro's sādhanā.
- **Deśa, Kāla, Paristhiti**: Place, time, and circumstance — the contextual triad that must frame every reading.
- **Graphotherapy**: Deliberately changing one's handwriting to reshape thought patterns and, in turn, the active hand's lines.

## 11. Diagnostic Self-Check

1. Has the hand's shape (rectangular/square) and relative finger length been confirmed before any prediction is attempted?
2. Has the active hand been correctly distinguished from the destiny hand for this specific client?
3. Is this sign being read in light of deśa, kāla, paristhiti, rather than as a rigid, universal rule?

# The Nails: Forms, Textures, Lunula & Clinical Signs
### Nakha Lakṣaṇam · Ardhacandra, Ridges & Constitutional Health

The front palm remains the single most useful source for reading a person's nature, with the back-of-hand shapes studied earlier contributing a second layer — and the nail now adds a third. Many readers lean on nails to describe nature, but that use should be treated with caution; the nail's traditional and more dependable role is reading **health**, in the same way a doctor checks the nails first when assessing illness. Rather than memorising the thirty-nine or forty nail categories that many textbooks list, this chapter follows a simplified system of six basic nail types built from three combinable axes — length, width, and thickness — which, layered onto the hand-element, thumb, and finger readings already covered, can bring a reader's overall accuracy in describing nature to roughly seventy percent.

## 1. Foundational Principles & Anatomy

A nail's ideal size is measured against the first, topmost phalange of the finger it belongs to: the nail should occupy roughly **fifty percent** of that phalange's length. Two things must be excluded when taking this measurement. First, an overgrown nail — one that has simply been allowed to grow long — should not be used as the basis for judgment; only the natural, trimmed length counts. Second, skin overgrowth onto the nail must also be excluded: a healthy nail normally shows a white line near its tip, but in some people the cuticle grows up past this line and covers part of the true nail surface, and this overgrown skin should never be counted as part of the nail itself. Traditional texts describe the generic "normal" nail as wide, long, and pink — useful as a baseline, but the real diagnostic value comes from the specific type categories that follow.

## 2. Structural Axes & Elemental Shape

Nail shape can be approached in two separate ways. The first simply carries over the whole-finger-tip classification taught earlier for fingers — round tips reflecting Air, spatula tips reflecting Fire, square tips reflecting Earth, and conical tips reflecting Water — since the nail sits at the fingertip and naturally echoes the same overall shape. The second examines the nail's own base or growth shape independently of the finger's tip shape: a nail can show a distinctly square base even where the finger's overall tip shape would suggest something else, and the two should be read as separate layers of information.

A firm caution governs how far nail-based nature reading should be taken. Going too deep into nail-derived personality predictions creates confusion and inconsistency with readings already drawn from the hand type and the fingers — a reader cannot issue one single, coherent prediction while pulling simultaneously from too many independent sources without a clear hierarchy among them. The working hierarchy is: nature is read primarily from the front-palm hand-element type, with astrology and other sources blended in as a secondary refinement; nails should be used only in a very limited, general way for nature ("small nails: might be investigative, might be short-tempered"; "large nails: might be creative") and are put to their fullest, most reliable use in reading **health**. A further measurement rule applies throughout the health reading that follows: nail color must always be judged against the **front palm's** skin tone, never the back of the hand, since the back is naturally darker and would distort the comparison.

## 3. States of Development — Length, Width & Thickness

### Length Axis

| State | Physical Sign | Character / Psychological Manifestation | Real-World Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Small / Short** | Visibly short against the 50% benchmark; arises either from nail-biting or naturally | Researcher/investigative mindset — goes deep into any subject engaged with, like a small object travelling farther than a large one dropped under a bed; alert, sharp intuition; reduced emotional expression, higher practicality; hardworking, straightforward, blunt speech | Depth-seeking can tip into nitpicking ("baal ki khaal nikalna"); can turn aggressive/short-tempered when results aren't found quickly, especially paired with a fiery hand; shows impatience; softened by a watery hand or a backward-bending thumb |
| **Large / Long** | Long relative to the benchmark; widely considered aesthetically desirable | Water-element qualities — emotional, caring, sensitive, creative; prefers a relaxed, unhurried working pace | Prone to anxiety that is harder to manage than a small-nailed person's; connects to the "water finger" qualities taught earlier |

### Width Axis

| State | Physical Sign | Character / Psychological Manifestation | Real-World Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wide (Chauṛā)** | Broad at the base, staying wide rather than narrowing; judged on an axis independent of length | Hardworking; sharp-minded, though often with a temper; straightforward/plain-spoken (स्पष्टवादी); adjusts well in any situation; tends toward a thicker, less tapered shape | Can become quarrelsome, especially combined with a small nail |
| **Narrow / Tight (Sankrā / Tang)** | Reduced width along the same axis | Traditional texts link this to timidity (a term the transcript records as likely कायरता, though the exact word was unclear); reinterpreted for the modern context as struggling to take bold steps, hesitating to speak up in front of others, and preferring to stay in the background (the "backbencher"); sensitive, prone to anxiety and stress, easily disturbed | Strongly associated with health issues — described as a significant health indicator |

### Thickness Axis

| State | Physical Sign | Character / Psychological Manifestation | Real-World Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Thick (Moṭā)** | Visually tight and dense; shows none of the thin-nail signs — no curling, no self-breaking, no visible skin showing through | Earth-element qualities — stability, discipline, stubbornness (jid), life stability; takes on responsibility readily; practical, hardworking; sees a decision through once made; prefers a set routine | Regarded as the **best nail type overall**, especially combined with a wide nail |
| **Thin / Papery** | Curls downward or upward as it grows, or breaks repeatedly on its own; traditionally linked to a deficiency of hemoglobin | Excessively sensitive, particularly with a watery hand; with a fiery hand, temper and irritability increase instead, with "overreaction" always ready to surface; can be delicate, with body weight that fluctuates easily | Strongly associated with poor health and stomach issues |
| **Medium** | Neither clearly thick nor clearly thin | Can "handle/balance both," carrying a mix of thick-nail and thin-nail qualities | A moderate profile rather than a pull toward either extreme |

Thick-nail stubbornness is also said to take on a distinct character depending on the hand's underlying element:

| Element | Character of Stubbornness |
|---|---|
| Fire | A very strong desire to complete one's own work or agenda |
| Water | A different kind of stubbornness, which can at times be harmful to the person themselves |
| Earth | Rooted and resistant to change — dislikes disruption to an established way of life |
| Air | Dislikes being disturbed while pursuing research or fine-detail investigation |

## 4. Signs, Markings & Practical Indications

### Combining the Axes

Rather than memorising named categories, the three axes above can be combined directly, and the worked examples below illustrate the method. **Small + Wide** layers the investigative drive of a small nail onto the consistent effort of a wide one, and raises the tendency toward anger or temper. Adding **Thin/Papery** to that same pairing introduces greater sensitivity and a marked worsening of health; with a watery hand the person tends to go quiet or cry heavily when upset, while with a fiery hand the result is greater aggression and hastiness — the small nail's drive to go deep collides with the fire element's restlessness, producing someone who investigates several subjects at once without settling into any one of them, which breeds irritation. **Small + Narrow** combines the researcher temperament with real difficulty presenting or communicating one's findings to others; health issues, especially of immunity, are described as the most fundamental thing a small nail indicates on its own, and a timid, subservient quality can leave such a person feeling internally active yet outwardly left behind — a pattern that intensifies sharply if paired further with a short, clubbed ("murderer") thumb, significantly increasing the risk of uncontrolled aggressive reactions. **Large/Long + Wide** works in a relaxed, unhurried way, carries some anxiety, is highly hardworking, adaptable, and talented, and — being generally outspoken — shows visible anger when feeling unheard; when this pairing becomes additionally thick and rigid, it shifts toward Earth-element practicality and rule-abidance. **Small + Thin** is a combination repeatedly observed across many hands in connection with throat disease, respiratory disease, tuberculosis, and lung issues. **Small + Thick** raises willingness for physical labour specifically, favours a disciplined, timetabled approach to work, balances well against a fiery hand's hastiness, and is considered an especially friction-free pairing with an airy hand. **Long + Thick** works methodically, brings stability and practicality, carries some of the anxiety and emotion of "long," and has also been associated, in some reference sources, with heightened intensity within romantic relationships; overall it balances the emotional and practical sides of life well. **Long + Thin (Papery)** is named explicitly as the **least favourable nail combination overall**: thinness points to health problems and length to heightened emotion, and since both trace to the Water element together they point specifically toward blood-related and hormonal issues, with irritability compounding from both directions.

### Reading Health from the Nail

Four methods carry the health reading: the nail's **color**, the **chandramā** (lunula), the **lines** on the nail (vertical and horizontal), and its overall **shape and texture** (dryness, moisture, and specific patterns such as spoon-shaped or clubbed).

**Spoon-shaped nail.** A mildly concave, scooped nail, present either since childhood or developed from a current health condition. During a person's Guru (Jupiter) antardaśā, nails commonly become somewhat uneven or ridged — a bumpy, alternating pattern linked to protein deficiency that is usually temporary rather than permanent. A spoon-shaped nail overall is not a good health sign: it can indicate thyroid issues, heart disease, anemia, and liver conditions (a liver check is specifically recommended whenever this shape appears), or a general deficiency of protein or vitamins such as D or B12. A minor nail-based issue of this kind typically shows improvement within about six months, though full confirmation of timing requires cross-referencing the heart line and life line (including markers such as islands, covered in a later chapter).

**Clubbed nail.** Distinguished from a small or normal nail by the disappearance of visible side skin — the nail extends fully across, wide and large, with no visible margin, and appears raised or bulging in a manner similar to the clubbed ("murderer's") thumb covered earlier. It can indicate severe disease, including severe and sexual diseases, lung problems such as TB, stomach infection and inflammation (bacterial infection and swelling), constipation, weak immunity, and autoimmune disease.

**Small nail (natural) and nervousness.** This reading applies specifically to naturally small nails, not nails made small by biting. Naturally small nails can indicate underlying nervousness or anxiety. Nail-biting itself is typically driven by tension and nervousness, and the habit is said to weaken the root chakra, which is in turn associated with bone problems, a persistent underlying fear related to security, and possible lower back pain — so people with naturally small nails are advised to stop biting them further, since the habit compounds the underlying issue.

**Dry/crispy nail.** Nails that appear cracked, flaky, and brittle, like dried leaves — moisturiser can restore shine, but nails that remain persistently torn or cracked despite this are "dry" or "crispy." This indicates thyroid issues, hormonal issues, and bacterial infection; combined with a wide nail and a water-element hand, mood swings increase significantly, since the water element's emotional sensitivity compounds with the underlying dryness.

**Vertical lines and fluted nails.** Light, faint vertical lines are generally not a concern and are attributed to ordinary stress; clearly visible vertical lines, of the kind typically seen in old age, suggest a premature or accelerated decline pattern when seen on someone younger; and a **fluted nail**, where these ridges deepen into actual cracks revealing the inner skin structure, is a markedly more serious pattern. Vertical lines generally indicate diseases inherited from the father's side, general stress, zinc deficiency, and vāta-doṣa conditions. A fluted nail more specifically can indicate arthritis (the severe, hand-deforming joint condition) and a risk of brain hemorrhage; if a black mark appears within the fluted pattern, or a star or island forms in a similar area on the Mount of Mars, this further points toward brain-related problems.

**White marks — the exception.** Outside palmistry, a white spot is often read as calcium deficiency, but within this system it is read primarily as **financial gain**, not disease. The nail's full length, base to tip, is treated as representing roughly three months of time, and the mark's position estimates when the gain arrives:

| Mark Position | Estimated Time Until Gain |
|---|---|
| Near the base (lowest point) | About 2.5–3 months |
| Middle of the nail | Roughly 1.5–2 months |
| Close to the tip/edge | Very soon — "about to happen" |
| Already grown to the very edge / gone | May have already occurred |

White marks are considered far less concerning than black marks, which are always a negative health sign; a white mark can reflect something as mild as a minor calcium deficiency or a recent one-off stomach upset.

**Horizontal lines.** Running across the nail rather than up and down, these indicate diseases inherited from the mother's side (in contrast to the father's-side association of vertical lines), and are commonly associated with diabetes or a pre-diabetic condition (best cross-checked against the Jupiter and Venus mounts). They also indicate carried emotional trauma, whether from a past life or from a more recent period of significant distress — a line that remains suggests the person is still holding onto that trauma, while one that fades suggests they are healing from it.

**Broken nail.** Cracking easily, whether from normal growth or from picking/scratching; thick nails do not typically break this way, so the pattern is most associated with thinner nails. It indicates general nutritional deficiency, and if persistent over a long period can point to a more serious underlying illness. A broken nail combined with a black mark can indicate a serious disease, including cancer; without a black mark, the reading stays modest — likely iron deficiency or a general nutritional shortfall, possibly lifelong.

**Inflated proximal cuticles.** The skin at the nail's base appears red, swollen, and thickened, comparable to a finger that has been caught in a door. Most commonly seen, in women, alongside joint pain and back pain (the general category of "old-age type" ailments) — though swelling caused by a known temporary factor, such as a current medication's side effect, should not be counted toward this reading. Three problems are specifically associated: bone/joint issues, lung issues (such as breathlessness when climbing stairs), and general skin problems.

**Chatakhad Jana (chipped/peeling skin at the nail sides).** Skin at the sides of the nail peels or chips away. This commonly indicates iron deficiency and is associated with stomach-related problems, connecting to the Solar Plexus chakra, which governs stomach issues.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **General rule for marks**: any black mark on any nail should be treated as a health-relevant sign tied specifically to whichever finger (and planet) it appears on — a black mark on the Jupiter finger's nail signals a Jupiter-related health issue, on the Saturn finger a Saturn-related one, and so on.

### Nail Color

| Color | Key Associations |
|---|---|
| **Pink** (matching skin tone, ±10–20%) | The healthiest nail color |
| **White** | Blood deficiency (anemia-type), laziness and a boring/lethargic disposition, low immunity, some dullness in nature, possible liver problems; the strongest color-based indicator of depression |
| **Yellow** | Blood-related issues, liver problems, commonly associated with bacterial infection |
| **Red** (a darker shade of pink, or scattered red dots) | Heart-related problems, blood pressure issues, a quick-tempered/aggressive disposition; can indicate depression, though less strongly than white |
| **Black, Blue, or Light Purple** | The most severe color condition; across the whole nail, can indicate a lifelong or end-of-life disease; as an isolated spot, the onset of a disease; associated with excess toxins, a sudden recent appearance should prompt a check for severe food poisoning, and can also indicate oxygen deficiency |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **General rule for any nail abnormality**: always check the liver first, since liver issues recur as an underlying factor across many of the conditions above — though the early signs may be the only thing visible at first.

### Chandramā (Lunula / Half-Moon)

The chandramā is judged by three visual conditions. The **ideal** condition reaches roughly to the point where the nail, divided in half and then in half again, meets that second dividing line — a moderate, well-proportioned crescent. A crescent that is **very small or barely visible** should be read as "as good as absent," in the process of gradually disappearing. A crescent that is **very large**, extending up close to half the nail, is also not favourable, despite appearing to be "more" of a moon.

The chandramā is traditionally a sign of good health, and acts protectively: even where a nail shows several unfavourable signs together — lines, red coloring, and the like — a good chandramā can buffer the person from their full impact. As health declines, the chandramā recedes, disappearing first from the fingers and last from the thumb, which is its most resilient position; if it has vanished from all fingers but remains in the thumb, a liver test is recommended, and as the underlying issue resolves, the chandramā will gradually reappear. An oversized chandramā is, in a secondary sense, also connected to finance — each finger's chandramā traditionally links to money and prosperity, though the full detail is deferred to a later chapter.

### Diagnostic Cautions

> [!CAUTION]
> A visibly bent or crooked finger — as observed in news footage of Rahul Gandhi's hand, and also noted on Nita Ambani's hand — does **not** indicate reduced intelligence. It indicates cunning or cleverness, consistent with the earlier lesson on a crooked finger without knots.

> [!CAUTION]
> When a finger or nail is damaged by an accident, the injury applies with full, hundred-percent effect specifically to that finger's own associated readings — it does not reduce the person's overall intelligence. An injured, nail-affected little (Mercury) finger, for example, affects Mercury-related matters such as marriage and childbirth readings, not intelligence, with full detail deferred to the dedicated Mercury finger chapter.

## 5. Chapter Summary & Comparison Table

| Reading Method | What It Measures | Key Markers |
|---|---|---|
| **Length** | Small/short vs. large/long, against the 50% phalange benchmark | Small = researcher, blunt, impatient; Large = emotional, creative, anxious (Water element) |

| **Shape/Texture** | Spoon, clubbed, dry/crispy, broken, inflated cuticles, chipped sides | Each pattern carries its own distinct disease associations (see Section 4) |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Memory Aids**: Length, width, and thickness are the three combinable axes of nature-reading — always re-applied on top of the hand-element, thumb, and finger readings already learned, since nails alone never give the complete picture. For health: vertical lines trace to the father's side, horizontal lines to the mother's side; a white mark means money (soon), a black mark means a health issue (serious); and a chandramā gone from the fingers but still present in the thumb calls for a liver check. Nail color must always be compared to the front palm, never the back of the hand.

## 6. Diagnostic Self-Check

1. Is the nail being measured at its natural, trimmed length, with any overgrown cuticle skin excluded, before judging it against the 50% benchmark of the first phalange?
2. Where does the nail sit on each of the three independent axes — length, width, and thickness — and how do those positions combine, rather than reading any one axis alone?
3. Does the nail's own base shape match or differ from the overall finger-tip's elemental shape, and is any nature-based conclusion being kept appropriately general rather than over-extended?
4. Has the nail's color been compared against the front palm's skin tone (not the back of the hand), and which of the five color categories does it fall into?
5. What is the chandramā's size and which fingers show it — is it present in the thumb but missing elsewhere, calling for a liver check, or is it unusually oversized?
6. Are any lines present, and are they vertical (father's side), horizontal (mother's side), or fluted (a more serious pattern requiring urgent attention)?
7. Is any mark on the nail black (a finger-specific health warning) or white (financial gain, timed by its position along the nail)?
8. If a finger or nail shows damage from an accident, is the reading being correctly confined to that finger's own specific indications rather than extended to the person's general intelligence or character?

# Children Lines: Fertility, Conception & Parental Responsibility

### Santāna Rekhā · Lines on Marriage Line, Venus & Thumb Base

Santāna Rekhā — the child line — is among the most delicate indications in the entire hand, both in the literal sense that the lines themselves are fine and easily missed, and in the interpretive sense that no single marking may be read in isolation. The reading begins not with the lines at all, but with a discipline: examine the hand's overall quality, study the hands of *both* husband and wife, and weigh the mounts of Venus, Moon, Mars, and Mercury together before a single child line is counted. Only once that groundwork is laid do the texts speak — first on the marriage line, which gives mere *possibility*; then on the family ring, which gives the *number*; and finally across a scatter of secondary signs — the Saturn finger, the life line, the bracelet lines, the Sun-and-Jupiter finger comparison — that refine timing, health, and circumstance. A caution applies with special force here: a troubling sign on the child line is read as a call for medical attention, never as a verdict pronounced to a client.

## 1. Foundational Principles: Reading the Hand Before the Line

A principle carried over directly from astrology applies here: even when a birth chart's fifth house looks favourable, problems in conception can still appear in practice, and the same humility must guide the reading of the hand. Before the child line is approached at all, three disciplines must be observed.

**First, deśa-kāla-paristhiti — place, time, and circumstance.** The śāstra was composed in a different social and medical era, and its predictions must be weighed against the world the client actually lives in.

**Second, examine both hands — husband and wife.** A problem in conception need not sit in one partner alone; it may belong to either, to both, or to neither, and the two hands together tell a more complete story than one hand read in isolation.

**Third, and above all, look at the quality of the hand first — "never skip it."** The nails reveal health conditions that can disrupt conception, such as thyroid imbalance or PCOD/PCOS, and tension and stress are named as the single biggest factor working against childbirth today. Stress now begins early — in the pressure of study and career — and the responsibility of a child adds a further layer of tension on top of it.

### 1.1 Society Has Changed — and So Has the Reading

Generations differ sharply here. Earlier, a child within a year or two of marriage was assumed; today, career comes first and couples are more reluctant to take on the responsibility. Medical science has also transformed the picture — egg and embryo preservation, IVF, and surrogacy now intervene in ways the classical texts never anticipated. The practical consequence for the reader: the chance of a child's death has become lower, but everything connected to conception itself has changed completely. Lines read in an older hand may still match the texts closely; a younger hand, shaped by this different world, will show far more variation, and the reading must flex accordingly.

### 1.2 The Four Mounts That Must Be Checked First

Before a single child line is sought, four mounts are examined together, because each governs a different piece of the picture.

| Mount | What It Shows | Diagnostic Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Śukra Parvata (Venus)** | Family and **desire** | Without desire, a child line is of little use. In the male hand, any cut, cross, or mole on Venus points to a conception problem from the male side. |
| **Candra Parvata (Moon)** | Mind; the **power to conceive** | Examined especially in the female hand. A disturbed Moon mount — a mole or mark — slightly raises the chance of miscarriage or abortion. |
| **Maṅgala (Mars) — inner and outer** | **Action** | Planning a child is itself an act of action, and the process of childbirth begins only once that action is taken. Inner Mars reflects mental action, outer Mars reflects external action; both must be sound. |
| **Budha Parvata (Mercury)** | The **reproductive organs** of both partners | A better-formed Mercury mount means fewer physical problems; the Mercury line, which reflects general health, should also be checked. |

In short: Venus is desire, Moon is mind — especially in the female — Mars is action, and Mercury is the organs. None of the four may be read alone; a collective study across all four, in both hands, is required before any conclusion is offered.

Wherever the problem appears — in the husband's hand or the wife's — the reader may infer which side the difficulty belongs to, bearing in mind that in actual childbirth difficulties, the cause need not sit in both partners; it may lie in one, in both, or in neither.

### 1.3 Responsibility Lines Recalled from the Life Line

A concept carried forward from the Life Line chapter returns here: lines running inward toward the jīvana rekhā (Life Line) represent responsibilities taken on. After marriage, once a career is already established, that new responsibility may take the form of a child, or it may take the form of a husband's family (for instance, a commitment to care for a mother-in-law without hired help). The child-birth indication can therefore also surface as a responsibility line — and such a line need not be permanent; it may appear for a period and then fade, tracking the heavier workload that falls on a mother, and at times on a father, while the children are young. This is generally seen most clearly in the mother's hand.

## 2. The Two Primary Indications: Marriage Line and Family Ring

Two distinct locations on the hand carry the weight of the child-line reading, and they answer two different questions. The marriage line answers *whether* children are possible; the family ring answers *how many*.

### 2.1 Indication One — Child Lines Above the Marriage Line

The earliest indication of childbirth appears on the vivāha rekhā itself, in the form of lines so fine that a lens — or even ink applied to the hand — may be needed to see them clearly. Reading them correctly requires a careful process of elimination.

1. Downward lines from the marriage line were taught in the Marriage Line chapter as disturbance lines of married life — **do not count them** as child lines.
2. Only **upward lines** — rising above the marriage line, not descending from it — are child lines.
3. Small, short lines **do not count**.
4. Count only the **clear-cut, long, standing line(s)**.
5. Where many lines of varying darkness crowd the region, remove the downward ones, remove the short ones, and of the upward branches that remain, take only the long ones.

> [!IMPORTANT] What This Indication Actually Gives
> This sign gives only **yogyatā** — capacity or possibility — of childbirth, **never the exact number**. The practical guidance to a client is to speak only in general terms: one or two, at most three, never more precisely than that.

> [!CASE] Gender and Multiplicity
> A **slightly darker, thicker** line indicates a male child; a **lighter** line indicates a female child, as stated in the śāstras. More than one line can also indicate a **miscarriage** rather than simply "more children" — one example hand, carrying more than one line, illustrates this point.

**The grid method (now largely retired).** The Mercury mount area can be divided into four vertical and four horizontal sections, and the marriage line should reach at least into this marked grid for the lines within it to be considered child lines — a technique meant to avoid confusing the child line with the health line or the marriage line itself. This parts-division was used in the past but is no longer the working method; today, the practice is simply to look for long, clear lines and report one or two children.

![Fig 1. Example Shown](/lecture-notes-images/30/01-method-1.png)

Example: in one case, a hand showed only one clear line, and the person in question has one son — matching the principle that a single, clearly standing line corresponds to a single clear indication. A hand carrying more than one line, by contrast, can also point toward a miscarriage alongside live births.

### 2.2 Indication Two — The Family Ring

> [!NOTE] What the Family Ring Is
> The family ring is the line that runs at the base of the thumb, marking the bifurcation between the thumb and the Venus mount. There is a bone near the thumb side and another bone on the Venus-mount side; the ring itself separates the two regions.

The second, and more decisive, indication for the *number* of children appears in two places: directly on the family ring, and in the area above the family ring running up to the thumb-side bone.

![Fig 2. Example Shown, Has 2 Kids & 1 Miscarriage](/lecture-notes-images/30/02-example.png)

**Islands on the family ring.** A marking called an island is read as negative almost everywhere else on the palm, but on the family ring it is read as very good. The number of clear islands equals the number of children — though it does not reveal their gender. An island must be clear-cut; where many islands appear but are heavily cut or crossed, they do not count as an indication of childbirth. The presence or absence of an island here also speaks to attachment: no island-like marking suggests less emotional bond with the children, even where they exist ("children are born, fine, we'll pay their fees; no bond"). A chain-type formation on the ring counts the same as an island.

**Upward vertical lines between the ring and the bone.** In the stretch running from the family ring up to the thumb-side bone, the clear upward vertical lines are counted as the number of children; the small, faint ones are disregarded. In one example hand, three clear lines appeared: two children and one miscarriage — two lines were complete, one was broken, illustrating that the count here can include a miscarriage alongside live children. Many books restrict this reading to the female hand alone, since only the female carries the power to bear a child, but in practice both hands can be taken.

> [!NOTE] Summary
> First, establish **possibility** from the upward lines above the marriage line. Second, establish **how many children** from the islands on the family ring and the lines between the ring and the bone. When a client asks "how many children?", the answer is generally drawn from the islands, read as clearly as they appear.

## 3. Structural Variations: Sibling Lines, the Girdle of Venus, and Related Regions

### 3.1 Sibling Lines (Bhāī-Bahan Rekhā)

![Fig 3. Siblings Line](/lecture-notes-images/30/03-siblings-lines.png)

Below the family ring, running down toward the Venus mount as far as the point where the life line joins — another bone sits here — the upward lines rising from this region are sibling lines. Because the Venus mount represents the whole family rather than blood relations alone, these lines need not refer only to biological siblings. Some lines are very prominent; others are small, broken, or start and stop. The more prominent lines can represent cousins with whom there is greater attachment — the degree of attachment to a person is read directly in how clearly the corresponding line appears.

> [!CASE] Case Example
> In one hand, three prominent lines corresponded to three siblings (four in total including the hand's owner), while the lines that start, break, and continue reflect people with whom contact has been occasional.

> [!CASE] A Close-Knit Family
> One hand showed a very large number of sibling lines. The facts behind it: three siblings and roughly twenty-eight cousins in total (one parent had five brothers and two sisters, each with three to four children of their own; the other side added three more siblings with two children each) — a genuinely close-knit extended family. Such a profusion of lines reflects either deep concern or deep attachment, matching the family's closeness.

### 3.2 Girdle of Venus

Observed on the same example hand, the Girdle of Venus begins in the region between the Mercury and Sun fingers and arcs across to the region between the Jupiter and Saturn fingers, forming a ring-like shape. It marks a person as over-sensitive, prone to possessiveness and a controlling nature — trying to manage others' lives — as well as over-protective, while also lending creativity. Whether the ring expresses mainly its positive or negative side depends on other factors in the hand, such as overall hand quality; a harder-type hand intensifies the controlling tendency.

## 4. Signs, Markings & Practical Indications

### 4.1 Garbhapāta Yoga (Miscarriage/Abortion Yoga)

![Fig 4. Cross on Child Line](/lecture-notes-images/30/04-cross-on-child-line.png)

> [!CAUTION] Garbhapāta Yoga
> A small, fine cross or star-like mark at the **start** of a child line, sitting on the marriage line itself, constitutes garbhapāta yoga — indicating a miscarriage or abortion in the early months. If the cross instead sits at the **top end** of the child line, the reading shifts toward no possibility of a child at all; even where conception occurs, the pregnancy may be carried for some time before a problem arises around the eighth or ninth month. Where a cross forms **and a line joins it**, the śāstra reads this as **akāla mṛtyu** — the child is born but later suffers an untimely or accidental death.

### 4.2 Dattaka Putra Yoga (Adopted Child)

Adoption arises in the hand through a distinct combination: a **slightly raised Saturn mount**, together with a **slightly raised upper Mars**, joined by a connecting line, constitutes dattaka putra yoga. Adoption itself can stem from two very different causes in practice: either the person does not wish to have a biological child (a pattern increasingly observed among younger women wary of the physical toll), or childbirth simply is not occurring. The texts speak only of a *putra* (son) owing to the male-dominated society in which they were composed, a bias astrology shares. As a further combination, many crosses on the Venus mount, indicating trouble in married life, are read generally as contributing to childbirth difficulty.

### 4.3 Timing and the Responsibility Line

Reading the life line after marriage for inward branches — responsibility lines — or for a thin line running alongside the life line (sometimes called, half-jokingly, a "parasite line," though a child is obviously not a parasite) gives a sense of when the burden of a child arrives and how long it lasts. A child's responsibility is substantial: in many other countries it tapers off within a few years, but in India it typically continues even once the children themselves are married with children of their own. A soft hand corresponds to stronger attachment to the child, and a family ring in good condition likewise points to strong attachment — parents who continue to worry even once their children have children.

> [!CASE] Lines Change With the Mind
> Lines of this kind can fade once the underlying mindset shifts — once a parent consciously decides, "my children are settled, now I will live my own life," the old lines fade. Example: an island appeared on one hand's Mercury line (health) alongside a small disturbance on the life line; the underlying health issue lasted six to seven months, but the island itself persisted for a full year afterward, reflecting how long the illness lingered, and had only begun disappearing in the last four to five months as the body fully recovered. This is offered as proof that lines change through mindset. A simple practice of manifestation follows from it: photograph the hand, hold a strong intention day and night, and expect impressions to begin showing after three to five months, the same timeframe any remedy is given to work.

### 4.4 Bracelet Lines (Maṇibandha Rekhā)

![Fig 5. Manibaddha Rekha Example](/lecture-notes-images/30/05-manibaddha.png)

The bracelet lines are flagged here for fuller treatment later, but one indication relevant to this chapter is given now: in the female hand, if the **first bracelet line rises upward** (ascending before curving back down) rather than running cleanly across, this is not favourable for conception, and points to the problem originating from the female side.

### 4.5 Further Indications Across the Hand

**Mercury finger and mount.** A short Mercury finger — noticeably shorter than the Sun finger's top phalange — indicates low interest both in children and in physical intimacy with one's partner; this reading is only applied when the Mercury finger is unusually short relative to the rest of the hand, not when all the fingers are uniformly thin. A mole on the Mercury mount, or a clear, unconnected jālī (grille) formed by crossing lines on the mount, is unfavourable. A smaller Mercury mount area — caused by a low-set finger whose joint line cuts into the mount — points toward trouble in childbirth.

**Jupiter mount and finger.** Jupiter is the putra kāraka (significator of children), so the whole Jupiter finger and mount must be examined. A problem in the Jupiter finger is read as trouble specifically regarding a male child, though the texts differ somewhat on this point. A Jupiter mount in good condition indicates no trouble in childbirth. Both hands should be read for these conditions — though the Moon mount carries extra weight in the female hand specifically, since it reflects the power of conception.

**Marriage line turning upward.** A marriage line that curves upward first suggests no marriage at all; where marriage has occurred, it instead indicates a lack of interest in the partner — and where there is no interest in married life, the blunt reading is: forget childbirth.

**Life line and infertility.** A short life line indicates infertility. Specifically, if the life line stops before reaching the Venus mount — ending instead at lower Mars — this is read as a sign of infertility, applicable to both male and female hands, connected to the absence of the Y chromosome. The age at which the life line ends should always be checked.

**Sun finger vs. Jupiter finger.** Viewed from the front of the hand, comparing the Sun and Jupiter fingers only (never against Saturn) offers a secondary sūtra on gender and count.

| Condition | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Both fingers equal in length** | Two sons or two daughters; or two to four children in equal measure (two sons and two daughters, or one of each) |
| **Sun finger longer than Jupiter** | Greater possibility of a male child |
| **Jupiter finger longer than Sun** | Condition favouring a female child |

The reasoning given is that while Jupiter generally signifies a male child in astrology, by the Kālapuruṣa scheme the Sun represents the male child instead — hence a longer Sun finger tilts toward a son. This sūtra is explicitly not to be used alone to declare a child's gender; it is offered for knowledge rather than as a standalone verdict.

### 4.6 The Saturn Finger and the Branching Child Line

A cross on the **middle phalanx of the Saturn finger**, checked mainly in the female hand, is a further indication of no child — but this sign alone must never be the basis for a conclusion; the whole hand's condition and overall quality must also be weighed. In practice, where such a sign appears, the advisable guidance is that the couple may need medical help, rather than a direct statement that there will be no child.

The way a child line branches carries its own set of meanings.

| Formation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| One branch going downward, or one going upward, from a child line | Twins (juṛwā santān) |
| The same branching on both sides at once | The same indication, and also the possibility of more than one child born together |
| Branches continuing from branches, extending further | Many children together — for instance, six or seven at once |

> [!CAUTION] A Common Misreading
> "More than one child" from branching does **not** mean two separate lines side by side — two distinct lines were already taught to mean two children. Here, the distinguishing feature is **one line giving off another from itself**, indicating children arriving at the same time.

Twins carry a second layer of meaning: a possible excess of some body part in the child — an extra finger or toe, for example. Exactly what form this will take is difficult to say in advance. The classical texts describe this configuration as producing a healthy child, though this is not a guarantee of long life; a branching child line of this kind is not considered a good sign for the child.

### 4.7 Quality and Darkness of the Child Line

As with every line in the hand, the child line must be judged by its colour, darkness, and depth — and it should not be very dark. A child line need not be uniformly dark through its length; it may begin thin and darken later, or the reverse, and each pattern carries a distinct reading.

| Pattern Along the Line | Reading |
|---|---|
| Light at the start, darker later | The start of the line corresponds to the child's birth and early years; a weaker start may mean health trouble at birth (such as a stomach upset) that improves as the child grows |
| Very dark at the start | Trouble in the child's health at the start, improving later if the line then turns normal or light |
| Very dark, then turning light | A different prediction from the pattern above — read on its own merits |
| Normal darkness throughout, then turning healthy | A further distinct reading, again not to be collapsed into the other patterns |

> [!CASE] Clarifying the Reading
> A line very dark at the start and light afterward should not simply be read as "the child falls ill later" — the condition of the rest of the line must be examined first. A very dark start is itself unfavourable and points to trouble **at the start**, with the remainder of the line then read separately to see how matters proceed from there.

### 4.8 Rules for Symbols on the Child Line and Nearby

- Any negative mark — an island, cross, cut, or sudden break — is noted wherever it falls on a line.
- A **cross is favourable only on the Jupiter mount**; nowhere else.
- An **island is favourable only on the family ring**; nowhere else.
- All other impure signs remain unfavourable wherever they appear on the palm.
- The triangle and square are the most positive signs and are commonly found; the cross is also easy to find. Rarer signs include the swastika, lotus, canopy, and trident.

### 4.9 Rings Under the Fingers

A ring under a finger is read as a distinct, separately-positioned mark — a line merely passing through on its own path, such as a fate line crossing between the heart and head lines, or a Sun line crossing a finger ring, does not count as a cross or symbol in its own right.

| Ring | Favourable Reading | Unfavourable Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Saturn (under the Saturn finger)** | Aptitude for research and spirituality | Introverted, isolated, headstrong, overly practical, avoids society; check the heart line alongside it — ending at Saturn worsens the reading, reaching Jupiter softens it, and a watery hand makes the person more emotional about it |
| **Sun/Apollo** | Creativity, talent, name and fame | An excessive desire for name and fame, tipping into stubbornness and ego |
| **Mercury** | A mind suited to business | Rare; associated with lower moral values and a tendency toward fraud or risky behaviour; combined with a murderer's-type thumb, a Jupiter finger not tilted toward Saturn, and a very active Mars, this can fall into a seriously negative category |

The Ring of Solomon, belonging to Jupiter, was covered in its own chapter and is not repeated here.

### 4.10 Case Studies

> [!CASE] A 30-Year-Old Married Woman, No Child Yet
> A married female client, about thirty years old, with no child yet, was read from a shared photograph. The reading began, as always, with the hand overall: mental condition via the heart line, and the starting condition of the life line.
>
> ![Fig 6. 30F Without Child As of 21/10/2026](/lecture-notes-images/30/05-example.png)
>
> The heart line was very dark, chain-like, and uneven; the life line was long, with its starting condition requiring attention; the Mercury mount was not active or in good condition. Two marriage lines were visible with crosses but no vertical line — the lower lines present were downward disturbance lines, not child lines. On the family ring, one line carried only a single tiny, faint island-like mark, with the rest appearing as crosses, and two lines showed a slight break in the middle.
>
> The reading given was that a possibility of one child exists, but it is small, and the hand overall showed very high stress — a factor that further reduces the possibility. The advice was to reduce stress, since lines of this kind can change within five to six months, and to ask the client about any earlier health problems. The client was independently confirmed to have IBS, with all medical check-ups otherwise clear and doctors attributing it to stress — reinforcing the reading that emotional and mental stress needed to be addressed before childbirth became more likely.

> [!CASE] A Female Client, Age 30, Read in Full
> A more detailed case followed on the same theme: a thirty-year-old housewife, married at twenty-two, connected to a spiritual organisation before her marriage and drawn toward Kṛṣṇa bhakti. She had briefly worked in a corporate role and then teaching before choosing to be a housewife; her married life was described as "normal," though without strong attachment between husband and wife. Health-wise, she suffered from IBS and lower back pain.
>
> The heart line showed a different colour from the rest of the hand and appeared broad, read as tension and as married-life issues — since the question concerned childbirth, both family and married life needed examination together. The same colouring appeared in the left (prārabdha) hand, meaning it was written into her circumstances rather than something changeable by her own effort. The life line showed a long struggling stretch before widening — read as a jolt followed by expansion, though the expansion itself was not very fruitful since the line remained dark through it; a large island on the life line was read as the client having grown used to a difficult situation rather than it still actively affecting her. The Jupiter finger was low-set compared with Saturn, reinforcing the early-life struggle. The Moon mount was not raised and carried too many lines, indicating poor quality.
>
> On the family ring, more than one ring was present, with one clear and the other faint and marked by blackish disturbance; near it, after marking the age of thirty on the life line using the age-ruler method, that point fell in the middle of a very large island running to roughly age forty-one — itself read as ongoing trouble, alongside excessive, disturbed Mars lines and numerous family-disturbance lines cutting across the hand. The overall reading was that a miscarriage had likely occurred, and that chances of childbirth still existed but required medical help, with roughly two years given as a timeframe; the advice included consulting a doctor and examining the husband's hand and Moon mount as well.

> [!CASE] A Male Client, Approximately 45, Gurgaon
> A second case, unrelated to childbirth but illustrative of reading the family ring and surrounding signs together, concerned a wealthy, soft-handed male client in his mid-forties. His family ring showed one large, clear chain. His life line, though appearing short at first glance, joined the fate line and was judged complete once its fainter continuation was accounted for. Small islands appeared on the Moon mount, and a line curving through that region was read as the liver line — the "sister line" of the Mercury line — pointing toward health concerns, often associated with drinking, and prompting a question about possible addiction. A bluish tint in the palm was read as active Rāhu influence, intensified by a running Mars–Saturn daśā. Yellowish discolouration at the nail base and vertical ridging on the nails reinforced a liver-and-kidney health concern, leading to advice for medical check-ups and more exercise.

## 5. Chapter Summary & Comparison Table

Santāna Rekhā rewards patience and refuses shortcuts: the hand's overall quality, the condition of Venus, Moon, Mars, and Mercury, and the attachment between partners must all be weighed before the marriage line and family ring are even consulted. The marriage line's upward branches establish only possibility; the family ring — through its islands and its upward lines toward the thumb-side bone — establishes number, including miscarriages alongside live births. Secondary signs across the Saturn finger, the life line, the bracelet lines, and the Sun-Jupiter finger comparison refine the picture further, while a disciplined understanding of which symbols are favourable where (cross only on Jupiter, island only on the family ring) prevents misreading markings that would be unfavourable anywhere else on the palm.

| Indication | Location | What It Reveals |
|---|---|---|
| Upward lines above the marriage line | Marriage line, Mercury mount area | Possibility (yogyatā) of childbirth, not the exact number |
| Islands on the family ring | Base of the thumb | Number of children, including miscarriages; also attachment |
| Upward lines between family ring and thumb-side bone | Base of the thumb | Confirms number of children |
| Upward lines below the family ring | Toward the Venus mount, near the life line | Sibling lines (not necessarily blood siblings) |
| Fine cross at the start of a child line | On the marriage line | Garbhapāta yoga (miscarriage/abortion) |
| Cross at the top end of a child line | Above the marriage line | No possibility of a child; or late-pregnancy complication |
| Cross joined by a line | On a child line | Akāla mṛtyu (child born, dies prematurely) |
| Raised Saturn + raised upper Mars + joining line | Saturn mount, upper Mars | Dattaka putra yoga (adoption) |
| Short life line ending at lower Mars | Life line | Infertility |
| First bracelet line rising upward | Wrist | Conception difficulty from the female side |
| Cross on Saturn finger's middle phalanx | Saturn finger | Indication of no child (never read alone) |
| One branch (up or down) from a child line | On the child line | Twins; branches of branches = multiple births |

## 6. Sanskrit Terms Introduced

- **Santāna Rekhā** — the Child Line
- **Deśa-Kāla-Paristhiti** — place, time, and circumstance; the interpretive framework applied before any reading
- **Garbhapāta Yoga** — the miscarriage/abortion combination
- **Akāla Mṛtyu** — untimely or accidental death
- **Dattaka Putra Yoga** — the combination indicating an adopted child
- **Kuṭumba Valaya** — the family ring at the base of the thumb
- **Bhāī-Bahan Rekhā** — sibling lines
- **Maṇibandha Rekhā** — the bracelet lines at the wrist
- **Putra Kāraka** — the significator of children (Jupiter)

## 7. Diagnostic Self-Check

1. A client's marriage line shows two clear downward lines and one clear upward line above it. How many of these count as child-line indications, and what does the upward line alone tell you — possibility, or an exact number?
2. On the family ring, you find two clear, uncut islands and, in the region between the ring and the thumb-side bone, three upward lines — two complete and one visibly broken. What number would you report to the client, and how would you account for the broken line?
3. A child line begins with a small, fine cross exactly where it meets the marriage line. What yoga does this represent, and how would your reading differ if that same cross appeared instead at the line's upper end, or if a line were seen joining the cross?

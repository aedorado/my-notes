# 2. Graha: Basic Definition and Classification

> [!INFO] Definition
> A **graha** is that which is seen **moving** on the rāśi-cakra (zodiac).

- **Neptune and Pluto are not studied.** Reason given: they come to the zodiac once in about **125 years** and then leave it, so they cannot be computed. Teacher: they come in between, but even NASA forgets them, so it is proper for us to forget them too.
- **Sun and Moon** need separate understanding. Verse recited (as transcribed): *bhānu pitā sa jantūnām candro mātā tadaiva ca* **[wording unclear in transcript]**. Meaning: the Sun is the father of all living beings and the Moon the mother; understand them like mother and father.
- Sun and Moon are *tārā* (stars), not grahas, but Jyotiṣa calls them grahas.
- The other five: **Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn**, also called *pañcatārā* in some places. They need separate study because each shows one of the five elements:

| Graha | Element (tattva) |
|---|---|
| Mars | Fire (agni) |
| Mercury | Earth (pṛthvī) |
| Jupiter | Space (ākāśa) |
| Venus | Water (jala) |
| Saturn | Air (vāyu) |

- Nine grahas = Sun + Moon (2) + five + Rahu–Ketu (2).
- Everything in the world (objects, living beings, trees and plants) and every family member is allotted to some graha. The more the grahas are expanded, the easier Jyotiṣa becomes.

# 3. Nakṣatra and Rāśi as Milestones

## 3.1 Measures
- Zodiac = **360°**. Nakṣatra = **0° to 13°20′**; rāśi = **0° to 30°**. Both act like milestones on the zodiac at equal distances.
- 360° ÷ 12 = 30° per rāśi. 360° ÷ 27 = 13°20′ per nakṣatra.
- Second nakṣatra runs from 13°20′ to 26°40′ (and so on).

> [!EXAMPLE] Teacher's derivation of 13°20′
> 1. 360 ÷ 27 = 13, remainder 9°.
> 2. Convert the remainder 9° to minutes: 9 × 60 = **540′** (the Teacher said "multiply by seven"; see Open items).
> 3. 540′ ÷ 27 = **20′**. So one nakṣatra = **13°20′**.
> 4. Each nakṣatra has **4 caraṇas (quarters)**. 13°20′ = 800′ (13° = 780′, +20′). 800′ ÷ 4 = 200′ = **3°20′** per caraṇa **[the Teacher's conversion back to degrees was garbled in the transcript]**.

## 3.2 Why 108
- 27 nakṣatras × 4 caraṇas = **108**. This is why we chant a **108-bead mālā**: a graha may be in any nakṣatra or caraṇa; if we chant 108 times we reach it; not knowing where, we chant the whole mālā.
- Also 12 rāśis × 9 grahas = 108.
- A student (Vishal) asked whether this is a coincidence. Teacher: **not a coincidence; this is the reason.**

## 3.3 Geocentric vs heliocentric
- Science places the **Sun at the centre**; Jyotiṣa takes the **Earth as the centre (bhū-kendrita)** and the other grahas move around it. The Sun does not actually move; it must be seen as moving in relative motion, like the Sun seeming to go from east to west while the Earth rotates.
- Teacher: "Only this much needs to be understood" (answer to a student's question).

## 3.4 Milestone analogy
- On a road from one city to another, milestones at 30, 25, 20 km are placed at a regular distance. In the same way, nakṣatras are placed at regular distances and **measure the entire zodiac**: Aśvinī first, then Bharaṇī, …, the 27th is **Revatī**, also 13°20′.
- **Homework:** the Teacher showed the angular extent of the first nine nakṣatras; students should work out all 27 themselves (by hand once, so it stays in the mind), with the remaining two sets of nine done by themselves.

> [!QUESTION] Why do both nakṣatra and rāśi exist as milestones? (Vishal)
> Teacher: take it as one milestone at 1 km and another at 5 km; nakṣatra's milestone is at 13°20′ and rāśi's at 30°. It will become clearer when grahas are studied in nakṣatras (nakṣatra praveśa and rāśi praveśa). Vishal added that since nakṣatras get split among rāśis, he wondered why it was not made simpler; he accepted that it will be understood later.

## 4.2 Gaṇḍānta (breaking point)
> [!IMPORTANT] Teacher's explanation
> - At the end of Karka, **Aśleṣā's four caraṇas end together with the rāśi**: a *breaking point*. This is where **gaṇḍānta** originates (lagna gaṇḍānta and nakṣatra gaṇḍānta will be taught later).

> - A child born in Aśleṣā or Maghā is born in **mūla**. After **27 days (sātaisā)** a puja is done again: called **mūla śānti**.
> - The same occurs after every nine nakṣatras (the Teacher: nine nakṣatras here, then it ends; nine more there, then it ends): at the ninth position both rāśi and nakṣatra end, forming gaṇḍānta.

# 5. Rāśi: Nature (Guṇa-svarūpa)

## 5.1 Why rāśis differ
- Rāśis are formed from groups of stars in the sky (the Teacher mentioned that Aśvinī looks like a horse); this much is enough.
- **Analogy:** the world's weather and people differ by place (Delhi hot, Shimla cold, J&K cold; food and appearance differ). On a road, near Meerut/Ghaziabad you say "be careful, the area is not good", at Haridwar "no problem, a religious town", and every 10–20 km you describe the area differently (dense forest, elephants on the road).
- So Meṣa and Vṛṣa differ; every rāśi has its own guṇa-svarūpa; understanding them makes the subject easier.

> [!SUCCESS] Principle (to build belief, as the Teacher put it)
> Whatever place's ownership a graha holds, that graha's nature will be in accordance with that place.

## 5.2 The twelve rāśis by number
1 Meṣa, 2 Vṛṣa, 3 Mithuna, 4 Karka, 5 Siṁha, 6 Kanyā, 7 Tulā, 8 Vṛścika, 9 Dhanu, 10 Makara, 11 Kumbha, 12 Mīna.

> [!TIP] Memory aid given by the Teacher
> **"Look at the number, call it by name"** (*dekhnā aṅka hai, pukārnā nām hai*). When bhāvas are written, numbers (not names) are written in the chart; learn rāśis by number (1 Meṣa, 2 Vṛṣa, 3 Mithuna, 4 Karka, 5 Siṁha, …). Rāśi-to-graha division will come later; for now only the nature of rāśis.

## 5.3 Odd/even: Puruṣa–Strī, Krūra–Saumya

| Odd (viṣama) | Even (sama) |
|---|---|
| 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 | 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 |
| **Puruṣa (male) rāśi** | **Strī (female) rāśi** |
| **Krūra (cruel/fierce)** | **Saumya (gentle)** |

- Even rāśis: Vṛṣa, Karka, Kanyā, Vṛścika, Makara, Mīna.
- Use of puruṣa/strī rāśi: where male/female must be judged (will the child be a son or daughter; will the boss be male or female). For children: first look at the **rāśi in the 5th**, the rāśi of the **5th lord** and the rāśi of the **kāraka Jupiter**: are they male or female rāśis.
- Krūra = angry. Analogy given: when two cars scrape at a crossing, men leave the steering and shout; women hold their hand and say "come home"; women also fight but at home. So strī rāśi is saumya and puruṣa rāśi is krūra.
- Application: Meṣa lagna or Meṣa rāśi: "you may get angry" can be said (how to separate lagna and rāśi comes later).

> [!EXAMPLE] Female with a male rāśi in lagna
> - **Who:** A woman with Mithuna rāśi in the lagna (hypothetical, given by Teacher).
> - **Interpretation given:** her nature will be **puruṣocita** (masculine): courageous, fearless, bold. 
> - **Reasoning:** the lagna rāśi is a puruṣa rāśi. Teacher qualified: it is not that women are not courageous, but men take more courageous work, have more daring, can carry more weight; women are generally gentler, simple, calm, somewhat timid.

> [!QUESTION] Why is odd called puruṣa and not even? (Chetan Chaudhary)
> A: Teacher: "**This we do not know.** The odd one has been called puruṣa."

## 5.4 Three prakṛtis: Cara, Sthira, Dvisvabhāva
- Three natures; 12 ÷ 3 = 4 rāśis in each. Distribution by number:

| Prakṛti | Meaning | Rāśis |
|---|---|---|
| **Cara (movable)** | mind moves: wants one thing now, another soon | 1, 4, 7, 10 |
| **Sthira (fixed)** | fixed: if going to a movie, goes; study, then study | 2, 5, 8, 11 |
| **Dvisvabhāva (dual)** | cannot decide on his own; depends on someone | 3, 6, 9, 12 |

- Method: distribute 1, 2, 3 in order and continue (or add 3 each time: 1+3=4, 4+3=7, 7+3=10; 2→5→8→11; 3→6→9→12). "Whatever formula you remember."
- Dvisvabhāva illustration: "let's go play" → "let's go to a movie" → "mummy will scold" → "ask mummy". It is also called *common* rāśi (the Teacher also said "well time" **[unclear in transcript]**).
- **Anger examples:**
  - **1 (Meṣa)**: puruṣa + krūra + cara → anger comes, but goes; does not stay.
  - **5 (Siṁha)**: puruṣa + krūra + sthira → anger stays; may last a day or two; can trouble us.
  - Dvisvabhāva puruṣa (the Teacher referred to the puruṣa rāśi in the dvisvabhāva group): anger sometimes on the same matter, sometimes not.
- In each prakṛti there are **2 puruṣa and 2 strī** rāśis. Only three prakṛtis exist; there is no fourth.

Grahas change the nature: Mars in Meṣa and Jupiter in Meṣa differ. **Do not ask "only Jupiter or only Meṣa?"** Coordinate everything; then what to say will come to mind. That is what the course teaches.

> [!QUESTION] On whom does a dvisvabhāva person depend: mother, spouse, child? (Raunak, with Vishal's follow-up)

## 5.5 Four elements in rāśis
> [!IMPORTANT] Five elements exist, but rāśis are divided into **four**
> Five elements (agni, pṛthvī, vāyu, jala, ākāśa) are spoken of, but with only 12 rāśis they cannot be split into five; so four elements are used, **3 rāśis per element**. Adding 4 each time: 

| Element | Rāśis | Cara | Sthira | Dvisvabhāva |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agni (fire) | 1, 5, 9 | 1 | 5 | 9 |
| Pṛthvī (earth) | 2, 6, 10 | 10 | 2 | 6 |
| Vāyu (air) | 3, 7, 11 | 7 | 11 | 3 |
| Jala (water) | 4, 8, 12 | 4 | 8 | 12 |

*(The prakṛti column is the earlier cara/sthira/dvisvabhāva numbering applied to each element's rāśis; the Teacher spoke the sequence orally, in part garbled in the transcript. The "implied" assignment is arithmetic from his own numbering.)*

- Teacher: every element has all three prakṛtis (not one prakṛti per element). "Beauty": the arrangement of rāśis is very artful; you will be surprised as you study.
- **Meṣa** natives (puruṣa + fire + cara): **krūra**, with **aggression in nature — certain**.
- **Siṁha** (sthira + agni + krūra): anger stays; hard to appease. **Sthira** work is to stand firm on one's words, be obstinate (*jiḍ*); "what we said, we stand by".

> [!EXAMPLE] Reading obstinacy from a chart
> - **Who:** the students' own charts (Teacher's instruction).
> - **Chart:** lagna in rāśi number **2** and the Moon in rāśi number **5**.
> - **Interpretation given:** the native is **obstinate (jiddī/aḍiyal)**; very hard to change one's views; at home this is often not good ("today's world says sorry quickly; one with a sthira rāśi who doesn't want to say sorry will not").

# 8. Rāśi Forms (Ākṛti)

# 14. Principles Compilation

## A. Course method and learning rules
1. Learn the four limbs first, in order: nakṣatra, rāśi, bhāva, graha; study them "one word at a time".
2. Nakṣatra is the *mūla* (root): needed later for daśā, sāḍhesātī, pāyā and gocara; the 27 nakṣatras must be memorized (by rote if needed) and by number.
3. Do **not** memorize combinations; understand why a result arises (logical and philosophical approach).
4. A question is answered first from the **bhāva**, then the **graha**; keep the circle small and go one by one.
5. **Listen carefully to the question** before deciding which bhāva and graha to use.
6. Apply any interpretation to charts to test it: if it applies in 6–7 of 10 charts, it is sound.
7. *Tāratamyena vācyāni*: weigh all the factors against each other (more than one possible, weigh all) and then speak; coordinate bhāva, graha, rāśi.
8. In the bhāva chart, say the **bhāva number**, not the rāśi number, for a place (rāśi 7 in the first house = first bhāva). Look at numbers, speak names.

## B. Graha basics
9. A graha is what is seen moving on the zodiac; Neptune and Pluto are not studied (they come once in ~125 years).
10. Sun and Moon are *tārā* but called grahas; Sun = father, Moon = mother of all living beings.
11. Elements in grahas: Mars—fire, Mercury—earth, Jupiter—space (ākāśa), Venus—water, Saturn—air.
12. Rahu and Ketu: no physical body, no ownership; sit as king in the bhāva in which they sit; treated as servants; later.
13. One-word kārakatva: Sun—ātmā; Moon—mana; Mars—bala; Mercury—buddhi; Jupiter—jñāna; Venus—kāmanā; Saturn—duḥkha.
14. Trees: Sun—timber trees; Moon—milky/sap trees; Mars—thorny, pointed, crooked; Mercury—leafy green plants without flowers; Jupiter—fruit trees; Venus—flowering trees; Saturn—wild bushes.

## C. Nakṣatra, caraṇa, rāśi measures

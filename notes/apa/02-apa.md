## 2. What the nakṣatra gives: rāśi and daśā

The teacher said two things come from the **nakṣatra (lunar mansion)**: the **rāśi (sign)** and the **daśā (planetary period)**.

### 2.1 Rāśi from nakṣatra (examples given)

- Moon in Aśvinī, Bharaṇī, or the first **caraṇa (quarter)** of Kṛttikā → **Meṣa (Aries)**.
- Moon in the remaining three caraṇas of Kṛttikā (3), Rohiṇī (4) or two caraṇas of Mṛgaśirā → **Vṛṣa (Taurus)**.

### 2.2 The easier way in a chart

> [!INFO] Definitions (as given)
> - The sign in which the **Moon** is placed = the person's **janma rāśi** (birth sign).
> - The sign placed in the **lagna (ascendant)** = the person's **lagna rāśi**.
> - **Lagna rāśi** shows *ākṛti* (appearance), *prakṛti* (nature) and *svabhāva* (temperament).
> - **Candra rāśi** (Moon sign) shows the *mānasik sthiti* (mental state).

*Reasoning:* once the mental state and the appearance/nature/temperament are understood, one can understand what kind of person the jātaka is: intellect, thinking and behaviour.

### 2.3 Daśā from nakṣatra

- The nakṣatra through which the Moon is transiting at the time of birth → **the lord of that nakṣatra gives the daśā at birth**.
- It can be found by calculation or "normally" (by knowing the lordship); both will be covered (see §4–§6).

### 2.4 Deferred uses of the nakṣatra

The teacher said the nakṣatra is also used in **avakahaḍā cakra**, **ghāta cakra**, **aṣṭakūṭa guṇa-milān (compatibility matching)** and **nāmākṣara (name syllable)**. These will be taught *after pañcāṅga*; when students are a little stronger it will be easy to understand. At this stage "we will read nakṣatras only this much; there is no need to read more now."

## 4. Mūla nakṣatras

### 4.1 Which nakṣatras

> [!INFO] Definition
> The nakṣatras of **Ketu and Budha** are called **mūla nakṣatras**: Aśvinī, Maghā, Mūla (Ketu) and Āśleṣā, Jyeṣṭhā, Revatī (Budha) — six in all. A child born in these is said to be **"born in mūla"**.

### 4.2 Traditional practice

- The father does **not see the child's face for 27 days**. After 27 days, when the same nakṣatra returns, a **śānti (propitiatory rite)** is performed and then the father sees the child's face.
- Teacher on the present day: *"Nowadays this does not happen — a photo is taken by mobile at birth, shown to everyone, sent on WhatsApp, everybody sees it. No matter."*

### 4.3 Only certain caraṇas count

Each of the six nakṣatras has four caraṇas. The teacher said **not the whole nakṣatra** is mūla:

- the **first two caraṇas of Ketu's nakṣatras** (Aśvinī, Maghā, Mūla), and
- the **last two caraṇas of Budha's nakṣatras** (Āśleṣā, Jyeṣṭhā, Revatī).

*Explanation by junctions (teacher):*

| Junction | Mūla portion |
|---|---|
| Revatī → Aśvinī | Revatī's **last two** caraṇas and Aśvinī's **first two**. Revatī's first two caraṇas join Uttara Bhādrapada and are not mūla; Aśvinī's last two go toward Bharaṇī and are not mūla. |
| Āśleṣā → Maghā | Āśleṣā's **3rd and 4th** caraṇas and Maghā's **first two**. |
| Jyeṣṭhā → Mūla | Jyeṣṭhā's **last two** and Mūla's **first two**. |

*Teacher emphasized repeatedly:* the whole nakṣatra is **not** mūlīya (mūla-affected).

> [!WARNING] Common mistake (as described by the teacher)
> Temple/less-learned astrologers look in the pañcāṅga: if at **sunrise** the nakṣatra was a Ketu nakṣatra (e.g. Aśvinī) but by the **time of birth** Bharaṇī had come, they still say "born in mūla". The teacher said *this is wrong*; it goes by the **time of birth**.

> [!SUCCESS] Rule
> Judge by the nakṣatra running **at the moment of birth** (Moon's nakṣatra), **not** by the *udayā* (sunrise) nakṣatra. The pañcāṅga writes the nakṣatra that is running at sunrise.

### 4.4 Teacher's example when a student asked him to explain "udayā" again (Student spk1)

- Example as spoken: sunrise at 6:30 with the last caraṇa of Āśleṣā running; two hours later Maghā begins. A birth in Maghā's first two caraṇas → mūla.
- But if the child was born in the evening, not even in Maghā's 3rd–4th caraṇas, and Pūrva Phālgunī had already come, then it is not mūla — even though the pañcāṅga, which shows the morning nakṣatra, shows Maghā.
- *Teacher's conclusion:* one born when Pūrva Phālgunī was running is not called mūlīya. (Pañcāṅga will be taken up later.)

### 4.5 Questions on mūla (in order)

> [!FAQ] Q (Student spk13): What is mūla nakṣatra, and what are the results if a child is born in it?
> **Teacher:** It is known to everyone: after 27 days, when the same nakṣatra returns, its śānti is performed. Yajurvedācāryas/pandits/brāhmaṇas will come, tell you the procedure and materials, and conduct the pūjā.

> [!FAQ] Q (Vishal, spk0): What happens if it is not done?
> **Teacher:** A *maraṇātmaka* (death-like) effect comes upon the parents, the maternal uncle and the child himself; any relative may be affected in different ways. *Maraṇātmaka* means trouble to life, **fear of death** — it is **not necessary** that the person dies. The śānti is done to avoid this.

> [!FAQ] Q (Vishal): Why does the "Pūrva" nakṣatra always come first and "Uttara" later? Is there a reason?
> **Teacher:** Yes, there is a reason. Pūrva = earlier, Uttara = later ("earlier Phālguna and later Phālguna"). The reason connected with months: the nakṣatra of the **full moon** gives the month its name. In the month of Āśvin, the full-moon day has the Moon in **Aśvinī** (the teacher said this is certain). The full moon of Phālguna falls in either Pūrva or Uttara Phālgunī; of Āṣāḍha in Pūrva or Uttara Āṣāḍhā; of Bhādrapada in Pūrva or Uttara Bhādrapada. Other examples: Caitra – Citrā; Vaiśākha – Viśākhā; Jyeṣṭha – Jyeṣṭhā; Pauṣa – Puṣya. The teacher said "we are very calculative", and the Pūrva/Uttara naming exists so that the full moon falls on the day of a matching nakṣatra.

> [!FAQ] Q (Student spk13): If a child is born in mūla in the morning, can the śānti be done the same afternoon/evening?
> **Teacher:** No. It will be done only **after 27 days** — the child must be pure (the *chaṭhī* stage), the mother must have bathed, etc. In our tradition a **sūtaka** (birth impurity) is observed at childbirth, so puja is not done immediately.

> [!FAQ] Q (Student spk13): In the scientific age people ask: what difference can puja make? Does that mean if puja isn't done it will be very bad?
> **Teacher:** It will not "become bad". He gave an analogy (see below).

> [!EXAMPLE] Teacher's analogy for śānti
> When you go to the Prime Minister you address him with honorifics; when someone is angry you pacify him ("keep quiet, forgive, we will not repeat the mistake"). We say the same to God: "this happened by mistake; forgive the child and protect us." Just as an angry person relents at your humble request, **chanting the mūla-nakṣatra mantras and performing the pūjā/śānti pacifies them**; what is dear to them is offered.

> [!FAQ] Q (Student spk14): Say the Moon is in Revatī. A nakṣatra takes about 20 hours to cross; if the last 10 hours (3rd and 4th caraṇas) are mūla, many children are born in those 10 hours — are all considered mūla? And the effect cannot be the same for all.
> **Teacher:** Yes, all of them are born in mūla. When someone asks him for dates for a planned operation delivery he gives 2–3 days and tries that **the mūla day is not given — "the first thing we do is protect them from mūla"**. On the question of the effect: the mūla portions of the six nakṣatras together amount to the span of **three nakṣatras** (spoken: "six nakṣatras, four-four caraṇas added, three nakṣatras" **[unclear in transcript** — consistent with the two-caraṇa rule above, 6 × 2 caraṇas = 12 caraṇas = 3 nakṣatras]); children born in that span of the month are mūla-born, all get the doṣa, and all need the śānti.

> [!FAQ] Q (Student spk15): Mūla natives have struggle in life? (Her son is Revatī 4th caraṇa; the mūla śānti was done after 27 days, with waters from various places.)
> **Teacher:** It is **life-threatening in childhood**; challenges do not come later. *"They say mūla-born children may not survive, but if they survive, none is better than them"* **[unclear in transcript** — "मुनिया", likely "मूलिया"]. After the śānti everything is fine.

> [!FAQ] Q (Student spk4): The soul comes with its prārabdha; how can we tamper with it by planning a delivery to avoid mūla?
> **Teacher:** We can, as far as possible. Analogy: before surgeons existed a heart patient died — that was his prārabdha; now with open-heart surgery or a stent the person lives. If a heart attack is in the prārabdha it will happen, and if being saved by the doctor is written, one will be saved. *"Prārabdha exists, but our karma exists too; karma transforms prārabdha, so always do karma."* He quoted the Gītā line (*karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana*): do karma; the concern for the fruit is not yours.

> [!FAQ] Q (Student spk13): How does mantra recitation affect the grahas? (Science community asks.) A person is addressed with words, but God is not embodied.
> **Teacher:** Just as using many good words for a distinguished guest pleases the guest, good words for God please God; read the meaning of a few mantras and you will see we do for God what we do for an honoured person. The deeper questions should be asked of a good *pravacanakartā*.

> [!CAUTION] Scope (teacher)
> *"We will teach jyotiṣa, not adhyātma-cintana."* Spiritual contemplation must be pursued by the students themselves. He added that doubting Īśvara leads to "crores of births in hell", that he does not want that, believes in Īśvara and asks students to do the same: "Īśvara exists by belief only."

## 5. Moon's degree → nakṣatra, caraṇa, daśā (practice with a chart-less example)

> [!EXAMPLE] Exercise: Moon at 140°
> - **Who/what:** an assumed Moon, no person or chart. Teacher: *"Forget everything else, just assume."* (Students first guessed 29° and 60°; the teacher fixed **140°**.)
> - **Chart:** none; only the Moon's longitude (0° to 360°). Teacher context: the Moon goes from 0° to 360° and "enjoys" (*bhoga* — passes over) all 27 nakṣatras in 27 days.
> - **Observed (procedure):**
>   1. Convert 140° to minutes: 140 × 60 = **8,400** minutes. (Transcript: "गुण सात" — [ASR for "गुणा साठ", ×60]; a student gave 8,400.)
>   2. Divide by **800 minutes** (one nakṣatra = 13°20' = 800'). **10 nakṣatras pass**, remainder **400'**. So the Moon has gone beyond Maghā (the 10th) and is in the **11th, Pūrva Phālgunī**.
>   3. One caraṇa = 200'. 400' = **two caraṇas completed (*gata*)**.
>   4. The Moon is therefore in Pūrva Phālgunī, **third caraṇa**.
> - **Interpretation given:** Pūrva Phālgunī → lord **Śukra**, so the daśā at birth is Śukra's.
> - **Reasoning (daśā balance):** Śukra's four caraṇas = **20 years**; one caraṇa = 5 years; two completed = 10 years; the balance is **10 years**. *"At birth Śukra's daśā of 10 years was obtained."*
> - **Outcome:** one student (spk8) did not follow the calculation; the teacher said it is not essential, a computer will do it. Another (spk12) said he would listen to the first chapter again.

> [!TIP] Shortcut by degrees (teacher)
> Karka (Cancer) spans 90°–120° and Āśleṣā ends at 120° (taught on day 1). 140° − 120° = 20°. Maghā occupies 13°20' after 120°; 20° − 13°20' = **6°40'** left, i.e. **6°40' into Pūrva Phālgunī** = two caraṇas gone (3°20' per caraṇa implied by 200').
> *Teacher's slips corrected in the same passage:* he first said "6°20'" and "13°40'" and corrected to 6°40' and 13°20'; he said "190° to 120°" meaning 90° to 120°; he first said "in the 10th nakṣatra, second caraṇa" and then correctly said 10 had passed and the 11th had come.

> | spk10 | Citrā, 2nd caraṇa | Not accepted |

> [!FAQ] Q (Student spk13): The two caraṇas are *gata* (completed) — do we call it the second caraṇa or the third?
> **Teacher:** The **third**. "Two caraṇas have *surely* passed." He had written "two caraṇas *gata*", not "in the second caraṇa".

> [!SUCCESS] Alternative method (Student spk12; teacher: "you can do that too")
> 8,400 ÷ 800 = 10.5; each **0.25 is one caraṇa**, so 0.5 = two caraṇas. The teacher said mathematics has many methods.

> [!SUCCESS] General rule (teacher)
> Whatever the Moon's degree, divide it by **13°20'**; the **remainder** gives the *bhoga* — how much of the nakṣatra has passed — and from this the daśā balance at birth.

### 7.3 Why Sūrya and Candra have one rāśi each, and the others two

> [!EXAMPLE] The teacher's story
> Sūrya's and Candra's rāśis are single: Sūrya = **Siṃha**, Candra = **Karka**. Sūrya is the king and Candra the queen; to settle a dispute "king and queen settle it". Since 12 rāśis cannot be divided into halves among 7 grahas, they took one each. Sūrya is a *puruṣa* (male) graha and took a male rāśi; Candra has female nature and took a female rāśi. **Remaining:** 12 − 2 = 10 rāśis and 7 − 2 = 5 grahas → each of the five gets two rāśis.

> [!CAUTION] Teacher's caution on gender
> All grahas are *male*; what differs is *strī-ocita* (feminine-natured) or *puruṣa-ocita* (masculine-natured) character. Candra is a *strī-kāraka* (significator of women), not a woman. "Do not say Guruji taught that Candra is a woman."

> | Śukra | Tulā | Vṛṣa |

## 8. Sign dignities and the maitrī chakra

### 8.1 Natural friendship – put aside

The teacher said that in many places students are made to **memorize** that Sūrya's friends are Candra, Maṅgala and Guru, Sūrya considers Budha neutral and Śukra and Śani enemies — *"Memorize. ## 10. Sāhacarya niyama (rule of the "companion" sign)

> [!INFO] Definition (teacher)
> **Sāhacarya** means that **one graha has two rāśis**. When one of its rāśis falls in the 2nd, 8th or 12th house, we look where its **other rāśi** falls. *(Transcript variants: "sahacarya/sāhacarya"; term kept as heard.)*

> [!SUCCESS] The rule (as built through the examples)
> 1. It applies to the lords of the **2nd, 8th and 12th** — **not** to 3rd, 6th, 11th (Student spk22 asked; teacher: no).
> 2. If the graha's **other rāśi falls in a trikoṇa (5th or 9th)** → **no doṣa** of the lordship of the 2nd/8th/12th is incurred.
> 3. If the other rāśi falls **elsewhere** (7th, 3rd, 11th…), even a kendra, since it is not trikoṇa → **doṣa applies**.
> 4. If the other rāśi falls in the **lagna**: the lagna is **not counted as a trikoṇa**, but there are exceptions: the rule of doṣa does not apply if the lord of the 8th is **a friend of the lagneśa, or is himself the lagneśa, or is the lord of a trikoṇa** *(the teacher said "two rules" but listed three conditions)*.
> 5. **Sūrya and Candra** have one rāśi each (Siṃha, Karka), so sāhacarya cannot apply; they are **exempt from the doṣa of *being the lord* of the 8th, 12th or 2nd**.
> 6. Where the doṣa applies, the lord **afflicts the house in which it sits and the graha it sits with**. Where it does not, the graha is effectively "not the 12th lord, not the 8th lord", gives no pain to the place it goes to, and is **not a māraka**.
> 7. The same condition seen in the maitrī chakra: with the lagna sign placed as the mūlatrikoṇa chart, the lord of 2/8/12 that is marked **friend** gets no doṣa; one who is not friend gets it (e.g. with Kumbha as lagna: Budha becomes friend automatically; Guru is a graha but "not friend", so it incurs the doṣa).

> [!WARNING] Common mistake (teacher)
> The Sun and Moon are exempt from the doṣa of **lordship**, **but not from being *placed* in the 8th, 12th or 2nd**. *Teacher emphasized repeatedly:* Sūrya in the 8th, 12th or 2nd is afflicted (*pīḍita*) — Sūrya is a *krūra* graha and spoils the place. Many people practise "10 years" and don't know this. "Doṣa of being the lord does not apply; doṣa of being placed there does."

### 11.1 Idea and analogy

- **Similar houses** = houses of the same group that give the same kind of result (the groups in §9).
- *Analogy:* at a wedding someone puts ₹1,001 in an envelope; when you go to his wedding you give the same (about 90% of people give back what they received; time may raise the amount but the *value* is equal — e.g. a ₹2,000 saree returned by a ₹5,000 saree of the same quality). **Grahas too, by the rāśi in which they sit, give equal result.** "No one wants to give one rupee more." The teacher said one can trust that **Śani will do for Maṅgala what Maṅgala does for Śani; Guru for Sūrya what Sūrya does for Guru; Śani for Sūrya what Sūrya does for Śani.**

> [!TIP] Method (teacher)
> 1. Take the two grahas A and B.
> 2. Make A's rāśi the lagna, and note which houses B's rāśi(s) fall in (B's results for A).
> 3. Make B's rāśi the lagna, and note which houses A's rāśi(s) fall in (A's results for B). *(If a graha has two rāśis, make a chart for each.)*
> 4. **Either the same house appears, or a house of the same group (a similar house).** "Nothing else will fall." No third rule is needed — "just the house, and the group of the house".

# Principles Compilation

## A. Nakṣatra, rāśi and daśā

1. The sign in which the Moon is placed is the **janma rāśi**; the sign in the lagna is the **lagna rāśi**.
2. **Lagna rāśi** shows appearance, nature and temperament; **Candra rāśi** shows mental state.
3. The **lord of the Moon's nakṣatra at birth** gives the **daśā at birth**.
4. **Nakṣatra lords:** Ketu — Aśvinī, Maghā, Mūla; Śukra — Bharaṇī, Pūrva Phālgunī, Pūrvāṣāḍhā; Sūrya — Kṛttikā, Uttara Phālgunī, Uttarāṣāḍhā; Candra — Rohiṇī, Hasta, Śravaṇa; Maṅgala — Mṛgaśirā, Citrā, Dhaniṣṭhā; Rāhu — Ārdrā, Svāti, Śatabhiṣā; Guru — Punarvasu, Viśākhā, Pūrva Bhādrapada; Śani — Puṣya, Anurādhā, Uttara Bhādrapada; Budha — Āśleṣā, Jyeṣṭhā, Revatī.
5. Counting the nakṣatras 1–27 and adding digits to one digit gives the graha (1 Ketu, 2 Śukra, 3 Sūrya, 4 Candra, 5 Maṅgala; 6–9 by the groups above).
6. **Moon's degree ÷ 13°20' (800')**: whole nakṣatras passed + remainder = how much of the current nakṣatra is *gata*. A caraṇa = 200'.
7. **Two caraṇas gata → third caraṇa.**
8. **Daśā balance:** for Śukra, 4 caraṇas = 20 years; each caraṇa = 5 years; two gata → 10 years balance.
9. **Daśā order (years):** Śukra 20, Sūrya 6, Candra 10, Maṅgala 7, Rāhu 18, Guru 16, Śani 19, Budha 17, Ketu 7; daśās after the birth daśā follow this order.

## B. Mūla

10. **Mūla nakṣatras** are the six of **Ketu and Budha**: Aśvinī, Maghā, Mūla, Āśleṣā, Jyeṣṭhā, Revatī.
11. Only the **first two caraṇas of Ketu's** and the **last two caraṇas of Budha's** nakṣatras are mūla — not the whole nakṣatra.
12. Mūla is judged by the **nakṣatra at the moment of birth**, **not** by the sunrise (*udayā*) nakṣatra written in the pañcāṅga.
13. The **śānti** is done **after 27 days**, when the same nakṣatra returns; not the same day, because of the **sūtaka** and the child's/mother's purity.
14. Without the śānti the effect is *maraṇātmaka* (fear of death/trouble to life) on parents, maternal uncle, child and relatives; the danger is in **childhood** and not later challenges.
15. Karma transforms prārabdha; planning a delivery to avoid a mūla day is permitted "as far as possible".
16. The **full moon** of Āśvin falls in Aśvinī; the months of Phālguna, Āṣāḍha and Bhādrapada have a full moon in Pūrva or Uttara of their nakṣatra; "Pūrva" = earlier, "Uttara" = later.

## C. Rāśi ownership

17. **Lords of the rāśis (1–12):** Maṅgala, Śukra, Budha, Candra, Sūrya, Budha, Śukra, Maṅgala, Guru, Śani, Śani, Guru.
18. **Sūrya** owns only Siṃha; **Candra** only Karka. The other five each own **two rāśis, one male (puruṣa) and one female (strī)** — and also **one saumya and one krūra** — so **every graha has both natures**.
19. All grahas are male; "strī" or "puruṣa" refers to the **nature** (Candra is a strī-kāraka, not a woman).
20. **Dignity table (spoken numbers):** Sūrya own 5, exalted 1, debilitated 7, mūlatrikoṇa Siṃha; Candra own 4, exalted Vṛṣa, debilitated Vṛścika, mūlatrikoṇa Vṛṣa *(for this method)*; Maṅgala own 1, 8, exalted Makara, debilitated Karka, mūlatrikoṇa Meṣa; Budha own 3, 6, exalted Kanyā (6), debilitated Mīna (12), mūlatrikoṇa Kanyā; Guru own 9, 12, exalted Karka, debilitated Makara, mūlatrikoṇa Dhanu; Śukra own 2, 7, exalted Mīna, debilitated Kanyā, mūlatrikoṇa Tulā; Śani own 10, 11, exalted Tulā, debilitated Meṣa, mūlatrikoṇa Kumbha.
21. Mūlatrikoṇa is an **aṃśa**, not the whole rāśi.

## D. Maitrī chakra

22. Make the graha's **mūlatrikoṇa rāśi** the lagna; mark **friend in 2, 4, 5, 8, 9, 12**; mark friend also in its **own exaltation rāśi**; all the rest **enemy**.
23. A graha with two rāśis: **both friend → friend; both enemy → enemy; one each → neutral (sama)**.
24. Only the **exaltation of the graha being examined** is turned to friend; **Rāhu and Ketu** take no part.
25. For Candra, use **Vṛṣa** as mūlatrikoṇa (Karka gives a wrong answer); Candra has **no enemy**; Candra's friends are **Sūrya and Budha**; its own rāśi in an enemy house is **ignored**.
26. Sūrya's chart: friends Candra, Maṅgala, Guru; neutral Budha; enemies Śukra, Śani. Śukra's chart: enemies Sūrya, Candra; neutral Maṅgala, Guru; friends Budha, Śani. Candra–Śukra: sama. Guru–Śani: sama (both ways).
27. Budha regards Candra as an enemy; Candra regards Budha as a friend. Sūrya and Śani are not enemies in the "stone for stone" sense.
28. **No graha can be akāraka** (teacher's claim).

## E. House classifications

29. **Kendra:** 1, 4, 7, 10 — *śubha* places, but **kendra lords are not always auspicious** (their lords can be enemies of each other).
30. **Trikoṇa:** 1, 5, 9, counted as every 4th place; **1 is counted among kendras**; **trikoṇa lords are never enemies** (sometimes sama) → yoga results.
31. **3, 6, 11:** aśubha sthāna — but the **6th** causes the trouble; 3 and 11 are good.
32. **6, 8, 12:** duḥkha sthāna / trika bhāva.
33. **2 and 7:** māraka sthāna; the **2nd is the stronger māraka** because its 7th aspect falls on the 8th (the ariṣṭa sthāna) and activates it; the 7th's aspect falls on the lagna.
34. **2, 8, 12:** sama sthāna (good or not good); the sāhacarya rule applies here. Houses of one group give **similar** results.

## F. Sāhacarya niyama

35. Sāhacarya applies to the lordship of **2, 8, 12**, **not** 3, 6, 11.
36. If the lord's **other rāśi is in a trikoṇa (5 or 9)** → **no** doṣa; if it is elsewhere (even in a kendra such as the 7th) → **doṣa applies**.
37. If the other rāśi is in the **lagna** (not counted as trikoṇa): no doṣa if the lord is **himself lagneśa, a friend of the lagneśa, or a trikoṇa lord**.
38. **Sūrya and Candra** have one rāśi each: **no doṣa of lordship** of 2/8/12; but **placed** in 2/8/12 they are afflicted.
39. If the doṣa applies, the lord **afflicts the place it sits in and the graha it sits with**; if not, it is **not** a māraka and does not pain the place it goes to.
40. Sitting in a **trikoṇa does not cancel** a doṣa that applies, and a doṣa that does not apply does not work there.
41. In the maitrī chart of the lagna sign, the lord of 2/8/12 marked **friend → no doṣa**; **not friend → doṣa**.

## G. Similar-house (reciprocity) rule — the teacher's own research

42. Two grahas **return equal results** to each other: for A and B, with each rāśi of A as lagna look at B's rāśis' houses, and vice versa.
43. Either the **same house** or a **house of the same group** appears; no other rule is needed.
44. **Ownership (svāmitva)** gives the same kind of result; **position (sthiti)** changes it.
45. Ultimately **the lord of the house** has to deliver the result.
46. Examples: Sūrya–Śani (6th/7th ↔ 8th/7th), Guru–Śani (2, 3, 11, 12 both ways), Candra–Budha (12, 3 ↔ 2, 11).

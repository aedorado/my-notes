## 8. Jyotiṣa as Vedāṅga, and its place in the world

### 8.1 Vedāṅga, not Vedānta
- **Teacher** (responding to Brijbhushan's mention of Vedānta/Vedāṅga): the two differ slightly. Jyotiṣa is **Vedāṅga**: the **sixth limb (aṅga) of the Veda**, called the **netra (eye)**.
- It shows the Veda its path. Nothing happens without *tithi* (lunar day) and *muhūrta*, and the Veda does not advance a step without tithi-muhūrta.

> [!EXAMPLE] Teacher's example: Āyurveda
> To uproot a medicinal herb, Āyurveda also seeks a tithi for the day of uprooting: whether the potency (*tāsīr*) will be better if uprooted on *pūrṇimā* (full moon) or on *amāvasyā* (new moon). The teacher presented this to show how far muhūrta-thinking reaches. *(He posed the choice as a question and did not state which is better.)*

### 8.2 Jyotiṣa and science
- Jyotiṣa showed the world the path; it lags today because "we pushed it back".
- If jyotiṣīs had not given information about the cosmos, scientists would not have pursued it; science has come this far by walking the path jyotiṣa showed.
- **Rāhu and Ketu** are called *chāyā-graha* (shadow planets) and imaginary points. Today even NASA must accept them, because without them an eclipse cannot be declared.
- Therefore the teacher says **jyotiṣa is the originator of science**; it is unfortunate that jyotiṣa fell so far behind.

### 8.3 The teacher on *calita* (shifting planets between houses)
> [!WARNING] Teacher's position
> - The matter of *calita* is *mithyā* (false).
> - In **Ṣaḍbala** (sixfold strength), the *daśama madhya* (midpoint of the 10th house) is considered, and there a planet sometimes goes up or down. But it is **never written** that one should shift the planet up or down in the phalita chart: "nowhere".
> - If anyone can read out a śloka showing it is written that a planet is to be moved up or down for reading, he will teach it.
> - He says KP teachers teach it (as he hears from his students), and Vedic teachers also tell it, but in Vedic there is no written rule for it. (The transcript says "वैदिक में स्थिति बताने का कहीं कोई rule नहीं लिखा"; wording **[unclear in transcript]**.)
> - **Reason given:** the Vedic teacher where one goes to learn often does not himself know what is and is not in Vedic jyotiṣa, since he never read the books; he taught what his guru taught, is earning well, and has no reason to find out which part is Vedic.
> - Closing remark: whoever wants to receive in a certain way gets a guru of that kind.

*Teacher emphasized repeatedly:* *calita* is false and has no scriptural basis.

## 9. How the course will proceed: following the Veda

> [!IMPORTANT] Approach
> Study jyotiṣa in the way the Veda is studied; then knowledge will open. *("उसी star को लेकर" in the transcript: the phrase is **[unclear in transcript]**.)* The subjects the Veda speaks of will be solved through jyotiṣa as well.

### 9.1 Three guṇas
- *Sattva, rajas, tamas.* The class will study which planets and which signs (*rāśi*) have which guṇa.

### 9.2 Five tattvas (elements)
- The whole world is made of **five tattvas**; science has not found a sixth.
- The class will study which planets are the **kāraka** (significator) of each tattva.

| Tattva | Kāraka as taught | Error the teacher pointed out |
|---|---|---|
| Jala (water) | **Śukra (Venus)** | Even big astrologers look at the Moon instead of Venus; they do not know the difference between Moon and Venus |
| Agni (fire) | **Maṅgala (Mars)** | They say look at the Sun; "no, look at Mars" |

- The teacher did not say where such people acquire their knowledge.
- **Reading advice:** even 10–20 pages of Bṛhat Parāśara Horā would give basic information. He asked (the addressee is unclear in the transcript) that the next 10–20 pages of the PDF he sent be read.
- Knowing the five tattvas tells us what is what.

### 9.3 Varṇas and directions
- Varṇa-based analysis is done in the **Gītā**, and varṇas are discussed in the Vedas; "if we do not discuss varṇas in jyotiṣa, what do we do?"
- There are **four varṇas**; the class will understand which varṇa belongs to which planet.
- **Directions (diśā):** the class will understand which direction is ruled by which planet.

### 9.4 Classification of planets (the teacher's own scheme)

| | Earlier teaching | Now taught as |
|---|---|---|
| Scheme | **7 + 2**: seven planets Sun to Saturn, plus Rāhu and Ketu = nine | **2 + 5 + 2** |
| The "2" | | **Sūrya (Sun) and Candra (Moon)** |
| The "5" | | **Maṅgala, Budha, Guru, Śukra, Śani** (Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn), called *pañcatārā* **[spelling per transcript; ASR possible]** |
| The last "2" | | **Rāhu and Ketu** |

- Sun and Moon are *tārā* (luminaries/stars). The planets *in the root sense* are only the five: Mars, Mercury, Guru, Venus, Saturn.
- The **greatest rājayoga** is formed when these five planets form it: the **Mahāpuruṣa yogas**. The teacher named **Mālavya** and **Ruchaka**; the first name in the sentence is **[unclear in transcript]** ("अंत महापुरुष"). He said "Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus form them"; Saturn is not named in that sentence.
- **Teacher stressed:** if Sun and Moon are not understood separately, jyotiṣa will not be understood.

### 9.5 Sun and Moon as father and mother
- Quoted verse: *sūryo pitā ca jantūnāṁ candro mātā tathaiva ca*.
- The Sun is the father of all living beings; the Moon is the mother of all, the nourisher.
- So the Sun is understood as the **life-giver** and the Moon as the **nourishing mother**; Sun and Moon will be studied in this way, and Mars, Mercury, Guru, Venus, Saturn separately.

### 9.6 Method for finding the kāraka of any object

> [!TIP] Method
> 1. Identify which **tattva** the object belongs to.
> 2. The planet connected with that tattva is its **kāraka**.
>
> Reason given: it is the tattva that connects things to planets. This applies to any object one wants information about.

# Principles Compilation

### A. Nature and place of Jyotiṣa
1. Jyotiṣa is **Vedāṅga**: the sixth limb of the Veda, called the eye (netra); it shows the Veda its path.
2. Nothing proceeds in the Veda without tithi-muhūrta (example: the tithi for uprooting a herb in Āyurveda).
3. Rāhu and Ketu are shadow planets / imaginary points, yet even NASA must accept them to declare eclipses.
4. The teacher holds jyotiṣa to be the originator of science.

### B. Planet classification
5. Teach and read planets as **2 + 5 + 2**: Sun and Moon; Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn; Rāhu and Ketu.
6. Sun and Moon must be understood separately from the five, or jyotiṣa will not be understood.
7. The planets in the root sense are only five: Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn.
8. The Mahāpuruṣa rājayogas (including Mālavya and Ruchaka) come from these five planets. *(Condition: as the teacher said, "Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus form them"; Saturn not named in that sentence.)*
9. Sun = father, life-giver; Moon = mother, nourisher of all (*sūryo pitā ca jantūnāṁ candro mātā tathaiva ca*).

### C. Kāraka method
10. **Water (jala) tattva → Venus**, not the Moon.
11. **Fire (agni) tattva → Mars**, not the Sun.
12. To find the kāraka of any object: (a) find the object's tattva; (b) the planet connected to that tattva is its kāraka.
13. Five tattvas make the whole world; no sixth has been found. Also to be studied: three guṇas (of planets and signs), four varṇas (of planets), and the direction ruled by each planet.

### D. Scope rules (what to apply now / leave for later)
14. Use only the **five planets** plus Sun, Moon, Rāhu, Ketu; do **not** study Pluto, Neptune, Herschel (they show on the zodiac once in 125 years, then recede; life is shorter than that).
15. The class is **phalita only**: no gaṇita taught.
16. Mathematics is needed mainly for chart-making, daśā determination and graha-spaṣṭa; not much elsewhere.
17. **Do not apply bhāva-calita** (shifting planets between houses). The teacher holds it has no scriptural rule; only *daśama madhya* appears in Ṣaḍbala.
18. **Praśna and varṣa** (tājika) are for later (course in January); gocara is covered.
19. Nakṣatras are not part of this phalita class except within gocara and where needed; the detailed study is Minu madam's class.
20. Pañcāṅga is taught within the Muhūrta course; it is not necessary to begin this course but is needed for making charts and finding positions; it is gaṇita.
21. For now read only the recommended books; after the class read Tārāvalī and Bṛhat Jātaka.

### E. Study method
22. Ask questions on the same day's subject for better linking.
23. No rote memorization is required; the teacher will do the work.
24. Keep watching YouTube alongside the class (fine detail there; deep contemplation and complete course here).
